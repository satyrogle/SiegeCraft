# Show the fight, hide the Sovereign

**The stills trailer failed at the root, not in the details. None of the eight shipped indie trailers the researchers sampled used story stills, and the three that opened on story had full hand animation plus a publisher, a franchise or a showcase slot behind them.** Valve says Discovery Queue viewers give a video less than ten seconds and tells you to plan for no sound, and one studio lost about a third of its viewers in the first two seconds. So the new approach is one rule for both jobs: the game films everything. Build the prologue in engine with the real sprites on a real battle stage, record it headless, and cut the trailer from that scene and from real fights. Open on play, land the cult's boast and its wipe in the first six seconds, then sell the hook as a chain: an order changes a fight, then a tribe learns your trick and the why line proves it. The Sovereign stays a rule, not a picture: one sound, a palette drain, one white frame, and half the army simply gone. PixelLab pays off on sprites from your approved designs, not on stills or video, and the game already holds AI made art, so Steam's disclosure applies whatever the trailer shows. **Two missing assets block the punchiest beats: the cult soldiers have no sprites, and no battle stage is approved yet.** Only one piece can come from what exists today, a short clip of a tribe learning your trick, and even that needs the orders and the why line on screen, which is not checked. And the 1440x900 recordings are the wrong master: record at 1x, crop to 480x270 and scale by exactly 4.

## Story stills copied nobody who shipped

The researchers sampled eight official trailers frame by frame with the sound off on 16 Oct. That is a small sample, and no source tied wishlists or sales to any of them, so read it as a pattern, not proof. The fast ones show play almost at once. **Vampire Survivors shows play at 0.3 seconds**, then a card at 2 seconds boasting 120,000 positive reviews and a card at 4 seconds reading 0 vampires ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=aS7JqyHdQQA)). Loop Hero shows its loop road by about 4 seconds ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G37bO9vg8RY)), and Into the Breach gives one pedigree card at half a second and its grid at 4 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sJR1OpHNgzc)). The slow ones paid for the wait. Cult of the Lamb's reveal is wordless hand animation, with red eyed figures around the lamb at 10 seconds and play at 35 to 55 ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6uM7CQGZ6rE)). Kingdom Rush Vengeance holds play until about 30 seconds ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hTdWNCxofTc)), and Hades goes from its studio logo straight into hand drawn animated fights ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Bz8l935Bv0Y)). The best case for story first is Ironhide's own Vengeance teaser, which had no gameplay at all ([Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-rush-vengeance/the-kingdom-rush-series-is-making-a-much-awaited-comeback/)), but it was selling a new game in a known series. Derek Lieu says a long intro without play only suits an established studio ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/19/hades-ii-early-access-trailer-review)). Simon Carless says slow burn reveals suit live showcase crowds, not Steam shoppers, and by his estimate play that first shows up 15 to 30 seconds in is too late. Chris Zukowski found viewers skip along the bar hunting for raw gameplay ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/game-trailers-breaking-down-a-key)).

Steam's own guidance points the same way. Valve shows the first two trailers before the screenshots, wants the first one to be mostly gameplay with the HUD showing, and warns that **Discovery Queue viewers have less than ten seconds, and some watch with no sound** ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer)). Failbetter lost **about 35% of viewers in the first two seconds** and found its sweet spot at 50 to 60 seconds ([PCGamesInsider](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/interviews-and-opinion/68588/insight-how-effective-were-failbetters-trailers-for-sunless-skies/)). Zukowski says a minute is fine, and The Alters' 3:23 cut lost viewers steadily while its 1:30 cut did not ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2025/09/25/a-deep-dive-in-the-games-of-the-spring-showcases/)). The sources split on length. Lieu's simplest Steam trailer runs 90 to 180 seconds of 3 to 5 second shots, with the HUD on, the core loop first, nothing shown more than three times, and no logos or cinematics the player doesn't drive ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/the-simplest-trailer-to-make-for-your-steam-page)). With the assets you have, a minute is the honest length.

The laughs in these trailers come from deadpan cards and deaths, not from story. Vampire Survivors' boast then undercut won Lieu's best copy of 2022 ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/12/23/my-favorite-game-trailers-of-2022)). Noita's launch trailer plays disasters that end in death as punchlines, with a deliberate pause before a chain reaction ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/10/17/noita-10-launch-trailer)). Dead Cells shows its resurrection three times, which Lieu calls the rule of threes ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/11/14/the-rule-of-threes-in-game-trailers)). The one scary thing is always a trace. Inscryption's dealer is two glowing eyes in the dark at 6 seconds and is never lit ([YouTube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Rl3NTzzw-2o)). Kingdom Rush Vengeance has a glowing figure in darkness at 25 seconds, and Cult of the Lamb has red eyes at 10. That is the shape Kindred Siege needs: the cult's boast, the undercut, deaths and nerve breaks as punchlines, and her only as a trace.

Text has to read muted without fighting the picture. Hades II's cards fill the screen for a beat, then sit at the top of the frame while play runs ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/19/hades-ii-early-access-trailer-review)). Never ask people to read text and watch play in the same shot, and test the cut with every card deleted ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/9/14/10-common-indie-game-trailer-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them)). Write cards from three words and lead with verbs ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/4/19/how-to-write-trailer-title-cards-with-strong-messaging)), and skip opening logos unless people know the studio ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2021/3/29/where-to-put-logos-in-a-game-trailer)). One Steam detail moves the cards. Steam builds a 6 second microtrailer from six 1 second clips of your first visible video, and you can't pick them ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer)). So in the first trailer, put each card over a frozen game frame, not black, then park it at the top. Any second Steam grabs then still shows the game (opinion).

## The hook sells as an order and its result, inside ten seconds

The auto battlers that broke out were carried by demos and streamers, not trailers. Backpack Battles went influencer first, with a publisher who said visuals were not its strength, ran a demo from June 2023 with about 38 weekly patches, and sold **640,000 units by April 2024** ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-backpack-battles-sold-650k-copies)). Peglin had about 66,000 wishlists a week before launch, sold 80,204 units in week one, and its demo's median playtime was 67 minutes ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/05/04/how-peglin-made-1049413-in-1-week/)). The We Harvest Shadows case comes down to five words: graphics attract, the demo sells ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2024/11/18/how-we-harvest-shadows-220000-wishlists-in-3-months/)). So the trailer has a narrow job. Earn the click and make the hook readable in seconds. The demo does the selling.

Strategy trailers are hard because decisions are invisible and change is slow. Lieu's fixes are to start with one element and add complexity, colour code the sides, punch in to close ups, show the HUD in only a few shots, and make hidden systems visible ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/11/2/how-to-make-a-trailer-for-a-realtime-strategy-game)). His Tactical Breach Wizards trailer gives rewind its own beat (die, rewind, retry, succeed) with no card, just a giant zoomed Rewind button and a sound, and keeps the HUD on for the first command only ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/10/24/tactical-breach-wizards-gameplay-trailer)). Show the consequence, not just the action ([Premortem Games](https://premortem.games/2023/01/05/want-to-make-a-great-game-trailer-show-the-consequence-of-actions/)). Kindred Siege has something pure auto battlers lack: input inside the fight. Treat each order like that Rewind button. Zoom on it, give it a sound, and cut straight to what it does.

Games with enemies that remember sold the idea when the memory had a face, a mark and one specific past event. At E3 2014, Shadow of Mordor's orcs taunted you for retreating, and in the live demo the crowd picked a captain, the hero died, and that captain grew stronger ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2014/06/e3_2014_shadow_of_mordor_details_its_incredibly_interesting_and_dynamic_nemesis_system); [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/previews/64479-middleearth-shadow-of-mordor-preview)). Its designer wanted villains who survive and return with scars you can see ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-draws-from-burnout-football-and-an-architecture-book/)). **Metal Gear Solid V sold a learning enemy in one beat: a tranquilliser dart bounces off a new helmet, because you overused headshots** ([Outcyders](https://www.outcyders.net/news/metal-gear-solid-v-the-phantom-pain-conference-gamescom-2014)). Shadow of War even sold enemy memory as comedy in its TV spots ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/348133-middle-earth-shadow-wars-tv-trailers-hilarious)), which suits the cult. Alien: Isolation only gave the impression of learning, and players spread the claim that it learns for it ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation); [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/alienisolation-20240823-307508/)). Your why line can beat that, because it is true and anyone can check it.

The researchers sketched a ten second version, and nobody has tested it. For the first 3 seconds, Hold parries a tribe's cast with big, readable feedback. From 3 to 5, the same tribe comes back with the same face and colours, and the why line pops up as zoomed in game UI with a sting, held long enough to read muted. From 5 to 8, you Hold again, they wait it out, and the cast lands. From 8 to 10, a different order answers it, or the tribe breaks on nerve and joins. Show the trick succeed first, so the change reads as an answer to your skill and not a random spike. Into the Breach's Justin Ma wanted every death to feel like the player's own fault, with the reason clear ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)), and the why line does that job here. Use one lesson per short clip and two or three across the full trailer. **A clip like this needs only squads that already have sprites, so it is the one trailer piece you can make now.** Build it from a lesson the sim already teaches. Whether the art build shows orders and the why line on screen is not checked. One risk sits outside this research: the notes did not look at the Nemesis patent, and that check is already with your solicitor.

## Only the game itself keeps story scenes on model

Small teams tell most of their story in engine with the game's own sprites and ration the expensive set pieces. Blasphemous made only a few hand painted pixel cutscenes because they were so hard to produce ([Gamereactor](https://www.gamereactor.eu/the-game-kitchen-on-blasphemous-ambiguous-story/)). Iconoclasts' solo developer worried that hours spent on cutscenes came out of gameplay ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/the-10-year-making-of-iconoclasts)). Darkest Dungeon had one artist, kept the narrator it hired for its first teaser, and opens after the disaster ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon)). Dead Cells rendered small 3D models as pixel art to keep its characters consistent, and paid with flicker and a custom renderer ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/art-design-deep-dive-using-a-3d-pipeline-for-2d-animation-in-i-dead-cells-i-)). Everyone fights the same consistency problem, and every fix is one source. Your one source is the approved sprite.

The researchers ranked the options for one developer. First is a scripted scene on the real battle stage with the real sprites, run by a sequencer: moves, reaction poses, hard cuts, hit stop, flash frames, shake, effects and sound. It stays on model by construction, and the only new art is a few sprite states. Second is text cards over black, and silence. Third is a few inserts drawn at native pixel size, such as a face or a hand. Fourth is narration over in engine action, the Darkest Dungeon way, if the writing carries it. Stills with parallax, comic panels, full hand animation and Spine puppets follow, in that order. **Every still and every panel is a fresh drawing of each character at a new angle, which is exactly where your AI stills drifted.**

Comic panels need a straight answer, because Kingdom Rush uses them. Frontiers tells its story in comic panels at the start of each level ([HonestGamers](https://www.honestgamers.com/14686/switch/kingdom-rush-frontiers/review.html)) and credits dedicated comic artists and a comic writer ([fan wiki](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Kingdom_Rush:_Frontiers)). But Kingdom Rush's game art is illustration, so its comics matched for free. A pixel game's comics don't. The one comic version that stays on model is panels cropped from your own in engine frames, the same budget trick Max Payne played with photo panels ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/the-max-payne-comics-will-explain-why-rockstars-hard-bo-5907413)). That idea is mine, not the notes'. Animated illustration is out too. Cult of the Lamb's launch trailer and Hades II's reveal came from paid animation studios ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/12/23/my-favorite-game-trailers-of-2022)), and full hand animation costs the most per second of anything here. **Stills also fail on a point that has nothing to do with drift: a still has no speed.** Even if PixelLab keeps the leader on model, a picture can't show an army dying in one second, and that second is the whole opening.

Unity has the parts. Timeline sequences cinematics and gameplay together ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.timeline@1.7/manual/index.html)). Cinemachine blends are not pixel perfect until they end ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.cinemachine@2.10/manual/CinemachinePixelPerfect.html)), so cut, don't blend. URP 14's Pixel Perfect Camera expects one pixels per unit value for every sprite, with point filtering ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.render-pipelines.universal@14.0/manual/2d-pixelperfect.html)), and fractional scaling mixes pixel sizes ([itch.io](https://itch.io/post/13463827)). At 480x300, a 2x punch in shows a 240x150 window and a 3x shows 160x100, both on the grid, so hide the UI while punched in. A real close up of the leader should be a native size drawing, around a 64 to 96 pixel bust in his palette, never an upscaled sprite or an AI still. The Pals exist only as portraits, which is enough for talk boxes but not for action. **Built in engine, the prologue doubles as trailer footage**: the recorder takes it at 1x, and its first seconds become the trailer's cold open with no extra art. The 14 Oct intro report's rules still hold for the in game version: short on every launch, skippable after the first time, cuts instead of tweens.

## The Sovereign works as a sound, a drain and a gap

Film and games scare with less. Spielberg says Jaws is scarier with less shark ([Dark Horizons](https://www.darkhorizons.com/spielberg-on-jaws-regrets-over-shark-harm/)). Ridley Scott says that if you show the monster too often, people get used to him ([Yahoo](https://malaysia.news.yahoo.com/alien-40-ridley-scott-explains-064310528.html)). In Puss in Boots: The Last Wish, Death stays hidden early, and his entrance is a whistle while the colours drain ([TheWrap](https://thewrap.com/puss-in-boots-oscars-director-death)). Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 ambushes its expedition as it lands and kills most of them ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clair_Obscur:_Expedition_33)). EarthBound's Giygas is a swirling red face with no clear body ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giygas)), and Undertale's Omega Flowey breaks the game's pixel look on purpose ([Undertale wiki](https://undertale.fandom.com/wiki/Omega_Flowey)). A light game can break its own style once, for its one horror.

Kingdom Rush is the model for the cult, not for her. Its villain Vez'nan plays the lovable idiot tyrant ([CGM](https://www.cgmagonline.com/review/game/kingdom-rush-vengeance-mobile-review/)), so he is part of the joke. Handsome Jack stayed hateable through constant presence ([GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/u8r4)), and Cult of the Lamb uses its cute style as a lens on dark content ([Devolver](https://www.devolverdigital.com/blog/post/Cult-of-the-Lamb-Dont-Starve-Together-Crossover-but-make-it-an-interview)). The researchers' rules for her follow from that. She never jokes, and no joke lands on her. Comedy stops when she arrives and starts again only after a beat of silence. One sound, one colour behaviour and one rendering rule belong to her alone, such as no outline, a colour outside the master palette, or pure light. She stays present through what she leaves behind, and she is rationed.

They also named why the still of her standing on the fallen leader failed. It answered what she looks like. She was smaller than the leader under her, so she read as cute. Standing on a beaten foe is a comic victory pose. A still has no speed. And off model AI art looked pasted in. My read on her line is that it is a metaphor for contempt, so stage the contempt instead of printing it. She doesn't fight. Half the army is gone before anyone swings, she doesn't bother with a second blow, and she is gone before the dust settles. The humiliation lands on the leader, and the researchers offer his weapon cut in half, his own banner falling on him, and his officers staring at him. The laugh comes back on the cult only after the silence. Keep the camera on the army's side, with the attack coming from off frame, from above or from the dark edge of the stage. The most of her you ever show is an edge, a hand, eyes in the dark or a shadow, vague enough not to fight her design later. If the opening plays as an auto battle, let the player give orders until she arrives, then take control away, so her attack reads as not a fight at all. And since Valve warns that some viewers watch with no sound, the palette drain and the white frame have to carry her alone. The sound is the bonus for people who unmute (opinion).

The 14 Oct intro report got the shape right and her presence wrong. It matches the new notes on cutting instead of tweening, on one sound owning the moment, on jokes landing on the cult, and on confidence followed by a wipe. These parts no longer hold:

| The 14 Oct report said | The new notes and your calls say | So |
|---|---|---|
| Show her line as small plain text and hold it for 1.5 seconds, in all three outlines | Her line is a metaphor. She never jokes, and a printed line turns contempt into a bit | Drop the words and stage the contempt |
| She lands on the leader with her foot on him, in outlines A and B | That is the rejected still: it shows her, reads as a comic victory pose and has no speed | Drop it and put the humiliation on him |
| One frame of her full silhouette, in outlines A and C | An edge, a hand, eyes or a shadow at most, so nothing fights her design later | Shrink it to an edge or a shadow |
| Painted plates are cheap because they need paintings, not code | Every new picture costs credits or your hours, plus an approval, and the battle stage is needed anyway | Reuse the title and the battle stage |

## PixelLab pays on sprites, and the disclosure applies anyway

No public report was found of anyone making story cutscenes, key art or trailer shots with PixelLab or Retro Diffusion. The reports cover sprites and sprite animation. One reviewer rates PixelLab the standout tool and says its style reference makes matching art easy, but finds its docs out of date and burning credits on experiments frustrating ([Jonathan Yu](https://www.jonathanyu.xyz/2025/12/31/pixellab-review-the-best-ai-tool-for-2d-pixel-art-games/)). The limits are hard. A Pro scene image takes up to four labelled references plus a style image, tops out at 688x384 at 16:9, and a 342 to 512 pixel square costs 25 generations ([PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-sl-image-pro)). Every animation tool stops at 256x256, so a 480x300 scene can't be animated in one pass ([PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/animate-with-text-pro)). PixelLab says errors pile up over chained generations and animation needs some cleanup by hand ([PixelLab guide](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/guides/rotating-a-character)), and references should be 1x art, because upscaled pixel art gives poor output ([PixelLab API docs](https://api.pixellab.ai/v2/docs)).

The arithmetic says where the credits go. **About 2,300 generations buys roughly 92 Pro scene images**, and at two to four tries per keeper that is about 23 to 46 approved stills for the whole game. The plain animate tool charges by total pixels, so a 64x64 sprite over 8 frames costs 1 generation and a 128x128 sprite over 16 frames costs 4 ([PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/animate-with-text-pro); the tool's own description gives the same figures). That is hundreds of small sprite animations for the price of those stills, though that tool's quality on your sprites is not checked. Your Pro test fits the wider evidence. Reference images are the lever in every tool, identity mostly holds within one call, proportions drift between calls, and one or two characters per generation is the safe load ([roboticape](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)). So spend credits on sprites from approved designs, one or two characters at a time. Then lock each one to the master palette and check its silhouette, colour count and signature piece against the character sheet.

Image to video does not rescue it. Every source that tried it on pixel art says raw output breaks the grid. The best open pipeline makes 33 frames with Wan 2.2, keeps 6 or 12, redraws each one, then forces one grid and one palette. It needs a 24 GB card, small wobbles remain, and its author warns that simply scaling the video down ruins it ([GitHub](https://github.com/cueqzapper/pixel-video-sprites)). Kling's own guide says keep motion slow and small, because big camera moves blur pixel edges ([Kling](https://kling.ai/topics/animate-pixel-art-ai)). No shipped game trailer made this way was found, and your card has 12 GB. The one disclosed shipped precedent for AI in a game's art used it only as a base for some backgrounds and props, then edited it heavily by hand ([Totally Human](https://www.totallyhuman.io/blog/the-surprising-number-of-steam-games-that-use-genai)). Local ComfyUI made your approved title but lost the characters, so it stays off the trailer path.

Steam rewrote its disclosure form on 16 and 17 January 2026. Press reports say content in the game, on the store page or in marketing must be disclosed, and the disclosure shows on the store page ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/01/valve-tweak-steam-ai-disclosure-form-for-developers-to-clarify-its-for-content-consumed-by-players/)). The public Steamworks page names neither store pages nor marketing ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)), and the live form sits behind the login, so treat marketing as covered. SteamDB lets players filter out disclosed games ([VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/steamdb-now-lets-players-filter-out-games-that-use-generative-ai)). In one study of 9,879 Steam games, disclosed games got **about 52.6% fewer reviews**, but that is correlation only and a separate paper disputes its control group ([Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Study-suggests-AI-is-hurting-video-game-sales.1328725.0.html)). A study of 508,192 reviews found lower recommendation rates, with players reading AI use as low effort from the developer ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11539)). Trailers are where it blows up. Postal: Bullet Paradise was cancelled a day or two after its reveal trailer drew AI claims ([VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/postal-game-developer-shuts-down-after-its-project-was-cancelled-due-to-allegations-of-generative-ai-use)). LEVEL5 apologised and promised to keep AI out of its promotional videos ([Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/level-5-says-some-suspected-ai-oddities-from-trailers-were-actually-human-errors-apologizes-for-fueling-doubt-with-careless-use-of-ai-in-some-of-its-videos/)). Fans caught wrong finger counts and a swapped signature prop in the James Pond Legacy trailer, and the publisher's denial didn't hold ([Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2026/08/james-pond-publisher-denies-ai-generated-trailer-but-no-ones-buying-it)). Project Shadowglass was made by hand and still got accused, because it copied the viral AI dark fantasy pixel look ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Shadowglass)).

**Bad news: your approved title came out of local ComfyUI and your squads out of PixelLab, so the disclosure already applies.** The goal is not an AI free trailer. It is a trailer with no AI look: only footage the game renders, one pixel size, one palette, one light. Write the disclosure plainly and say what was made how, because a clear disclosure beats a denial that cracks. Keep work in progress files and layered files as evidence, because your 80s dark fantasy pixel look sits close to that viral style.

## The 1440x900 recording is the wrong master

Steam takes uploads up to 1920x1080, at 30 or 60 fps and 5,000 kbps or more, with H.264, AAC and 16:9 preferred. Valve warns that unusual resolutions are a common cause of failed processing ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer)). Your recorder writes 1440x900, which is 16:10 at an odd 3x scale. The odd scale causes a second problem. Video stores colour at a lower resolution than brightness, so pixel art bleeds colour unless it was scaled by an even factor first, and by one forum's account 3x still bleeds ([TASVideos](https://tasvideos.org/Forum/Posts/443481)). So the 66 second Bloodridge fight needs recording again. If you have its seed, that costs nothing.

The fix uses the frame you already draw. 480x270 is exact 16:9 and becomes 1920x1080 at exactly 4x, or 3840x2160 at 8x. Record 1x frames losslessly, as FFV1 or PNG, at a constant capture step, then crop, scale and encode later. Scale with ffmpeg's `neighbor` option, because the other scalers blend pixels ([ffmpeg docs](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-scaler.html)). Steam tops out at 1080p, so it gets the 4x file. YouTube gets the 8x file, because its own guide allows 35 to 45 Mbps at 2160p and 30 fps against 8 Mbps at 1080p ([YouTube help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1722171?hl=en)). Thirty fps suits a mostly still auto battler camera (opinion). The open question is the crop. It cuts 30 rows, and whether your HUD lives in them is not checked. If it does, add a 480x270 trailer camera in engine instead.

Two Unity details matter. `Time.captureDeltaTime` steps game time by a set amount for constant rate recording, but it does not touch `Time.unscaledTime` ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Time-captureDeltaTime.html)). Anything on unscaled time, such as a hit stop timer or a UI tween, will drift off the recording clock, and whether the game uses unscaled time there is not checked. Unity Recorder only runs in the Editor, not in builds ([Unity](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.recorder@4.0/manual/index.html)), so your own headless recorder is the right tool. It also hands you what Noita's trailer needed debug tools and nearly four hours of footage over a week to get ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/10/17/noita-10-launch-trailer)): the same seed replays the same fight, with the HUD on or off, for as many takes as you like. In the edit, use hard cuts, whole number punch ins, and shakes and pans in whole source pixels. Lieu recently faulted a trailer for stutter and motion blur ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2026/8/23/dear-passengers-vs-banana-airways)).

Pick the track first, cut cards and big hits on hard beats, and use game sounds as percussion ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2021/2/1/sound-design-driven-game-trailers)). Hades II spends its first seven seconds on sound design before the music cue comes in on strong beats ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/19/hades-ii-early-access-trailer-review)), and trailer music in general runs slow intro, build, climax, a fake out stopdown of silence, then a final button ([Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2018/4/17/how-production-music-teaches-trailer-structure)). **Both silences belong to her.** The wipe sits in the lead in, before the music starts, and her last trace sits in the stopdown. Ask your audio collaborator for a cue at a tempo that lands on whole frames at 30 fps (90 BPM gives 20 frames a beat, 120 gives 15, 150 gives 12), plus stems and the hit points in frames. Have the sim log the tick of every hit and kill, and the cuts can line up by code. The researchers sketched a code pipeline for the edit: crop, nearest neighbour scale, white flash frames, cards drawn as 480x270 pixel font images, one music file and an edit list. It suits hard cuts and flashes, while fancy motion graphics are easier in an editor. Nobody has run it yet.

Next Fest needs at least one public trailer, and Valve uses only that one. The October 2026 edition closed registration on 31 August and the next is planned for February 2027 ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/marketing/upcoming_events/nextfest)), so its deadline lands well before February; the date is not checked. Keep two trailers in front with gameplay first, hide old ones, hold back a 30 second announcement cut for an exclusive, and make 30 and 15 second social cuts ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2023/12/05/why-am-i-not-getting-wishlists-a-step-by-step-solution/)). The channel matters as much as the cut: the same Spelunky trailer got about 100,000 views on Nintendo's channel and 7,971 on the developer's ([HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/03/21/if-you-build-it-they-wont-come-marketing-fallacies-and-missed-opportunities/)). A custom thumbnail must be a frame from the video at 1920x1080 and should avoid text ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/store/trailer)), so plan one strong text free frame inside the cut.
