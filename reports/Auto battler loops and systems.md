# Auto battlers click between the fights

**Every hit auto battler puts its real decisions between the fights, and Kindred Siege skips that half.** Across Teamfight Tactics, Dota Underlords, Auto Chess, Hearthstone Battlegrounds, Super Auto Pets, Storybook Brawl, The Bazaar, Backpack Battles and Mechabellum, **six blocks show up every time**: a build phase then a fight you do not steer, a random offer to pick from, a small budget each round, a way to upgrade what you own, a synergy you can see, and a loss budget. Interest, streak gold, shared pools, trait thresholds, crafting, grids and live opponents are each missing from at least one hit, so they are optional. When Riot built a solo PvE mode, it kept gold, levels, champions and augments and swapped health for 3 lives, so the build makes the genre, not the opponents. Combat is a rules engine with dice in a few narrow, readable places (targets, crits, misses) and a forced end that stops stalls. No studio publishes its seeds or tick rate. The easy games use few numbers, one flat price, printed rules and no timer. The sticky ones pay out through risk the player chose, a pick from three offers, rewards in layers and fights that are close for real. Kindred already has a seeded fight that goes past the genre with orders, and a meta layer of camp, materials, codex and learning chiefs. So "click and play" comes from the missing middle, and most hooks to fill it already sit in Jacob's locks. The mercenary rival works if it is announced ahead, learns only from habits the player repeats, taxes those habits instead of killing them, and pays in power a normal fight cannot give. **But the full rival reverses two locked calls and sits near the Nemesis patent**, so it needs Jacob's call and the solicitor's answer before anyone builds it.

## Fights run on set rules, with dice only where players can plan

No studio in this set publishes where its fights are computed, how they are seeded or how fast they tick. What is public splits the games into two shapes. Queue games take turns in a set order. Battlegrounds attacks leftmost first, the side with more minions starts, and a tie on count is settled at random ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlegrounds)). Super Auto Pets fires start of battle abilities in attack order, then the two front pets hit each other at the same time ([Super Auto Pets wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)). Storybook Brawl's front row attacks first ([HSReplay](https://articles.hsreplay.net/2022/09/23/storybook-brawl-the-perfect-game-for-battlegrounds-players-2/)). Clock games run on timers. Teamfight Tactics (TFT) units gain mana per attack by class, **10 for assassins, marksmen and fighters, 7 for casters and 5 for tanks** in current wiki values ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/TFT:Mana)), and The Bazaar and Backpack Battles fire items when their cooldowns fill ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/keywords-and-terms); [Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)). The Battlegrounds wiki says combat takes no time at all, and a player noted the result is set the moment the turn ends ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlegrounds); [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/hearthstone/t/battlegrounds-turn-timer/65935)). That is compute first, animate after, though Blizzard has never confirmed it. Kindred cannot compute ahead like that, because its orders arrive mid fight, but its sim is still kept apart from the view and gives the same result for the same seed and orders.

Dice survive in combat, but only in narrow channels. Battlegrounds picks targets at random unless a Taunt is up ([Blizzard](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23156373)). Super Auto Pets' Mosquito hits a random enemy at the start of battle ([Super Auto Pets wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Mosquito)). TFT rolls a **base 25% crit chance** ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/TFT:Critical_strike)) and has carried dodge items from Set 1 to Set 18 ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/TFT:Dodge)). Backpack Battles rolls accuracy, with each Luck stack adding 5% and each Blind stack taking 5% away ([Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)). Mechabellum players report missile spread and anti air results that swing between rounds, with no developer reply ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/669330/discussions/0/3833172420309249835)). Timing itself does not jitter in the documented clock games: Haste doubles charge speed, Slow halves it and Freeze stops it ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/keywords-and-terms)). Determinism is a choice, and it costs. The open source Pokémon Auto Chess calls an unseeded random, so its fights cannot be replayed from a seed ([GitHub](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/keldaanCommunity/pokemonAutoChess/master/app/utils/random.ts)). Riot formed a team in April 2016 and reached a first version that December to make League of Legends' server deterministic. The payoff was replays that restore minute 40 of a match in **under 3 minutes** ([Riot Tech Blog](https://www.riotgames.com/en/news/determinism-league-legends-introduction)).

The only designer rule on combat dice in this set is Riot's. Its first TFT review says to keep randomness in positive, controlled places ([Riot, Set 1 learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-tft-set-1-learnings)). The Imperial trait was a coin flip on double damage for your carry, and players accepted it because they chose it and could predict it. Phantom and Hextech could set a unit to 100 HP or strip its items with no answer, and drew a strong backlash. Zephyr's disable stayed because players could see it coming and play around it. So for a seeded sim the question is not whether dice exist. It is whether the player can see them and plan around them.

Every documented clock game also forces a fight to end. The Bazaar's sandstorm starts at **30 seconds** ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-bazaar/mak-new-hero-season-1)). Backpack Battles' fatigue starts at **17 seconds** and grows each second by `DMG(n) = DMG(n-1) + floor(DMG(n-1)/10) + 1` ([Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Fatigue)). TFT's overtime starts at 30 seconds, then raises damage and attack speed by x1.1 a second while healing and shields fall 10% a second ([tftips](https://tftips.app/en/specs/battle)). Fights are computable enough that players built their own simulators. Bob's Buddy runs upwards of 10,000 sims per Battlegrounds combat and its odds matched tens of thousands of real rounds closely, but it shows them only after the board is locked ([HSReplay](https://articles.hsreplay.net/2020/04/24/introducing-bobs-buddy/)). No game here shows an official forecast before combat. Studios show inputs instead. The Bazaar lets you inspect a monster's loadout before the fight since patch 13.0 ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/patch-notes)), and Battlegrounds names your next opponent but hides their board ([Blizzard](https://hearthstone.blizzard.com/en-us/news/23156373)). Replays are rare. Mechabellum lets you take control of a past battle ([Dreamhaven](https://pressreleases.triplepointpr.com/2025/03/25/dreamhaven-announces-partnership-with-game-river-to-publish-tactical-auto-battler-mechabellum/)), Backpack Battles shows a full log of every action after each fight ([PC Games](https://www.pcgames.de/Indie-Games-Thema-239276/Specials/Backpack-Battles-Builds-Early-Access-Inventar-getestet-Auto-Battler-1442869/2/)), and replays built from recorded commands break when balance changes, as Company of Heroes 2 players found ([COH2.org](https://www.coh2.org/topic/29774/how-to-make-old-replays-work/post/307165)).

Kindred already has what Riot had to build later: a seeded sim on a 30 tick a second clock, a forced end where running out of time loses, and a replay of seed plus orders down to the tick. Its save also writes a battle rules identity and every tuning value next to a half played fight (CampaignSaveRules.cs), which is the guard Company of Heroes 2 replays lacked. Two things follow. First, any dice added later belong in Riot's plannable channels, shown on screen. Second, the set lineups Jacob locked on 11 Oct were his answer to the same fight swinging from about 1% to 98% for a sensible bot, depending on what came before (DESIGN_LOCKS.md). They make each fight one test you can learn. But Valve's Left 4 Dead talk warns that static enemy placement hurts replay, because players memorise it and find one strategy that wins every time ([Booth, 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). Once a player solves a set lineup, the replay value has to come from the player's side: a different squad, gear and orders each run. That is the half Kindred is missing. The sim could also compute win odds the way Bob's Buddy does. Shown after a fight they teach. Shown before, they turn each choice into "pick the biggest number".

## Six blocks are mandatory, and everything else is optional

Put the nine games side by side and six blocks appear in every one. Everything else is missing from at least one well reviewed hit.

| Block | How the hits do it |
|---|---|
| Build, then a fight you do not steer | every game; in Mechabellum placed units cannot be moved or controlled |
| A random offer to pick from | TFT and Backpack Battles show 5; Battlegrounds 3 to 6 minions; Mechabellum 4 shared reinforcement picks |
| A small budget each round | Super Auto Pets 10 gold; Battlegrounds 3 coins rising to 10; Mechabellum the same supply for both players |
| An upgrade path for what you own | TFT 3 copies for two stars and 9 for three; Battlegrounds golden triples; The Bazaar bronze to diamond by buying a copy; Backpack Battles recipes |
| A synergy you can see | TFT traits; Battlegrounds tribes; The Bazaar's 18 tags; Backpack Battles star and diamond slots; Mechabellum counters |
| A loss budget | TFT and Battlegrounds health with damage that grows; Super Auto Pets 5 lives and 10 wins; Backpack Battles 10 wins before 5 losses |

The sources behind the table: TFT's shop shows 5 champions from a pool every player shares ([tftips](https://tftips.app/en/specs/shop)), and 3 copies make a two star, 9 a three star ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teamfight_Tactics)). Battlegrounds charges 3 for any minion and 1 to refresh, gives 3 coins rising by 1 a round to 10 that do not carry over, and fuses 3 copies into a golden minion that discovers one from the next tier ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlegrounds)). Super Auto Pets gives 10 gold a turn, charges 3 per pet and 1 per roll, matches Arena players against teams other players built on the same turn, and ends a run at 10 wins or 5 lost lives ([Super Auto Pets wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)). The Bazaar upgrades an item when you buy a copy of the same quality ([Bazaar wiki](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Items)). Backpack Battles shows 5 items with 1 gold rerolls ([Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)) and ends at 10 wins or 5 losses ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles)). Mechabellum has no random unit shop, but from round 2 both players see the same 4 reinforcement options and each takes one ([gameplay.tips](https://gameplay.tips/guides/mechabellum-guide-to-unit-and-mechanism-details.html)), and placed units cannot be moved or controlled ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechabellum)). The optional list is long. Battlegrounds and Super Auto Pets have no interest and no carryover. Super Auto Pets and Storybook Brawl unlock tiers by turn instead of selling XP ([HSReplay](https://articles.hsreplay.net/2022/09/23/storybook-brawl-the-perfect-game-for-battlegrounds-players-2/)). Battlegrounds launched with no items and no alliances ([Hearthstone Top Decks](https://www.hearthstonetopdecks.com/how-does-hearthstone-battlegrounds-compare-to-other-auto-battlers/)). Super Auto Pets Arena, Backpack Battles and The Bazaar fight saved builds of other players, not live ones ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700/Backpack_Battles/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1617400/The_Bazaar/)). So interest, streak gold, shared pools, XP buying, trait thresholds, crafting, grids and live opponents are habits of one family, not rules of the genre.

The feeling the genre sells is simple: my plan, proven by a fight I watch. The blocks form four loops that deliver it. The round loop is the engine: an offer, a choice under a budget, a placement, a fight, then a reward and a hit to the loss budget, then the next offer. The run loop gives it an end. Async and PvE formats settle on about **10 wins against a small loss budget**: Super Auto Pets 10 wins with 5 lives, Backpack Battles 10 wins before 5 losses, and The Bazaar 10 wins with 20 Prestige, where a loss costs more on later days ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/prestige)). Stakes rise as a run goes on. TFT and Battlegrounds charge more damage at later stages and for each enemy left standing ([tft.ninja](https://tft.ninja/guides/game-mechanics/player-damage-calculation); [Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlegrounds)), and Battlegrounds had to cap early damage at 5, then 10 from turn 4 and 15 from turn 8, so games did not end too soon ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Patch_31.2.0.212197)). The variety loop changes the rules each run: TFT augments, Battlegrounds heroes and anomalies, Super Auto Pets packs, Backpack Battles classes. The meta loop of unlocks between runs is optional in PvP but standard in the single player relatives, from Wildfrost's hub town that grows between runs to Hadean Tactics' 20 Corruption levels ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1811990); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260590)).

Two routes give the same answer. The table is one. Riot gave the other. Its Tocker's Trials was a solo PvE mode with 30 rounds and 6 bosses. It kept the set's champions, augments, gold and levelling, swapped health for **3 lives** and dropped timers ([Riot](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/new-tft-workshop-mode-tockers-trials)). Riot removed the opponents and kept the build. I tried to break the six block claim and could not. Mechabellum has no random shop, but it keeps a draft of 4. Battlegrounds has no banking, but it keeps a trade: the tavern upgrade gets 1 coin cheaper for each round you wait, so you choose between tiering up and buying power now ([Hearthstone wiki](https://hearthstone.wiki.gg/wiki/Battlegrounds)). The hybrids closest to Kindred let the player act during fights and still build between them. Hero's Hour runs its overworld and town in turns and its battles in real time ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1656780)), Thronefall builds by day and defends at night ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150)), and guides say Kingdom Rush pays bonus gold for calling a wave early ([AOL](https://www.aol.com/news/2014-07-30-kingdom-rush-tips-and-tricks.html)). Even the busy player games run an economy under the action.

## Easy games show few numbers and print every rule

The games reviewers call easy keep the numbers tiny. Super Auto Pets runs on 10 gold, 3 per pet, 1 per roll and 5 team slots ([Super Auto Pets wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Basics)), and its pets have just attack and health ([Indie Hell Zone](https://indiehellzone.com/2021/10/26/super-auto-pets/)). Battlegrounds is buy 3, sell 1, refresh 1. A 2019 review said Battlegrounds was easy because it reused Hearthstone cards, had no items or alliances and charged the same for everything, while other auto battlers took tens of hours to feel comfortable ([Hearthstone Top Decks](https://www.hearthstonetopdecks.com/how-does-hearthstone-battlegrounds-compare-to-other-auto-battlers/)). Counting the separate systems a new player meets in a first game, from the rules above, gives about 9 for Super Auto Pets, about 10 for Battlegrounds and about 18 for TFT. That count is ours, not a published figure, but the games praised as easy sit at the low end. IGN called Underlords the easiest auto battler for novices, partly for its interface, while VentureBeat found the Auto Chess mobile game confusing for newcomers ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dota_Underlords); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dota_Auto_Chess)).

They teach in small steps and keep the hands free. TFT's tutorial is three steps: recruit, deploy, battle ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/TFT:Tutorial)). Its lead designer David Abecassis set the rule "tough choices, not touch execution", so the team capped the units on the field, let copies merge to clear space, and let any two components make an item ([Pocket Tactics](https://www.pockettactics.com/teamfight-tactics-design)). Super Auto Pets Arena, The Bazaar and Backpack Battles' unranked mode have no turn timer ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1714040/Super_Auto_Pets/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1617400/The_Bazaar/); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700/Backpack_Battles/)). Bad North's developers kept the rules and actions few and hid many systems so new players could start fast ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/68638/indie-spotlight-plausible-concept-on-bad-north/)).

Every hit shows its synergy. TFT teaches traits in the second tutorial step, The Bazaar prints 18 item tags and 11 enchantments on its cards ([Bazaar wiki](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Items)), and Backpack Battles marks neighbour effects with star and diamond slots ([Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)). Super Auto Pets builds its ability text on **14 trigger words** ([Super Auto Pets wiki](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Glossary)). Hidden rules show up only as a garnish. Battlegrounds left some trinket rules for players to discover, but its lead designer built trinkets to work without homework, and every offer holds at least one option costing 2 or less ([Hearthstone Top Decks](https://www.hearthstonetopdecks.com/developer-insights-battlegrounds-trinkets/)). Rule changers appear before you commit: a Battlegrounds anomaly applies to the whole lobby and shows before hero choice ([BlizzardWatch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2023/09/12/anomalies-hearthstone-battlegrounds-season-5/)). During the fight the screen carries bars, timers and status icons, and the numbers wait for a recap. TFT added a damage recap in patch 12.6 ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/V12.6_(Teamfight_Tactics))), Backpack Battles added status indicators and tooltips at 1.0 ([Mein MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/90-prozent-positiv-auf-steam-indie-hit-backpack-battles/)), and Mechabellum guides tell beginners to check each unit's damage before upgrading it ([Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/mechabellum/mechabellum-tips-guide-reinforcements-research-points-specialists/)). Wildfrost puts a countdown on every card, so the player sees when each will act and can push it back ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1811990)).

For Kindred this cuts both ways. The fight choice cards (icon, name, short tags, full text on hover) and the card that says what a tribe learned from you already follow the show before you commit pattern. The hunt captain's visible wind up is the Wildfrost shape: an order should answer something on screen. But the two hidden combos run against every hit here. Print them, or cut them. And Jacob's "easy Pokémon" lock and the evidence agree on size: one offer, one currency, one upgrade rule, one visible synergy and one loss budget is five new ideas. Keep each to one or two numbers, the way Super Auto Pets keeps its shop to 10 gold, 3 a pet and 1 a roll.

## Players come back for chosen risk, layered rewards and close fights

Riot's reviews over three years show one stable rule: keep random upside the player opted into, cut random downside with no answer ([Riot, Set 1 learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-tft-set-1-learnings)). Rare chase outcomes, like fielding nine Blademasters, made the moments players remembered, and Riot said it would add more. From Set 2, a top four finish never cost ranked points, so half of every lobby leaves with a win. In 2021 Riot said players wanted daring, high payoff moments, and promised economy traits with more dice, treasure and comebacks ([Riot, Reckoning learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-teamfight-tactics-reckoning-learnings/)). In 2022 Mort called augments' variance the reason TFT is fun to come back to, and said novelty beat nostalgia ([RiftFeed](https://riftfeed.gg/more/tft-set-7-dargonlands-mort-interview)). Riot's head of gameplay said in 2026 that forced risk and reward items in Set 5 drew criticism, so later risk mechanics were made opt in ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/teamfight-tactics-turns-7-riot-games-discusses-lessons-learned-and-the-road-ahead-exclusive-interview/)).

The risk is always a trade between two things the player can read. Bank gold for interest, which TFT pays at 1 per 10 held up to 5, or spend it now ([League wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/TFT:Gold)). Tier up or buy power. Keep a unit for an upgrade or sell it. Gamble on greed early in The Bazaar, when a loss costs only the day number in Prestige, or play safe ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/prestige)). Taking the calm choices away backfires. Hyper Roll cut interest and streak gold to make TFT games about 20 minutes long. Riot found it was not easier to learn and felt chaotic with no downtime ([Riot, Reckoning learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-teamfight-tactics-reckoning-learnings/)), and that constant decisions could make it more stressful than normal TFT ([Riot, Gizmos and Gadgets learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-teamfight-tactics-gizmos-gadgets-learnings/)). It ended at about **2% of TFT playtime** and was retired in 2025 ([esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/teamfight-tactics/tft-roadmap-2025-no-more-hyper-roll-pengus-party-returns-and-new-set-revival)). Counters follow the same logic. In Set 1, 100% heal block made healing builds useless, and cutting it to 80% kept them alive ([Riot, Set 1 learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-tft-set-1-learnings)).

Rewards come in layers: each shop and fight, the result of a run, and rare chase outcomes. The Bazaar pays chests at 4, 7 and 10 wins ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/day-guide)), and Super Auto Pets gives a life back on turn 3 if you lost one ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets)). The small offer is the standard shape: TFT's augments are a choice of 3 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teamfight_Tactics)), and Storybook Brawl's triple reward was 1 of 3 Treasures ([HSReplay](https://articles.hsreplay.net/2022/09/23/storybook-brawl-the-perfect-game-for-battlegrounds-players-2/)). Riot named the tension it solves when it found its Armory gave players choice but less variety from game to game than the Chosen mechanic ([Riot, Reckoning learnings](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-teamfight-tactics-reckoning-learnings/)). Content volume matters as much as the system. IGN's 6 of 10 early access review of Backpack Battles said there were too few items to keep runs interesting ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backpack_Battles)), and Mortdog said auto battlers stay fun for only two to four months, which is why TFT ships three sets a year ([MeinMMO](https://mein-mmo.de/chef-tft-spiel-kurz-vor-tod-heute-besser-als-je-zuvor/)).

Lab research backs three parts of this. Ryan, Rigby and Przybylski found that feeling free to choose, feeling competent and feeling connected each predicted enjoyment and future play ([ACU Research Bank](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8q128/the-motivational-pull-of-video-games-a-self-determination-theory-approach)). Abuhamdeh, Csikszentmihalyi and Jalal found suspense and competence raise enjoyment separately. Enjoyment peaked when players were slightly ahead, and **69% chose to replay the game they had narrowly won** over one they won easily ([BPS Research Digest](https://bps.org.uk/research-digest/why-risk-losing-more-fun-easy-win)). Clark and colleagues found near misses raised the urge to keep playing only when the player had personal control, in a small slot machine study ([PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2658737/)). Kindred's orders are personal control, so a close fight the player steered sits near those conditions. Nobody has tested that transfer in an auto battler.

The line between pull and manipulation is clear in the research. Harm evidence is strongest where randomness meets money: loot box buying correlates with problem gambling at r = 0.27, positive in 12 of 13 publications ([BCU](https://pureportal.bcu.ac.uk/en/publications/loot-boxes-problem-gambling-and-problem-video-gaming-a-systematic/)). Play time alone showed little to no causal effect on wellbeing across about 39,000 adults ([Oxford Internet Institute](https://www.oii.ox.ac.uk/major-new-study-finds-little-evidence-for-causal-connection-between-well-being-and-video-game-playing/)). For a premium single player game the real risk is grind, which Zagal, Björk and Lewis list as a time based dark pattern ([Chalmers](https://research.chalmers.se/en/publication/177148)). Rigging close losses would be manipulation. The honest version is fights that are close for real. Between runs, unlocks that add options are praised and flat power grinds are not. Supergiant made softening the sting of failure a focus of Hades from the start ([Inverse](https://www.inverse.com/gaming/hades-god-mode-interview)). Hearthstone Mercenaries, a grind heavy PvE party battler, lost content support about 16 months after launch, with players citing its coin economy and no end game ([BlizzardWatch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2023/02/07/hearthstone-mercenaries-mode-ends/)). The Bazaar locked hero content behind a paid pass in open beta, drew pay to win anger, and reversed it within about a month ([PC Gamer via Yahoo](https://tech.yahoo.com/gaming/articles/back-month-spent-strangling-itself-161941715.html)).

The market says the single player niche sells. Single player relatives on Steam sit between about 82% and 96% positive: Thronefall 96.1% of 23,876 reviews, Monster Train 2 95.3%, Loop Hero 92.7%, 9 Kings 92.4%, He is Coming 84.8% ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2742830); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1282730); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2784470); [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490)). Loop Hero sold a million copies by December 2021 ([GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2021/12/loop-hero-from-four-quarters-hits-a-million-sales)) while its Steam average fell to the low hundreds ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1282730)), so for a premium game copies sold matter more than concurrent players. Thronefall's two person team reportedly sold a million in its first year at $12.99 ([The Pragmatic Engineer](https://newsletter.pragmaticengineer.com/p/thronefall)). Standalone PvP games mostly shrank: Underlords fell from a 202,254 player peak in June 2019 to an 822 average in September 2026 ([SteamCharts](https://steamcharts.com/app/1046930)). The niche's complaints point at item pools and passive fights. An automated summary of He is Coming reviews lists a diluted item pool among common complaints ([Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2824490/he_is_coming)), and a Despot's Game review grumbled that once a fight starts you just sit and watch ([GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/eugene-sax/despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-review/); [Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/despots-game-review/)).

## A small team needs a combat kit, data records and a bot lab

The clocked games share one small kit: a clock, timers per unit or item, a status table with per stack rules, a short list of named triggers, one ordering rule and one forced end. Queue games like Super Auto Pets swap the clock for a turn order and keep the rest. Backpack Battles defines each status per stack. Empower adds 1 weapon damage, Heat makes every item 2% faster, Poison deals 1 damage every 2 seconds, Block absorbs 1 damage ([Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)). The Bazaar lists 5 trigger types and 11 enchantments ([Bazaar wiki](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Items)). TFT writes its traits as timers: Elderwood grows every 2 seconds, Shurima ascends after 8 ([Riot](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/game-updates/tfts-6-year-bash/)). Ordering is where shipped games stay vague. A Super Auto Pets developer confirmed pets act by attack, but players still find ties hard to predict ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1714040/discussions/0/3198118348349750330)), and The Bazaar's wiki calls its internal cooldown unclear ([Bazaar wiki](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Items)). A deterministic sim has to write tie breaks down, cap how often one trigger can fire, and test both before items multiply.

Content is data, and the engine never special cases one card. Riot's match data stores each trait as a name, a unit count, the tier reached and the tiers available, which is a breakpoint model ([Riot DevRel](https://www.riotgames.com/en/DevRel/teamfight-tactics-match-history)). One Bazaar card carries a value per tier, so Hourglass cuts its neighbours' cooldowns by 4%, 8%, 12% or 16% ([BazaarDB](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/1sk99jmuf77zkyxy9rt7trq8u/Hourglass)). One design buys four balance points. Pokémon Auto Chess runs a precompute step after any data change and applies synergy and item effects through shared hooks at the start of each fight ([GitHub](https://github.com/keldaanCommunity/pokemonAutoChess); [simulation.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/keldaanCommunity/pokemonAutoChess/master/app/core/simulation.ts)). It is GPL 3.0 and not deterministic, so it is a map to read, not code to copy.

For a single player game, authored boards per stage are the cheapest opponent that works, and they replay perfectly in a deterministic sim. Pokémon Auto Chess bots are just authored steps, each a board unlocked after enough rounds, with no reaction to the player ([bot.ts](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/keldaanCommunity/pokemonAutoChess/master/app/core/bot.ts)). Difficulty in public single player sources is a budget or a curve. One developer cut an enemy damage exponent from 1.25 to 1.15 to tame late spikes ([itch.io](https://danmarchand.itch.io/dont-die-collect-loot/devlog/364982/minor-balance-change)), and players of another game complained about runaway one sided fights when enemies scaled every turn ([itch.io](https://starea.itch.io/goblins-can-conquer/devlog/1150976/goblins-can-conquer-v03-update)). FTL's designers tuned around an expected **10% win rate** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)).

Riot's Game Analysis Team is the model for a bot lab. It keeps a library of reference boards (82 "board strings" for one set), runs them against each other daily, writes down which boards should beat which, blacklists specific augment pairs, and still playtests because sims miss pacing ([Riot](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/dev/talking-tactics-game-analysis-team-gat/)). Research tools do the same with less staff. Metagame autobalancing tunes numbers until a target graph of who beats whom holds ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.04419v1)), and Cardonomicon varies agent skill to expose balance traps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.01423v1)). Kindred's bots (doing nothing, spam, random, cheat, plain and reading) are this lab already. What it lacks is the reference board library and a written expected ordering, so that violations flag themselves.

The cheapest high value system is a structured event log out of the sim: time, source, target, effect, amount. One log feeds the recap, a scrubbable replay, the combat text and the bot reports. Backpack Battles' log is the cheapest "why did I lose" tool in this set ([PC Games](https://www.pcgames.de/Indie-Games-Thema-239276/Specials/Backpack-Battles-Builds-Early-Access-Inventar-getestet-Auto-Battler-1442869/2/)), and Kindred's own innovation claim, a replay of why, needs the same log.

Content is where Kindred is furthest behind. TFT started as about 12 developers over 18 weeks on top of League's engine ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teamfight_Tactics)). The smallest full content set in this research is one Super Auto Pets pack, **60 pets and 16 foods** ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Auto_Pets)). Backpack Battles' two developers shipped 38 weekly patches by April 2024 and called the pace extremely taxing ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-backpack-battles-sold-650k-copies)); its Steam page now lists over 500 items ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700/Backpack_Battles/)). Kindred has 5 classes, 3 tribes, 1 item and 2 combos. As the pool grows, He is Coming's answer to a diluted pool is worth copying: items you equip raise the drop rate of related items, and you get three banishes a run ([Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)).

| System | Kindred today |
|---|---|
| Seeded, clocked sim with a forced end | has it: 30 tick a second clock, replay to the tick, running out of time loses except in Hold the line |
| Status and trigger words, one ordering rule, trigger caps | partly: nerve and the orders exist; one item; two hidden combos |
| Content as data with a validation step | not checked |
| Offer and budget between fights | missing |
| Upgrade path for owned pieces | camp forge only, one recipe |
| Visible synergy | missing |
| Loss budget | missing: one lost fight ends the run |
| Authored opponents per stage | has it: set lineups per tribe and fight kind |
| Bot lab | bots from doing nothing to reading the screen; no reference board library or written expected ordering |
| Event log feeding a recap and the replay of why | partly: fights are kept as replay evidence (ReplayEvidence.cs); a recap fed by an event log not checked |
| Rules identity stamped on saves | has it: battle rules identity and every tuning value saved with a half played fight |

## Kindred has the fight and the meta, but not the middle

Kindred's outer loops exist. A run leaves camp, meets one tribe per tier, plays 2 path fights and a chief for each tribe, and ends on the Sovereign's hunters. Materials, camp upgrades, the codex and what the chiefs learned carry over, while recruits, gear and chief orders reset (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 10 and 11 Oct). A tier holds about four tribes and a run meets one, and a Pal leads each fight, so the variety loop has a home too. The fight goes past the genre: no hit in this research gives orders mid fight. What is missing is the middle of the round loop. Between fights there is no offer to pick from, no budget to spend, no way to upgrade what you hold and no synergy you can see. There is no loss budget either, because in the current code one lost fight ends the run (Campaign.cs). With nothing to build, the order buttons carry every decision, and the bots show the strain. Spam wins no whole runs now, and the hunters counter it, but Unleash spam still wins most war parties and Skullpeak's trial, and doing nothing still wins a third to a half of Skullpeak war parties (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 11 Oct balance calls). The learning chiefs hit the same wall. With one item and no builds, the only things they can learn are button habits. A build space gives the learning system something worth learning and gives the player a way to answer it.

Jacob's seven complaints map onto the missing blocks, and each block has a hook already in his locks. The right column is a proposal, not a decision.

| Complaint | What the hits do | Smallest Kindred version, and the lock it hangs on |
|---|---|---|
| No risk | trade two readable goods: bank or spend, tier up or buy, cheap early stakes and costly late ones | spend materials between fights on run gear, or carry them home for camp (materials, 10 Oct); one harder fight card per tribe that pays more (fight cards, 11 Oct) |
| No reward | pick 1 of 3 after fights; power spikes from merges; chests at milestones | after each fight, pick 1 of 3 from the beaten tribe's kit: a gear piece, a recruit or an order upgrade (Pals forge from what you won, 2 Oct) |
| No mitigation | Block, Shield, Taunt, a front line, armour tested by a threat that ignores it | gear that soaks, punishes or sidesteps a visible wind up, so each threat has more than one answer (the hunt captain's wind up, 11 Oct) |
| No builds | a pool of choices that adds up differently each run | converts from up to six tribes, the leading Pal and chief orders already vary by run; the offers make them add up |
| No items | 60 pets and 16 foods in the smallest Super Auto Pets pack | several recipes per broken chief, not one (recipes from broken chiefs, 2 Oct) |
| No combos | printed trigger words that chain | gear that fires on Unleash, while Holding, after Fall Back, when the Focus Fire target dies, or when a chief's nerve breaks |
| No synergy | a visible count or neighbour bonus | 2 or 3 fighters of one tribe in the squad unlock a printed bonus, which also pays for "kill fewer, keep more" (2 Oct) |

Two numbers set the size, and one of them is bad news. A slice run is about 7 fights: 2 path fights and a chief for Bloodridge, the same for Skullpeak, then the hunters. A winning slice run pays 130 materials (10 a fight, 30 a chief or the hunt, in CampaignRules.cs), and the Lava Sun Tabard costs 50, so materials are already scarce enough to make "spend now or carry home" a real trade. A pick of 1 from 3 after each of the first 6 fights shows 18 items a run, so a pool much smaller than that repeats inside one run. The bad news: the 8 Dec slice scope in the locks is "one forged gear piece" (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 2 Oct), and the slice is eight weeks out. A slice with one gear piece cannot answer "no items, builds, combos or synergy". Answering the complaint means growing the slice, and that is Jacob's call. The loss budget is the other missing piece of risk. Every async and PvE format here lets a run survive some losses (Super Auto Pets 5 lives, Backpack Battles 5 losses, Tocker's Trials 3 lives), while a Kindred run ends on the first. A small budget lets the player gamble one fight on a greedy build without throwing the run away.

The orders stay, but the build has to make different orders right in different runs. The hybrids that do well keep in fight input few and high level while units decide the details: Bad North's broad commands ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/688420)), Hero's Hour's spells and attack orders ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1656780)), Legionbound's row swaps and spells ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360)). Where they curb spam, they cap the input or tie it to something that can be lost. Marvel Rivals' auto battler mode allowed one damage action per round ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/marvel-rivals-new-limited-time-mode-is-easily-the-best-way-to-play-the-game-since-its-most-recent-update/)), and Hero's Hour's line spells cannot be cast once the hero is dead ([itch.io](https://thingonitsown.itch.io/heros-hour/devlog/288150/fanfare-update-heros-hour-v-14)). Kindred already prices Hold and Unleash in its own nerve and leads each fight with a Pal. The research adds two levers: cap uses per fight, not only cost, and make the right order depend on what the enemy shows, the way Zephyr worked for Riot. Gear keyed to order verbs does the rest, because a Hold build and an Unleash build then want different buttons. Valve's pacing work also argues for a quiet build beat between fights, since it found constant, unchanging combat tiring and long lulls boring ([Booth, 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)).

### The mercenary rival has two blockers before it has a design

Bad news first. Reading "final enemy" as the run's last fight, where the Sovereign's hunters sit now, two locks stand in the way. On 11 Oct Jacob locked that the hunters "do not learn between runs", and on 12 Oct that they "stay faceless: no names" (DESIGN_LOCKS.md). A named mercenary tribe that learns across runs reverses both. It must also fit the 24 tribe lock, so it is the Alpha hunters recast or one of the 23, not a 25th. The second blocker is legal. The Nemesis patent, US 10,926,179 B2, was granted on 23 Feb 2021 and runs to about 2035 or 2036 ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en); [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/2021/02/warner-brothers-granted-patent-for-nemesis-system-from-middle-earth-video-games.html)). Its description covers rivals whose lines recall past events, who can come back after defeat or even death, who get promoted when a superior is removed, and who carry a player interaction score that raises their odds in later fights ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)). A named captain who remembers you, comes back and is replaced by a promoted lieutenant sits close to that. The solicitor question from 8 Oct is still open. This is not legal advice. It is the reason to ask before building.

There is a version that fits the locks today. The hunters already arrive knowing what the tribes on the path know: they Hold when you Unleash and cover whoever you focus (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 11 Oct). Letting them read what you did in this run and come ready for it is adaptation inside a run, which the 11 Oct lock allows. The full rival, named and learning across runs, is the version Jacob described, and it waits on both blockers.

Two games give the shape for either version. In 9 Kings the rivals are AI kings with their own decks. You know which king attacks next but not with what, and beating them lets you loot cards from their decks ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2784470); [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)). In He is Coming a new hunter arrives every three days from a pool of over 30, you can preview it, and each one breaks a common build: the Razorclaw Grizzly ignores armour, and the Black Knight absorbs your attack power ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490)). The preview gives the player a shopping goal for the days before. For Kindred, that means the mercenaries announce what they learned from you before the last tier, on the same kind of card that already shows what a tribe learned. The player then builds toward an answer, which joins the rival to the build half instead of leaving it as one more fight.

The adaptation has to feel fair, and the sources agree on how. Gate each counter behind a habit the player repeats. Alien: Isolation only starts searching lockers after the player has escaped that way several times ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)), and Killer Instinct's Shadows add a behaviour only after seeing it several times, and sometimes pick a weaker move as bait ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)). Show it. Metal Gear Solid V's soldiers put on helmets after repeated headshots ([Metal Gear wiki](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Revenge_System_(enemy_preparedness))). Let the player undo it. In the same game the map shows the enemy's counters, and players report raiding supplies so the helmets never arrive ([Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/metal-gear-solid-v-the-phantom-pain/the-basics-6/gameplay-9/dynamic-difficulty); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/527274698151810828)). Tax habits instead of killing them, the way Riot's 80% heal cut kept healing alive where 100% killed it. Adapt the approach, not raw strength. Valve's Director changed how often threats came, not how hard they hit, and left bosses alone ([Booth, 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)). Hidden easing makes players feel patronised and invites them to play badly on purpose ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-the-designer-s-notebook-difficulty-modes-and-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-)). Keep the hunt bounded. Assassin's Creed Odyssey players complained that mercenaries interrupt almost anything, always seem to know where you are, and keep coming after kills ([Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/812140/discussions/0/5532118063934204190)). Kindred's hunt comes once, at the end of the run, and scales with tribes joined, which is the bounded shape. Keep it small. Monolith's own playtests pulled Nemesis back from warring factions, morale bars and a sprawling hierarchy screen to its first idea of personal villains ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-couldve-been-way-more-1681120649)).

For "big rewards", the precedents say pay in something a normal fight cannot give. 9 Kings pays in the rival's own cards. He is Coming adds two inventory slots for each hunter beaten, from a base of 8 ([Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)). Slay the Spire's burning elite drops the Emerald Key, one of three keys to Act 4 ([Slay the Spire wiki](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Keys)). Shadow of Mordor ties loot to the rival's power: guides say rune level equals the Uruk's Power, and a Death Threat makes a captain stronger with a 40% chance of an Epic Rune ([Gamer Guides](https://www.gamerguides.com/shadow-of-mordor/guide/miscellaneous/leveling-up/runes)). That rule turns adaptation into something the player wants, since the longer the mercenaries learn, the more they pay. For size, Risk of Rain 2's Shrine of the Mountain makes the boss harder and adds one more item per player for each use ([Risk of Rain 2 wiki](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Shrine_of_the_Mountain)). A mercenary also gives Kindred the other lever from Assassin's Creed Odyssey: a bounty can be paid off ([Vulkk](https://vulkk.com/2018/10/05/assassins-creed-odyssey-mercenaries-and-bounties-system/)), so the player could spend materials to shrink the hunt or face it whole for the bigger prize. FTL shows the opposite case. Its rebel elite pays 1 fuel on purpose, because the fleet is a clock, not a prize ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)). Kindred's hunters pay 30 materials today, the same as a chief (CampaignRules.cs), so right now they behave like FTL's fleet.

My call, for Jacob's yes or change. Build the inside the run version first: the hunters read your top habits this run, a bounty card before the last tier says what they are bringing, and their counters tax a habit rather than shut it down. It fits both locks, the bot lab can tune it, and it joins the rival to the build half. Then, once the solicitor answers, add the across runs layer Jacob asked for: a named captain, memory shown in taunts, and a payout that grows with how much they have learned. For "big", pay at least a chief's 30 materials plus one thing no chief gives, such as the hunt captain's order, a Pal recipe or a permanent camp unlock. As a starting value to tune, not a finding: each step of danger the player opts into pays about one more chief reward, which is Risk of Rain 2's shape.

## Conclusion

The fight is not Kindred's problem. It is the most unusual thing Kindred has, since no hit in this research lets the player give orders mid fight. The problem is that the decisions every hit makes between fights do not exist yet, so the orders carry the whole game alone. That is why one button, or no button, can still win a fight, and why the learning chiefs can only learn button habits. Seen that way, builds are not a feature next to the learning system. They are what the learning system needs to mean anything. A tribe that learns "you lean on Bloodridge shields and Hold" is a story; a tribe that learns "you press Unleash" is a balance patch. Most of the build half is wiring Jacob's own locks (materials, recipes from broken chiefs, chief orders, kill fewer keep more, fight cards) into one small choice after every fight, not a new loop.

The order of work follows from the risks: the build half first, because everything else leans on it; the inside the run hunters second, because they fit today's locks and give the build a target; the named mercenary rival last, after the solicitor and Jacob's call on the two locks. How sure: **LIKELY** that the six blocks are what make the genre click. Two routes agree (the nine game table and what Riot kept in Tocker's Trials) and I could not break it, but no source measures retention per block, and "optional" only means one well reviewed game lacks it. **GUESS** on every Kindred number here (offer count, pool size, reward size, danger steps). The bot lab should set them.
