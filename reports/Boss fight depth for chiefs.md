# Make sparing a chief cost something

The best boss fights ask a question you can read, accept more than one answer at different costs, and make sparing the boss cost something. Kindred's chiefs ask the question, but four of their five spells take only one answer, and sparing them is free. That is why capture wins every time. Of 35 bot chief fights since the chief rules went in on 20 Oct, **18 ended in a capture and none in a kill**, and the bot has no rule for the kneel at all ([HANDOFF_LOG.md:9978](../HANDOFF_LOG.md#L9978)). At a quarter health the chief stops fighting, his side loses 50 nerve a second, and his Wardens take the blows aimed at him, so he can barely be killed even on purpose. Capture then pays his whole band, his spell and a codex entry, while a kill pays one Princess piece. Games where mercy feels deep do the opposite. Monster Hunter, Fire Emblem, Deltarune and Shadow of War each make sparing cost something in the fight, risk something after it, or give up a prize only killing pays. Kindred already has most of the parts: the 3 second tell, Perfect Hold, nerve on both sides, bodyguards, and a hunt that grows with every tribe you take. The work is pricing mercy and giving each chief more than one question. The report ends with five chief fights to pick from. Kindred facts cite the code, the locks and the log. Everything else said about Kindred is inference, and none of it has been played.

## Capture wins 18 of 18 because mercy is free

### Kindred today: the kneel makes the choice for you

Jacob called the yield, the bodyguards and the second phase basics, and they went in on 20 Oct ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:437](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L437)). At half health a chief's second phase brings two reserves in and he casts his spell again. At a quarter health he kneels. He does nothing more, his side loses **50 nerve a second out of a full 1000**, and while your side holds, your fighters spare him and hit someone else. While his band stands, his Wardens guard him, his Medic treats him first, and snares and Openings do not take on him ([BattleSimulation.Chief.cs:6](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleSimulation.Chief.cs#L6)). Hits on a guarded fighter land on the Warden instead ([BattleSimulation.cs:1460](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleSimulation.cs#L1460)). So once he kneels his nerve runs out fast, and killing him means cutting through his Wardens first. The Hold that spares him costs 1 point ([BattleTypes.cs:789](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleTypes.cs#L789)).

The bot numbers show the flip. Before Sprint 1, chief fights ended **19 captured and 9 killed** out of 53. With the class spells and no kneel, chiefs fell far more often: **9 captured and 29 killed** ([HANDOFF_LOG.md:9958](../HANDOFF_LOG.md#L9958)). With the kneel it is **18 captured and 0 killed** out of 35. None of the bot pilots react to a kneeling chief ([CampaignPilot.cs](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Learning/CampaignPilot.cs)). The bot captures without ever deciding to.

The prizes are lopsided too. A capture brings his whole band from that fight, fallen ones included, plus his spell, and marks the tribe as joined in the codex. A kill brings one Princess piece, and the tribe never counts as won over ([Campaign.cs:1484](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Learning/Campaign.cs#L1484)). That piece raises one stat by 15 and carries his special ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:318](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L318)). The bounty rises the same either way ([Campaign.cs:1531](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Learning/Campaign.cs#L1531)). The only cost on the capture side is one more fighter in the run's final hunt for each tribe that joined ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:235](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L235)), and nothing shows it when he kneels. The chief card even says a kill only gets you his gear ([Campaign.cs:156](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Learning/Campaign.cs#L156)).

### Proven in other games: mercy costs something wherever it feels deep

Monster Hunter has the cleanest kill or keep choice. In Rise a monster can be captured once it drops below a hidden line, **28%, 23% or 21% of its health** by threat level, and then only with a trap and two tranquilisers. Capture pays its own reward table and ends the quest sooner, but costs you the carves ([MH Wiki, Capturing in Rise](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_%28MHRise%29)). Some monsters break or jump out of traps depending on their state (same page). In World a hunter who hits too fast can kill the monster by accident ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103008/monster-hunter-world-how-to-trap-a-monster)). Wilds shows how the choice dies. Once the two reward pools drew close, writers concluded neither path has much advantage and it comes down to speed ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/monster-hunter-wilds-should-you-capture-or-slay-monsters)).

Other games put the price inside the fight. In Fire Emblem: Thracia 776 the captor fights at half stats during the attempt, his weakened attack has to finish the target, and carrying the captive cripples him until he drops them off ([Fire Emblem Wiki, Capture](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Capture)). Deltarune's Jevil pays violence with a weapon and mercy with an armour, so each path fills a different slot. Mercy costs TP every turn, and his last phase hits harder every turn, so even mercy has a deadline ([Deltarune Wiki, Jevil](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Jevil)). Persona 5 opens its Hold Up only once every enemy is knocked down, then offers damage, money or a recruit in one menu ([Wikipedia, Persona 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_5)). Metal Gear Solid 3 pays a non lethal boss win with that boss's own camouflage ([Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/metal-gear-solid-3-snake-eater/stamina-kill-guide-and-rewards-list-mgs3/)). Pokémon's catch formula makes a badly hurt target about three times easier to catch than a healthy one, and sleep doubles the odds again ([Dragonfly Cave](https://www.dragonflycave.com/mechanics/gen-iii-iv-capturing)). In all of these, catching is a skill of restraint and setup, not a free bonus.

Some games put the risk after the fight. In Shadow of War a broken captain can be killed or dominated, and a dominated one can later betray you ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/6192-shadow-of-war-domination-guide-the-nemesis-system-betrayal-death-threats-resurrection-and-more-orc-dominating-mechanics-explained)). And some bosses speak through the gate itself. Undertale's Asgore destroys the Mercy button at the start of his fight ([Wikipedia, Asgore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgore)).

### Inferred for Kindred: keep capture the heart, but let players fail it

Across these games the deep versions share a short list. The gate needs skill. The window can be read but carries risk. Capture costs something in the fight, or risks something after it. The kill has its own prize. The run's state changes which path is better. The boss shows who he is through the gate. And the outcome changes him or his people next time. Kindred has the kill prize and chiefs who remember you across runs ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:203](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L203)). Everything else is missing or hidden. Any squad that reaches a quarter health gets the kneel, the spare costs one point, the hunt cost never shows, every chief kneels the same way, and a one stat piece cannot compete with a whole tribe plus the codex.

Capture should stay the heart. Jacob locked that the catch is the heart of the game, and said capture is not easy ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:273](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L273)). The problem is not that players capture. It is that nobody can fail to, and nothing in a run ever makes the kill the smart call. Wilds is the warning for the fix. When both paths pay similar things, the choice shrinks to whichever is faster. The two prizes have to differ in kind, like Jevil's weapon and armour, not just in size.

## Deep bosses ask questions your squad can already answer

### Proven in other games: one warning, a few answers, and a second bar that answers fill

Double Fine built Psychonauts 2's bosses from moves that each run telegraph, attack and recovery, tuned per boss and per phase ([Game Developer, Psychonauts 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/using-a-modular-system-of-maneuvers-to-design-i-psychonauts-2-i-s-boss-fights-in-a-hurry)). The depth sits in what a correct answer pays. In Sekiro a deflect costs the attacker far more posture than the defender, the right counter to a thrust or a sweep deals heavy posture damage, and a full posture bar opens a deathblow that has to be taken within a few seconds ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)). Every dangerous attack carries the same red warning symbol ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection)), so one warning and a few answers carry across every boss. Activision's own guide says an enemy's posture recovers more slowly as its vitality falls, and against fast recovering enemies it advises cutting vitality first, then breaking posture ([Activision support](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics)). So health and posture work together, and neither bar alone is the fight. A fan wiki gives the player's own recovery as full speed above three quarters vitality, then 66%, 33% and 1% (Fextralife, Posture). Elden Ring's hidden stance does the same job and refills at 13 points a second after a pause, so backing off lets a boss reset ([Elden Ring wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance)).

Good bosses leave more than one answer, each with its own cost. Slay the Spire's Corrupt Heart switches between twelve small hits and one 40 damage hit, so no single defence is always right, and it can lose at most 300 health a turn, so one giant turn cannot delete it ([StS wiki, Corrupt Heart](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Corrupt_Heart)). Fire Emblem: Three Houses monsters lose their next turn when every barrier on them breaks, and the right weapon breaks a barrier at once, so a squad built for it gets a cheaper answer ([Fire Emblem Wiki, Barrier](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Enclosing_Wall)). Mega Crit said it wanted enemies that test different strategies, so that no single strategy always wins ([Game Developer, Slay the Spire](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-)).

The strongest bosses break one rule the player leans on, and let an everyday tool fix it. Darkest Dungeon's Siren turns one of your heroes against you, and a Guard on that hero stops it ([DD wiki, Siren](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Siren)). Battle Brothers' Lindwurm sprays acid on melee attackers, so reach and ranged weapons become the plan ([Dev Blog #98](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4prf)). Monster Hunter stops strong answers turning into spam. A wiki draft page for Rathalos lists a Pitfall Trap holding 10 seconds and losing 2 for every repeat, down to 2, and a Flash Pod losing 4 a repeat, down to 5. His enrage cuts both ways: for 100 seconds he hits 10% harder and moves 10% faster, but also takes 10% more damage ([MH Wiki, Rathalos](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)). Backing off and trading blows are both fair answers.

The genre closest to Kindred is the weakest guide. Teamfight Tactics kept making its monster rounds steadier loot drops ([TFT patch 14.6 notes](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/teamfight-tactics-patch-14-6-notes-2025/)), and Dota Underlords cut creep rounds from its main modes in December 2019 ([Liquipedia](https://www.liquipedia.net/underlords/2019-12-18_Patch)). Their monsters deliver loot. They are not climaxes.

### Inferred for Kindred: the Eclipse already works this way, the newer spells do not

Kindred's base unit is right. A chief spell winds up for **90 ticks, 3 seconds** ([Moves.cs:21](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/Moves.cs#L21)). A cut in names it and shows the colour of the order that answers it ([HANDOFF_LOG.md:8850](../HANDOFF_LOG.md#L8850)). Answers already hurt his nerve. A parried Eclipse costs his side 100 and a parried heavy swing 40 ([BattleSimulation.Heavy.cs:28](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleSimulation.Heavy.cs#L28)), and smashing Steel Storm's wall costs 300 ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:391](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L391)). After his spell goes off, dodged or not, the chief takes double damage for 4 seconds ([Moves.cs:37](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/Moves.cs#L37)). That is Sekiro's payoff and Monster Hunter's tired window, already built.

The gap is the number of answers. The Eclipse has three. Hold parries it, Fall Back dodges it, and Focus Fire can break the cast. Tribes that learn your parry wait out your Hold, which pushes you to the other two ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:349](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L349)). That is the deep version. **The other four spells have one answer each, and three of them share it.** Only Unleash beats Steel Storm. Only Fall Back beats Dust Shark, Blood Call and Tide, and nothing parries, soaks or breaks them ([Moves.cs:151](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/Moves.cs#L151), [Moves.cs:218](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/Moves.cs#L218)). So for most chiefs the tell is a lookup: see the chief, press Fall Back. Giving each spell the Eclipse's shape, one best answer that pays most plus a weaker one, turns the lookup back into a choice. Kingdom Rush adds a timing idea. Its final boss locks your towers with a spell you can only dispel in its first few seconds ([Kingdom Rush wiki](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Vez%27nan)), the opposite of a late parry. A chief with one early question and one late one makes timing part of the read.

Two more gaps. His nerve never refills on its own, since the battle code has no such rule, so stalling costs nothing and there is no Sekiro style reason to keep the pressure on. And making one button spam lose is still open in the locks ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:208](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L208)). Monster Hunter's shrinking traps fit it. The same order used on the same chief inside a short time could lose strength, and a correct read could reset it.

## Phases should change the question, not the health bar

### Proven in other games: thresholds that ask for a plan

FromSoftware moves its lines around. Fan guides put Margit's second phase near two thirds health and Godrick's near half ([PC Gamer, Elden Ring bosses](https://pcgamer.com/elden-ring-bosses-locations); [Gamer Guides, Godrick](https://www.gamerguides.com/elden-ring/database/bosses/stormveil-castle-bosses/godrick-the-grafted)), and one guide puts Radahn's at 80%, where he gains ranged and pulling attacks ([Gamepressure, Radahn](https://www.gamepressure.com/elden-ring/starscourge-radahn/z0f7ab)). World of Warcraft escalates late. Of the thirteen hard frenzies its wiki lists, eleven trigger on health, and all eleven fire at or below a third ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).

The deeper phases turn the line into a decision. Slay the Spire's Champ, once below half health, wipes his debuffs, gains Strength and then executes ([StS wiki, The Champ](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Champ)), so players either save their burst for after the reset or push through before it. Darkest Dungeon's Heart of Darkness kills a hero of the player's choice when it acts at or below two thirds and one third of its health. The only way to stop it is to go from above the line to dead before it acts ([DD wiki, Heart of Darkness](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Heart_of_Darkness)). Battle Brothers' Kraken enrages when you destroy its tentacles or hurt its head, so the player picks when phase two starts ([Dev Blog #109](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4hhp)). The Bazaar's Lord Arken carries an item that fires all his other items the first time he drops below half health ([Bazaar wiki, Lord Arken](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Lord_Arken)), and players choose their monster from three with the rewards shown first ([Bazaar wiki, Monsters](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters)). The phase gets planned for before the fight.

Kill order does the same job. In World of Warcraft, Shannox gains 30% damage and attack speed for each of his hounds you kill, and Horridon attacks 50% faster and hits twice as hard when his ally Jalakk dies (Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer). Darkest Dungeon's Swine Prince lashes out at the whole party if anyone hits his pet ([DD wiki, Swine Prince](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Swine_Prince)). Each kill gives something and costs something. Into the Breach's designers found that one extra enemy can turn a fun fight into an impossible one ([Game Developer, Into the Breach](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)), which makes reinforcements the touchiest number in a boss fight.

Escalation across a ladder adds rules more than health. Slay the Spire's bosses gain health and damage at Ascension 4 and 9, then new movesets at 19 ([StS wiki, Bosses](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Bosses)). Supergiant gave the Bone Hydra four head variants and tuned its Extreme Measures boss remixes both up and down ([Supergiant patch notes](https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/hades-the-blood-price-update-patch-notes)). Monster Hunter shortens Rathalos's tired window from 60 to 50 to 40 seconds as rank rises (MH Wiki, Rathalos). Harder means tighter, not only longer.

### Inferred for Kindred: phase two is a stat jump that pays the skilled capture less

Kindred's phase two is the common shape. At half health two reserves join and he casts again ([BattleSimulation.Chief.cs:53](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleSimulation.Chief.cs#L53)). Every chief shares the same line, and nothing gets decided there. One rule runs backwards. Reserves who never came in do not join on a capture ([Campaign.cs:1499](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Learning/Campaign.cs#L1499)). So breaking his nerve before half health, the hardest capture there is, brings up to two fewer fighters than waiting for the kneel. Every game above pays the harder path more.

The fixes are small. Move the line by tribe. Let the player trigger it, for example by killing his second Warden. Show what phase two brings on the road before the fight, as The Bazaar does. Make it a burst or pay choice, as the Heart of Darkness does. Pay the early break, not the late one. And scale the seven tiers by remixing a chief's spell or adding a second question, as Slay the Spire and Hades do, rather than adding health. Treat the reserve count with care, as Into the Breach warns.

## A watching player needs the target, the shape and the answer

### Proven in other games: show what, who, how big and when

Slay the Spire first showed nothing about an enemy's next move, then tried a preview the player had to select, then settled on intent icons. Playtesters found intents with attack numbers more engaging and better for planning ([Wikipedia, Slay the Spire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Spire)). Each act's boss portrait sits on the map from the start ([StS wiki, Bosses](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Bosses)). Into the Breach shows every attack's direction and target before you move ([Wikipedia, Into the Breach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Into_the_Breach)), and its designer Justin Ma said they wanted a game where "every death felt like your own fault" ([Game Developer, Into the Breach](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)). Darkest Dungeon's Prophet marks ranks at the start of a round and hits them at the end, so the warning names a place, not just a threat ([DD wiki, Prophet](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Prophet)). Battle Brothers plants hints about its Ijirok in events and places before you ever fight it ([Dev Blog #122](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4cd6)).

A watcher can learn a rhythm. League of Legends' Baron uses an ability every sixth attack, in a fixed cycle after a random first one, with windups of 2 seconds or 1.25 seconds ([LoL wiki, Baron Nashor](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Baron_Nashor)). Final Fantasy XIV keeps one set of markers and sounds for each kind of mechanic across all its content ([CGW, Common mechanics](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Common_mechanics_and_markers)).

Too much to track hurts. Riot merged Atakhan's two forms into one and cut a Void Grub spawn because players had to think about big monsters too often ([Riot dev post](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-season-2-gameplay-changes-preview/)). XCOM 2's Alien Rulers first acted after every single soldier action, and Firaxis narrowed that to attacks and moves in their sight ([GameBanshee, quoting IGN](https://www.gamebanshee.com/kdawd)). And orders must be obeyed. In Majesty, heroes follow your bounty flags only when it suits them, and reviewers found that frustrating at times ([GameBanshee review](https://gamebanshee.com/a84)).

### Inferred for Kindred: one question at a time, with its target shown

Kindred's 3 second tell is longer than Baron's windups, which suits a player choosing between four orders rather than steering one hero. The cut in that carries the answer's colour already follows the Final Fantasy XIV lesson: players learn four colours, not one spell per tribe. What the tell lacks is target and shape. A second question per chief only reads if the tell says who gets hit and how: one big hit, a lunge at one marked fighter, a flurry, or the back row. The fight already times casts with the parry moment marked and names every nerve swing of 5% or more with its cause ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:333](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L333)), so the same plates can carry it. A fixed cycle after a random first spell lets the player plan points. The road map can show the chief, his spells and his phase two before the fight, like Slay the Spire's portrait, and his tribe's war parties can use small versions of his spells, so the chief fight is never the first time you see them.

Some things do not carry over. One fan wiki puts Sekiro's deflect window at about 0.2 seconds (Fextralife, Deflection). Kindred's Perfect Hold is half a second and goes through an order ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:289](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L289)), so the skill should stay in when to spend points, not in reflexes. Raid checks like stacks, spreads and towers need positioning the player does not control. Learning a boss by dying dozens of times works in Sekiro because retries are fast and the difficulty assumes many deaths ([PlayStation Blog](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=202767)). A chief who beats you takes one of your fighters ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:315](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L315)), so he has to read on first sight. And World of Warcraft's hard enrage timers, mostly 5 to 10 minutes (Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer), are too long for fast fights. A slow ramp that punishes stalling fits better.

## Conclusion

The research moves the problem from balance to price. Capture does not win because it is strong. It wins because it costs nothing, a kneeling chief can barely be killed, and a kill pays nothing that lasts past the run. So the first fixes are not new mechanics. Make the kneel cost something, and leave him killable while he kneels. Then give the kill one thing that lasts. The codex, the game's long goal, counts the tribes you win over, so a kill adds nothing toward it. If a killed chief's piece got its own codex page, finishing the codex would need both paths across runs, the way Jevil's weapon and armour are both worth having. The fight depth problem is separate and just as cheap to start: give every chief spell the Eclipse's shape, so three of them stop sharing Fall Back.

The five options below are five answers to one question: what should a capture cost? Some combine. Break him standing and Mercy has a price fit together, and Every chief kneels his own way can sit on any of the others. If Jacob wants the smallest step aimed at what the bots measured, it is option 3. If he wants the biggest change to how the fight plays, it is option 1. Whatever he picks, the test after he plays it is the same. Run a bot that always captures, one that always kills, and one that reads the run. The choice is real only if the reader beats both, and his own play decides how it feels ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:301](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L301)). Confidence: LIKELY on the diagnosis, which comes from the code and the log's bot numbers. LIKELY that the patterns carry over, since several games show each one. GUESS on how any option feels until it is played.

## Five chief fights to pick from

Each one builds on the 3 second tell, Perfect Hold, both nerve bars, the half health phase, the kneel and the bodyguards. Every number is a starting value.

| Option | The idea | Changes a lock? | Size |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1. Break him standing | His nerve refills, slower as he gets hurt, and the kneel becomes a short window you must finish | No | Medium |
| 2. The last question | At a quarter health he casts once more. Answer it and he kneels, burst him first and he dies | No | Small, medium with a second spell |
| 3. Mercy has a price | The kneel shows both prices, his band fights on while you spare him, and he can really be killed | No | Small |
| 4. Every chief kneels his own way | One temperament per chief changes his kneel: proud, craven, treacherous or devoted | Partly, the 20 Oct kneel | Medium |

### 1. Break him standing

**What you see.** His nerve bar refills on its own, and a mark on it shows how fast. It is quick while he is healthy, slow once he is hurt, and almost stopped below a quarter health, the shape Sekiro uses for posture. Every answered tell takes a named bite out of it. At a quarter health he still kneels, but now a 3 second timer runs over him, like Sekiro's deathblow window.

**What you decide.** Where your points go. Answering his tells breaks his nerve. Damage orders hurt his health, which slows his refill but brings the kneel, and the risk of killing him, closer. Stall and his nerve comes back, as Elden Ring's stance does. The plan is to have his nerve low by the time he kneels. In the window his side still drains as it does today, but only for 3 seconds, so the window finishes a break. It cannot do the whole job.

**Capture or kill.** Capture becomes the skill path. Read his tells, keep the pressure on, and finish the break inside the window. Miss it and he gets back up with some nerve back and never kneels again, so the fight ends in a hard nerve break or his death. The kill becomes the safe path for a squad that would rather tank his tells than answer them. A thin roster or a squad that reads badly should kill. A full roster with points banked should go for the break. Capture becomes what Jacob said it was: not easy.

**What it takes.** Sim, Claude's side: a new refill rule for the chief's nerve in health bands, the kneel's drain cut to a 90 tick window in which his refill stops, a get back up rule for when the window closes, and bigger nerve bites for answers, built on the existing parry and smash costs. Screen, Codex's side: the refill mark on his nerve bar and the timer on the kneel. Tests in ChiefRulesTests. Medium. No lock changes, since capture is still breaking his nerve while he stands. The risk is overshooting. With the class spells and no kneel, chiefs died about three times as often as they were caught, so the window has to be wide enough that good reads win.

### 2. The last question

**What you see.** At a quarter health he does not kneel straight away. He stands his ground, his cut in slams in, and he winds up one last spell with its answer colour shown, as every chief spell does today.

**What you decide.** Burst or read. Spend your points now on Focus Fire and Unleash to kill him before the 3 seconds run out, which skips the spell. Or keep points back and answer it. Answer it right and he kneels for real, and Hold spares him. Get it wrong and the spell lands in full, which for a row hitting spell is now up to 30% of each target's health ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:391](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L391)), and he never kneels. So the second half of the fight is about reaching the line with points in the bank, the way Slay the Spire players save their burst for the Champ's reset.

**Capture or kill.** Capture means facing his deadliest spell with your back row open. A kill means bursting through his bodyguards in 3 seconds, which only works if you thinned his band first. A healthy squad with points banked reads it. A hurt squad with a low Medic bursts. It is the Heart of Darkness choice: get through the line before he acts, or pay what it asks.

**What it takes.** Sim: when he crosses a quarter health, start his spell's windup through the existing cast path instead of kneeling, and kneel only if the release was parried, dodged, smashed or broken. Screen: a last stand banner and the existing cut in, with the kneel cue only after the answer. Small with his own spell. But four of the five spells have one answer, so the cheap version tests points and nerve, not reading. The full version gives each chief a second spell shape for this moment, which adds design, effect art and a cut in line per chief. One call to make: whether a soak counts as an answer. If it does not, a chief who learned your parry asks a harder last question, which puts the learning system to good use.

### 3. Mercy has a price

**What you see.** When he kneels, a panel like Persona 5's Hold Up shows both prices for a few seconds. Spare: who joins, by count and class, his spell and the spell on your fifth button it replaces ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:387](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L387)), and one more hunter at the end of the run. Kill: his Princess piece, its stat and its special, and which chief ahead it helps against. The same piece shows on the road map before the fight, like a Slay the Spire boss portrait.

**What you decide.** Which price to pay, with both in view. Hold spares him. Without it, your fighters cut him down. While he kneels his Wardens stop guarding him, so a kill is a real option, and his nerve drains slower than today's 50 a second, so there is time to choose. Sparing is no longer free. His band makes a last stand while you hold, hitting harder the way a rallying tribe already does when one of theirs falls ([BattleSimulation.Skill.cs:15](../Assets/Scripts/Sim/Battle/BattleSimulation.Skill.cs#L15)). A capture can cost you hits or a fighter, the way Thracia's capture costs the captor.

**Capture or kill.** The run decides. Short on fighters? Capture. Is the next chief's spell one his piece blunts? Kill. Love the spell already on your fifth button? Kill and keep it. Bounty high and the final hunt already big? Kill and keep it smaller. For this to hold, chief pieces need to be built as counters to chiefs ahead, the way each Metal Gear Solid 3 camouflage is tied to its boss. Skullpeak's piece already halves big hits on its wearer ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:318](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L318)), so it already blunts big chief spells for one fighter.

**What it takes.** Sim: in the kneel, clear his band's guard on him, lower the drain, and switch on his band's rally while you hold. Campaign: the panel's numbers, all of which the code already knows. Screen, Codex's side: the panel, plus the kneel clip Codex has already asked for ([HANDOFF_LOG.md:10001](../HANDOFF_LOG.md#L10001)). Design: one counter line per chief piece. Small, and no lock changes. The locked prizes stay the same and just get prices.

### 4. Every chief kneels his own way

**What you see.** One word on the chief's fight card and his codex page, with one line under it. Proud: he never kneels, so he has to be broken by nerve while he still fights. Craven: he kneels sooner than the others, but runs if you do not spare him within 2 seconds, which brings back the 2 Oct idea of a chief who escapes and returns leading a splinter group ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:87](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L87)). Treacherous: his kneel can be a feint with its own small tell, and a Hold during the feint lets him strike your Medic. Devoted: his band throws itself in front of him, so sparing him means outlasting them.

**What you decide.** The plan starts on the road. A proud chief needs a squad that breaks nerve. A craven one needs points banked for a fast spare. A treacherous one needs patience at the kneel. A devoted one needs a squad that can take hits.

**Capture or kill.** It changes per chief, so a run captures some chiefs and kills others, and the codex teaches each one. Undertale shows how much a gate can say: Asgore breaking the Mercy button tells you who he is in one move. The Nemesis patent describes captains built from strengths, weaknesses and fears ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)). Chiefs already learn from your earlier runs ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:203](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L203)), so a chief you spared last time could come back treacherous.

**What it takes.** Sim: four small kneel rules keyed to a chief trait, and the trait in each chief's data. Screen: the card word, a feint tell and codex lines. Medium. It partly changes the 20 Oct base, since a proud chief never kneels. Two cautions. Riot's pullback on Atakhan argues for one temperament per chief, never two. And a temperament that changes because of earlier runs is the same kind of memory the open solicitor question about the Nemesis patent already covers ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:203](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L203)). Google Patents still lists that patent as active.

### 5. Kill fewer, keep more

**What you see.** A join counter on the chief fight: how many of his fighters would join if he broke right now. Each bodyguard's line on the fight card says what its death does to him. Kill his Medic and nobody heals him, but he hits harder. Kill his last Warden and your marks land on him, so the Trapper's Snare sets up the Marksman's crit, but his band rallies.

**What you decide.** Who to kill and how many. Every death makes the fight easier now and the capture smaller later. Focus Fire becomes a choice of target, and Hold becomes restraint toward his whole band, not only toward him.

**Capture or kill.** A squad that needs fighters fights gently and breaks nerve with his band alive. A squad that needs a quick safe win kills his band, then him, for the piece. In between, you kill only his Medic and take a smaller tribe. With his whole band dead a capture brings only his spell, so the last call becomes spell or piece. Builds split too. A Trapper, Marksman and Skirmisher squad lives on marks, which only land once his Wardens fall, so it leans to the kill. A Warden and Medic squad can outlast him, so it leans to the capture. This is Jacob's own 2 Oct rule, kill fewer and keep more ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:86](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L86)), and it matches World of Warcraft's Shannox and Horridon, where each death gives something and costs something.

**What it takes.** Small code. The join rule in Campaign.cs counts only fighters still standing, and a few death rules go into the chief file. Screen: the join counter and the bodyguard lines. But it reverses the 13 Oct call that his whole band joins, fallen ones too ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:319](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L319)), so it is Jacob's call first. One risk: if gentle play means holding all fight, Hold becomes the new spam. Hold's own nerve drain ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md:89](../DESIGN_LOCKS.md#L89)) is the brake to check.
