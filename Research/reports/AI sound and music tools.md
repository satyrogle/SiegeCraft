# Two free local models cover Kindred Siege audio

Yes. Your RTX 3060 can make every sound Kindred Siege needs, for free: **Stable Audio 3 Small SFX for effects and ambience, and ACE-Step 1.5 for music**. Both run natively in ComfyUI and both allow commercial use of what they make ([Stability AI](https://stability.ai/news-updates/meet-stable-audio-3-the-model-family-built-for-artistic-experimentation-with-open-weight-models); [ACE-Step on Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5)). Now the bad news. **Steam makes you disclose any AI audio that ships, that text shows on your store page, and players rank AI audio effects among the uses they dislike most** ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey); [Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/)). No tool, local or paid, hands you a finished game loop or a set of adaptive layers, so cutting loops and lining up stems stays manual work. No source has timed any of these models on a 3060, so every speed figure below comes from a bigger card. A track made from a prompt alone gives you a right to use it, not a copyright you can easily enforce ([US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). Many well known models are off limits: MusicGen, MMAudio, Woosh and YuE2 are non commercial, and HunyuanVideo-Foley's licence excludes the UK outright. The route is simple: generate locally, clean up in a free editor, play it through built in Unity audio with one small music script, log every file, and tick Steam's AI box honestly. Paid tools are a fallback, and ElevenLabs has the clearest written terms of them.

## The route: local first, paid only to fill gaps

| Job | Use | Cost | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Battle and UI SFX, ambience | SA3 Small SFX in ComfyUI | Free, register with Stability | About 0.5 s a take on an RTX 5080, so you can make dozens of takes and pick |
| Better SFX for signature sounds | Stable Audio Open 1.0 locally, or ElevenLabs Sound Effects | Free, or $6 a month | SAO 1.0 is slower but one tester rated it higher; ElevenLabs gives 48 kHz WAV |
| Battle and map music | ACE-Step 1.5 2B turbo in ComfyUI, instrumental, BPM and key fixed | Free, MIT | The 12 GB setup is the 2B model plus the 1.7B planner |
| Short music loops and beds | SA3 Small Music or Medium | Free, register | Instrumental only, takes BPM in the prompt |
| Loop points and stems | PyMusicLooper; RoFormer separation if the generator gives no stems | Free | Cut every stem at the same sample points |
| Loudness | `ffmpeg-normalize` | Free | Normalise the full mix once, same gain on every stem |
| Playback | Built in AudioSource, `PlayScheduled`, AudioMixer | Free | FMOD and Wwise are not needed at this size |
| Paperwork | Log per file, Stability registration, Steam AI disclosure | Free | Backs up your promise to Valve |

The smallest first test is the six sine cues already in `Assets/Scripts/Slice/CampaignArtSound.cs`. Accepted, Unavailable, Shield, Cast, Break and Reward last between 0.16 s and 0.72 s, which sits well inside SA3's SFX and `One-shot` categories. Make eight takes of each, keep the best, and compare them against the sine versions in a real fight. That one pass proves the ComfyUI setup, the trim and normalise batch, and Unity import before any music work starts. Two project notes. The Slice folder belongs to Codex under the repo's CLAUDE.md, so swapping those clips in goes through `HANDOFF_LOG.md`. And audio must only read battle events; it can never feed back into the deterministic sim. The current cue code already works that way.

## Stable Audio 3 and ACE-Step 1.5 are the only local picks that clear every bar

**Stable Audio 3** came out on 20 May 2026. Three of its four models have open weights: Small SFX, Small (music) and Medium. Large is API only ([Stability AI](https://stability.ai/news-updates/meet-stable-audio-3-the-model-family-built-for-artistic-experimentation-with-open-weight-models)). The Small models have under half a billion parameters and make up to 120 s. Medium has 1.4B and makes up to 380 s. All output 44.1 kHz stereo ([GitHub](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3)). VRAM needs are low. On an H200, **Small peaked at 1.69 to 2.40 GB and Medium at 5.07 to 6.52 GB** ([GitHub](https://github.com/Stability-AI/stable-audio-3)). A Windows SFX tool measured Small SFX at **about 0.5 s per take and about 2.5 GB** on an RTX 5080 ([local-sounds](https://github.com/vl3c/local-sounds)). Both should fit your 12 GB card with room spare (LIKELY). A 3060 is several times slower than those cards, so expect a few seconds per clip (GUESS). Stability says every SA3 model was trained on licensed data, with Small SFX trained on a cleaner subset of Freesound ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17991v1)).

SA3 needs **ComfyUI v0.22.0 or later**. Your installed version is not checked ([Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/stable-audio-3-day-0-support)). For Small SFX, `stable_audio_3_small_sfx.safetensors` (2.3 GB) goes in `models/checkpoints` and `t5gemma_b_b_ul2.safetensors` (1.2 GB) goes in `models/text_encoders`. One user runs it at 8 steps, CFG 1.0, with the PingPong sampler from a community node ([jurn.link](https://jurn.link/dazposer/index.php/2026/05/22/how-to-install-stable-audio-3-0-small-sfx-safetensors-and-run-it-in-comfyui/)). Two comments on Comfy's blog say output sounded awful with Sage Attention on and fine once ComfyUI started without it ([Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/stable-audio-3-day-0-support)). If your launch flags turn Sage Attention on, test both ways. ComfyUI sorts SA3 prompts into Music, Instrument, SFX and `One-shot`, and Comfy's own examples include 3 s whooshes, 8 s gravel footsteps and instrument loops tagged with BPM ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/audio/stable-audio/stable-audio-3); [Comfy blog](https://blog.comfy.org/p/stable-audio-3-day-0-support)).

**ACE-Step 1.5** (January 2026, with a 4B XL in April) is the best local music model on paper. The repo's own 12 GB setting is the 2B model with the 1.7B planner; XL needs offloading and quantisation at 12 GB ([GitHub](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)). It makes tracks from 10 s to 10 minutes, takes BPM, key and time signature, and the developers quote **under 10 s per full song on an RTX 3090** ([GitHub](https://github.com/ace-step/ACE-Step-1.5)). A 3060 has about a third of a 3090's compute, so think tens of seconds per song (GUESS). Weights are **MIT**, and the model card says you can use the generated music commercially ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/ACE-Step/Ace-Step1.5)). Its developers place its quality between Suno v4.5 and v5. That is self reported and nobody independent has checked it ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.00744v1)). In ComfyUI the single file `ace_step_1.5_turbo_aio.safetensors` goes in `models/checkpoints`. Cover and repaint are still marked coming soon there, so use the standalone Windows package if you need them ([ComfyUI docs](https://docs.comfy.org/tutorials/audio/ace-step/ace-step-v1-5.md)). It is a music model and is not built for SFX.

Quality splits by job. For short hits, SA3 Small SFX is fast but not the best. One ComfyUI user called its results reasonable at blistering speed, and rated **Stable Audio Open 1.0 higher on quality and on following mix instructions, at about 50 times slower** ([jurn.link](https://jurn.link/dazposer/index.php/2026/05/22/how-to-install-stable-audio-3-0-small-sfx-safetensors-and-run-it-in-comfyui/)). Generated SFX often start with a few milliseconds of silence or a slow attack, which you hear on every hit, so trim every take ([Summer Engine](https://www.summerengine.com/blog/ai-sound-effect-generator-for-games)). Ambience is the easy case. Sharp impacts that must land on one frame are the hard one. For music, ACE-Step gives structure plus BPM and key control, while SA3 Small Music and Medium give instrumental loops and beds. No source tested seamless loop output from any model, so plan to cut loops yourself.

## Paid tools: ElevenLabs is cleanest, Suno and Udio carry the most risk

For paid SFX, **ElevenLabs Sound Effects** is the best documented. Clips run up to 30 s and there is a loop switch. Non looping effects come as 48 kHz WAV, but looped ones come as MP3 only ([ElevenLabs docs](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/capabilities/sound-effects)). Starter costs **$6 a month** and lists a commercial licence ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)). The terms, updated 9 October 2026, say free plans are non commercial, paid plans are commercial, and you keep all rights in the output ([ElevenLabs terms](https://elevenlabs.io/terms-of-use)). The docs and pricing page disagree on credits per effect, so check in the app. Hosted Stable Audio starts at $12 a month for 660 credits under the same Community License as the local models ([Stability explainer](https://stability.ai/explainers/stable-audio-vs-competitors-licensing-export-rights-and-self-hosting-compared)). Adobe Firefly Generate Sound Effects went live on 20 August 2026 and spends premium credits ([Adobe](https://blog.adobe.com/en/publish/2026/08/20/adobe-firefly-expands-its-creative-ai-studio-generate-music-speech-and-sound-effects-in-one-place); [Adobe FAQ](https://www.adobe.com/cc-shared/fragments/products/firefly/plans/faq)); it only makes sense if you already pay Adobe. Krotos builds ambience from a recorded library rather than generating it ([Newsshooter](https://www.newsshooter.com/2025/01/02/krotos-ai-image-to-ambience-feature/)), and Soundly has no text to SFX generator at all ([Soundly](https://getsoundly.com/)).

For paid music, **ElevenLabs Music** has the clearest licence for your case. Its terms define an Indie Game as one with under $500,000 lifetime game revenue, from a developer under $1M a year, and allow it from Starter up ([ElevenLabs music terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)). Starter gives 30 minutes of downloads a month but bans putting the music on streaming services. Creator allows that, and lossless WAV starts at Pro ([ElevenLabs music terms](https://elevenlabs.io/music-terms)). Music costs 900 credits a minute ([ElevenLabs pricing](https://elevenlabs.io/pricing)).

**Suno** is the risky big name. v6 launched on 9 September 2026 ([The Rundown](https://www.therundown.ai/news/suno-v6-music-partners-paid-fan-remixes)). Pro gives 20 song downloads a month and Premier 60 ([Suno pricing](https://suno.com/pricing)). Its terms assign ownership to paid users, but also say Suno makes no promise that any copyright exists in the output, and only songs downloaded through approved channels can be used commercially ([Suno terms](https://suno.com/terms)). UMG and Sony sued Suno again over v6 on 18 September 2026 ([Billboard](https://www.billboard.com/pro/umg-sony-hit-suno-with-lawsuit-after-new-ai-music-model/)). A Munich court ruled on 31 July 2026 that Suno's outputs reproduced GEMA songs ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/munich-district-court-rules-on-ai-generated-music-gema-v-suno)). **Udio is unusable**: downloads have been off since 30 October 2025 ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/10/31/udio-downloads-disabled-umg-deal/)) and 2026 reviews say they are still paused ([Dupple](https://dupple.com/learn/suno-vs-udio)).

## Steam disclosure is compulsory and your copyright is thin

Valve's content survey defines pre generated AI content as anything made with AI tools that ships with the game and is consumed by players, and says this **includes sound** ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). The January 2026 rewrite dropped disclosure for behind the scenes tools like code assistants, but kept it for AI content in the game, its marketing or its store page ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam)). You also promise Valve the game holds no infringing content ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/steam-softens-its-stance-on-ai-generated-content-opening-the-floodgates-to-the-vast-majority-of-games-that-use-it)). Some survey answers cannot be edited after approval without contacting Steam Support, so get it right first time ([Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)). In short, every AI WAV in the build means ticking the box and naming the tools. Your current sine cues are plain code synthesis, not generative AI, and need nothing (LIKELY). The exact January 2026 form wording was not found in a Valve source.

Players are hostile on paper. Quantic Foundry's late 2025 survey of 1,799 gamers found **85% below neutral on generative AI in games and 63% at the most negative option**, with audio effects among the most disliked uses ([Quantic Foundry](https://quanticfoundry.com/2025/12/18/gen-ai/); [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1664229/attitudes-towards-genai-in-video-games-by-use-case/)). Real cases are milder. My Winter Car disclosed AI radio music and still hit 95% positive on over 11,000 reviews ([GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/games/survival/despite-ai-generated-textures-audio-and-music-steam-users-shower-hotly-anticipated-survival-game-in-11-000-95-percent-positive-reviews-in-just-a-few-days/)). Aniimo, accused of undisclosed AI music, launched to mixed reviews and had to apologise ([Sportskeeda](https://www.sportskeeda.com/esports/aniimo-mostly-positive-steam-four-days-launch-amid-alleged-ai-use-controversy)). The pattern: disclosed, supplemental AI audio in a good game survives, while hidden or bad AI audio gets punished. A small solo game has less goodwill to spend than either.

Ownership is weak everywhere. The US Copyright Office said on 29 January 2025 that prompts alone do not make you the author; only human selection, arrangement or changes can be protected ([US Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf)). In the UK, CDPA section 9(3) still gives computer generated works to whoever made the arrangements ([legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/9)), but the government's 18 March 2026 report signals it could be removed ([HSF Kramer](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s9-3-cdpa-could-go)). Do not build value on it. The EU AI Act's Article 50 has applied since 2 August 2026, but its deep fake rule does not touch fantasy battle music. The Commission's guidance puts content that defies nature, like dragons, outside it, and marking output is the tool maker's job, not yours ([AI Act Article 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Gaming Tech Law](https://www.gamingtechlaw.com/2026/07/ai-act-transparency-obligations-top-10-questions/)). The practical upshot: the more you edit, layer and arrange by hand, the more of the result is yours.

The real legal risk is an output that copies a real song. The Munich court found Suno reproduced works like "Daddy Cool" from short prompts ([Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/munich-district-court-rules-on-ai-generated-music-gema-v-suno)). If you ship something like that, you broke your promise to Valve, whatever the vendor's licence says. So never name artists or songs in prompts, listen for hooks you recognise, and keep a log per file: tool, model version, plan, date, prompt and edits. If you ever release the soundtrack, DistroKid now asks for AI credits at upload ([Digital Music News](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/05/14/distrokid-ai-credit-disclosure-what-it-looks-like/)), and turning on a Content ID add on could get streamers of your own game claimed (LIKELY). itch.io has its own AI disclosure with a sound box ([itch.io](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging)).

## Built in Unity audio and one small script handle adaptive music

The Unity side has one gap. A third party tracker lists Unity 2022.3 LTS as past end of support, with 2022.3.62f3 as the final release ([endoflife.ai](https://endoflife.ai/unity/2022.3)), and the Audio Random Container only arrived in 2023.2 ([Unity 2023.2 manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/cn/2023.2/Manual/AudioRandomContainer-UI.html)). Everything else is there. `AudioSource.PlayScheduled` on the `AudioSettings.dspTime` clock is Unity's own preferred way to stitch music clips ([Unity docs](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/AudioSource.PlayScheduled.html)). Work out clip length as `(double)clip.samples / clip.frequency`, alternate two sources because a scheduled source cannot be rescheduled, and queue about a second ahead ([John Leonard French](https://johnleonardfrench.music/ultimate-guide-to-playscheduled-in-unity/)). AudioMixer snapshots blend game states through `TransitionToSnapshots`, and Duck Volume gives side chain ducking from a Send ([Unity snapshots](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/ScriptReference/Audio.AudioMixer.TransitionToSnapshots.html); [Unity mixer manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/ru/current/Manual/AudioMixerInspectors.html)). **FMOD is free only under $200k company revenue and a $600k budget, and needs a splash logo; Wwise Indie is free under a $250k budget** ([FMOD](https://www.fmod.com/licensing); [Audiokinetic](https://www.audiokinetic.com/en/wwise/pricing/)). Neither earns its keep for two to four stems and a few stingers.

For fights that last seconds, **vertical layering beats horizontal switching**. Layering fades stems of one piece in and out and suits intensity. Horizontal switching swaps whole sections and is much harder to make flow ([CRI part 1](https://blog.criware.com/index.php/2020/04/20/dynamic-music-with-adx2-part1-vertical-layering/); [CRI part 2](https://blog.criware.com/index.php/2020/05/20/dynamic-music-with-adx2-part2-horizontal-sequencing/)). Hades II is the one roguelike whose music structure is public. Each song is an FMOD event, stems are toggled on and off, and shared drums, bass and guitar keep playing while only the differing stems crossfade ([Thunderstore](https://thunderstore.io/c/hades-ii/p/NikkelM/Music_Maker_API/)). Copy that. Per act, make one theme in two to four stems: bed, percussion, lead, danger. The map plays the bed. Battle start adds percussion on the next bar. Intensity read from sim state drives lead and danger volume. Win, loss and boss phase fire stingers on a group that ducks the music. Start every stem at the same `PlayScheduled` time at volume zero and only ever fade them, or they drift out of phase. Switch screens on the next bar with `SetScheduledEndTime` on the old set and `PlayScheduled` on the new. These are my suggestions and are not tested here. Free scripts exist but are small: robertrumney/adaptive-music and Doremi are MIT, while Altifox Audio Manager is `CC BY-NC` and cannot ship ([adaptive-music](https://github.com/robertrumney/adaptive-music); [Doremi](https://github.com/Meorge/Doremi); [Altifox](https://github.com/AltifoxStudio/Altifox-Audio-Manager)). Writing your own small music director is the realistic path.

Getting AI music into that shape takes a few free tools. **PyMusicLooper** finds beat aware loop points and can export them in samples or split a track into intro, loop and outro files ([PyMusicLooper](https://github.com/arkrow/PyMusicLooper)). For stems, take the generator's own first. Otherwise use a RoFormer model through UVR5 or `audio-separator`: BS-RoFormer won the 2023 Sound Demixing Challenge, and Demucs is archived ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.01809); [Demucs](https://github.com/facebookresearch/demucs)). Separated stems bleed, so use them as add on layers, not solos. Essentia confirms BPM and key ([Essentia](https://essentia.upf.edu/tutorial_rhythm_beatdetection.html)). Cut every stem at the same sample points, trim seams to zero crossings to kill clicks ([Bugnet](https://bugnet.io/blog/how-to-fix-unity-audiosource-clip-pop-on-loop-restart)), then normalise the full mix once and put that same gain on every stem. Keep source files as WAV, because MP3 can pad silence and break loops in Unity ([Unity forum](https://forum.unity.com/threads/gapless-mp3-playback.66090/)). For loudness, PlayStation's guidance is `-24 LUFS` for console and `-18 LUFS` for portable, with true peak at `-1 dBTP` ([Audiokinetic](https://audiokinetic.com/en/mastering-a-game-with-wwise-part1)), and a survey of 52 games averaged `-17.78 LUFS` ([Designing Sound](https://designingsound.org/2012/07/30/video-games-and-loudness-standards-interview-with-sonys-garry-taylor/)). For a PC and Steam Deck game, aim the whole mix around `-16` to `-18 LUFS` and sit music a little lower to leave room for hits. That is a suggestion, not a standard.

SFX need a different routine. Make four to eight takes per sound, keep three to six that match, and play them as random variations. Build signature sounds (crit, death, boss hit) from layers: a sharp transient, a body for tone, a sub for punch and a short tail. A body with no transient sounds muffled, and a transient with no body sounds thin ([Violet Recording](https://violetrecording.com/how-to-make-impact-sounds/)). One `ffmpeg-normalize` batch can trim silence and peak normalise a whole folder ([ffmpeg-normalize](https://github.com/slhck/ffmpeg-normalize); [FFmpeg docs](https://ffmpeg.org/ffmpeg-filters.html#silenceremove)). This command is not tested here:

```
ffmpeg-normalize raw/*.wav -nt peak -t -1 -prf "silenceremove=start_periods=1:start_threshold=-50dB:stop_periods=-1:stop_threshold=-50dB:stop_duration=0.1:stop_silence=0.05" -ar 48000 -c:a pcm_s16le -of clean
```

In Unity, import short SFX as PCM, frequent noisy hits as ADPCM (3.5 times smaller than PCM and far lighter on CPU than Vorbis), and music as Vorbis. Streaming carries about 200 KB overhead per clip, so do not stream stems that must start together ([Unity manual](https://docs.unity3d.com/2022.3/Documentation/Manual/class-AudioClip.html)). With no Audio Random Container, write a small pool of AudioSources with random pitch and volume and a cap per sound, so a big auto battle does not stack thirty identical hits at once.

## Conclusion

Money is not the limit here; editing time is. The local models are free and fast enough to throw most takes away, so the real work is picking, trimming, looping and mixing. That hand work does three jobs at once. It makes loops seamless, it supplies the human authorship that copyright law looks for, and it makes the result sound less like stock AI to players who are primed to dislike it. Spend it on the sounds players hear hundreds of times per run: the hit, the break, the reward.

The risk sits with you, not the vendor. Every licence above, paid or free, still leaves you promising Valve that the build holds nothing infringing, and of the terms read for this research, only Stability's paid Enterprise tier mentions indemnity. So the log per file is your defence, not admin. Confidence: the model and licence picks are LIKELY; anything about speed on a 3060 is a GUESS until the six placeholder cues are regenerated and timed; community opinion is thin because Reddit could not be read for this research.
