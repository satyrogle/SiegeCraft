# Legal and platform rules for AI generated sound effects and music in a commercial Steam game (UK solo dev, as of 13 October 2026)

Not legal advice. Facts and sources only. Every rule is dated. Items marked FLAG may be out of date or rest on secondary sources only.

## 1. Steam / Valve: AI disclosure rules, what to disclose, does audio count, how players react

### Takeaway
Steam still requires disclosure of any generative AI content that players hear or see, and Valve's own content survey names "sound" as covered, so AI music and SFX that ship in the game must be disclosed (pre generated) and described, and the description is shown on the store page. Player sentiment toward generative AI in games is strongly negative in surveys (Quantic Foundry, Dec 2025: 85% below neutral, audio effects among the most disliked uses), but real cases show disclosed, supplemental AI audio in an otherwise strong game does not reliably sink reviews, while undisclosed or low quality AI audio draws backlash.

### Cited Findings

**Original rules, 10 January 2024**
- Valve announced its AI rules on 10 January 2024 in a Steamworks Development post, splitting AI use into "Pre-Generated" (made with AI tools during development) and "Live-Generated" (made by AI while the game runs). [GamingOnLinux, Jan 2024](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/valve-announces-new-rules-for-games-with-ai-content-on-steam/); [MobileSyrup, 10 Jan 2024](https://mobilesyrup.com/2024/01/10/valve-announces-guidelines-for-games-that-use-ai-steam/); original post: [Steam News, "AI Content on Steam"](https://store.steampowered.com/news/group/4145017/view/3862463747997849618) (page did not render text when fetched; content confirmed via press).
- For pre generated content the developer must "promise Valve that your game will not include illegal or infringing content, and that your game will be consistent with your marketing materials." For live generated content the developer must also state the "guardrails" used to stop illegal output. [TechRadar, Jan 2024](https://www.techradar.com/gaming/steam-softens-its-stance-on-ai-generated-content-opening-the-floodgates-to-the-vast-majority-of-games-that-use-it); [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/valve-announces-new-rules-for-games-with-ai-content-on-steam/)
- Valve said it would include much of the AI disclosure on the game's Steam store page, and would add an in game Steam overlay tool for players to report illegal live generated content. [GamingOnLinux](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2024/01/valve-announces-new-rules-for-games-with-ai-content-on-steam/); [Checkpoint Gaming, Jan 2024](https://checkpointgaming.net/news/2024/01/valve-announces-changes-to-ai-content-policies-on-steam/)

**Current Steamworks content survey wording (fetched 13 Oct 2026, page undated)**
- Pre generated is defined as "Any kind of content that ships with your game and is consumed by players" created with the help of AI tools during development. Live generated is "Any kind of content created with the help of AI tools while the game is running." [Steamworks docs: Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- The survey says this "includes content such as artwork, sound, narrative, localization, etc." Sound is named explicitly. Music is not named separately but falls under "sound, ... etc." [Steamworks docs: Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- Valve will "evaluate the output of AI generated content in your game the same way we evaluate all non-AI content." Survey results are used "to help customers understand how the content in your product matches with their expectations." "Efficiency gains through the use of these tools is not the focus of this section." [Steamworks docs: Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- The FAQ says some survey answers cannot be edited after approval without contacting Steam Support. Valve also does not ship live generated AI Adult Only Sexual Content. [Steamworks docs: Content Survey](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)

**January 2026 revision (first revision since 2024)**
- Valve rewrote the AI disclosure form on 16 January 2026 (some outlets date it 17 January). It was spotted by Simon Carless (GameDiscoverCo). The form was rewritten, not removed. [Game Developer, 16 Jan 2026](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam); [Notebookcheck, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html); [Gigazine, 20 Jan 2026](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20260120-steam-updates-ai-disclosure-guidelines)
- After the change, "AI powered tools" used for workflow efficiency (for example code assistants) do not need disclosure. Disclosure is required for any generative AI content that ends up in the game or its associated materials, including marketing assets and the store page. [Game Developer, 16 Jan 2026](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam)
- New form labels quoted: "AI to generate content for the game" (text box describing the tools used; also covers marketing and store page assets) and "AI content generated during gameplay" (checkbox if the game itself produces AI content). [Game Developer, 16 Jan 2026](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/valve-tweaks-and-clarifies-ai-disclosure-rules-for-steam). KitGuru describes three levels of usage in the new form. [KitGuru via search summary](https://www.kitguru.net/desktop-pc/mustafa-mahmoud/steam-updates-its-gen-ai-disclosure-policies/) FLAG: exact full form wording not published in any source found; check the live Steamworks form.
- Developers are responsible for stopping inappropriate or illegal AI content reaching players; games that fail can be removed. [Notebookcheck, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Steam-updates-AI-disclosure-form-requiring-developers-to-report-visible-and-in-game-AI-but-not-background-tools.1206103.0.html)
- No further Valve rule change after January 2026 was found up to October 2026.

### Inferences
- Shipping Suno/ElevenLabs/other AI music or SFX in the build means ticking the pre generated box and describing the use. The description is public on the store page. Using AI only to help write code, or as a mixing aid that leaves no generated audio in the game, appears not to need disclosure after January 2026.
- Disclosure itself carries no Valve penalty, but because survey answers can be locked after approval, it is safer to disclose accurately at submission than to amend later.
- Backlash risk is highest when AI use is undisclosed and then discovered, when AI audio is clearly low quality, or when AI is used in marketing. A small solo game has less goodwill buffer than Arc Raiders or My Winter Car, so the Quantic numbers are a real wishlist and review risk, especially for story driven players.
- The "promise" that the game contains no infringing content is the developer's warranty to Valve. If an AI track later turns out to copy a real song, the developer, not the AI vendor, is on the hook to Valve.

### Gaps
- Exact text of the January 2026 form and how the store page disclosure box looks now were not found in a primary Valve source.
- No hard data found on wishlist or sales impact of an AI disclosure label specifically, or on review bombing triggered by AI music alone on a small indie title.
- PC Gamer's original July 2025 article with the 7,818 figure was not fetched directly.

## 2. Copyright of AI output: can the dev own or protect AI generated music? (US, UK, EU)

### Takeaway
In the US, a track generated from prompts alone is not protected by copyright (Copyright Office, 29 Jan 2025); only human authored parts, selection, arrangement or modification can be. In the UK, CDPA s9(3) still gives a 50 year "computer generated works" right to the person who made the arrangements, but its fit with modern AI is doubtful and the government's 18 March 2026 report signals it will probably be removed, with no legislation yet. The EU AI Act Article 50 transparency rules have applied since 2 August 2026, but for fictional game music and SFX the developer's duty is light or nil; the machine readable marking duty sits on the AI tool provider.

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- US Copyright Office, *Copyright and Artificial Intelligence, Part 2: Copyrightability*, released 29 January 2025. [Copyright Office report PDF](https://www.copyright.gov/ai/Copyright-and-Artificial-Intelligence-Part-2-Copyrightability-Report.pdf); [Manatt summary](https://manatt.com/insights/newsletters/copyright-office-releases-new-report-on-copyrightability-of-ai-works)
- Conclusion: given current technology, prompts alone do not give enough human control to make the user the author of the output; prompts function as instructions conveying unprotectable ideas, and identical prompts can produce different outputs. [Baker Botts](https://ourtake.bakerbotts.com/post/102jxrk/copyright-office-releases-part-2-of-artificial-intelligence-report); [Privacy World, Feb 2025](https://www.privacyworld.blog/2025/02/copyright-office-copyrighting-ai-generated-works-requires-sufficient-human-control-over-the-expressive-elements-prompts-are-not-enough/)
- Protection requires "sufficient human control over the expressive elements." Human authored material perceptible in the output, and creative selection, arrangement or modification of AI output, can still be protected. [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-generative-ai-outputs-ai); [Sidley, Feb 2025](https://sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2025/02/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-artificial-intelligence-and-copyrightability)
- The report treats audio among generative outputs; no music specific carve out was found in summaries. [Lowenstein Sandler](https://www.lowenstein.com/news-insights/publications/client-alerts/us-copyright-office-issues-report-on-copyrightability-of-generative-ai-outputs-ai)
- Suno's own help text now says paid users may be granted commercial use rights but "generally are not considered the owner of the songs since the output was generated by Suno"; earlier wording said paid subscribers owned their songs. [Music in Africa](https://musicinafrica.net/magazine/suno-adjusts-ai-music-ownership-terms-after-warner-music-partnership/); [Digital Music News, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/22/suno-warner-music-deal-changes/)

**United Kingdom**
- CDPA 1988 s9(3): for a computer generated literary, dramatic, musical or artistic work, the author is "the person by whom the arrangements necessary for the creation of the work are undertaken." Its only court consideration was Nova Productions v Mazooma Games. [Authors Alliance, 19 May 2025](https://www.authorsalliance.org/2025/05/19/the-uks-curious-case-of-copyright-for-ai-generated-works-what-section-93-means-today/); statute: [legislation.gov.uk s9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/9). Term for computer generated works is 50 years from the end of the year of creation: [legislation.gov.uk s12(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/12) (statute text not re fetched in this pass).
- UK Copyright and AI consultation: launched 17 December 2024, closed 25 February 2025 [gov.uk consultation page](https://www.gov.uk/government/consultations/copyright-and-artificial-intelligence); Statement of Progress published 15 December 2025 [UCL Copyright blog, 23 Dec 2025](https://blogs.ucl.ac.uk/copyright/2025/12/23/uk-government-publishes-progress-statement-on-ai-and-copyright-consultation/). FLAG: Lewis Silkin describes the consultation as taking place in December 2025, which conflicts; the Dec 2024 to Feb 2025 dates are the ones commonly given. [Lewis Silkin, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/03/24/opt-out-cop-out-uk-government-rethinks-its-position-on-copyright-and-ai-102mnx9)
- On 18 March 2026 the government published its Report on Copyright and Artificial Intelligence plus Impact Assessment, required by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025. It dropped its preferred broad TDM exception with opt out and will not legislate "at this stage." [Lewis Silkin, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.lewissilkin.com/en/insights/2026/03/24/opt-out-cop-out-uk-government-rethinks-its-position-on-copyright-and-ai-102mnx9); [Bird & Bird, 2026](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/uk/copyright-,-a-,-aiin-the-uk-the-debate-rolls-on)
- On s9(3): the report discusses "the history, and lack of economic relevance" of the computer generated works provision; future reform may include "removing copyright protection for purely AI-generated works." [Addleshaw Goddard, 2026](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/intellectual-property/report-copyright-ai-tale-creativity-innovation/). HSF Kramer headline: report "concludes more evidence is needed although s9(3) CDPA could go." [HSF Kramer, Mar 2026](https://www.hsfkramer.com/notes/ip/2026-03/uk-government-report-on-copyright-and-ai-concludes-more-evidence-is-needed-although-s9-3-cdpa-could-go). Search summaries of law firm notes say the government considers s9(3) should likely be removed rather than amended, while works made with AI assistance stay protected (FLAG: body of HSF/Fieldfisher notes returned 403; wording via search snippets only).
- No deadline set for any reform; s9(3) remains law as of October 2026 on all sources found. [IP Tech Blog, Mar 2026](https://www.iptechblog.com/2026/03/uk-government-consultation-on-copyright-and-artificial-intelligence-the-sound-of-a-can-being-kicked-down-the-road/); [Farrer](https://www.farrer.co.uk/news-and-insights/uk-copyright-and-ai-strategic-pause-or-stuck-in-the-past/)
- The report records "general consensus that outputs should be labelled," with the harder question being "how to do it"; government will work with experts on best practice, and is considering a new digital replica / personality right. [Addleshaw Goddard](https://www.addleshawgoddard.com/en/insights/insights-briefings/2026/intellectual-property/report-copyright-ai-tale-creativity-innovation/)
- Academic work argues s9(3) should be repealed and fits modern generative AI poorly. [King's College London](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/portal/en/publications/time-to-repeal-section-93-of-the-copyright-designs-and-patents-ac/); [JIPLP article](https://academic.oup.com/jiplp/article/19/1/43/7485196)

**EU AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689)**
- Article 50 applies from 2 August 2026. [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/); [Legalithm](https://www.legalithm.com/en/blog/eu-ai-act-what-applies-2-august-2026)
- The Digital Omnibus, Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 July 2026, did not delay Article 50 generally; it delayed high risk rules (Annex III to 2 Dec 2027, Annex I to 2 Aug 2028) and gave only the Art 50(2) marking duty a grace period to 2 December 2026 for systems already on the market before 2 Aug 2026. [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/); [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/); [Gaming Tech Law, Jul 2026](https://www.gamingtechlaw.com/2026/07/ai-act-transparency-obligations-top-10-questions/). FLAG: one source gave 2 Feb 2027 (earlier draft); later sources agree on 2 Dec 2026.
- Art 50(2): "Providers of AI systems ... generating synthetic audio, image, video or text content, shall ensure that the outputs ... are marked in a machine-readable format and detectable as artificially generated." Not applicable to assistive standard editing. [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Art 50(4): "Deployers of an AI system that generates or manipulates image, audio or video content constituting a deep fake, shall disclose that the content has been artificially generated or manipulated." For "evidently artistic, creative, satirical, fictional or analogous" works, the duty is limited to disclosing the existence of such content "in an appropriate manner that does not hamper the display or enjoyment of the work." Art 50(5): information given "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure." [artificialintelligenceact.eu, Art 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)
- Commission final guidelines on Article 50 published 20 July 2026; Code of Practice on Transparency of AI Generated Content published 10 June 2026. A deep fake is content that "appreciably resembles existing persons, objects, places, entities or events"; the guidelines note content defying the laws of nature (e.g. dragons) cannot mislead and falls outside. Penalties up to EUR 15m or 3% of worldwide turnover, the lower applying to SMEs. [Gaming Tech Law, Jul 2026](https://www.gamingtechlaw.com/2026/07/ai-act-transparency-obligations-top-10-questions/)
- Territorial scope: the Act covers providers and deployers established in third countries where the AI system's output is used in the Union (Art 2(1)(c)). [EUR-Lex, Regulation (EU) 2024/1689](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj) (text not re fetched in this pass).

### Inferences
- A UK solo dev cannot rely on US copyright to stop someone copying a purely prompted AI track. The more the dev composes, edits, rearranges, layers and mixes by hand, the stronger any claim to protection of those human parts.
- In the UK, s9(3) may currently give the dev a thin 50 year right in purely AI generated music, but (a) it is untested for generative AI, (b) commentators doubt such output meets the modern originality test, and (c) the government has signalled removal. Do not build value on it.
- Suno's "not considered the owner" wording means the practical right a dev gets is a contractual licence to use commercially (on paid plans), not ownership. Keep plan and download records.
- EU AI Act for a UK dev selling into the EU: fantasy battle music and SFX are not deep fakes of real people or events, so Art 50(4) likely does not bite. Possible exceptions: realistic cloned voices of real people, or realistic recordings presented as real events. Even then, for a fictional game only an "appropriate" disclosure that does not hamper play is needed; the Steam store disclosure plus a credits line would plausibly serve. Watermarking is the AI vendor's job, not the game dev's.

### Gaps
- Thaler v Perlmutter (DC Circuit, 2025) and any 2026 Supreme Court step were not checked in this pass.
- No UK court has ruled on s9(3) for generative AI music; no reliable view found on whether such output meets UK originality.
- Full gov.uk report text not fetched; s9(3) wording relies on law firm summaries.
- No source found that applies Article 50 specifically to game music or SFX.

## 3. Infringement risk: label lawsuits against Suno and Udio, GEMA cases, 2025/2026 settlements

### Takeaway
The legal fight is half settled: UMG and Warner settled with Udio (Oct/Nov 2025) and Warner settled with Suno (Nov 2025), but Sony sued Udio again (July 2026), UMG and Sony sued Suno again over its new v6 model (18 Sept 2026), and a German court found Suno's outputs themselves infringe GEMA works (31 July 2026). For a game dev, the live risk is not the vendor being sued but an output that closely reproduces a protected song, which the dev then ships and warrants to Valve as non infringing.

[Music Business Worldwide, Jul 2026](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-files-new-lawsuit-against-ai-platform-udio-asserting-over-30000-sound-recordings-a-judge-barred-it-from-adding-to-its-original-case/); [Digital Music News, 22 Dec 2025](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2025/12/22/suno-warner-music-deal-changes/); [Engadget, Sep 2026](https://www.engadget.com/2262978/sony-music-and-umg-say-sunos-new-models-still-violates-their-copyright/)

**Suno terms that matter to a dev (current)**
- Suno blog, 10 August 2026: new Terms of Service and download limits from 3 September 2026. Free: 7 lifetime trial downloads, "not eligible for commercial use." Pro: 20 downloads per month. Premier: 60 per month (unlimited in Suno Studio). Limits apply to songs made before 3 Sept too. Paid plan downloads "remain yours to use commercially or personally." **GEMA (Germany)**
- GEMA v OpenAI, Munich Regional Court, November 2025: lyrics memorisation found infringing; under appeal. [Bristows](https://inquisitiveminds.bristows.com/post/102ngsl/gema-v-suno-munich-court-finds-ai-music-training-and-outputs-infringe-copyright); [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/munich-district-court-rules-on-ai-generated-music-gema-v-suno)
- GEMA v Suno, Munich Regional Court I, 31 July 2026, case 42 O 763/25: court barred, for six compositions (including "Rasputin," "Daddy Cool," "Forever Young"), training reproduction in the US, memorisation in the model on German servers, offering the model to the public, and reproduction and communication to the public through outputs. Works were reconstructed from short open ended prompts. Suno ordered to disclose scale of use and pay damages; appeal possible. First European ruling that training entirely outside the EU can create German liability; US law applied to US training and fair use rejected. [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/munich-district-court-rules-on-ai-generated-music-gema-v-suno); [Variety](https://variety.com/2026/digital/news/suno-loses-ai-lawsuit-gema-1236825010/); [JUVE Patent](https://www.juve-patent.com/cases/munich-regional-court-stops-suno-using-gema-protected-music/); [Forbes, 5 Aug 2026](https://www.forbes.com/sites/virginieberger/2026/08/05/suno-lost-to-gema-why-the-ruling-should-worry-ai-music-companies/)
- Full written judgment not public at time of most commentary; analysis relies on court press materials. [Bird & Bird](https://www.twobirds.com/en/insights/2026/germany/munich-district-court-rules-on-ai-generated-music-gema-v-suno)

### Inferences
- The Munich ruling shows a court accepting that AI outputs can reproduce specific protected songs. A dev who ships such an output in a game sold in Germany would be distributing an infringing copy, whatever the vendor's licence says. The defensive move is to listen critically for recognisable melodies or hooks, avoid prompts naming artists or songs, and favour heavily edited or instrumental, non derivative output.
- Using Suno now means using v6 (prior models retired), which is itself under a fresh UMG/Sony suit. The suit targets Suno, not users, but an adverse ruling could later affect what users can keep doing with outputs.
- Udio outputs are effectively unusable in a game while downloads stay disabled.
- Keep a provenance log per asset: tool, model version, plan tier, date, prompt, edits. This supports the Valve warranty, Content ID disputes and any later claim.

### Gaps
- No case found where a game developer or end user was sued over AI music output.
- Whether Suno or other vendors indemnify users against infringement claims was not verified (terms not fetched in full).
- Sony's status with Suno beyond the Sept 2026 suit, and any settlement after September 2026, not found.

## 4. Practical risks: YouTube Content ID on streamers, soundtrack distribution (DistroKid, Spotify)

### Takeaway
Content ID matches fingerprints of registered reference files; it does not detect "AI" as such, so the risk is (a) an AI track that resembles a registered song, (b) someone else registering similar AI output, or (c) the dev's own distributor registering the soundtrack and then claiming streamers. No authoritative YouTube rule on AI music Content ID eligibility was found. For releasing the OST, DistroKid and Spotify both moved to AI disclosure at upload in 2026 and require full rights, ban impersonation of real artists, and label rather than ban AI music.

### Cited Findings

**Content ID**
- Content ID compares uploads against reference files from rights holders; it matches audio fingerprints and does not detect that a track was made by Suno or any AI model. AI output can still trigger a match if it echoes a registered recording closely enough. [Undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/ai-music-content-id); [SunoWatermark](https://sunowatermark.com/youtube-content-id-ai-music/) FLAG: vendor and SEO blogs, not YouTube documentation.
- One source claims that as of July 2025 fully AI generated audio is not eligible for Content ID registration; no corroboration found. [SunoWatermark](https://sunowatermark.com/youtube-content-id-ai-music/) FLAG: unverified.
- YouTube has a request based takedown route (since 2024) for AI content mimicking an identifiable artist's singing voice. [MusicFlowAI summary](https://www.musicflowai.com/monetize-ai-music/suno-youtube); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/youtube-looks-add-protections-for-musicians-generative-ai-use-cases/691428/)
- DistroKid offers Content ID as an add on service for music it distributes. [Dynamoi guide](https://dynamoi.com/ko/learn/ai-music-distribution/ai-music-youtube-content-id) FLAG: secondary.
- Labels allege Suno and Udio stream ripped YouTube to build training data; Sony's July 2026 Udio complaint includes an anti circumvention count over YouTube. [Music Business Worldwide](https://www.musicbusinessworldwide.com/sony-music-files-new-lawsuit-against-ai-platform-udio-asserting-over-30000-sound-recordings-a-judge-barred-it-from-adding-to-its-original-case/)

**DistroKid**
- DistroKid added an "AI Credits" step at upload (reported 14 May 2026) asking which parts involved AI (lyrics, vocals, instrumental performance, full composition); applies to new uploads only. No AI credit needed for pitch correction, AI assisted mixing or mastering. [Digital Music News, 14 May 2026](https://www.digitalmusicnews.com/2026/05/14/distrokid-ai-credit-disclosure-what-it-looks-like/); [RightsDocket](https://www.rightsdocket.com/insights/ai-music-disclosure-distrokid-spotify-apple-music-2026)
- DistroKid requires you own 100% of rights, including the legal right to distribute content made with AI tools. [Jack Righteous guide](https://jackrighteous.com/en-us/blogs/ai-music-distribution-guide/ai-music-distribution-rules-distrokid-spotify-apple-deezer) FLAG: secondary.

**Spotify**
- Spotify AI Credits beta began 16 April 2026, starting with DistroKid uploads; opt in; shown in mobile credits; Spotify says disclosure does not trigger downranking or removal; declarations are self reported. [Chartlex](https://www.chartlex.com/blog/business/spotify-ai-song-credits-disclosure-2026); [Songprint](https://songprint.space/blog/spotify-ai-music-policy-2026)
- Spotify bans vocal impersonation unless authorised and runs anti spam measures; "AI Persona" badges for AI artists rolling out from August or September 2026 (sources disagree on date), reportedly excluded from recommendations by default. [Songprint](https://songprint.space/blog/spotify-ai-music-policy-2026); [Undetectr](https://undetectr.com/blog/spotify-for-creators-ai-music-2026) FLAG: dates conflict; check Spotify for Artists docs.
- Spotify's 2026 Form 6-K filing references AI policy context. [SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001639920/000114036126017211/ef20071303_ex99-1.htm) (not read in detail).

### Inferences
- Biggest self inflicted risk: if the dev releases the OST via DistroKid and opts into Content ID, YouTube may claim streamers' videos of the dev's own game. Either skip Content ID for the OST or whitelist channels.
- Because purely AI tracks may not be copyrightable (section 2), the dev may have a weak basis to assert Content ID claims or to fight a third party who registers near identical AI output. Human edited arrangements strengthen both positions.
- Releasing the OST needs a paid Suno plan download (commercial rights), honest DistroKid AI credits, and no artist name prompts. The OST is a separate release from the game, with its own disclosure.
- A "streamer safe" statement on the store page and a provenance log help streamers dispute wrong claims.

### Gaps
- No official YouTube Help page found stating whether AI generated music can be registered in Content ID.
- No documented case found of streamers getting Content ID claims specifically from AI music in a game.
- DistroKid's and Spotify's own help pages were not fetched; details rely on press and blogs.

## 5. Other storefronts and consoles (itch.io, Epic, Nintendo, Sony, Xbox)

### Takeaway
itch.io has a generative AI disclosure field (since November 2024) with a "sound" category; it is strictly enforced for asset pages and encouraged for games, and undisclosed AI assets lose browse visibility. Epic has no disclosure rule. No public developer facing AI disclosure rule was found for Nintendo, PlayStation or Xbox stores as of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- itch.io added a generative AI disclosure question to the project edit page; it covers content made with gen AI tools "even if" the developer has "hand-edited it." Developers tick affected areas (graphics, sound, text and dialogue, code) and the project gets an "AI Generated" tag with sub tags; projects with none get "No AI." [itch.io developer update](https://itch.io/t/4309690/generative-ai-disclosure-tagging); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/itch-wants-asset-creators-to-disclose-when-they-ve-used-generative-ai)
- itch.io is "strictly enforcing disclosure for all game asset pages due to legal ambiguity around rights associated with Generative AI content"; undisclosed AI asset projects are not removed but are hidden from browse pages. [GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/itch-io-now-requires-creators-to-disclose-ai-generated-content/1100-6527920/); [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/production/itch-wants-asset-creators-to-disclose-when-they-ve-used-generative-ai)
- Whether disclosure is mandatory for games (not assets) is disputed: one source says strongly encouraged for games; a forum post says required only for assets. [Mobile Marketing Magazine](https://mobilemarketingmagazine.com/itch-io-mandates-genai-disclosure-for-creators/); [GamesHub](https://www.gameshub.com/news/news/itch-io-generative-genai-tag-2647206/) FLAG.
- Epic Games Store has no AI disclosure requirement; Epic owned Sketchfab has required a "CreatedWithAI" label since 11 December 2025. [Tech Insider, Jul 2026](https://tech-insider.org/steam-ai-disclosure-2026/) FLAG: secondary.
- Console stores: overviews say there is no clear AI disclosure on console storefronts (Nintendo, PlayStation, Xbox). [The Conversation](https://theconversation.com/are-video-game-developers-using-ai-players-want-to-know-but-the-rules-are-patchy-274850)
- Xbox requirement XR-018 covers user generated content, defining it to include AI assisted content visible to others online, and requires in game reporting tools; it is a moderation rule, not a developer disclosure rule. [OtakuKart](https://otakukart.com/xbox-tightens-ugc-and-ai-content-rules-leaving-sony-and-nintendo-behind/) FLAG: secondary.
- Nintendo's president raised IP concerns about generative AI in an investor Q&A; Nintendo has signalled it is not using it in its own games. [Creative Bloq](https://www.creativebloq.com/entertainment/gaming/i-love-that-nintendos-rejecting-generative-ai); [ACS Information Age, 2024](https://ia.acs.org.au/article/2024/nintendo-says--no--to-generative-ai.html)

### Inferences
- If the game later goes to itch.io, tick "sound" in the AI disclosure. For consoles, no public disclosure form exists, but console certification and publisher contracts normally include IP warranties, so the same provenance log applies.
- Nintendo's IP caution suggests a later Switch port could draw closer scrutiny of AI audio, but no rule says so.

### Gaps
- Console certification requirements (Sony TRC, Nintendo lotcheck, Xbox XR) are under NDA; no public AI specific rule found.
- No primary Epic policy page fetched.
