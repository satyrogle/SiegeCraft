What the HUD needs to learn.

All three KS layouts are rejected. This board studies real interfaces before another design pass. Click a screenshot to inspect it. The observations below are our visual analysis; the proposed KS applications are not approved design decisions.

01 / Identity + selected fighter

Darkest Dungeon

Observe
Heavy black shapes, scratchy borders and illustrated icons belong to the same world as the characters. Gold links the selected fighter to the skill panel; red brackets identify targets.
For KS
Give selection, command and target a connected visual language. Design a consistent family of silhouettes and borders, not five unrelated coloured buttons.
Boundary
The dense statistics panel suits deliberative turns. KS needs faster reading and more open battle space.

02 / Squad ownership + command context

Bad North

Observe
The pink squad flag, ground selection and portrait ribbon agree. Three circular controls sit with that commander; the selected Move control has a bright rim and a short label.
For KS
Make it immediately clear who receives an order and which target is armed. Use shape, label and outline as well as colour.
Boundary
KS commands can affect an entire formation. Do not imply every directive belongs to one selected fighter.
Screenshot source ↗ · Developer: broad squad commands ↗
03 / Formation continuity + cause and effect

Unicorn Overlord

Observe
Mirrored corner formations preserve each fighter's position and health while the large figures perform an attack. A single action banner names Iron Crusher; opposing standards establish the sides.
For KS
Keep formation identity stable during motion. Reserve a distinct moment for an important order or chief action; reduce competition from routine hit text.
Boundary
Its staged automatic exchanges are not KS's live directive interface. Copying the whole HUD would leave KS commands unresolved.

04 / Combat priority + a repeatable icon family

Hades

Observe
Repeated diamond icons form a coherent set. Boss bars, player health and currency occupy different edges; the active threat remains in the scene.
For KS
Establish a clear rank between urgent threat, directive readiness and background information. Give the icon family a deliberate shared shape.
Boundary
Hades follows one hero. KS needs multiple fighters, two formations and live orders; its minimal combat HUD cannot transfer wholesale.

A paired state, not just a pretty screenshot

Different decisions need different density.

Here the same diamond language expands into three choices with names, effects and numerical changes. The battle is visually subdued behind the decision.

KS proposal: combat should favour quick directives and threats; Items, Pals and upgrades deserve richer explanations when the player is choosing. One permanent information density will serve neither well.

Developer reasoning / Into the Breach

Readability must shape the design.

Matthew Davis's GDC postmortem discusses UI-guided design, building iconography, showing enemy targets and attack types, and limiting complexity to keep the interface legible. That supports designing signals alongside actions. It does not justify adding prediction data that KS's simulation does not expose.

Why the rejected layouts fell short

I solved the wrong level.

They mostly rearranged bars. They reused the same generic plates, gave A and C too little structural difference, and left the busy combat feedback untouched. They did not demonstrate a coherent icon family or the targeting, unavailable and active states.

Pixel equality proved that the fighters were preserved. It did not prove a good interface.

Direction to discuss — not an approved treatment

A KS identity across the whole game.

My recommendation is a restrained dark graphic system: strong silhouettes, bone-readable type, muted material accents and authored command symbols. Let tribe emblems supply identity without turning the permanent HUD into Bloodridge-only decoration.

Combine Darkest Dungeon's consistency, Bad North's context and Unicorn Overlord's formation continuity. Use Hades as a hierarchy reference. No direct asset copying, borrowed mechanics or renderer migration.

Before another pass: choose the visual language from actual references, then make one KS concept showing normal combat, targeting and an unavailable command on the same real fight. Audit at world 1× / UI 2× and in motion before calling it a viable direction. This board contains no new KS mockup.
