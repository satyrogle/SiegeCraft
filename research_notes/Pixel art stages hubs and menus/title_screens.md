# Pixel art title screens, main menus and opening sequences

Method note for the report writer: most per game layout facts below come from my own viewing of single captured frames, because Game UI Database, MobyGames and PCGamingWiki blocked automated access (Cloudflare checks, which I did not bypass, and HTTP 403). Lossless 1920x1080 captures came from Interface In Game (Blasphemous, Stardew Valley, Hollow Knight). The rest are thumbnail frames of YouTube title screen capture videos (mostly the "u2numb" channel, which records each game's title screen; frames are 1280x720 or 480x360 and JPEG compressed). A still frame cannot show what animates, so motion claims for those games are marked as not verified. Positions are approximate.

## 1. How are the title screens of the reference games laid out and built?

### Takeaway
Every screen I could view uses one focal subject that carries the premise (a character, a place, a campfire, a symbol), a logo drawn in the game's own visual language and set in the art's quietest zone, and a short menu of 1 to 5 items, either stacked under the logo on the centre axis or set on the side opposite the focal subject. Several games split it into a logo plus "press a button" screen and a separate menu screen.

### Cited Findings

#### General pattern source
- A menu based title screen either offers choices immediately or waits for a press start and then offers them. ([Giant Bomb, Title Screen](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Title_Screen))

### Inferences
- Menu lengths seen: 1 item (Darkest Dungeon), 3 (Chained Echoes, Owlboy), 4 (Blasphemous, Stardew buttons), 5 (Hollow Knight). Five is the top of the range.
- Two layouts cover every viewed screen, plus a logo only variant:

| Layout | Games | Where the list sits |
|---|---|---|
| Centre axis stack | Hollow Knight, Chained Echoes, Owlboy, Darkest Dungeon, Octopath, Sea of Stars, Stardew, The Messenger | Under the logo, in a calm band of sky, sea, haze or black |
| Side split | Blasphemous (menu screen), Dead Cells | Opposite the focal subject, over the darkest or flattest third |
| Logo alone | Blasphemous (press screen), Hyper Light Drifter, Eastward | Prompt only, under the logo |

- No viewed screen puts a flat strip or panel behind the list. The quiet zone is painted into the art (dark trunks, flat sky, the inside of a cloud ring).
- Blasphemous separates logo and menu into two screens, so a big character close up never fights the logo for attention.

### Gaps
- No title screen frame found for Kingdom Rush or Children of Morta.
- States after "press start" for Sea of Stars, Dead Cells, Octopath, The Messenger, Eastward and Hyper Light Drifter were not seen (stills only).
- A second low resolution Sea of Stars capture may show figures at the campfire; I could not enlarge it to confirm, and no source says whether the fire scene tracks save progress.
- No developer write up found on how any of these title screens was composed or layered, apart from what the Stardew wiki reveals.

## 2. How do studios make the logo and the menu part of one composition? Where does the list sit relative to the focal point, and how does it stay legible?

### Takeaway
The art is composed with a reserved quiet zone, and the logo is drawn with the same rendering rules as the art and carries a motif the scene repeats (moon, owl, torch, mountain, flame, wooden sign). The list then sits in that quiet zone, under the logo on the centre axis or opposite the focal subject, and stays legible through outlines, a strong selected state and low detail behind it, not through a pasted panel.

### Cited Findings
- Key art guidance: one focal subject, one environment cue, and enough empty space for the title or UI text; reserve a quiet, low detail area without strong edges for type, with the hero off centre; check that area for bright clouds or hard edges that would fight the title; keep clear space so the title survives store crops. ([Morphic, How to create game key art](https://morphic.com/resources/how-to/create-a-game-key-art); [GameDev.net, key art draft workflow](https://gamedev.net/blogs/entry/2297919-a-small-prompt-workflow-for-quick-indie-game-key-art-drafts))
- An indie key art devlog says the art itself must leave space for the name, logo and ratings. ([Blue Flame devlog, How to create a Key Art](https://foxssake.itch.io/blue-flame/devlog/112780/how-to-create-a-key-art-for-your-game))
- A film and TV key art guide lists an off balance composition with no room for the title as a mistake and recommends a centred logo lockup. ([discovery+ key art guide](https://brand.discoveryplus.com/brand-identity/key-art-guide/))
- A logo devlog notes most game logos carry a glow or outline so the lettering reads on dark backgrounds. ([Hooked devlog, Logo](https://lowfontaine.itch.io/hooked/devlog/181575/logo))
- Logo motifs seen in the captures (question 1): Sea of Stars turns the O into a crescent moon and overlaps the scene's moon; Owlboy turns the O into an owl face; Darkest Dungeon splits its wordmark with a torch emblem; The Messenger stacks its wordmark under a mountain inside a moon disc; Dead Cells puts a flame between its words; Stardew paints its name on a wooden farm sign with leaves.
- The Darkest Dungeon torch is a core mechanic: the light meter tracks torchlight in dungeons; low light raises stress, ambushes and enemy strength but raises loot and crits. ([Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Light Meter](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Light_meter))
- The Messenger's task is to carry a scroll to the highest peak in the world. ([Use A Potion review](https://useapotion.com/2018/09/the-messenger-review))
- List placement seen (captures in question 1): Blasphemous right aligned in the right third over dark trunks, opposite a character filling the left two thirds; Dead Cells logo and prompt in the left third over flat sky, opposite the castle; Chained Echoes and Owlboy centred under the logo inside a cloud ring or vortex; Hollow Knight centred over dark haze; Darkest Dungeon one framed button under the focal estate; Stardew a row of buttons along the bottom edge.
- Selected state treatments seen: colour change plus a small icon (Blasphemous), a gold glow lozenge (Chained Echoes), an underline (Owlboy), ornaments on both sides (Hollow Knight), a framed button (Darkest Dungeon). Text treatment: white pixel text with a dark outline (Chained Echoes, Owlboy).

### Inferences
- The Messenger's mountain in a moon disc emblem appears to show the scroll's destination peak, so even a minimal black title carries the premise (my reading of the capture).
- A logo reads as part of the picture when it follows the art's rendering rules (pixel edges, palette, the scene's light) and repeats a motif from the scene. A logo in a different rendering language reads as a sticker.
- The rejected Kindred Siege menu (one illustration, a pasted logo, a five item list on a dark dithered strip at the left) matches none of the viewed examples: none uses a strip, and in the side split examples the dark or empty third is part of the painting.
- When the focal subject is a place (Darkest Dungeon's estate, Dead Cells' island), the centre axis stack works: logo above, place in the middle, short list or prompt below. When it is a big character close up (Blasphemous), the list goes opposite the character and the logo can move to its own screen.

### Gaps
- No studio postmortem found explaining a specific title screen composition decision for these games.
- Contrast values behind the menu text were not measured.

## 3. What animation do they add, and how is it built?

### Takeaway
Title screen motion in pixel games is small, local and loopable: drifting clouds and parallax, birds, fire flicker, water glints, floating particles and a few interactive props. The cheap production methods are whole pixel parallax loops sized to divide the screen width, palette (colour) cycling inside one indexed image, and separate sprite overlays for logo parts and props.

### Cited Findings
- Stardew's title screen animates clouds and two flying birds, has an entrance where the menu comes down, and hides interactive sprite props in the logo (birds and foliage from the R, waving Junimos, an alien behind the E, butterflies from a screw). ([Stardew Valley Wiki, Secrets](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Secrets))
- Hollow Knight treats the menu background as a swappable layer with its own ambient sound per style and a desaturation effect for two styles. ([Hollow Knight Wiki, Menu Styles](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Menu_Styles_(Hollow_Knight)))
- Parallax loop method (Slynyrd Pixelblog 23): example canvas 96x56 at 1x; scroll speeds must divide the canvas width evenly (for 96: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 12, 16, 24, 32 or 48 px per frame); the farthest layer moves 1 px per frame and each nearer layer takes the next faster valid speed; loop length in frames equals the screen width; each layer is repeated as many screen widths as its speed in px per frame; tween with width minus one frames and delete the extra frame for a seamless loop; any other looping element needs a cycle that divides the total frame count (a 4 frame cycle fits 96); movement is whole pixel only (sub pixel motion needs engine upscaling); slower than 1 px per frame is possible but can look jittery; use atmospheric perspective; avoid strong focal points in repeating layers; add foreground subjects after the loop works; practise on clouds or stars. ([Slynyrd, Pixelblog 23, Parallax Scrolling](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2019/11/12/pixelblog-23-parallax-scrolling))
- Colour cycling: in a 256 colour indexed image, groups of palette entries rotate over time, which animates every pixel using them at once with almost no memory. Mark Ferrari's scenes animate rain, snow, ocean waves, moving fog, clouds, smoke, waterfalls, streams and lakes from one flat 640x480 image with one palette and no layers. A blend mode (BlendShift) fades between steps for smoother motion; an optimisation is to redraw only the pixels that use animated colours. ([EffectGames, Old School Color Cycling with HTML5, 2011](http://www.effectgames.com/effect/article-Old_School_Color_Cycling_with_HTML5.html))
- Ferrari's GDC 2016 talk (8 Bit and 8 Bitish Graphics, Outside the Box) demonstrates colour cycling and palette shifting as background animation without frame by frame drawing. ([Wikipedia, Color cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_cycling); [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/thimbleweed_park_artist_will_s))
- Modern engine version: store each pixel's palette index (for example in the red and green channels as UV into a palette texture) and shift the lookup in a shader over time; sample palette texels at their centres with point filtering, clamping and no mipmaps, or boundary colours come out wrong; a Godot shader supports full or partial sprite cycling with optional blending between steps. ([Aseprite community](https://community.aseprite.org/t/export-indexed-color-as-uv-coordinate-pixels-and-a-palette-lookup-texture/14154); [Unity forum, palette swapping shader](https://discussions.unity.com/t/palette-swapping-shader-for-single-channels/676602); [Godot Shaders, Advanced Palette](https://godotshaders.com/shader/advanced-palette-godot-4/))
- Pedro Medeiros (Saint11) teaches pixel art effects as step by step animated GIF tutorials rather than written guides. ([Creative Bloq](https://creativebloq.com/news/artist-makes-animated-gif-tutorials-to-teach-you-pixel-art))
- Light and glow elements visible in captures (motion not verified from stills): the Sea of Stars campfire as the only warm light, the glowing flame in the Darkest Dungeon logo emblem, the glitch (colour offset) treatment on the Hyper Light Drifter logo, the neon bloom on the Dead Cells logo, floating particles in Hollow Knight. (captures in question 1)

### Inferences
- A campfire title scene can get its motion from a short flame and ember sprite loop, a pulsing light pool, colour cycled water glints and slow cloud parallax, with very few drawn frames.
- Making every loop's period divide one shared frame count (the Slynyrd rule) keeps all loops in sync.
- Keep moving detail out of the menu zone; motion near text hurts reading.

### Gaps
- No breakdown found of how Sea of Stars, Dead Cells, Blasphemous, Owlboy or Chained Echoes animate their title screens (layer counts, frame counts); I could not watch the videos.
- Search results list a fire tutorial (number 11) in Saint11's GIF series, but I could not open its frames.

## 4. How do games tie the title screen to their premise, and how do opening sequences flow into the menu or the first fight?

### Takeaway
The title art usually shows the premise's place or symbol (the inherited estate, the prison island, the destination mountain, the sea and moon, a campfire, the family home). The strongest flows go: menu, a short premise sequence (narrated cinematic, wordless cinematic, in engine scene or playable dream), a forced first fight or tutorial with fixed characters, then the hub.

### Cited Findings

#### Title art and premise
- Darkest Dungeon's title shows the ruined estate the player inherits (capture in question 1); in the opening cinematic the Ancestor's letter describes unearthing something terrible beneath the estate and calls the heir home to the Hamlet; he also narrates battles. ([The Artifice](https://the-artifice.com/darkest-dungeon/); [Tropedia](https://tropedia.fandom.com/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon); search excerpt, pages not fetched)
- Dead Cells' title shows the island castle and a ship (capture in question 1); the Prisoner wakes in the depths of the island's prison with amnesia and does not speak. ([Wikipedia, Dead Cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Cells))
- Octopath's eight travelers' first initials spell OCTOPATH in the order of their first chapters and of their clockwise placement on the map. A fan comment says the title screen cycles through the travelers in that order (unverified). ([LP Archive](https://lparchive.org/Octopath-Traveller/Update%2001); [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/921570/discussions/0/1642042464745681603); [Nintendo Life comments](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2018/07/video_8_little_facts_about_octopath_traveler_that_you_might_not_know))
- In Sea of Stars, campfires are where you make camp, switch leader and see the party training; the title screen centres on a campfire. ([Common Sense Gamer](https://commonsensegamer.com/sea-of-stars-zale-or-valere/); capture in question 1)
- Children of Morta uses an overhead view of the family homestead in place of a conventional menu, and the house grows new rooms as you progress. ([Tech Gaming review](https://www.tech-gaming.com/children-of-morta/); [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/a-gorgeous-game-about-a-monster-slaying-family-1823131882))
- Hollow Knight's menu styles unlock through endings and challenges, so the title screen records progress. This is a known pattern, the evolving title screen (background changes as the game progresses), distinct from a randomized title screen. ([Hollow Knight Wiki](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Menu_Styles_(Hollow_Knight)); [Giant Bomb, Evolving Title Screen](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Concepts/Evolving_Title_Screen); [TV Tropes, Randomized Title Screen](https://www.tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/RandomizedTitleScreen))

#### Opening sequence formats
- Narrated cinematic, travel cinematic, then a forced first fight (Darkest Dungeon): after the opening cinematic, a second cinematic, Road Home, shows the stagecoach crashing because its driver seems to have gone mad. Then the Old Road tutorial: always the same two level 0 heroes (Reynauld the Crusader, Dismas the Highwayman); the simplest map, two rooms joined by one corridor; a Brigand Cutthroat halfway down the corridor where combat is explained; one curio; a final room with a trapped strongbox guarded by a Bloodletter and a Fusilier; no retreat and no return. On clearing, the player is sent to the Hamlet with 5,000 gold for provisions; a dead hero is replaced off the stagecoach. ([Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Old Road](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Old_Road))
- Wordless cinematic (Hyper Light Drifter): an evocative opening shows a cataclysm and giant titans shattering, then the player wakes into the aftermath. One account describes the camera closing on a diamond that fills with light, the world turning an ominous red, a glitch, and the Drifter appearing; the same account says the game asks for your name on starting. ([PC Gamer review](https://www.pcgamer.com/hyper-light-drifter-review); [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/games/hyper_light_drifter/b/pc/archive/2016/03/22/playing-the-opening-hours-of-hyper-light-drifter%27s-mysterious-world); [The Tech](https://thetech.com/2020/04/15/hyper-light-drifter-qt))
- Playable dream (Owlboy): a black screen with white text about the revered owls; Otus appears and his mentor puts him through flight and throwing lessons he fails; then a walk while shadowy townsfolk follow as the ground falls away; fade to black, Otus wakes and the whole thing was a dream. ## 5. What resolutions and pixel scales do these screens use?

### Takeaway
The only base resolutions I could source are 640x360 for Blasphemous and Owlboy (community claims, not official). The general rule is one base canvas scaled by whole numbers (640x360 x3 = 1080p, x6 = 4K) with logo, menu font and art on the same pixel grid, or else a deliberate high resolution UI layer over the pixel art (Dead Cells, Octopath), because mixed pixel sizes (mixels) look inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- Blasphemous: Steam users report a native 640x360 with 1080p and other exact multiples pixel perfect; at 1920x1200 a black frame appears and players switch the scaling mode from pixel perfect to scale; 4:3 modes misbehave. ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/774361/discussions/0/1638669204735629860); [Steam discussion 2](https://steamcommunity.com/app/774361/discussions/0/3270186971456259848))
- My measurement of the lossless 1920x1080 Blasphemous captures: horizontal runs of identical colour in the art, the menu text and the version text step in multiples of about 3 px and mostly start on a 3 px grid; the logo's runs also step by 3 (16, 19, 22, 25, 28, 31 px) but are not snapped to the same grid offset. This suggests logo, menu font and art share one x3 pixel size (matching 640x360), with the logo placed off grid. The PNGs carry some resampling noise, so treat it as approximate. ([main menu PNG](https://interfaceingame.com/wp-content/uploads/blasphemous/blasphemous-main-menu-1920x1080.png); [press any button PNG](https://interfaceingame.com/wp-content/uploads/blasphemous/blasphemous-press-any-button-1920x1080.png))
- Owlboy: a post in a Steam community forum (the Aseprite one) gives a base resolution of 640x360 (not official). ([Steam community thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/431730/discussions/0/3200369647710806615))
- Whole number scaling: 640x360 goes x2 to 720p, x3 to 1080p and x6 to 4K; pixel art only looks right at its designed size or whole multiples. ([Diary #11, So You Want a Resolution](https://lunar-chippy.itch.io/iron-village-prototype/devlog/705894/diary-11-so-you-want-a-resolution); [GDevelop forum](https://forum.gdevelop.io/t/about-resolution-pixel-art-and-size/48825/9))
- Mixels: mismatched pixel sizes in one game, which happen when small pixel art is enlarged in the engine; the fix is redrawing at the right size. ([itch.io community post](https://itch.io/post/15697736)) Unity's pixel perfect setting does not make UI sizes consistent, and pixel text distorts at non integer window sizes; one developer moved the whole UI to vector because each pixel text size needed its own font. ([Unity forum](https://discussions.unity.com/t/pixel-perfect-gui-is-not-pixel-perfect-at-all-distorted-text-in-game-tab/892120); [Crystal Realms devlog 24](https://marcomeijer.itch.io/crystalrealms/devlog/835888/weekly-devlog-24-hd-user-interface))
- Treatments seen in captures (question 1): pixel logo plus pixel menu font on pixel art in Blasphemous, Chained Echoes, Stardew and The Messenger, and apparently Sea of Stars (pixel stepped logo, pixel copyright font). High resolution UI over pixel art in Dead Cells (glowing logo, smooth serif prompt) and Octopath (serif logo and prompt over HD 2D).
- Mark Ferrari's colour cycling scenes are 640x480 with 256 colours. ([EffectGames](http://www.effectgames.com/effect/article-Old_School_Color_Cycling_with_HTML5.html))
- Darkest Dungeon and Hollow Knight are hand drawn and Kingdom Rush is cartoon art, so pixel scale does not apply to them. (captures in question 1; [Wikipedia, Kingdom Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_Rush))

### Inferences
- 640x360 at x3 is the best evidenced base among these references. By arithmetic, 480x270 (x4 to 1080p) and 320x180 (x6) also divide 1920x1080 evenly.
- For Kindred Siege, the title logo, menu font and title scene would match the battle art only if drawn at the same base (or a deliberate exact multiple); a high resolution painting reduced to look pixelated produces mixels next to native sprites.

### Gaps
- Base resolutions for Hyper Light Drifter, Sea of Stars, Eastward, Chained Echoes, The Messenger, Dead Cells, Children of Morta and Stardew were not found in reachable sources (PCGamingWiki returned 403).
- Pixel grid measurement was only possible on lossless captures; the Stardew capture is resampled (thousands of colours in the logo area), and YouTube frames are compressed.
