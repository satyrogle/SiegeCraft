# Boss fight depth in games where the player watches and gives orders

Research for Kindred Siege chief fights, 20 Oct 2026.

Tags: **[opened]** means I fetched the page and read it. **[summary]** means I only saw the search engine's summary of that page, because the site blocked fetching (Fandom, Liquipedia, Mobalytics, Prima Games) or there was no budget left to open it. Treat [summary] items as unverified. Player reports are marked as player reports.

## 1. TFT, Dota Underlords and Auto Chess: what do PvE rounds and objective monsters test, and how?

### Takeaway
In the big auto battlers, PvE rounds are mostly loot delivery plus a light board check: can you kill this in time, and is your layout right for this special targeting rule. Designers kept making them flatter and more guaranteed, and Underlords removed creep rounds from its main modes in December 2019. TFT's only real boss content is a side mode, Tocker's Trials (30 rounds, 6 bosses, 3 lives, no timer).

### Cited Findings
**TFT**
- Launch era schedule: round 1 two melee minions; round 2 two melee and one ranged; round 3 two melee and two ranged; round 9 three Krugs; round 15 one Greater Murk Wolf and four Murk Wolves; round 21 one Crimson Raptor and four Raptors; round 27 one Dragon; round 33 one Rift Herald; round 39 one Elder Dragon. [opened] ([Esports Tales, July 2019](https://www.esportstales.com/teamfight-tactics/item-and-gold-drop-rate-by-pve-round))
- Krugs, Wolves and Raptors always drop one or more items or 5 gold. Dragon, Rift Herald and Elder Dragon always drop items. Riot uses an item cap per round, so finding many items early lowers later drop chances and finding none raises them. [opened] ([Esports Tales](https://www.esportstales.com/teamfight-tactics/item-and-gold-drop-rate-by-pve-round))
- Riot Mort, Principal Game Designer, on why loot varies: "We want there to be lots of different strategies and experiences so that every game doesn't feel the same." [opened] ([Esports Tales quoting Mort](https://www.esportstales.com/teamfight-tactics/item-and-gold-drop-rate-by-pve-round))
- Patch 10.3 made Krugs drop 2 items guaranteed instead of 1 minimum. [summary] ([LoL wiki, V10.3 TFT](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/V10.3_%28Teamfight_Tactics%29))
- Patch 14.6 (2025): "Stage 2, 3, and 4 PVE rounds are now generally more consistent, and will always drop some items and some gold." This followed a 14.4 loot change made "to combat inflation". [summary] ([TFT patch 14.6 notes](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/teamfight-tactics-patch-14-6-notes-2025/))
- Later sets moved the late PvE rounds. One guide lists Mech Cannon and Mech Melee at round 33, Rift Herald at 39 and Elder Dragon at 45. [summary] ([Magic Game World list](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=35778), [Magic Game World guide](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=35823))
- Set 12 "Crab Rave": a third party guide says the Monster Crab appears at stage `5-7` with 20,000 HP, 200 armor and 200 magic resist, that its damage cannot be avoided, and that the fight is "a race against time". The search summary also says Riot's Tocker's Trials post jokes about design talks over whether Crab Rave should be as terrifying as it is. [summary] ([Tacter guide](https://www.tacter.com/tft/guides/how-to-beat-crab-rave-in-set-12-510e990e))
- Tocker's Trials launched in patch 14.17 (27 Aug 2024) and rotated out in 14.19 (24 Sep 2024). "30 Rounds, 6 Bosses, 3 Lives." Solo, no timers, the player chooses when to start each round. It uses the current set's champions and augments, without Charms. Chaos Mode unlocks after a normal clear. Riot promises "boards you'd never encounter anywhere else in TFT" and calls the mode highly experimental. [opened] ([Riot announcement](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/new-tft-workshop-mode-tockers-trials))
- Tocker's Trials score counts surviving champions, unused items, combat completion time and leveling up champions. Riot's support page says the mode is less balanced than PvP and invites players to find exploits. [opened] ([Riot support](https://support.riotgames.com/en-us/tft/events/teamfight-tactics-tocker-s-trials))
- The mode came back in later sets: guides and stat pages exist for Set 12, Set 14 and patches 16.3 and 17.5. [summary] ([Tacter Set 12](https://tacter.com/tft/guides/guide-tft-set-12-with-tockers-trails-normal-mode-6167444a), [U.GG Set 14](https://u.gg/tft/tockers-trials/comps), [METAsrc 16.3](https://www.metasrc.com/tft/tocker/16.3/stats/champions))

**Dota Underlords**
- Round 15 creeps "act like Assassins, jumping beyond your frontlines", so guides tell players to flip the board: ranged units in front, tanky melee behind. [summary] ([Pro Game Guides positioning guide](https://progameguides.com/underlords/dota-underlords-positioning-guide/))
- 18 Dec 2019 patch: creep rounds removed from Standard and Duos. Items now come every 5 rounds, and "Item quality does not change if you win or lose the round." [summary] ([Liquipedia patch page](https://www.liquipedia.net/underlords/2019-12-18_Patch))
- 25 Feb 2020 patch: Standard item rounds every 4 rounds instead of 5, and each item round's choices can be rerolled once. [summary] ([Underlords wiki patch page](https://dotaunderlords.fandom.com/wiki/February_25,_2020_Patch))

**Auto Chess (Drodo)**
- Dev log, 28 Dec 2020: rounds 10, 15, 20, 25, 30 and 35 each give one guaranteed common item from the "main creep". Round 40 (Glacier Clan) has two creeps, one drops a common item and one a rare. Rounds 45 and 50 give one guaranteed rare. Random drop rates were removed so every player gets an equal number of items. [opened] ([Auto Chess dev log](https://ac.dragonest.com/en/news/detail/ea294a4af9a))
- The same log gives fight tips that show what each creep round checks: Wolf Pack at round 15 (swap Tank and DPS positions), Lethal Ursa (the red Ursa is the main creep because it attacks faster), Wildwing Ripper at round 25 (bring physical ranged attackers against Swamp Wildwing), Black Dragon King at round 35, Nian Beast at round 45. [opened] ([Auto Chess dev log](https://ac.dragonest.com/en/news/detail/ea294a4af9a))

### Inferences
- These rounds test three things, all answered before the fight starts: raw damage against a clock (Krugs, Monster Crab), layout against a special targeting rule (Underlords round 15 jumpers, Auto Chess wolves), and finding the one creep that holds the loot (Auto Chess "main creep"). None has a mid fight tell the player answers.
- Every change found pushed PvE toward guaranteed, flat loot and away from punishing losses. Designers treated PvE as economy plumbing, not as a climax. So mainstream auto battler PvE is a weak model for deep chief fights. Tocker's Trials is the closer model.
- Tocker's Trials shows what Riot does when it does want PvE depth: strange enemy boards, lives instead of HP, no timer so the player can study the board, and a score for efficiency (time, survivors, unused items) so a win has degrees.

### Gaps
- Could not find the names, round numbers or mechanics of Tocker's Trials' six bosses. Riot's pages point to the game client.
- No Riot statement found on what Krugs, Wolves or Raptors are each meant to test.
- Could not confirm whether losing a TFT PvE round costs player HP. One search summary said no, but I do not trust it.
- Underlords creep line ups for rounds 20 to 50, before the 2019 removal, were not found.

## 2. Loop Hero: bosses summoned by the player's own building (Lich, Priestess, Hunter, Omega)

### Takeaway
Loop Hero makes the boss's arrival the player's own doing. Every card placed fills the boss meter, so getting stronger and calling the boss are the same act. The boss then punishes weak preparation at its arrival point (empty tiles next to camp become palace tiles that buff it), and the player can always walk away with part of the loot instead.

### Cited Findings
- The boss meter is a bar at the top left. It fills as you place cards. When it is full, the chapter boss appears on your campfire tile. More kills give more cards, so the meter fills faster. [summary] ([Magic Game World](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=125891), [Happy Mag](https://happymag.tv/loop-hero-how-to-beat-chapter-1/))
- When the Lich spawns, every empty tile next to the campfire becomes a Lich's palace tile (TrueAchievements calls them Temple tiles). One guide says each palace adds 5% to the Lich's health and damage. Another says the Lich is 35% stronger if you leave those tiles empty. [summary] ([Magic Game World](https://www.magicgameworld.com/?p=125891), [TrueAchievements](https://i2.trueachievements.com/a388525/punching-bag-achievement), [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/loop-hero-beat-the-lich-guide), [GamerJournalist](https://gamerjournalist.com/how-to-beat-the-lich-in-loop-hero/))
- Counters: fill the tiles around the campfire before the meter fills, or use the Oblivion card the game hands you at the first boss to delete palaces. [summary] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/3112522283879913233), [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/loop-hero-beat-the-lich-guide))
- Execute tiles: Blood Grove instantly kills enemies at 15% HP or less, Hungry Grove at 20% or less. With the campfire inside a Hungry Grove's range, a guide says you "only need to reduce the bosses HP by 80%". [opened] ([GamePretty Omega guide](https://gamepretty.com/loop-hero-how-to-beat-omega-as-rogue-with-hardly-any-camp-items/))
- Priestess (chapter 2): five stained glass windows, each with a "20% chance of blocking an attack". She summons them back "pretty regularly". TheGamer says she can only be reliably beaten with the Necromancer, because skeletons break windows fast. [opened] ([TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/loop-hero-tips-defeat-chapter-two-boss-the-priestess/)) Search summaries add that her attacks summon either two Angels or the 5 windows. [summary] ([PC Gamer boss guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/loop-hero-boss-secret-first-rewards/))
- Hunter (chapter 3): comes with hounds. Hit a hound 3 times and it vanishes, forcing a target swap, then comes back later hitting harder. Every 2nd Hunter attack fills his dogs' attack bars by 50%. If one of his allies dies, the Hunter and the remaining dogs heal and deal more damage. [summary] ([PC Gamer boss guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/loop-hero-boss-secret-first-rewards/))
- Omega (chapter 4): the meter has a marker for each earlier boss (Lich, then Priestess, then a third). A guide shows you can skip the Priestess: spawn her just after passing the campfire, then dump stockpiled cards so the meter reaches the third marker before you get back to camp, and fight the Hunter instead. Omega "becomes stronger every loop", so reach him early. The Smoke Screen trait lets you dodge his heavy attacks "for a few seconds". [opened] ([GamePretty Omega guide](https://gamepretty.com/loop-hero-how-to-beat-omega-as-rogue-with-hardly-any-camp-items/))
- Omega deletes all your cards and unequipped inventory items. [summary] ([PC Gamer boss guide](https://www.pcgamer.com/loop-hero-boss-secret-first-rewards/))
- Walking away: retreat on the campfire tile keeps 100% of materials, retreat anywhere else keeps 60%, dying keeps 30%. [summary] ([BlueStacks tips](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/loop-hero/lh-tips-tricks-en.html), [Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/misc/loop-hero-death-what-happens-when-you-die/))

### Inferences
- The lever is that the summon is tied to the player's own progress. The player picks the boss's timing by picking their pace, and the meter makes "am I ready?" a visible decision.
- The palace ring is a preparation test with no mid fight input: the fight's difficulty depends on what the player built at the arrival point earlier. The Oblivion card is a one shot fix if they did not.
- Chapter 4's markers let a skilled player choose which boss they face by timing card dumps against their position on the loop. That is skill from timing a few actions, the same shape Kindred Siege wants.
- The 100/60/30 retreat ladder puts a clear price on walking away, so fighting the boss is a gamble the player opts into.
- Execute groves mean the player prepares a finisher in advance. Kindred Siege's yield line at a quarter health is the same kind of line in the boss's HP.

### Gaps
- No Four Quarters statement found on why the boss meter is tied to card placement. The interviews found only cover the game's origin.
- How many cards fill each boss meter was not found.
- The Lich's own abilities in the fight, and any second phase, were not in the pages I could read.

## 3. The Bazaar, Backpack Battles, Mechabellum, Despot's Game and similar: boss or elite encounters and what they demand of a build

### Takeaway
Newer auto battlers do bosses as readable boards: a fixed build you can inspect before choosing to fight, often with a trigger that changes the fight partway (The Bazaar's Lord Arken fires everything at half health). The Bazaar is the strongest example. Mechabellum keeps bosses in side modes. I found no PvE bosses in Backpack Battles and almost nothing documented about Despot's Game bosses.

### Cited Findings
**The Bazaar**
- "The third Encounter in a Day will always be a PvE Monster fight." The player picks one of three monsters: one always Bronze, one always Silver, one Gold or higher. Hovering shows the rewards. Rewards are always gold, XP and one of the monster's own items or skills. [opened] ([Bazaar wiki, Monsters](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters))
- Legendary monsters carry a Legendary item or skill that cannot be upgraded and can only be got by beating them. The wiki calls it "much stronger than a skill of a tier below". [opened] ([Bazaar wiki, Monsters](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters))
- Day 10+ pool: Boss Harrow, Hulking Experiment, Master Alchemist, Lord of the Wastes, Trash Titan, Volkas Enforcer, Property Baron, Frost Street Champion, Void Colossus, Awakened District, Void Golem, Lord Arken, Veteran Octopus. Day 8 and Day 9 have their own pools. [opened] ([Bazaar wiki, Monsters](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters))
- Lord Arken: level 15 Legendary, 4700 HP, drops 6 gold and 3 XP. Board: Gatling Gun (25 damage, Multicast 2, cooldown 6/5/4 s, first use each fight at half cooldown); The Eclipse (large vehicle, 15 s cooldown, "uses all your other items", then each item use deals 100 damage, and it triggers the first time its owner falls below half health each fight); Thrusters (the item to its left flies, a vehicle there gets 5/10/15% shorter cooldown); Vitality Potion (heals 50/100% of max HP, 1 ammo). Skills: Flurry of Blows (the first 4/8 weapon uses each fight charge an item by 1 s), Strength (weapons +10/15/20/25 damage), Unwavering (each item use gives 20/40 shield). [opened] ([Bazaar wiki, Lord Arken](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Lord_Arken)) Note: the fetch tool paraphrased the Eclipse trigger as "the player falls below half health". Bazaar item text is written from the owner's side, so I read it as Arken's own half health. Check in game.
- Mobalytics (Aug 2026): Legendary encounters give a base 6 gold and 4 XP, and gold now scales with the encounter's tier. This conflicts slightly with the wiki's 3 XP for Lord Arken. [summary] ([Mobalytics PvE guide](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/pve-encounters-and-drops))
- Every fight has a sandstorm that normally starts about 30 seconds in. The hero Mak's Bottled Tornado starts it at 7 seconds. [summary] ([PCGamesN on Mak](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-bazaar/mak-new-hero-season-1))

**Backpack Battles**
- Store listings describe only fights against other players' saved builds (Unranked, Ranked, Custom rules, Lobbies). I found no PvE bosses. [summary] ([game listing](https://www.co-op.gg/pc/game/backpack-battles), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/qa/app/backpack-battles/id6572290447))

**Mechabellum**
- Each round you unlock one new unit, deploy up to two more, place them, and buy upgrades, all from one resource. The fight then runs itself for 60 seconds. Surviving units damage the opponent's HP. The two towers come back each round. (2023 article, so details may have changed since.) [opened] ([GamingOnLinux, May 2023](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2023/05/mechabellum-is-an-auto-battling-supreme-commander-im-completely-hooked/))
- Survival mode gives "a different enemy set every round", which the author says teaches counters. There are solo and duo survival modes. [opened] ([GamingOnLinux author comment](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2023/05/mechabellum-is-an-auto-battling-supreme-commander-im-completely-hooked/)); [summary] ([Godless Internets review, Sep 2024](https://godless-internets.org/2024/09/30/mechabellum))
- A May 2024 press release says a 4 player free for all mode added "new epic boss units". The Season 2 update added a Titan class unit, Death Knell, in Survival and Brawl, as the top form of the Melting Point. [summary] ([Game Developer press release](https://gamedeveloper.com/press-release/mechabellum-unveils-new-game-mode-and-key-art), [PCGamesN Season 2](https://www.pcgamesn.com/mechabellum/season-2-update))

**Despot's Game**
- Fights are automatic after placement, and once a fight starts you cannot step in. Bosses are the climax, and beating the final boss unlocks the King of the Hill multiplayer mode. One player found the final boss easy and disappointing. A Steam review says the AI sometimes ignores the Tank, "especially in boss Fights". A paid Challenges add on has 20 fixed missions with no randomness. [summary, which claim comes from which page not checked] ([Pure Nintendo review](https://purenintendo.com/review-despots-game-nintendo-switch/), [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1227280/discussions/0/3109140779861329583), [Indie Ranger review](https://indieranger.com/review-despots-game), [Nintendo eShop, Challenges](https://www.nintendo.com/store/products/despots-game-challenges-70050000036579-switch))

### Inferences
- The Bazaar shows a boss can be fully open book and still demand a plan, if it is a build with a trigger. Arken's Eclipse at half health is a phase two the player can plan around before the fight: burst him past 50% while protected, or bring shields and healing for the burst. Kindred Siege already has a half health phase. The lesson is to show its content (who joins, what gets recast) before the fight, so road choices can answer it.
- Picking one of three monsters by tier, with the unique reward only from the hardest, is the player choosing their own risk. Kindred Siege's capture reward fits this shape.
- The sandstorm is a shared, visible clock that ends stalls. Nerve bars could do the same job if both sides start losing nerve after a set time.
- Mechabellum's survival mode suggests that a different enemy mix every round teaches counters by exposure, which is cheap to build in a deterministic sim.

### Gaps
- No Bazaar designer (Tempo, Reynad) interview on monster design found.
- No Despot's Game boss names or mechanics found.
- No stats for Mechabellum boss units found.
- Backpack Battles may have added PvE after the sources I saw.

## 4. Battle Brothers: legendary encounters and unique enemies (Kraken, Lindwurm, Ijirok, legendary locations)

### Takeaway
Each Battle Brothers boss breaks one rule that normal fights rely on, so the player has to change gear, formation or turn order before the fight, not just bring more strength. The developers also plant hints about a boss before the fight.

### Cited Findings
- Lindwurm (Dev Blog #98, 2017): the only enemy taking two tiles, an upper body and a tail. They share HP but move and attack separately. The tail can hit several men at once, stagger them or knock them across the field. When it takes HP damage from a melee attack, the attacker may be sprayed with acid that eats armor for several turns. Polearms and ranged attacks avoid the spray. Players can bottle the acid and throw it at heavily armored enemies. Rewards include a Lindwurm helmet and shield that ignore the acid. [opened] ([Dev Blog #98](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4prf))
- Players: attacking only with two tile weapons avoids acid. The Lindwurm Scale Cloak gives 40 durability, costs 2 max fatigue, and makes body armor immune to the acid. [summary] ([Gameplay Tips crafting list](https://gameplay.tips/guides/3470-battle-brothers.html))
- Kraken (Dev Blog #109): one of three boss type enemies, each guarding a legendary location. Most of it stays under the swamp. Tentacles reach across the map, sink into the mud and come up elsewhere. At the start it wraps tentacles round brothers and drags them toward its maw. A grabbed brother can attack the tentacle to break free, and nearby brothers can help. A brother who ends a turn next to the maw is lost, shredded "in a single turn". Destroying several tentacles or hurting the head enrages it: tentacles then snap at everyone and crush whoever they hold, who lose HP at the end of each turn. Designer line: "an opponent to test your veteran party against, and quite possibly the end of many an ironman run." [opened] ([Dev Blog #109](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4hhp))
- Players: a grab sends the target to the end of the turn order, and the pull happens on the grabbed man's turn, so turn order decides who gets saved. Tips: tight formation, maces to stun tentacles, whips, and a goblin trophy accessory that makes the party immune to grabs. [summary, player reports] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/365360/discussions/0/1742229167220118993))
- Ijirok (Dev Blog #122, Warriors of the North): one of two bosses in a legendary quest across two legendary locations, ending in a legendary reward. It charges from range to gore and trample, and unlike other charges this one deals damage. Melee defense can block it but the impact still knocks back and staggers. At the start of each turn it shifts into an icy otherworld and comes back out somewhere else to charge again. Cold spreads and the ground freezes for good. Units near it get "Chilled": lower initiative and action points for one round. You get roughly one round to hit it before it charges from a new angle. Events and locations hint at its nature before the fight. [opened] ([Dev Blog #122](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4cd6))
- Players: daze it with a staff sling, or use hammers to daze it and push it last in turn order. [summary, player reports] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/365360/discussions/0/1642038749325554279))
- Black Monolith: one player reports facing about 15 Necrosavants, 20 Honor Guards, 1 Legionnaire and 4 priests. Another says high damage builds with one or two very good tanks do best. Legendary locations only show up when you get close, and where they spawn depends on your origin. [summary, player reports, thread attribution not checked] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/365360/discussions/0/3173324051170418550))

### Inferences
- Each boss tests a different preparation axis. Lindwurm tests weapon choice (reach and ranged). Kraken tests formation and rescue. Ijirok tests initiative and formation against something that relocates. The Monolith tests sustained damage and tank count. None is "same fight, more HP".
- The Kraken's enrage comes from the player's own actions (killing tentacles or hurting the head), not from an HP percentage. The player chooses when phase two starts.
- The Kraken's grab and rescue creates a mid fight team decision: spend actions freeing a man, or race the boss.
- The Ijirok's hints show the road itself can teach the boss before the fight.
- Caution: Battle Brothers is turn based and the player moves every unit, so its lessons carry over as preparation and rule breaking, not as order timing.

### Gaps
- No HP or damage numbers from developer posts. The Fandom wiki would have them, but it blocked fetching.
- The full list of legendary locations (Sunken Library, Witch Hut and so on) was not confirmed.

## 5. Kingdom Rush, Total War, Warcraft 3 and Dota 2's Roshan, League's Baron, Dragons and Elder Dragon: how does a boss create team decisions about timing, contesting and risk?

### Takeaway
In MOBAs the boss is a clock plus a prize. It has a known or semi known spawn time, a reward big enough to swing the game, and a fight the other side can steal with one well timed button. In tower defence and Total War the boss either attacks the player's few controls (Vez'nan disables towers) or caps a chain of objectives, so the player's limited active tools get spent at the right moment.

### Cited Findings
**League of Legends: Baron Nashor** (all [opened], [LoL wiki, Baron Nashor](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Baron_Nashor), patches up to V26.13)
- Spawns at 20:00 (V26.01 cut it back from 25:00, which V25.S1.1 had set). Respawn 6:00. His spawn animation starts 20 s before he spawns (V14.1).
- Hand of Baron: all living teammates get 180 s of bonus AD and AP, Empowered Recall, and an aura that greatly boosts nearby allied minions.
- Baron's Gaze: he takes 50% less damage for 8 s from the unit he most recently hit with a basic attack, one unit at a time.
- Void Corruption: up to 100 stacks lasting 8 s, each stack cuts 0.5 armor and magic resist, 50 at most.
- Every 6th basic attack is an ability. The first is random, then a fixed cycle: Acid Pool (2 s windup, 3 pools in a cone, 60% slow for 2.5 s), Acid Shot (2 s windup, a line), Tentacle Knockup (erupts after 1.25 s, knocks up for 1.25 s), rear area attack (0.5 s stun).
- Forms since V14.1, picked at random each game: Hunting (lightning on every champion for 20% of current HP, halved for each extra hit), Territorial (pulls a cone toward him, and a small wall appears in front of the pit), All Seeing (void rifts at the two furthest visible champions within 2200 units, 1000 magic damage over 3 s, and a long side tunnel appears).
- Health 17,792 to 19,190 depending on level.

**League: Elder Dragon, Atakhan, Rift Herald, Smite**
- Elder spawns each time a team kills its 4th drake. Respawn 6:00. Its buff, Aspect of the Dragon, lasts 150 s for all living teammates. Elder's own basic attacks deal bonus damage equal to 5% of the target's current health. [opened] ([LoL wiki, Elder Dragon](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Elder_Dragon))
- The Elder buff executes enemies below 20% HP after hitting them. [summary] ([GameBoost definition](https://gameboost.com/bg/definitions/league-of-legends/elder-dragon)) EarlyGame (23 Jul 2026) says the execute lands "after a short delay", that Elder was meant to give a losing team a real chance if it wins the most important late game fight, that the worst feeling is a team with four dragons and Soul losing Elder to a steal, and that "Elder Dragon is not designed to feel fair". [opened] ([EarlyGame](https://earlygame.com/news/lol/league-of-legends-is-elder-dragon-actually-too-strong))
- Atakhan (2025): spawns on the top or bottom side "depending on which side sees the most PvP action", and Blood Roses appear near kills and around his pit. Riot's 15 Apr 2025 post merged his two forms into one (Thornbound) because it was "challenging to balance Voracious Atakhan's reward in a way that feels satisfying and fair" and to "pull back on some of the complexity". [opened] ([Riot dev post, Season 2 preview](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-season-2-gameplay-changes-preview/))
- Season 1 form logic: high kill games got Ruinous Atakhan, quieter games got Voracious. The form was decided by the 14 minute mark, and Atakhan spawned at 20:00 once per game. [summary] ([Tacter Atakhan guide](https://www.tacter.com/lol/guides/atakhan-new-monster-guide-in-lol-6a3dcb94))
- Same Riot post: neutral objectives had become too demanding in the early and mid game, and "the frequency and degree at which you need to think about epic monsters is too high for healthy game pacing". The second Void Grub spawn was removed. Rift Herald moved to 15 minutes and lost her damage reduction debuff to make fights "simpler, faster", "but at a higher HP cost to keep Herald contestable". [opened] ([Riot dev post, Season 2 preview](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-season-2-gameplay-changes-preview/))
- Smite: 600 true damage to large monsters, 15 s cooldown, last changed in patch 26.12. The Baron buff goes to the team that lands the killing blow no matter how much damage they did, which is why a timed Smite can steal it. [summary] ([Rift Patch Notes, Smite](https://www.riftpatchnotes.com/lol/spell/smite), [Esports.net Baron explainer](https://www.esports.net/news/lol/baron-nashor-explained/))

**Dota 2: Roshan and Tormentors**

### Inferences
- The MOBA pattern has four parts: a known or semi known clock, a prize big enough to force a fight, a contest (the real fight is often against the other team at the boss), and a last hit steal that makes one button's timing decide everything.
- Baron shows how to make a boss learnable while watching: a big move every 6th attack, a 1.25 to 2 s windup, a random first move and then a fixed cycle. Baron's Gaze punishes letting one unit soak every hit, so who tanks matters.
- The Tormentor is a pure focus test. A regenerating shield means slow damage never wins, and splitting the reflect among attackers rewards committing everyone at once. That is close to what Focus Fire and Unleash should mean against a chief.
- Kingdom Rush's tap to dispel is a short "answer early or lose it" window, the opposite of a late parry. A chief that uses both kinds of window makes timing a skill, not just picking the right button.
- Riot's 2025 pullback (fewer grub spawns, one Atakhan form, simpler Herald) is direct evidence that too many objective variants and too much to track hurt the game.
- Total War survival battles chain small objectives into a boss and let the player spend a resource mid battle on reinforcements and defences, which is close to Kindred Siege's order points.

## 6. Readability: how do these games show a boss's next big move to someone watching rather than controlling, and what worked or failed?

### Takeaway
What works: a cue that names the move and shows its timing, shown where the player already looks, with enough time for one answer (intent icons with numbers, a 1.25 to 2 s windup, a spawn animation that starts 20 s early, the boss's location on the map). What failed or got pulled back: previews the player had to dig for, intents without numbers, too many objective variants, and orders that units might ignore.

### Cited Findings
- Slay the Spire: enemies at first showed nothing about their next move, which clashed with permadeath. A "Next Turn" system, where you selected a target to preview its action, worked poorly with several enemies. It was replaced by Intent icons. Early intents had no attack numbers, and playtesters found showing numbers more engaging and better for planning. [opened] ([Wikipedia, Slay the Spire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Spire))
- Baron: 2 s windups on Acid Pool and Acid Shot, 1.25 s on Tentacle Knockup, big moves on every 6th attack in a fixed cycle after a random first. Spawn animation 20 s before spawn. [opened] ([LoL wiki, Baron Nashor](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Baron_Nashor))
- Atakhan spawns on the side with more PvP action and Blood Roses appear near kills, so the map itself shows where the next fight will be. [opened] ([Riot dev post](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-season-2-gameplay-changes-preview/))
- Dota 7.33 shows Roshan's current pit on the minimap. [summary] ([Prima Games 7.33 notes](https://primagames.com/tips/dota-2-new-frontiers-update-7-33-full-patch-notes-listed)) His respawn is a deliberate 3 minute window, so teams have to check rather than read a timer. [summary] ([Esports.net timers](https://www.esports.net/news/dota/dota-2-timers/))
- Kingdom Rush: Vez'nan's tower spell has a short window where tapping dispels it, then it locks for about 10 s. [summary] ([Kingdom Rush wiki, Vez'nan](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Vez%27nan))
- FTL (pausable real time, the player orders crew and systems, nothing is steered directly): players say level 3 sensors let you see when enemy weapons are charged, so you can time a hack or cloak, and that hacking the weapons also shows their charge. [summary, player reports] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/627456486726992553))
- FTL final boss (Rebel Flagship), player reports: three phases; phase 2 brings drone swarms and boarders; phase 3 adds a Zoltan super shield and a power surge laser barrage. [summary, player reports] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/458605578275718197)) A walkthrough says phase 2 is about drones, so disabling drone control is the priority. [summary] ([PortForward walkthrough](https://portforward.com/games/walkthroughs/Faster-Than-Light/The-Last-Stand.htm))
- FTL surge timing, player reports: surges come at a random 20 to 30 s interval and last about 6 to 7 s, and the next timer starts when the previous surge begins, so a long cloak on one surge can leave you open for the next. [summary] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/552359350148077508)) Another player says a level 1 cloak takes about 5 s and level 3 about 15 s, on 25 s and 35 s cycles, and surge timing does not change with cloak length. [summary] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/882960797653400436/))
- FTL Flagship, player reports: its systems are restored when the next phase starts but it cannot repair mid fight, and killing its crew in phases 1 and 2 removes the boarders in phase 3. [summary, thread attribution not checked] ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/558752449686705435))
- Tocker's Trials removed round timers, so the player starts each round when ready. [opened] ([Riot announcement](https://teamfighttactics.leagueoflegends.com/en-gb/news/game-updates/new-tft-workshop-mode-tockers-trials))
- The Bazaar shows a monster's rewards on hover before you pick it. [opened] ([Bazaar wiki, Monsters](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Monsters)) Its sandstorm at about 30 s is a clock everyone knows. [summary] ([PCGamesN on Mak](https://www.pcgamesn.com/the-bazaar/mak-new-hero-season-1))
- Majesty (indirect control RTS): the player never commands heroes. They post bounty flags: attack flags on enemies and explore flags in the original, plus Protection and Fear flags in Majesty 2 (a small Fear bounty scares low level heroes, a big one scares high level heroes). Heroes follow a flag only if the bounty suits them. Reviewers called the indirect control frustrating at times, and the game offers spells to make up for it. [summary] ([Giant Bomb, Majesty 2](https://www.giantbomb.com/games/3030-21454/), [GameBanshee review](https://gamebanshee.com/a84))

### Inferences
- Kindred Siege's 3 s tell is longer than Baron's 1.25 to 2 s windups, which seems fair for a watching player picking one of four orders (LoL players steer a champion directly, so the comparison is loose). Slay the Spire's lesson says the tell should show which order answers it and how much damage is coming, not just "something big".
- A fixed cycle after a random start lets a watcher learn the rhythm within one fight.
- Showing the clock (Bazaar sandstorm, Baron spawn animation) lets players plan when to spend points.
- FTL's sensors are an upgrade that buys information about enemy timing. That could be a road reward (a scout reveals the chief's cycle), but core tells should never be hidden behind it.
- Majesty is the warning case: when the player's input is a request the units may ignore, players get frustrated. Kindred Siege's orders should always be obeyed, and visibly so.

- No GDC talk on telegraphs for auto battlers found in this pass.
- No source on how The Bazaar shows item cooldowns during a fight.

## 7. What fits an order driven auto battler like Kindred Siege?

### Takeaway
The ideas that transfer best: each chief breaks one of the player's normal rules, some phases fire because of the player's own choices, big moves come in a learnable cycle with clear answer windows, a focus test that only committed orders beat, and road preparation that the chief fight then tests. Mainstream auto battler PvE (TFT, Underlords, Auto Chess) is the wrong model.

### Cited Findings
These point back to the sources above.
- Boss as rule breaker: Lindwurm acid punishes melee, the Kraken drags men to its maw, the Ijirok relocates every turn. (Section 4: [Dev Blog #98](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4prf), [Dev Blog #109](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4hhp), [Dev Blog #122](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4cd6))
- Phase triggered by the player: the Kraken enrages when tentacles die or the head is hurt. (Section 4: [Dev Blog #109](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4hhp))
- Trigger visible before the fight: Lord Arken's Eclipse at half health. (Section 3: [Bazaar wiki](https://thebazaar.wiki.gg/wiki/Lord_Arken))
- Learnable cycle: Baron's every 6th attack, fixed after a random start, 1.25 to 2 s windups. (Section 5: [LoL wiki](https://wiki.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/Baron_Nashor))
- Focus test: Tormentor's regenerating shield and split reflect. (Section 5: [Esports.gg](https://esports.gg/news/dota-2/dota-tormentor-patch-7-33/), [summary])
- Early answer window: Kingdom Rush tap to dispel. (Section 5: [Kingdom Rush wiki](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Vez%27nan), [summary])
- Boss timing chosen by the player: Loop Hero's meter and chapter 4 markers. (Section 2: [GamePretty](https://gamepretty.com/loop-hero-how-to-beat-omega-as-rogue-with-hardly-any-camp-items/))
- Steal by last hit: Smite on Baron. (Section 5: [Esports.net](https://www.esports.net/news/lol/baron-nashor-explained/), [summary])
- Too much complexity hurts: Riot's 2025 pullback on Atakhan, grubs and Herald. (Section 5: [Riot dev post](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/dev-season-2-gameplay-changes-preview/))

### Inferences
These mappings are mine. None is tested.
1. **One broken rule per chief.** Each tribe chief breaks one class rule, and that tells the player which order matters. Examples: acid blood splashes melee attackers, so the Skirmisher pays and the player leans on Fall Back or Marksman Focus Fire (Lindwurm). A grab drags a fighter toward the chief, and only Focus Fire on the grabbing bodyguard frees him (Kraken). A chief that relocates every few seconds, so Snares and Aimed Shot must be timed to his landing (Ijirok). The existing "marks do not land while his band stands" is already one of these.
2. **Two kinds of timing window.** The Hold parry is a late window (last half second). Add chief spells with an early window, where the answer must come in the first second or the spell locks in (Vez'nan's dispel). Same four orders, different timing, so the skill is reading which window this tell has.
3. **Phases the player triggers.** Besides HP thresholds, let some phases fire when the player does something, such as killing the second Warden or breaking a mark on him. Then the player chooses when phase two starts, like the Kraken's enrage.
4. **Show phase two before the fight.** Like Lord Arken's board, show what the chief does at half health (which reserves join, what he recasts) on the road, so the player can prepare.
5. **A visible cycle.** Chief spells come on a fixed cycle after a random first spell, like Baron. A watcher learns it within one fight. With paid orders, the player plans: "save 2 points for the bite in 6 seconds".
6. **A focus test.** A chief, totem or bodyguard with a regenerating nerve shield that only breaks if the whole squad hits it at once (Focus Fire or Unleash). Spread damage does nothing, like the Tormentor.
7. **Capture as a last hit race.** Smite shows that the last hit on a big target is tense. Capture already depends on breaking nerve while he stands. Making the nerve break visible and the final push a timed Unleash turns "break him or kill him" into a live decision.
8. **Road preparation tested on arrival.** Like Loop Hero's palace ring, what the player did on the road changes the chief's arena or buffs (tribes spared earlier add reserves, a scouted camp reveals his cycle). The Ijirok shows that hints before the fight work.
9. **Player chosen timing.** Loop Hero lets the player decide when the boss comes. A road option to challenge the chief early (less prepared, chief has fewer reserves) or late (better prepared, chief stronger) gives a readiness choice, with a Loop Hero style price for walking away.
10. **Keep variants few.** Riot cut Atakhan to one form and removed grub spawns because there was too much to think about. One broken rule plus one chief spell per chief is probably the limit for a 2.5D screen with five fighters.
11. **Do not copy TFT PvE.** TFT, Underlords and Auto Chess turned PvE into flat loot checks, and Underlords removed creep rounds from its main modes. That is the opposite of what chief fights need.

### Gaps
- No source tested exactly four paid orders against a boss tell. Every mapping above is my inference.
- No source measured how long a watching player needs to read a tell and choose an order. Baron's 1.25 to 2 s windups are the only comparison found, and LoL players steer their champion directly.
