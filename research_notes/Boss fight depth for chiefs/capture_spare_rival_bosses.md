# Bosses as characters: capture, spare, recruit, and rival bosses who remember

*Label key: "search snippet" means the claim was read in search results and the page itself was not opened (blocked or not fetched). "Anecdotal" means a player or forum report. Everything under Inferences is my reasoning, not a source.*

## 1. Monster Hunter: capture versus slay

### Takeaway
Capture in Monster Hunter is a reading and resource test. The monster must be under a hidden HP line (21 to 28% of max HP in Rise, set by threat level), then held in a trap and hit with two tranquilisers, and some monsters escape or wreck traps depending on their state. You trade the carves for a capture reward table and a faster finish. The choice stays live only while each path has something the other lacks; in Wilds, where rewards converged, guides now say the difference barely matters.

### Cited Findings
- In Rise, capture is an alternative to slaying that unlocks Capture Rewards at quest end but costs you the carves. The monster must be weakened, held in a Shock Trap or Pitfall Trap, and hit with two Tranq Bombs or two shots of Tranq Ammo. Elder Dragons and Apex monsters cannot be captured. ([Monster Hunter Wiki, Capturing (MHRise)](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise)))
- Rise thresholds are the same at every rank and scale with threat: 28% of total HP for low threat monsters (Great Wroggi, Aknosom), 23% for mid threat (Rathian, Jyuratodus), 21% for high threat (Magnamalo). ([Monster Hunter Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise)))
- The capture line is reached before the blue eye icon appears. That icon means near death, after which the monster limps off to sleep and recover health. An Assist Palico can call out when the real capture line is reached. In Rise, monsters are always marked on the map. ([Monster Hunter Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise)))
- Monster specific trap counterplay in Rise: Nargacuga jumps out of Pitfall Traps unless enraged; Zinogre charges up off Shock Traps in its normal state and destroys them when charged; Diablos destroys traps when it surfaces under them; Rajang jumps out of Pitfall Traps in its normal state and destroys Shock Traps when charged. Paratoad or Snowbeetle can make an opening to place a trap. ([Monster Hunter Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise)))
- Capture's edge in Rise: better odds on certain items than carving, and an earlier end. The earliest capture is at just over a fifth of total HP, and the quest end timer drops to 20 seconds instead of 60. ([Monster Hunter Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise)))
- In World, the usual cue is a skull icon by the monster's portrait on the minimap. The low health line differs by monster but is most often between 10% and 30% of max HP. ([Mobalytics MHW capture guide, search snippet](https://mobalytics.gg/mhw/guides/capture-guide))
- A World guide says capturing gives more rewards, including more research points, but specific parts are often more likely from killing. It recommends capturing most of the time and checking drop rates in the Hunter's Notes when chasing a part. ([GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/guides/365207-monster-hunter-world-capture-vs-kill-rewards-get))
- For Wilds (2025), slaying gives three extra carves (more with a Carving Pro meal) and capturing can save a few minutes. The article says the gap is no longer stark, blames Capcom's accessibility changes for weakening the unique rewards of both, and concludes there is not much advantage either way. ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/monster-hunter-wilds-should-you-capture-or-slay-monsters))

### Inferences
- Monster Hunter stacks three skills on a capture: knowing when (a hidden line, read through limping, icons and the Palico), setting up (trap plus two tranqs before the monster wakes or breaks free), and reading the monster's state (rage beats some traps). Killing is the default. Capture is a deliberate detour with its own ways to fail.
- The trade off only holds because each path has exclusive value: carves on one side, the capture table and time on the other. Wilds shows what happens when the pools converge: writers reduce the choice to speed. A Kindred Siege capture that is simply better than killing will flatten the same way.
- Scaling the line by threat (28, 23, 21%) gives tougher monsters a narrower window. That is a cheap way to make capture harder on harder bosses.

### Gaps
- No official per monster thresholds found for World or Wilds. World numbers are guide or player estimates. A Steam claim that the World skull icon does not appear if the monster was enraged when it hit critical HP is unverified.
- Counts of capture reward slots versus carves per game were not found. Capture Master and similar skills were not verified.

## 2. Pokémon: what makes catching a skill

### Takeaway
Catching multiplies three levers the player controls (how low the HP is, which status is on, which ball fits this moment) against one risk (a knockout ends the attempt). At full HP a legendary is close to uncatchable; weakening it roughly triples the odds and sleep doubles them again, yet even then each ball is a single digit gamble. The skill is restraint plus preparation.

### Cited Findings
- Generation III and IV formula: `X = ((3M - 2H) * C * B / (3M)) * S`. M is max HP, H current HP, C the species catch rate, B the ball bonus (Poké Ball 1, Great Ball 1.5, Ultra Ball 2; Master Ball always succeeds), S the status bonus (asleep or frozen 2; poisoned, paralysed or burned 1.5; otherwise 1). X of 255 or more is an automatic catch; otherwise the game runs four shake checks, and success is roughly X/255. At full HP the HP term cuts X to C times B over 3; near 0 HP it approaches C times B times S, about three times the full HP value. ([The Cave of Dragonflies, Gen III/IV capture mechanics](https://www.dragonflycave.com/mechanics/gen-iii-iv-capturing))
- Situational balls in later generations: Quick Ball 5x on the first turn and 1x after; Timer Ball grows with turns passed up to 4x at 10 turns; Dusk Ball 3x in caves or at night (3.5x in some generations). ([Bulbapedia Timer Ball, search snippet](https://Bulbapedia.Bulbagarden.net/wiki/Timer_Ball); [Dittobase Dusk Ball, search snippet](https://www.dittobase.com/ultra-sun-ultra-moon/items/dusk-ball); [PokéBase, search snippet](https://pokemondb.net/pokebase/205306/what-is-the-difference-in-pokeball-catch-rates))
- False Swipe always leaves the target with at least 1 HP (Normal type, power 40, accuracy 100, PP 40). ([Pokémon Database](https://pokemondb.net/move/false-swipe))

### Inferences
- My own calculation from the Dragonfly Cave formula, for a catch rate 3 legendary and an Ultra Ball: full HP and no status about 0.83% per ball; full HP and asleep about 1.6%; 1 HP and paralysed about 2.8%; 1 HP and asleep about 4.4%. Weakening is worth about 3x, sleep 2x, the right ball another 1.5x to 5x, and they multiply.
- The HP term flattens near the bottom, so the last slivers of HP add little while the knockout risk keeps rising. The good player stops dealing damage early and switches to status and ball choice. That is a real decision made under risk.
- Context balls (first turn, long fights, night) make the best tool depend on the moment of the fight, not on a fixed best item.
- Each failed ball costs an item and gives the target another turn to hit you, so capture is attrition you have to survive.

### Gaps
- The Generation V and later formula (critical captures, revised status bonuses) was not verified because Bulbapedia blocked fetches.

## 3. Talking, sparing and recruiting: Persona and SMT, Fire Emblem, Undertale and Deltarune

### Takeaway
In these games the recruit or spare window is earned by one specific skill: knock every enemy down for a Persona 5 Hold Up, finish a target with a crippled capture attack in Fire Emblem, tire a boss with costly actions in Deltarune, endure and act correctly in Undertale. The payoff is a different kind of thing than damage pays (a party member, a different gear slot, a better ending). Hard gates (level caps, story bosses that cannot be taken) stop recruiting from ever being the universal answer.

### Cited Findings
**Persona 5 and Shin Megami Tensei V**
- In Persona 5, hitting a weakness knocks an enemy down and grants an extra turn. A Hold Up happens only if all enemies are knocked down. During it the party can launch an All Out Attack, demand money or items, or convince a Shadow to become one of Joker's Personas. ([Wikipedia, Persona 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_5))
- In Royal, each Shadow has one of four personalities shown above its level, and each likes one answer style and dislikes two: Gloomy likes Vague, Irritable likes Serious, Timid likes Kind, Upbeat likes Funny. Wrong answers anger the Shadow and the negotiation fails into a fight. Personas above your level, and bosses, cannot be obtained this way. ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/13327-persona-5-royal-demon-negotiation-answers-hold-up-guide))
- A Shadow above Joker's level, or one whose Persona you already own, usually leaves an item instead of joining. ([Gamer Guides, search snippet](https://gamerguides.com/persona-5/guide/story-walkthrough/april/delivering-justice))
- In SMT V, negotiation happens mid battle. You answer by the demon's personality, and it may ask for items, money, HP or MP. Only demons of lower level than the Nahobino can be recruited, but a higher level demon remembers a successful negotiation and joins once you outlevel it. A full moon blocks negotiation and a new moon makes it easier. ([Wikipedia, Shin Megami Tensei V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Megami_Tensei_V))
- Guides say a failed negotiation can let the demon attack at once and cost your turn, and items handed over in a failed deal are gone. ([RPG Site SMT V tips, search snippet](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/11952-shin-megami-tensei-v-tips-for-demon-negotiating); [Pro Game Guides, search snippet](https://progameguides.com/shin-megami-tensei/how-to-recruit-demons-in-shin-megami-tensei-v/))

**Fire Emblem**
- Thracia 776 Capture: the captor's constitution must exceed the target's. Units with constitution 20, and mounted units unless forced to dismount (for example by Sleep), cannot be captured. The captor needs a range 1 weapon. During the attempt the captor's stats are halved (the wiki is inconsistent on exactly which stats). The target must hit 0 HP during that attempt or it is not captured; an unarmed target is taken instantly. Carrying a captive halves the captor's Str, Mag, Skl, Spd and Def, and halves movement if the captive's constitution is over half the captor's. Captives can be released (gone for good) or traded with, which the wiki calls the main way to get weapons, items and money. Enemies capture your units too and carry them off the map to a prison chapter. ([Fire Emblem Wiki, Capture](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Capture))
- Fates: only Niles (Kidnap) and Orochi (Capture) can capture, with no constitution check. Captured generic units go to the castle jail and can be recruited; they lose their equipment and enemy only skills (such as Dragonskin) and cannot support or get endings. King Garon and Kotaro cannot be captured for story reasons, but named bosses Gazak and Haitaka can. ([Fire Emblem Wiki, Capture](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Capture))
- Talk recruiting needs a specific unit adjacent to the target, with hints given in dialogue. Examples: Marisa joins only if Gerik or Ewan talks to her (Sacred Stones); Clarine joins only if she talks to Roy during the Other Phase (Binding Blade). Talk can change a boss without recruiting him: when Nino talks to the boss Lloyd in Blazing Blade, he leaves his throne tile and attacks. Talk can backfire: in Path of Radiance, Jill defects to fight beside her father Shiharam unless she has an A support with Mist. ([Fire Emblem Wiki, Talk](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Talk))

**Undertale**
- Players can appease monsters with the right actions and spare them instead of killing. Some bosses can be finished peacefully only by surviving until their dialogue ends. Killing earns EXP (raising LOVE) and gold. The game can be finished without a single kill, and endings depend on kills. Toby Fox felt it was wrong that the only way forward was killing Toriel, which led to the spare option, and he never considered removing the option to fight. ([Wikipedia, Undertale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undertale))
- Toriel must be spared many times ([Wikipedia, Toriel, search snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toriel)). Unlike other fights, her name does not turn yellow to show she can be spared, so there is no cue. Players report anywhere from about 10 to about 40 presses; one analysis says about two dozen. ([Medium analysis, search snippet](https://lesmocon.medium.com/undertale-second-chances-in-morality-e0346fe37377); Steam discussions surfaced by search, anecdotal, which thread said which not checked, for example [this thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/391540/discussions/0/135509124600082604))
- Asgore destroys the MERCY button with his trident at the start of the fight, so you have to fight him. It comes back only once he is defeated, and then you choose to kill or spare him. Critics praised this moment. ([Wikipedia, Asgore, search snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgore); [Undertale Wiki, search snippet](https://undertale.fandom.com/wiki/Encounter))

**Deltarune**
- Most regular enemies become Tired at 50% HP or less (about 33% for most Chapter 1 enemies). Tired shows as a blue name and blue Zz, and from Chapter 3 a TIRED label. Tired enemies can be spared with Pacify, Sleep Mist or Scythemare. This generally does not apply to bosses, though some (Berdly in his coaster) tire at set HP lines. MERCY is a 0 to 100% bar and the enemy can be spared at 100%; pressing SPARE alone usually adds about 10%. ([Deltarune Wiki, Tired](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Tired))
- Jevil, the Chapter 1 secret boss, has 3500 HP with phase changes at 2800, 2100 and 1400 HP and then a final phase; at 525 HP or less he skips to a fake out before his last attack. Winning by violence gives the Devilsknife, a weapon. Winning by mercy means raising his Tiredness to 9 (Hypnosis adds 1 for 50% TP; Pirouette adds 0.5 for 20% TP) and then casting Pacify; he also tires on his own from turn 30 of the final phase (one turn sooner per Tiredness level). Mercy gives Jevilstail, an armour. His lines change each phase, flavour text warns before his biggest attack, and in the final phase his attack rises 0.5 per turn (to a cap of 11) while his defence drops. Fighting him gives only two thirds of the normal TP. ([Deltarune Wiki, Jevil](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Jevil))

### Inferences
- The Persona 5 Hold Up is the closest match to a chief kneeling. The window opens only after a skill feat (every enemy down), and then one menu sets damage (All Out Attack) against a recruit, money or an item. The opportunity cost is on screen at the moment of choice.
- Fire Emblem's Thracia capture has the strongest cost structure seen anywhere in this research: the attempt halves your unit's stats, it only works if that weakened attack finishes the target, and carrying the captive cripples the captor until he is dropped off. Capture is a tactical sacrifice, not a bonus.
- Jevil keeps both paths wanted: each gives a different equipment slot, mercy costs a scarce resource every turn, and the fight escalates so even mercy has a deadline.
- Undertale's two signature bosses teach opposite lessons. Toriel withholds the usual cue to test resolve. Asgore removes the spare option itself: a boss showing who he is by attacking the interface.
- Level gates (Persona, SMT V) and story locks (Fates, Persona bosses) make recruiting a sometimes answer. SMT V softens the failure by remembering the deal for later.

### Gaps
- No confirmed Fire Emblem boss who is recruited by Talk was found; the wiki's Talk page names none. Fates capture costs (gold, prison upgrades) were not covered.
- Exact Persona 5 rules for how many correct answers are needed, and SMT V level gap formulas, were not found.
- Papyrus and Undyne spare conditions were not verified.

## 4. Shadow of Mordor and Shadow of War: the Nemesis system

### Takeaway
Nemesis makes captains into characters by giving each one a generated name, rank, strengths, weaknesses and fears, storing every encounter as an event record that later drives dialogue, promotion and appearance, and letting defeated orcs return scarred unless beheaded. Kill, dominate and shame each change the future differently, and domination carries a betrayal risk. The designers anchored the system on memorable moments, above all the player's own death, and kept the cruellest taunts rare.

### Cited Findings
- Patent US10926179B2, *Nemesis characters, nemesis forts, social vendettas and followers in computer games*: assignee Warner Bros. Entertainment; inventors Michael de Plater, Christopher Hoge, Robert Roberts, Daniel Valerius, Rocky Newton, Kevin Stephens; priority 26 Mar 2015; filed 25 Mar 2016; published 23 Feb 2021. Google Patents shows its status as active with the date 11 Aug 2036 next to that status (most likely the expected expiry; the page read did not label it), and a 4th year fee paid in Aug 2024. ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en))
- The patent describes each nemesis with a random name and title, tribe, appearance, power level, rank, relationships, fighting style and traits (strengths, weaknesses, vulnerabilities, fears). Dialogue is chosen to show memory of, or reaction to, a past event; its example has a captain remark that he thought he had already killed you and will make sure this time. Soldiers can be promoted to captain after feats such as killing the player or a captain. Changes are queued as faction event records and applied at triggers such as the player's death or entering a new zone, and can change rank, appearance (battle scars, new armour), traits and power, or end in permanent death. ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en))
- Return from death depends on how the orc died. With its head intact it may come back with scars or a face covering; if beheaded it does not return (its head is shown on a spire). Event types include duels, executions, feasts and ambushes; winning raises an NPC's power, and losing weakens or kills it. A dominated bodyguard may betray its warchief to take his rank. ([Google Patents, description](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en))
- The independent claims (1, 19 and 36) are broad: detect a set interaction between the player's avatar and one opposing NPC, change another opposing NPC's parameters in response, and show an indication of the change. Hierarchies (claims 3 and 4), dialogue selection (claim 13) and changes to appearance, personality and power (claim 11) are in dependent claims. ([Google Patents, claims](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)) Not legal advice; this is a plain reading.
- A news report says the system stays patent protected until 2036 even after the studio closed. ([Mezha, search snippet](https://mezha.ua/en/news/shadow-of-mordor-s-nemesis-system-is-protected-by-patent-until-2036-even-after-the-studio-closes-300106/))
- Michael de Plater's stated inspirations: the tabletop habit of bringing a surviving foe back as a recurring villain; Madden's commentary that narrates a career; Batman villains and their physical and psychological scars. The team anchored the system on key memorable moments because scripting every story beat in an open world would be impossibly complex, with being killed by an enemy as the obvious anchor, and let players fill in the gaps. ([GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-draws-from-burnout-football-and-an-architecture-book/))
- De Plater's postmortem, as reported by Kotaku: it began as a simple idea of personal villains; pre production added warring Uruk factions with separate Morale and Discipline bars and a hierarchy screen that looked like a Christmas tree; those factions were cut and the team returned to the original focus; Domination and building your own followers came out of that exploration. Lessons: without clear benchmarks, effort went into side systems, and because the promise was not explained well the game felt more mechanical than intended. ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-couldve-been-way-more-1681120649))
- Lead systems designer Chris Hoge's GDC 2018 talk, *Helping Players Hate (or Love) Their Nemesis*, covered making orcs memorable, designing exceptional moments and keeping player and orc relationships going. ([GDC Vault abstract](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025150/Helping-Players-Hate-(or-Love))) A Spanish report says Hoge explained that taunts and jokes were the norm while the worst humiliations were kept for very rare events, such as an orc who kills you, takes your equipped sword, mocks it and breaks it. ([Vidaextra, search snippet, secondhand](https://www.vidaextra.com/accion/tierra-media-sombras-guerra-existe-orco-capaz-destruir-tu-mejor-espada-creadores-temian-que-cerrases-juego))
- Shadow of War: a captain is broken in combat (a green icon over his head), then can be killed or dominated. Weaknesses such as fire, poison or a fear of spiders break him faster. Dominate needs a story skill. A dominated captain can be recruited, sent away or left fighting. Dominated captains can betray you, challenging you to a fight to the death, after which you can kill him or dominate him again. Missions and fight pits level followers but risk permanent death. A Death Threat makes the target prepare, so the fight is harder but the loot better, one threat at a time. Orcs who kill you rank up, get new mocking lines and better loot. Killed orcs can randomly return, marked as having cheated death and angry about it. ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/6192-shadow-of-war-domination-guide-the-nemesis-system-betrayal-death-threats-resurrection-and-more-orc-dominating-mechanics-explained))
- Captains above Talion's level cannot be dominated; shaming lowers a captain's level and removes him for a later encounter, with a slim chance he turns deranged or stronger. ([Shadow of War Wiki, search snippet](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/File:Talion_ability.jpg); [Prima, search snippet](https://primagames.com/tips/shadow-war-build-army-and-gain-followers)) Iron Will resists breaking. One guide advises killing an Iron Will orc if two shames do not break it, and one forum player reports a legendary shamed six times picked up several new weaknesses. (search snippets, anecdotal; for example [this Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/356190/discussions/0/1489992713701691826))
- Shadow of Mordor fears: exposing a captain to a fear makes him much weaker and can strip strengths. Fears include Caragors, Ghuls, Graugs, burning, betrayal by allies and a rival. A captain who fears Caragors stops attacking and flees on sight of one. The tutorial captain is immune to ranged attacks, vulnerable to mounted beasts and afraid of Caragors. Traits are random and hidden until you gather intel. ([Gamer Guides, search snippet](https://gamerguides.com/shadow-of-mordor/guide/miscellaneous/the-nemesis-system/weaknesses))
- A related rival ladder, Assassin's Creed Odyssey: 5 bounty levels (level 1 sends one mercenary, level 2 up to two); hunting mercenaries come to your location, keep hunting when you leave unseen and follow you to sea; ranks run from 9 to 1, beating a higher ranked mercenary takes their place, and each rank gives a perk (rank 7: contract and bounty rewards up 20%; rank 2: better loot chance up 10%); some are weak to stealth; their info unlocks by meeting them, killing another mercenary or scouting; they can be recruited as lieutenants if knocked out with non lethal attacks; a bounty ends by paying it, killing its sponsor or waiting. ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2018/10/guide_assassins_creed_odyssey_mercenaries_-_how_to_beat_them_bounties_tiers_and_rewards)) Each game reportedly starts with 50 mercenaries, a mix of hand made and randomly generated ones. ([Vulkk, search snippet; which result said this was not confirmed](https://vulkk.com/2018/10/05/assassins-creed-odyssey-mercenaries-and-bounties-system/)) Vice called it the Nemesis system without any nemeses, because no mercenary hunts you for personal reasons. ([Vice, search snippet](https://vice.com/en/article/assassins-creed-odyssey-mercenaries))

### Inferences
- Nemesis gives three exits from one defeated captain, each with a downside: kill removes the threat (unless he cheats death), dominate adds a follower who may betray you, shame weakens him and sends him off to return later. No exit is free, so the choice depends on what your army needs.
- Character comes from memory plus visible consequence: the orc is changed by the meeting (scars, rank, lines). Kindred Siege's deterministic fight log already holds the events; the patent shows the path from event records to lines and trait changes.
- Fears are counterplay the player can prepare for (bring the feared thing), unlocked by intel. That is a template for chief weaknesses revealed on the road.
- Odyssey's lesson for a hunter ladder: ranks, traits and ambushes are not enough. Without personal history the hunters read as generic.

### Gaps
- Hoge's slides and video were not accessible; the talk details above are secondhand.
- No official numbers for betrayal odds, cheat death odds or shame effects.
- The patent's legal reach was not assessed; that is the owner's open solicitor question.

## 5. XCOM 2's Chosen, Metal Gear's non lethal bosses, Sekiro and Hades

### Takeaway
The Chosen recur in fights you cannot finish until you hunt down their stronghold, they grow by kidnapping and learning, and they taunt. Metal Gear pays non lethal boss wins with unique rewards and lets a few bosses be beaten by actions other than fighting (patience, past restraint). Sekiro ties the two bars together, so lost HP slows posture recovery, and Hades makes bosses react to how you won, not only whether you won.

### Cited Findings
**XCOM 2: War of the Chosen**
- Three Chosen: the Assassin (stealth and melee), the Hunter (long range rifle with tracking bullets) and the Warlock (psionics, can control several of your units). They attack XCOM occasionally during missions, are revived after being killed in battle, and are only removed for good when XCOM destroys their stronghold, which faction covert ops help find. They gain new abilities over time, can eventually attack the Avenger directly, and any left alive join the final mission. ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XCOM_2:_War_of_the_Chosen))
- Official text: they hunt the Commander and kidnap, interrogate and kill XCOM's soldiers, can invade the strategy layer, and have unique strengths and weaknesses introduced with each new campaign; raiding their strongholds defeats them for good. ([Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/593380/XCOM_2_War_of_the_Chosen/))
- A knowledge bar fills when they daze and kidnap soldiers; as it grows they gain battlefield strengths and new ways to drain your resources. Each takes extra damage from certain sources (explosives, or many attacks in one turn) and from one resistance faction; one reviewer's Chosen was weak to Reapers and to attacks from high ground. ([PC Gamer review, search snippet](https://www.pcgamer.com/xcom-2-war-of-the-chosen-review/); [GamingTrend impressions, search snippet](https://gamingtrend.com/feature/impressions/x-com-2-war-of-the-chosen-review-in-progress))
- The official Hunter description: he slowly tracks, wounds and disables units before the final blow, taunts the player during battles, and abducts troops to interrogate them for the Avenger's location. ([xcom.com, search snippet; the URL now redirects](https://xcom.com/news/xcom-2-war-of-the-chosen-the-hunter/amp))
- Tactics: a Chosen arrives after you break concealment and the mission timer stops; Flashbangs and Stasis do nothing; a Frost Grenade removes only one of its two actions; the Warlock retreats into a protective shell after enough damage; in the stronghold you must kill the Chosen several times, hitting its Sarcophagus between kills. ([XCOM substack](https://xcom.substack.com/p/xcom-2-wotc-tips-for-fighting-the))
- The Long War of the Chosen mod drops the general weaknesses and instead makes each Chosen take 25% more damage from its adversary faction, plus some immunities. ([Ufopaedia, search snippet](https://ufopaedia.org/Chosen_(LWOTC)))

**Metal Gear Solid**
- MGS3 stamina kills (draining stamina with non lethal weapons such as the Mk22, Mosin Nagant or stun grenades) give each boss's own camouflage: Ocelot, Animal (less weapon shake); The Pain, Hornet Stripes (bees, spiders and leeches leave you alone); The Fear, Spider (trades stamina for a higher camouflage index); The End, Moss (heals in sunlight); The Fury, Fire (less fire and explosion damage, no burning); Volgin, Cold War (enemies less likely to shoot you); The Boss, Snake (strong camouflage anywhere). The reward has to be picked up before the next event. The Sorrow's Spirit camouflage comes from examining his body. ([Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/metal-gear-solid-3-snake-eater/stamina-kill-guide-and-rewards-list-mgs3/))
- The Sorrow cannot be beaten with damage. You wade up a river while the ghosts of everyone you killed earlier come at you; their number scales with your kills, and a player who killed no one meets only earlier bosses. ([Bloody Disgusting, search snippet](https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3594373/sorrow-ghosts-looking-back-one-iconic-bosses-metal-gear-solid-3-snake-eater/); [Giant Bomb, search snippet](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Characters/The_Sorrow))
- The End can be beaten without the duel: save during the fight, wait about a week of real time, and he dies of old age; or shoot him early right after a cutscene where he appears in a wheelchair (sources disagree on where). Guinness lists it as the longest solution to a video game boss battle. ([Wikipedia, The End, search snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_(Metal_Gear)); [Guinness World Records, search snippet](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/457268-longest-solution-to-a-videogame-boss-battle))
- MGSV, Quiet: drain her stamina and then choose to spare her, and she becomes a sniper buddy; kill her and she does not. Kotaku's writer brought her down with two supply drops called onto her head, after a tranquiliser standoff that took about 40 minutes, and says even a lethal gun still leads to the spare choice. ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/here-is-a-good-way-to-get-quiet-in-metal-gear-solid-v-1728574710); [Prima, search snippet](https://vip-develop.primagames.com/?p=32818))

**Sekiro**
- Official mechanics: any landed attack, blocked or not, raises the target's posture; unblocked attacks also deal vitality damage; a broken posture opens a brief deathblow; stronger enemies need several deathblows. Enemy vitality is tied to posture recovery: "the lower the Vitality, the slower the Posture recovery." Against enemies with fast recovery, the official advice is to cut their vitality first and then break their posture. ([Activision support](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics))
- Deflects deal heavy posture damage to the attacker, and quick chains of deflects deal more. The deathblow window lasts a few seconds before the enemy recovers some posture. Enemies cannot regain posture while attacking, sprinting, grappling or staggered, and firecrackers can pause recovery. The wiki's recovery table (full rate at 75 to 100% vitality, 66% at 50 to 75%, 33% at 25 to 50%, 1% below 25%) sits in the section on Sekiro himself and is not confirmed for bosses. ([Fextralife](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- Recurring bosses with a choice: Owl asks Wolf to follow the Iron Code with him or stay loyal to Kuro. Siding with Owl leads to the Shura ending and fights with Emma and Isshin; staying loyal leads to fighting Owl (Father) in a memory of Hirata Estate. Genichiro is fought again later. ([Sekiro fan wiki, search snippet](https://sekiro-shadows-die-twice.fandom.com/wiki/Endings?oldid=9803); [Gamers Decide, search snippet](https://gamersdecide.com/articles/sekiro-all-endings))

**Hades**
- Polygon's Mike Mahardy found that Megaera dismissed his win because he had Mirror of Night upgrades. He spent a key to reset them, beat her again, and she acknowledged it. Greg Kasavin replied that she has three different follow up scenes back at the House depending on your relationship with her. ([GameSpot, search snippet](https://gamespot.com/articles/hades-writer-explains-script-philosophy-after-player-discovers-incredible-detail/1100-6482650/))
- Scale: over 22,000 lines of fully voiced dialogue from a studio of fewer than 20 people. ([GDC Vault abstract, GDC 2021](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026975/Breathing-Life-into-Greek-Myth)) A podcast summary reports over 300,000 words and about 8,500 lines for Zagreus, with the player seeing one or two new dialogue boxes per run. ([Game Log, secondhand](https://videlais.com/tag/hades/))

### Inferences
- The Chosen map closely onto Kindred Siege's rival hunters: recurring fights you cannot end (they retreat or revive), a visible growth bar fed by your failures (kidnaps), strengths and weaknesses rerolled per campaign, and a separate, deliberate mission to end them for good. The permanent kill is a strategic decision with its own cost, not a fight result.
- MGS3 makes the non lethal win the harder path and pays it with an exclusive item tied to who the boss is (The Pain's bees become bee protection). The Sorrow and The End are bosses whose fights express the player's history or patience.
- Sekiro's coupling fits "break his nerve while he stands" exactly: HP damage is the lever that makes the break possible, but too much HP damage ends the fight the other way.
- Hades reacts to how you won (the Mirror upgrades), which makes the boss feel like she is watching you.

### Gaps
- The Chosen knowledge thresholds, monthly actions and full strength and weakness lists were not verified from official sources (the official manual link returned 404).
- Sekiro boss posture recovery rates are not official.
- Hades' rules for picking which line plays (priorities, conditions) were not found.

## 6. Across these games: gates, windows, payoffs, keeping both paths alive, and bosses with character

### Takeaway
The deep versions share five traits: a gate that needs skill or a sacrifice; a window that is visible but imperfect; payoffs that differ in kind, so neither path is simply better; real risk on the capture side (failure, betrayal, lost resources); and bosses whose lines and moves answer the player's history and current actions. When the payoffs converge (Monster Hunter Wilds) or one path is just morally better (Dishonored's chaos), players stop choosing per fight.

### Cited Findings
**What gates a capture or spare**
- HP lines: Monster Hunter Rise 21 to 28% by threat ([MH Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise))); Deltarune Tired at 50% or less, Jevil's Tiredness 9 ([Deltarune Wiki, Tired](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Tired); [Jevil](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Jevil)).
- State lines: Shadow of War's broken state ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/6192-shadow-of-war-domination-guide-the-nemesis-system-betrayal-death-threats-resurrection-and-more-orc-dominating-mechanics-explained)); Sekiro's full posture ([Activision](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics)); Persona 5's all enemies down ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_5)); MGS stamina at zero ([Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/metal-gear-solid-3-snake-eater/stamina-kill-guide-and-rewards-list-mgs3/)).
- Level and story gates: Persona and SMT V level caps, Persona bosses ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/13327-persona-5-royal-demon-negotiation-answers-hold-up-guide); [Wikipedia SMT V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin_Megami_Tensei_V)); Shadow of War level cap ([Shadow of War Wiki, search snippet](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/File:Talion_ability.jpg)); Fates' story bosses and Monster Hunter's Elder Dragons ([FE Wiki](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Capture); [MH Wiki](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_(MHRise))).
- Tools and sacrifices: trap plus two tranqs (Monster Hunter); balls and status (Pokémon); halved stats and a must finish attack (Thracia); TP per tiring action (Jevil).
- Baldur's Gate 3 adds a mode switch: with weapons set to non lethal, knocking Minthara out counts as a victory, and since a late November patch she can then join from Moonrise Towers; before that patch, attacking the camp made her unrecruitable. ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/guides/baldurs-gate-3-how-to-recruit-minthara-without-killing-the-tieflings)) Guides surfaced by search add that only melee knocks her out (spells and ranged attacks kill her) and that a breakable bridge can drop her to her death; forum posts report failed attempts. ([Prima, search snippet; which guide said which not checked](https://primagames.com/tips/how-to-recruit-minthara-without-attacking-the-grove-in-baldurs-gate-3)) Her arrival is delayed into Act 2. ([GamesRadar, search snippet](https://gamesradar.com/baldurs-gate-3-minthara))

**How the window is shown**
- Monster Hunter: limping, the blue eye icon (Rise) or skull icon (World), and a Palico call out. Shadow of War: a green icon over the captain. Deltarune: blue name, blue Zz, the TIRED label and the MERCY percentage. Undertale: a yellow name, deliberately withheld for Toriel. Sekiro: the posture bar and a deathblow window of a few seconds. Persona 5: the Hold Up screen. Assassin's Creed Odyssey: a red skull on mercenaries far above your level. (Sources as cited in sections 1 to 5; Odyssey from [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2018/10/guide_assassins_creed_odyssey_mercenaries_-_how_to_beat_them_bounties_tiers_and_rewards).)

**What each path gives**
- Capture table and a 20 second quest end versus carves (Monster Hunter); boss camouflage only for stamina kills (MGS3); a sniper buddy only if Quiet is spared (MGSV); a follower versus removing the threat, with Death Threat loot only on the kill path (Shadow of War); a weapon versus an armour (Jevil); a Persona versus All Out Attack damage, money or an item (Persona 5); EXP and gold versus the pacifist ending (Undertale). (Sources as cited in sections 1 to 5.)
- Hollow Knight: when all three Mantis Lords are beaten (one fights alone first, then the other two together), they bow and let the Knight into Deepnest, and the rest of the tribe stops attacking. ([Hollow Knight Wiki, search snippet](https://hollowknight.wiki/w/Mantis_Lords); [Northeastern course blog, search snippet](https://experimentalgamedesign.sites.northeastern.edu/2019/11/10/indie-game-hollowknight)) This is a whole tribe changing sides after its leaders lose.
- Mount & Blade II: Bannerlord: after a battle you can release a captured lord (raises Charm), ransom him at a tavern (gold, raises Roguery), keep or imprison him, or execute him, which brings a massive relations penalty with his clan. ([Prima guide, search snippet; version dependent](https://primagames.com/?p=313358))

**What keeps players from always taking one path**
- Costs in the fight (Thracia's halved stats, Jevil's TP, Monster Hunter's consumables and lost carves); risk after the fight (Shadow of War betrayal, Bannerlord relations); hard gates (levels, story bosses); exclusive rewards on both sides. (Sources as above.)
- Counterexample one: Monster Hunter Wilds, where writers say the reward gap shrank and the choice is now mostly about speed. ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/monster-hunter-wilds-should-you-capture-or-slay-monsters))
- Counterexample two: Dishonored. Harvey Smith said low chaos only needed killing fewer than 20% of people, and the meter pushed binary choices; Dishonored 2 weighted characters differently and added a third, very high chaos state. ([Rolling Stone, search snippet](https://www.rollingstone.com/culture/culture-news/why-making-dishonored-2-was-so-hard-121280/); [GameRevolution interview, search snippet](https://www.gamerevolution.com/originals/12861-interview-dishonored-2s-harvey-smith-believes-dishonored-tv-series-would-be-cool-and-better-than-a-movie)) The first game could be finished without killing anyone, including mission targets. ([Wikipedia, Dishonored, search snippet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonored))

**What makes a boss feel like a character during the fight**
- Lines drawn from history: Nemesis dialogue chosen from stored event records ([Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)); Megaera reacting to Mirror upgrades ([GameSpot, search snippet](https://gamespot.com/articles/hades-writer-explains-script-philosophy-after-player-discovers-incredible-detail/1100-6482650/)); the cruellest humiliations kept rare ([Vidaextra, secondhand](https://www.vidaextra.com/accion/tierra-media-sombras-guerra-existe-orco-capaz-destruir-tu-mejor-espada-creadores-temian-que-cerrases-juego)).
- Phase lines, warnings and a last stand: Jevil's lines change each phase, text warns before his biggest attack, and his final phase escalates every turn ([Deltarune Wiki](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Jevil)).
- Refusal: Asgore breaking the MERCY button ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asgore)). Fear and flight: Shadow of Mordor captains fleeing what they fear ([Gamer Guides, search snippet](https://gamerguides.com/shadow-of-mordor/guide/miscellaneous/the-nemesis-system/weaknesses)). Your past made visible: The Sorrow's ghosts ([Bloody Disgusting, search snippet](https://bloody-disgusting.com/editorials/3594373/sorrow-ghosts-looking-back-one-iconic-bosses-metal-gear-solid-3-snake-eater/)).
- Reacting to what the player is doing right now, in Slay the Spire, a game where the boss's intent is shown in advance: the Guardian, after losing 30 HP (35 at Ascension 9, 40 at Ascension 19), gains 20 Block and switches to Defensive Mode with Sharp Hide (you take 3 damage per Attack you play, 4 at Ascension 19), and the threshold rises by 10 each time it shifts ([Slay the Spire Wiki, Guardian](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Guardian)). The Time Eater ends your turn and gains Strength once you play a set number of cards (12, per a fan wiki), and once below half HP it removes its debuffs and heals to 50% ([Slay the Spire Wiki, Time Eater](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Time_Eater); [Slay the Spire fandom, search snippet](https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/Blights)).

### Inferences
- A checklist for a deep capture choice, built from the cases above: (1) the gate needs skill; (2) the window can be read, but its timing carries risk; (3) capture costs something during the fight; (4) capture carries a risk after the fight; (5) killing has an exclusive reward; (6) the run's situation changes which path is better; (7) the boss expresses himself through the gate (Toriel, Asgore); (8) the outcome changes the boss or his people next time (Nemesis, Mantis Lords).
- Slay the Spire is the best fit for a game where the player steers rather than acts directly: the boss shows its intent, counts something the player does (damage taken, cards played) and changes stance at a known number. Those counters are readable and give the player something to plan around.

### Gaps
- No published data on how often players capture versus kill in any of these games was found; every "players usually do X" claim is a guide's opinion.

## 7. What could transfer to the Kindred Siege capture or kill chief fight

### Takeaway
Bad news first: the near automatic capture follows from a step that is almost free and certain (he kneels, you Hold, you capture). Every game surveyed makes capture cost something during the fight, risk something after it, or give up something that killing would have paid. Kindred Siege already has most of the parts (Hold drains your own nerve, only standing fighters join, the Alpha hunt grows with each tribe that joins, Princess gear on a kill, chief taunts); the work is making those trade offs visible at the moment of choice and dependent on the run.

### Cited Findings
- Current locks: capture the chief by breaking his nerve while he lives and his tribe joins you with his order; kill him and the tribe does not join but you take his Princess gear. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, lines 273 to 276](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- Exactly the chief's fighters still standing when he breaks join you; killing them all brings nobody. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, line 227](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- Holding drains your own nerve over time; Unleash drains it at half Hold's rate; Perfect Hold in the last half second before a big hit takes no damage and staggers the attacker. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, lines 89, 226, 289](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- The Alpha hunt grows within a run with each tribe that joins; the final hunt is 4 fighters and a captain plus 1 fighter per joined tribe. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, lines 204, 235](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- Chief taunts are in the slice; chiefs fight differently on later runs because they learned from earlier ones, and whether that is too close to the Nemesis patent is an open solicitor question. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, lines 203, 299](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- When you lose a fight, the winners capture one of your fighters by name. ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md, line 280](C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md))
- External anchors for the ideas below: Thracia capture costs ([FE Wiki](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Capture)); Sekiro vitality and posture coupling ([Activision](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics)); Jevil's two routes to two different rewards ([Deltarune Wiki](https://deltarune.wiki/w/Jevil)); Persona 5 Hold Up ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_5)); Nemesis betrayal, death threats and return rules ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/6192-shadow-of-war-domination-guide-the-nemesis-system-betrayal-death-threats-resurrection-and-more-orc-dominating-mechanics-explained); [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)); Chosen knowledge and strongholds ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XCOM_2:_War_of_the_Chosen)); Slay the Spire counters ([Guardian](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Guardian)); Wilds' converged rewards ([Gfinity](https://www.gfinityesports.com/article/monster-hunter-wilds-should-you-capture-or-slay-monsters)).

### Inferences
1. **Price the spare inside the fight** (Thracia, Jevil, Monster Hunter). Holding during the kneel should cost something the player could have spent elsewhere, order points or a visible chunk of your nerve, and the kneel should last only a few seconds, like Sekiro's deathblow window. Right now a short Hold is nearly free.
2. **Tie HP to nerve the Sekiro way.** The chief's nerve recovers over time, and recovery slows as his HP falls. The player then has to hurt him enough to make the break possible without killing him. Breaking him early, with more of his band standing, is the skilful capture and pays more fighters under the line 227 rule, much like Persona's all down requirement for a Hold Up.
3. **Make the yield depend on the chief's temperament, shown as intel before the fight** (Nemesis traits and Iron Will, Toriel's missing cue, Asgore's broken button, Shadow of Mordor intel). For example: a proud chief who never kneels and must be broken by nerve alone; a craven chief who kneels early; a treacherous chief whose kneel can be a feint that strikes if you Hold before a tell. One readable tell per temperament keeps it skill, not luck.
4. **Give the kill an exclusive reward that depends on the road ahead** (MGS3 camouflage, Jevil's weapon versus armour, Monster Hunter carves, Shadow of War Death Threat loot). If the Princess gear on offer is shown before the fight and counters the next chief on the path, the right answer changes from run to run. A kill could also be final in the Nemesis sense (beheaded orcs never return), while a captured or spared chief comes back changed.
5. **Show capture's after cost at the moment of choice** (Shadow of War betrayal, the Chosen's knowledge bar, Bannerlord relations). The locked Alpha hunt growth per joined tribe already is that cost; showing it on the kneel prompt ("+1 hunter") turns a hidden penalty into a decision.
6. **A desperation beat at the yield line** (Jevil's final phase, the Time Eater's one time heal, the Guardian's stance change). When HP crosses the line, the chief recasts or shifts stance once, with the 3 second tell, and one order answers it. Answer well and the kneel is honest; answer badly and you must kill him or lose people.
7. **Character through memory, with rare moments kept rare** (Nemesis lines from event records, Megaera, Hoge's rare humiliations). Pull chief taunts from the deterministic fight log, for example your most used order or a named fighter he killed last run, and save the cruellest lines for rare events.
8. **Rival hunters like the Chosen, with a personal reason** (XCOM, Odyssey, Vice's criticism). A visible knowledge bar fed by your losses (the locked rule that winners take one of yours by name is already a kidnap), strengths that reroll per campaign, one weakness revealed by intel, recurring until the player chooses a dedicated hunt to end one. Give each hunter a remembered grudge, or they read as generic.
9. **Measure the decision with the bots.** Run fixed policies (always capture, always kill) against a policy that reads the run's situation. The choice is real only if the situational policy beats both fixed ones. Wilds shows the decision dies once one path's rewards cover the other's.
- Patent caution: the independent claims cover an interaction with one opposing NPC that changes another opposing NPC's parameters, with the change shown to the player, and Google Patents shows the patent as active with 11 Aug 2036 listed against it. Ideas 4, 7 and 8, where enemies change because of fights, sit closest to it. Not legal advice; it belongs with the solicitor question already open.

### Gaps
- The sim code was not read, so how the bots decide at the kneel, and why they capture almost every time, is not checked.
- None of the numbers suggested (window length, recovery slowdown, costs) have been tested in the sim.
- No source gives player data on capture versus kill rates, so there is no external target split to aim for.
