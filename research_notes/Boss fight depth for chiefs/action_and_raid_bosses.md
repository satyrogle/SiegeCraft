# Boss fight depth in action games, Soulslikes, Monster Hunter and MMO raids

Reliability key used below: "search excerpt" means the claim came from a search result summary and the full page was not read (often because the site blocked fetching). "Unsourced wiki" means the wiki page itself cites nothing. Kindred Siege facts come from the research brief and from `DESIGN_LOCKS.md`.

## 1. FromSoftware (Dark Souls, Bloodborne, Sekiro, Elden Ring): how do telegraphs, punish windows, stagger, phases, moveset escalation and learning by dying create depth? Typical telegraph lengths and phase thresholds?

### Takeaway
FromSoftware bosses give a readable attack, a specific correct answer, and a payoff for answering it (stance break and riposte, posture break and deathblow), with a second hidden or visible bar that turns good defence and steady pressure into damage. Phases add moves, and their thresholds vary (fan guides give 80%, about two thirds and about half). The retry loop is deliberately fast because the difficulty assumes many attempts. No sourced numbers for typical telegraph (wind up) lengths were found.

### Cited Findings
- Miyazaki (4Gamer interview, Dec 2018, reported by Siliconera): Sekiro's "kengeki" combat needs a different playstyle from Dark Souls; "you first need to fiercely clash blades with the enemy" and then "find the right opening to attack the enemy's body parts" ([Siliconera, 4Gamer interview](https://www.siliconera.com/fromsoftware-president-talks-about-how-sekiro-differs-from-dark-souls/)). Secondhand translation; the Japanese original was not checked.
- Same interview: effective methods "in each encounter are set to be even more effective than usual"; with stealth and prosthetic tools, "how you fight and adapt is now important"; Miyazaki said you cannot just attack head on all the time or hide all the time, you must use everything you can; "I'd like people to enjoy the process of learning Sekiro's unique approach and slowly get better in the full game" ([Siliconera, 4Gamer interview](https://www.siliconera.com/fromsoftware-president-talks-about-how-sekiro-differs-from-dark-souls/)).
- Miyazaki (PlayStation Blog, 13 July 2018, interviewer Justin Massongill): "The idea of resurrection is that it helps the flow of the game"; without it, walking back every time you die would hurt the pacing. "The resurrection system was not introduced to make the game easier", it "actually can make the game harder", because difficulty was balanced around dying and resurrecting. "Just because you have this resurrection mechanic, it doesn't mean the player won't be afraid of death." "Ninjas can't take a lot of damage", and "if you screw up by a hair's breadth, it's over." Players who are not highly skilled can get through "if you think about it and play it smart" ([PlayStation Blog 2018](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=202767)).
- Miyazaki in a later Game Informer interview, as reported by PC Gamer: the balance between offence and defence has become more fluid and active, and Sekiro may be the clearest expression of that (search excerpt) ([PC Gamer, turning point](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/sekiro-was-a-big-turning-point-after-elden-ring-hidetaka-miyazaki-says-theres-one-more-level-we-can-crank-it-up-to/)).
- Elden Ring stance: every enemy has a hidden poise meter, "15 to 65 for normal enemies and 80 to 120 for bosses", with exceptions. Starscourge Radahn has 200 base. Lichdragon Fortissax has 140 base but takes only 40% poise damage from most attacks (80% from headshots), about 350 effective. Night's Cavalry has 80 base but takes 20%, about 400 effective. Ancient Dragons have 120 base, take 20% (80% from headshots), about 600 effective. Furnace Golems break from total regular damage instead ([Elden Ring wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance)).
- Elden Ring stance regeneration: after a delay of base poise divided by 13 seconds, poise regenerates at "13 poise per second". A 65 poise enemy waits 5 s, a 120 poise boss 9.2 s, Radahn 15.4 s ([Elden Ring wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance)).
- Elden Ring stance break: the enemy is stunned for "a significant amount of time" (no number given). Most enemies then allow a riposte, a critical hit that deals a large chunk of damage, knocks back, resets poise and ends the stun. The wiki advises hitting during the stun and finishing with the riposte, or riposting at once if you do not know the stun length ([Elden Ring wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance)).
- A fan blog lists Margit at stance 80 and Malenia at 80 in phase one, and says much of the boss roster sits at exactly 80 or exactly 120 (unverified blog) ([Gamertag Mythras blog](https://gamertagmythras.com/blog/elden-ring/elden-ring-stance-break-guide)).
- Phase thresholds in fan guides (not datamined): Godrick's second phase triggers "at around half health", with a cutscene ([Gamer Guides, Godrick](https://www.gamerguides.com/elden-ring/database/bosses/stormveil-castle-bosses/godrick-the-grafted)); Margit enters his second phase at around two thirds health ([PC Gamer, Elden Ring bosses](https://pcgamer.com/elden-ring-bosses-locations)); Radahn: one guide says phase two starts at 80% health, when he gains ranged attacks and moves that pull you in, and phase three at half ([Gamepressure, Radahn](https://www.gamepressure.com/elden-ring/starscourge-radahn/z0f7ab)), while another describes only a 50% event where he leaps into the sky and crashes down as a meteor ([PCGamesN, Radahn](https://www.pcgamesn.com/elden-ring/radahn-boss-fight)). The Radahn sources conflict.
- Not FromSoftware, but the same structure stated by a developer: Double Fine's Psychonauts 2 boss attacks "followed a pattern of telegraph, attack, and recovery", each part able to chain into sub chains of behaviour; variables were tuned per boss and "for the specific phase of the boss" (GDC 2023 talk by gameplay programmer Rebecca Vessal, per Game Developer) ([Game Developer, Psychonauts 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/using-a-modular-system-of-maneuvers-to-design-i-psychonauts-2-i-s-boss-fights-in-a-hurry)).

### Inferences
- The FromSoftware pattern: HP is the clock, the stance or posture bar is the skill meter. Reading an attack correctly pays twice, you take no damage and you fill the second bar.
- The stance regen rule (wait base divided by 13 seconds, then 13 per second) rewards staying on the boss. Backing off for long lets the boss reset, so retreat has a cost beyond lost time.
- Phase thresholds are not always 50%. The cited examples run 80%, about 66% and about 50%. Varying them per boss keeps phase changes from being a fixed rhythm.
- Phase changes add moves (Radahn gains ranged and pull attacks) rather than only health, which is moveset escalation.
- Fast retries (Sekiro resurrection, kept tempo) are what make "learning by dying" fun. The difficulty is tuned assuming many attempts. That is the part least likely to transfer to a run ending roguelike fight.

### Gaps
- No primary, datamined or wiki source found for typical telegraph (wind up) lengths in Dark Souls, Bloodborne or Elden Ring. Elden Ring's "delayed attacks" were not sourced in this pass.
- Bloodborne's rally (regain) system and Dark Souls 3 phase thresholds were not sourced.
- Exact stance break stun durations are not published on the wiki.
- A claim that cooperative summons make bosses take 40% less stance damage came only from Steam forum posts in search results and could not be traced to one thread. Unverified.
- No FromSoftware GDC talk or postmortem on boss design was found.

## 2. Sekiro's posture system and deflects: how does it turn defence into offence, and what are the numbers?

### Takeaway
Sekiro makes defending the main way to attack. A deflect costs the attacker far more posture than the defender, the correct answer to a perilous attack deals massive posture damage, and a full posture bar opens a deathblow, so HP mainly governs how fast posture recovers. Numbers come from an unsourced wiki: a 12 frame (0.2 s) deflect window that shrinks with spamming, and posture recovery that falls to 66%, 33% and then 1% speed as vitality drops by quarters.

### Cited Findings
- Deflect effect: a deflect "negates all damage from most attacks", builds the enemy's posture "by a significant amount", and briefly staggers most enemies, opening a counterattack window. Repeated deflects in a short time increase the posture damage ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection)).
- Deflect timing: "12 frames by default (0.2 seconds before the attack hits)". Recent guard releases shrink it to "as little as 4 frames, even 0 frames if you spam it too fast". The penalty clears after 30 frames or immediately after a successful deflect. Pressing too early just guards. Spamming is not recommended for single attacks but is "actually necessary" against rapid flurries ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection)). Unsourced wiki. Note: a search engine summary of the same page reported 30 frames default and 7 frames after a recent release; the fetched page text says 12, 4 and 0, so all of these numbers are unverified.
- A Steam user describes the window as roughly 500 ms, with 0.1 to 0.2 s left when spamming (anecdotal) ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/814380/discussions/0/1746772719869502260)).
- A Sekiro mod author notes that deflect frames are carried on enemy weapon hitboxes, and that most perilous attacks lack them (search excerpt) ([Nexus Mods](https://www.nexusmods.com/sekiro/mods/1129)).
- Asymmetry: deflecting deals a small amount of posture damage to the defender and a high amount to the attacker; deflects never break the defender's posture; blocking or being hit fills the defender's gauge and does nothing to the attacker; regular attacks cannot break the player's posture if he deflects them, even with a full bar ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Perilous attacks are flagged with a red kanji meaning "danger", cannot be blocked, and are dangerous or impossible to deflect ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection)). The appropriate counter deals "massive Posture damage"; the Mikiri Counter answers thrusts and deals "a large amount of damage to posture"; the leaping kick combat art answers sweeps and air attacks for "extremely high posture damage" ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Recovery: player posture starts recovering about 1 s after the last posture damage, enemy posture has no such delay; no recovery while attacking, sprinting, grappling or staggered; holding guard while not being hit greatly speeds recovery. Rate by remaining vitality: 100 to 75%: full rate; 75 to 50%: 66%; 50 to 25%: 33%; 25 to 0%: 1% ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)). The page presents the vitality bands generally; it does not say clearly whether enemies use identical bands.
- On break: the player can deathblow the enemy; if it is not done "within a few seconds" the enemy recovers a small portion of posture and the fight goes on. A broken player is staggered for "a brief period" and reportedly takes "significantly more damage" (marked untested) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Encounter specific tools: the Shinobi Firecracker deals significant posture damage to animals including the Guardian Ape and can stop some enemies' posture recovery for several seconds; Ichimonji deals high posture damage and restores some of your own ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Large enemies (Ape, Ogre, Hatred Demon, Taros) also have a separate hidden poise; depleting it causes a stagger of a few seconds ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Player growth: each upgrade (four Prayer Beads) adds 80 vitality and 30 posture, up to 1120 vitality and 420 posture; the page's totals only add up with base values it does not state ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)).
- Miyazaki wanted the combat to centre on "swords clashing", with the deathblow coming after sustained pressure rather than chipping health (PC Gamer, search excerpt) ([PC Gamer, lightsabers](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/sekiros-combat-would-be-perfect-for-lightsabers)).

### Inferences
- Three levers make posture deep. One: asymmetry, so a good read is an attack. Two: HP damage throttles posture recovery (down to 1% under a quarter vitality), so the two bars interact and neither alone is the whole fight. Three: the payoff (deathblow) expires in a few seconds, so it must be cashed in.
- The perilous kanji is one universal "this one is different" flag, and the attack's animation says which of a few answers is right. A single flag plus a small answer set is learnable across many bosses.
- The spam penalty (window shrinks with repeated presses, resets on success) keeps defence about timing without making flurries impossible. It is a precise anti mashing rule.
- Deflectability is authored per attack (frames on the hitbox), so designers decide attack by attack which moves can be parried. That is a design knob, not a global rule.

### Gaps
- No datamine or official figure for the deflect window; deathblow window and stagger lengths are only "a few seconds" on the wiki.
- The wiki pages fetched do not state that grab attacks are answered by dodging; widely believed but not sourced here.
- Multi deathblow bosses (several deathblow markers) were not covered by the fetched pages.

## 3. Monster Hunter (World, Rise, Wilds): part breaks and severs, flinches, enrage and exhaustion, limping and fleeing, capture, turf wars, wounds. What decisions does each give, and what are the thresholds?

### Takeaway
Monster Hunter puts many small decisions inside one long fight: which part to hit (breaks, sever, wounds), when to spend control tools that lose strength with each use, whether to trade blows during an enrage that raises both sides' damage, how to exploit fatigue, and whether to capture once the monster is low. Hard numbers: Rathalos (MHW Iceborne) rages for 100 s at x1.1 monster damage, x1.1 speed and x1.1 damage taken; fatigue lasts 60, 50 or 40 s by rank; traps lose 2 s and flashes 4 s per repeat; Rise capture opens at 28%, 23% or 21% HP by threat level.

### Cited Findings
- Enrage, Rathalos (MHW Iceborne): duration 100 s; monster damage x1.1; monster speed (movement and attack animation) x1.1; player damage x1.1, defined as the "multiplier on damage the player deals while enraged" ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)). Note: this is a user sandbox draft page on the wiki.
- Stamina and fatigue, Rathalos: max stamina 700 / 850 / 1000 (Low / High / Master Rank); fatigue duration 60 / 50 / 40 s; stamina recovered when fatigue ends 600 / 750 / 900; fatigued speed x0.9 ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)).
- Control tools with diminishing returns: Pitfall Trap 10 s (11 s if first used while exhausted), minus 2 s per repeat, floor 2 s; Shock Trap 8 s (8.8 s exhausted), minus 2 s, floor 2 s; Flash Pod 20 s, minus 4 s per repeat, floor 5 s ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)).
- Status thresholds rise each time they trigger: Stun (KO) 150, rising 120 per trigger to a 630 cap, lasts 7 s; Exhaust 225, rising 75 to 900, each trigger drains 200 stamina; Mount 40, rising 70 to 460; Paralysis 180, rising 130 to 570, 10 s; Sleep 150, rising 100 to 550, 40 s; Poison 250 to 700 (plus 150), 15 s, 150 damage; Blast 120 to 720 (plus 60), 100 damage; buildup decays 5 every 10 s ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)).
- Part breaks: Rathalos has breakable head, each wing separately, and a severable tail; each break has its own reward table (one Low Rank break table: scale 66%, shell 30%, plate 4%) and the severed tail is carved (Rathalos Tail 70% in Low Rank) ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)).
- Dung Pod: 50% chance per attempt to make a monster leave, attempted on contact and then every 5 s for 30 s ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)).
- Capture thresholds (Rise): 28% of total HP for lower threat monsters, 23% mid threat, 21% higher threat; the threshold is reached before the capture icon appears; Elder Dragons, Apex and Afflicted monsters cannot be captured (search excerpt of the wiki page) ([MH Wiki, Capturing MHRS](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_%28MHRS%29)).
- Capture practice (World): trap the monster, then two Tranq Bombs; if damage comes too fast, a skull icon flashes instead of the monster limping away, and "any more damage dealt has a good chance of killing the monster, voiding any attempt at capture" ([Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/103008/monster-hunter-world-how-to-trap-a-monster)).
- Player reports (unverified): hurt monsters limp, flee to their nest and sleep, which restores health; the skull is hidden while the monster is enraged; thresholds vary by monster, some said to need 10 to 15% ([Steam MHW thread A](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/1639801448917804334), [Steam MHW thread B](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/1735462352475642062)).
- Wilds wounds, from the director (Yuya Tokuda, PlayStation Blog, 13 June 2024, interviewer Brett Elston): "Attacking the monster naturally causes damage or wounds to happen"; wounds do not need Focus Mode; hitting wounds deals more damage; "The real merit of Focus Mode is to help players in their positioning and their distancing from monsters"; Focus Mode highlights wounds and enables special attacks that do more wound damage ([PlayStation Blog 2024](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=393257)).
- Tokuda on adapting mid hunt: "It was important for us to give players the ability to adapt to that, and switch their own kind of playstyles", about changing environments and monsters that change moment to moment, linked to carrying different weapons or elements ([PlayStation Blog 2024](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=393257)).
- Wilds guides (search excerpts; the exact page for each line was not confirmed): repeated hits on one spot build a wound, shown orange in Focus Mode and blue on Tempered monsters; a Focus Strike destroys a wound and "destroying a wound will always result in a stun of the Monster"; monsters take more damage at active wounds; destroying wounds often yields materials; some skills boost wound damage ([Dexerto](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/monster-hunter-wilds-wounds-explained-2973213), [Gamer Guides](https://earth.gamerguides.com/monster-hunter-wilds/guide/getting-started/gameplay/focus-mode-and-wounds-monster-hunter-wilds)).
- Tokuda via IGN (secondhand, GamingBolt): Wilds monsters have larger health pools and higher flinch thresholds than World because the team wanted players to use Focus Strikes often; he admitted Focus Strikes may have been a bit too strong in the beta ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/monster-hunter-wilds-will-have-monsters-with-higher-health-and-flinch-thresholds-than-world)).
- Turf wars: scripted mini events between specific large monster pairs, introduced in World and in every main series title since; one damages the other, and hunters can usually turn them to their advantage (search excerpt) ([MH Wiki, Turf Wars](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Turf_Wars)). World outcomes are a named winner, "Tie, both take damage", or random (Diablos vs Black Diablos) ([MH Wiki, MHWorld Turf Wars](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/MHWorld/Turf_Wars)). In Rise every monster involved takes damage, loses stamina and can have parts broken (guide, search excerpt) ([TouchTapPlay](https://www.touchtapplay.com/what-are-turf-wars-in-monster-hunter-rise/)). In Wilds some fire only once one monster is downed, for example Arkveld's absorption attack on a downed Quematrice (guide, search excerpt) ([GameWith](https://gamewith.net/monster-hunter-wilds/55210)). One Steam user says turf wars were made rarer after World because players found them too frequent (anecdotal) ([Steam Rise thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1446780/discussions/0/3825284962829669006)).

### Inferences
- Enrage is two sided: Rathalos hits 10% harder and moves 10% faster for 100 s but also takes 10% more damage. That makes "back off or trade" a real choice instead of pure punishment.
- Higher ranks shrink reward windows (fatigue 60 to 50 to 40 s) and raise stamina. Monster Hunter scales difficulty with tighter windows, not only more HP.
- Tolerance curves (traps minus 2 s per use, flash minus 4 s, status thresholds rising per trigger) let designers hand out strong control without letting players lock the boss forever. Each use becomes a timing decision: use it now, or save it for the exhausted window (trap lasts 10% longer then).
- Wilds raised HP and flinch thresholds only together with a new skill tool to cut through them (wounds plus Focus Strikes). More health is acceptable when it comes with a better way to burn it.
- Capture is the clearest "kill or keep" decision in these genres: a visible low health cue, a setup (trap) and a finisher (tranq), and a risk of overkill. The Rise thresholds of 21 to 28% bracket Kindred Siege's existing 25% yield.

### Gaps
- No turf war damage numbers found; the MHWorld table lists only outcomes.
- Part break HP, flinch values, base HP and capture or limp thresholds for World monsters were not in the fetched sections of the Rathalos page.
- Limping, nest sleeping and health regen evidence is from player forums only.
- Whether breaking specific parts removes or weakens attacks was not sourced in this pass.
- Wilds offset attacks, power clashes and perfect guard were not sourced (the PlayStation Blog interview did not cover them). Several Wilds guide details come from beta coverage and may have changed.

## 4. MMO raids (WoW, FFXIV Savage and Ultimate): role checks, adds, soaks and spreads, tank swaps, interrupts, intermissions, enrage timers, layering. How do designers stop fights being pure damage checks?

### Takeaway
Raids avoid pure damage checks by giving each role its own test (tankbusters and swaps, raidwides and healing cuts, add kill order, interrupts, stacks, spreads and towers), by building mechanics as separate modules with difficulty knobs and then combining them, and by using enrage as a frame on fight length rather than the challenge itself. WoW hard enrages mostly fall at 5 to 10 minutes; listed health triggered frenzies all fire at or below 33% health; and several add mechanics make kill order a real trade.

### Cited Findings
- Enrage purpose: enrages add urgency, make DPS more critical and stop undergeared raids from winning; hard enrages wipe the raid and most fall in the 5 to 10 minute range; soft enrages gradually raise difficulty and drain resources, triggered by time, health percentage or mistakes ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- Hard enrage examples: Lord Kazzak 3 min; Halfus Wyrmbreaker 6 min, where "the stacking debuff from killing drakes is needed to beat the timer"; Valithria Dreamwalker about 7 min; Norushen about 7 min; Oregorger 7 min 30 s; Operator Thogar 8 min 30 s; Brackenspore 10 min ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- Soft enrage examples: Gruul plus 15% damage every 30 s; Vaelastrasz loses a random player every 15 s to Burning Adrenaline; Toravon stacks a plus 25% frost damage debuff after each add wave; Chimaeron's phase two cuts all healing by 99%; Beth'tilac plus 5% damage every few seconds in phase two; Lei Shi gains cast speed each time she loses 10% health ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- Hard frenzy examples: Maexxna at 30% (plus 50% attack speed, plus 75% physical damage); Patchwerk at 5% (plus 25% damage, plus 40% attack speed); Deathbringer Saurfang at 30%; Shannox plus 30% damage and attack speed per hound killed; Gara'jal at 20%; Horridon plus 50% attack speed and plus 100% damage when War God Jalakk dies; Kor'kron Dark Shaman at 25%; The Butcher at 30%; Kromog at 30%; Sha of Pride at 30% (raidwide every 10 s); General Nazgrim at 10% (extra add wave); Tortos at 20%; Garalon (heroic) at 33% switches to melee the tank ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- No enrage at all: every Molten Core boss, nearly all of Blackwing Lair, and Heigan the Unclean, where fights over 30 minutes have been reported ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- FFXIV tankbusters: hit the highest enmity players for damage most other jobs cannot survive; signalled by a high pitched sound and a red downward arrow. Types: standard; AoE (others must move away from the tank); caution (striped ring, size usually not shown); shared (orbs show how many tanks must stand in to split it); tank tower. In high end content a shared buster is often taken by one tank with an invulnerability. In normal duties multi target busters go to the tanks regardless of enmity; in high end duties they go to the highest enmity players. In newer high end duties an expanding and contracting ring means the AoE size is deliberately not telegraphed ([CGW, Tankbuster](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Tankbuster)).
- FFXIV marker vocabulary: mechanics are warned by markers, "The type and extent of these mechanics are shown by associated markers", from simple circles up to PlayStation symbol markers in high end duties. Families listed: AoE shapes, stacks, tankbusters, tethers (spread, paired, transferable, proximity, chase, magnetic), towers (standard, tank, multi player, stack), knockbacks, gaze, proximity, number markers ([CGW, Common mechanics](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Common_mechanics_and_markers)).
- Stack markers split unsurvivable damage across the party and come with a rhythmic high pitched pulsing sound; spreads make players fan out so hits do not overlap; in one Ultimate fight the towers always need all 8 players and any missed tower instantly wipes the raid (search excerpts of Consolegameswiki) ([CGW glossary](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Acronyms,_abbreviations,_and_common_terms)). A tankbuster is an attack meant to be soaked by a tank; soaking is deliberately absorbing an attack (search excerpt) ([Destructoid, FFXIV terms](https://www.destructoid.com/final-fantasy-xiv-common-terms-explained/)).
- FFXIV designers on Eden's Gate (Famitsu, translated by AkhMorning): Daisuke Nakagawa: "my planning process is to repeatedly think, combine, and rethink"; mechanics were conceived separately and then combined, finding "what's missing and what was the extra that remained". Masaki Nakagawa (lead): "The specifics of the Delayed Spell mechanic was made to be easily ramped up or toned down in difficulty." On E2: "If the party has enough DPS, it is possible to defeat the boss before sequential cycles are unleashed." On E4's Cycle phases: "Each phase was made to be especially quick in tempo, to the point there wasn't any time for players to even resurrect." Nabeshima on a readability fix: "I made it so that the indicators will appear for only a short while" (added after players asked where to go). Kawamoto: Normal and Savage were built together because "making the Normal version overly packed would make the Savage version feel as if it lacks differences"; he wanted the first floor cleared "once in 2 to 3 weeks". Planning time: Titan, both modes, "about 30 days"; the second floor plan, "about a week" ([AkhMorning, Famitsu Eden's Gate](https://www.akhmorning.com/news/famitsu-interview-edens-gate-part-2/)).
- Masaki Nakagawa (GameSpot): raid design starts about six months to a year before release, and the next tier is already in progress when one launches (search excerpt; page blocked) ([GameSpot](https://www.gamespot.com/articles/how-final-fantasy-xivs-toughest-battles-are-designed-a-deep-dive-on-savage-raids/1100-6517559/)).
- Yoshida (2016): players said Alexander Savage was too hard, so the next tier was made easier to bring more people in (search excerpt) ([Nova Crystallis](https://novacrystallis.com/2016/08/yoshida-talks-final-fantasy-xivs-alexander-fan-feedback-and-the-quest-for-an-easier-raid/)).
- Ruby Weapon team: they spent a great deal of time on mechanics that would feel fresh, aiming for a fight "as close to an action game as we could possibly make it" (search excerpt) ([PlayStation Blog, Ruby Weapon](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=221747)).
- Ion Hazzikostas (PC Gamer interview, reported by Icy Veins; search excerpts, pages blocked): an "arms race" in which addons interpret mechanics, designers respond with more complex fights, and players then need addons; "A player might say this was a boring encounter... That may make us add a new mechanic..."; "Player sophistication has grown" since Molten Core; Blizzard plans to limit addon functions in raids and improve in game cues ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/blizzard-plans-to-reduce-add-on-reliance-in-future-raids/), [inkl copy of PC Gamer](https://www.inkl.com/news/wow-s-newest-raid-will-build-on-20-years-of-large-group-encounters-with-new-twists-and-perhaps-less-help-from-mods)).
- Hazzikostas (2022 interview): the aim of a new encounter is not to top the lists of most wipes or hardest bosses; Mythic tuning should let the best guilds separate without making content unattainable (search excerpt) ([Wowhead 2022 interview](https://www.wowhead.com/es/news/dragonflight-pre-launch-interview-with-ion-hazzikostas-329950)). On Private Auras, players build clumsy macro workarounds to feed information to addons (search excerpt) ([Wowhead Private Auras interview](https://wowhead.com/pt/news/wowhead-exclusive-interview-with-ion-hazzikostas-and-morgan-day-private-auras-343147)).
- Interrupt rotations: a 2026 third party guide to Mythic L'ura has 12 players interrupting across three boss frames, four per boss, with classes slotted into kick positions by cooldown timing (search excerpt) ([Seramate](https://seramate.com/news/2026/06/10/mythic-lura-macros-positioning-guide-1f164dd6)); kick order macros mark the target and set focus for later kickers (search excerpt) ([WoWUtils](https://wowutils.com/viserio-cooldowns/raid/interrupt-macro)).

### Inferences
- Role checks: each role gets a mechanic only it can answer (tanks: busters and swaps; healers: raidwides and healing cuts like Chimaeron's 99%; DPS: adds, interrupts, enrage). Molten Core era fights with no enrage and Patchwerk style tank and spank are the baseline later design moved away from.
- Kill order becomes a decision when add deaths change the boss: Shannox gets stronger per hound killed, Horridon doubles damage when Jalakk dies, Halfus can only be beaten in time with the debuff from killing drakes. Each kill gives and costs something.
- Of the 13 hard frenzies listed, 11 are health triggered and all 11 fire at or below 33% health, five at exactly 30%. Late fight escalation is the norm; a single midpoint phase is not.
- FFXIV designers treat each mechanic as a module with a difficulty knob, then layer them. At higher difficulty they withhold telegraph information (unsized rings, brief indicators, faster tempo) instead of only raising numbers.
- A DPS check that lets a strong group skip a mechanic (E2) rewards build quality without making the fight only a damage race.
- The addon arms race is a warning: when mechanics are piled on to keep fights interesting, readability collapses and players lean on tools. Cues must stay clear in the game itself.

- Typical raid cast bar or telegraph lengths were not found.

- Intermission and transition phase examples with thresholds (for example the Lich King) were not sourced in this pass.

## 5. Design theory: what separates deep bosses from health sponges, one shot gimmicks and random punishment?

### Takeaway
The recurring pattern in developer sources: build the boss from telegraph, attack and recovery units; make it an exam of verbs the player already owns; make spectacle come from the core combat rather than one off gimmicks; make mechanics modular with difficulty knobs; pair extra health with better player tools; and keep randomness inside readable bounds. Shallow bosses ignore the player's build, depend on lock and key gimmicks, or stack mechanics until they cannot be read.

### Cited Findings
- Psychonauts 2 (GDC 2023, Double Fine): maneuvers "followed a pattern of telegraph, attack, and recovery"; light randomisation "within the accepted parameters"; data driven variables tuned per boss and per phase; two of the bosses had only two months each for concept and prototype ([Game Developer, Psychonauts 2](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/marketing/using-a-modular-system-of-maneuvers-to-design-i-psychonauts-2-i-s-boss-fights-in-a-hurry)).
- Itay Keren (Untame), GDC 2018 boss battle design talk: frames the boss battle as an awe inspiring culmination that tests acquired skills, reveals story and serves as a memorable test of strength (page description only; the talk is free on the GDC YouTube channel) ([Game Developer, Keren talk](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/video-boss-battle-design-fundamentals)).
- Insomniac, GDC 2024 "Refining the Rogues Gallery": unique, memorable boss fights whose spectacle came through core combat rather than one off lock and key mechanics (session description, search excerpt) ([GDC schedule](https://schedule.gdconf.com/session/refining-the-rogues-gallery-building-boss-fights-in-marvels-spider-man-2/900253), [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1034367/Refining-the-Rogues-Gallery-Building)).
- A GDC talk on Sackboy: A Big Adventure walks through many early iterations and prototypes of one boss fight (search excerpt) ([GDC Vault, Sackboy](https://gdcvault.com/play/1028082/Designing-Bosses-For-Sackboy-A)).
- A Deus Ex: Human Revolution designer publicly apologised for its boss fights, "we are truly sorry" (headline only; reasons not read) ([PC Gamer, Deus Ex](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/deus-ex-human-revolution-designer-on-boss-fights-we-are-truly-sorry)).
- Miyazaki: per encounter tools "are set to be even more effective than usual", and the goal is to "enjoy the process of learning" ([Siliconera, 4Gamer interview](https://www.siliconera.com/fromsoftware-president-talks-about-how-sekiro-differs-from-dark-souls/)).
- Tokuda: higher HP and flinch thresholds were paired with Focus Strikes, a new tool to cut through them (secondhand) ([GamingBolt](https://gamingbolt.com/monster-hunter-wilds-will-have-monsters-with-higher-health-and-flinch-thresholds-than-world)).
- Hazzikostas: complexity added to keep fights interesting fed an "arms race" that hurt readability (search excerpt) ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/blizzard-plans-to-reduce-add-on-reliance-in-future-raids/)).
- FFXIV: mechanics built separately, then combined; a mechanic designed to be "easily ramped up or toned down in difficulty" ([AkhMorning, Famitsu Eden's Gate](https://www.akhmorning.com/news/famitsu-interview-edens-gate-part-2/)).
- Game Maker's Toolkit: no boss specific GMTK video was found in this pass; GMTK's "Boss Keys" series is about dungeon and world lock and key design, not boss fights ([Wikipedia, Game Maker's Toolkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Maker%27s_Toolkit)).

### Inferences
Synthesis across the sources above (my reading, not a quoted framework):
1. The unit is telegraph, attack, recovery. Depth comes from how many distinct correct answers exist and what the recovery window pays.
2. A boss is an exam of verbs the player already has. A boss that demands a verb the player's build lacks (the Deus Ex lesson, reason unverified here) reads as unfair.
3. Two interacting bars (HP plus stance or posture) give two ways to win and a reason to mix offence and defence. One big bar invites a sponge.
4. Escalate by adding and layering moves, shortening windows and withholding information, not by health alone (Radahn's new moves, Monster Hunter rank windows, FFXIV unsized rings).
5. Randomness should pick among readable moves (Psychonauts' bounded randomisation, Sekiro's kanji plus animation), never decide damage silently.
6. A consistent cue vocabulary (FFXIV markers plus sounds) beats more mechanics. Complexity that outruns readability turns into the addon arms race.
7. Reward skill and builds with shortcuts: riposte crits on stance breaks, extra materials on breaks, DPS that skips a mechanic.
8. One shot mechanics are acceptable when they are a readable exam answer (FFXIV towers that wipe if missed), and become gimmicks when nothing earlier taught the answer.

### Gaps
- The Keren, Insomniac and Sackboy talks were seen only through descriptions; their actual principles were not reviewed.
- No GMTK video on boss design was found, so no GMTK claims are included.
- No well sourced analysis specifically on "one shot gimmicks" or "random punishment" was found; points 5 and 8 above are synthesis.

## 6. What could transfer to Kindred Siege's indirect control squad auto battler, and what could not?

### Takeaway
What transfers is the part about decisions and resources, not reflexes: a second bar that good answers fill (Sekiro posture maps onto the chief's nerve), punish windows that orders cash in, diminishing returns on control and on spam, two sided enrages, bodyguard deaths that change the chief, a fixed cue vocabulary across all tribes, and tighter windows rather than more health at higher tiers. What does not transfer: frame tight timing, per unit positioning, many player coordination, learning through dozens of deaths, and long hard enrages.

### Cited Findings
- Kindred Siege already has: orders that cost earned points (earned by a parry, dodging a cast with Fall Back, interrupting a cast with Focus Fire, a kill, breaking nerve, a combo); Perfect Hold (Hold in the last half second: no damage, no nerve cost, the attacker staggers, Unleash straight after is a combo); tribes that learn your parry and delay their cast so a Hold only soaks, while Fall Back still dodges and Focus Fire can still break the cast; Hold and Unleash drain your own nerve; capture means breaking the chief's nerve while he lives (whole tribe joins with his order), killing gives his Princess gear; "making one button spam lose" is still open ([DESIGN_LOCKS.md](../../DESIGN_LOCKS.md)).
- Sekiro: deflects hurt the attacker's posture far more than the defender's; correct answers to perilous attacks deal massive posture damage; posture recovery slows by vitality band (100 / 66 / 33 / 1%) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture)); the deflect window is 12 frames (0.2 s) and shrinks to 4 or 0 frames with spamming, resetting after 30 frames or a successful deflect (unsourced wiki) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection)).
- Elden Ring: stance regen waits base divided by 13 seconds, then 13 per second ([Elden Ring wiki, Stance](https://eldenring.wiki.gg/wiki/Stance)).
- Monster Hunter: trap and flash durations shrink per use with floors; status thresholds rise per trigger; enrage raises monster damage, speed and damage taken by x1.1 for 100 s; fatigue windows shrink by rank (60 / 50 / 40 s) ([MH Wiki, Rathalos MHWI sandbox](https://www.monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/User:RampageRobot/Sandbox/MonsterPages/Rathalos/MHWI)); Rise capture thresholds 21 to 28% ([MH Wiki, Capturing MHRS](https://monsterhunterwiki.org/wiki/Capturing_%28MHRS%29)).
- WoW: Shannox, Horridon and Halfus make add deaths change the boss; soft enrages ramp (Gruul plus 15% damage every 30 s); health triggered frenzies cluster at or below 33% ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage timer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)).
- FFXIV: fixed marker and sound vocabulary across content ([CGW, Common mechanics](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Common_mechanics_and_markers), [CGW, Tankbuster](https://ffxiv.consolegameswiki.com/wiki/Tankbuster)); enough DPS skips a mechanic in E2; mechanics built with difficulty knobs ([AkhMorning, Famitsu Eden's Gate](https://www.akhmorning.com/news/famitsu-interview-edens-gate-part-2/)).
- Sekiro's resurrection exists to keep retry tempo, and its difficulty assumes many deaths ([PlayStation Blog 2018](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=202767)).

### Inferences
Transfers (each tagged LIKELY or GUESS; all are untested proposals, not verified in the sim):
1. Make the chief's nerve work like Sekiro posture. A correctly answered chief spell (right order inside the 3 s tell) and a Perfect Hold should take a big bite out of the chief's nerve, not only avoid damage. If his nerve recovers over time, HP loss should slow that recovery in bands (Sekiro's 100 / 66 / 33 / 1% ports cleanly to integer ticks). Capture then becomes the skill path and killing the brute path, and the two bars interact. This is the single strongest lever. LIKELY.
2. Answers should open a short punish window that an order cashes in, with an expiry (Sekiro deathblow "within a few seconds", Elden Ring riposte). For example, a parried chief hit lets marks land on him for a few seconds even while his band stands. That gives a skill route through the bodyguard rule instead of a fixed "kill the band first" solution. LIKELY.
3. Give each chief two or three tell types with different answers (the Sekiro perilous idea: one danger flag, the animation says which answer), drawn from one shared visual and sound grammar across all 24 tribes, the way FFXIV stack and tankbuster markers always look and sound the same. Learning then carries between chiefs. LIKELY.
4. Spam and lock rules from Sekiro and Monster Hunter fit the open "one button spam" problem: repeated Unleash or Hold on the same chief inside a short span loses strength, and a correct answer resets it (Sekiro's penalty clears on a successful deflect). The Trapper's holds on a chief get shorter each time down to a floor (traps minus 2 s, floor 2 s). Timing becomes the decision. LIKELY.
5. A two sided enrage after the phase two recast: for a fixed time the chief deals more and takes more (Monster Hunter's x1.1 both ways). The player chooses Fall Back to wait it out or Unleash to trade. Numbers are a GUESS.
6. Bodyguard deaths should change the chief, so kill order is a trade (Shannox, Horridon, Halfus): kill his Medic and the heals stop but he frenzies; drop his Wardens and marks land; kill the reserves and his nerve drops but his band shrinks (fewer join on capture, which DESIGN_LOCKS already ties to who is standing). LIKELY.
7. A fatigue window: after casting his spell or being parried, the chief is spent for a few seconds where Unleash hits harder; higher tiers shorten the window (Monster Hunter 60 / 50 / 40 s by rank). LIKELY.
8. Scale the 7 tiers with shorter tells and windows, two asks at once, or less telegraph detail (FFXIV unsized rings), not just more health. Wilds shows extra health only works when paired with a better tool. LIKELY.
9. Vary the phase threshold per tribe (FromSoftware runs 80%, about two thirds, about half) and add a late beat at or below a third (WoW frenzies). The 25% yield already sits inside Rise's 21 to 28% capture band. GUESS on exact values.
10. A DPS skip: if the squad drives the chief to the phase threshold fast, the two reserves arrive weaker or later (FFXIV E2). Rewards a strong build without making the fight a race. GUESS.
11. Prefer soft enrage to a hard timer: damage or nerve pressure ramps over time (Gruul style) so stalling with Hold loses, without a wipe timer at the end of a run. LIKELY.
12. Teach chief spells on the road before the fight (tribe members use small versions, the codex records answers), standing in for learning by dying, since a lost chief fight costs far more than a Sekiro retry. This also feeds the top priority tribes codex. LIKELY.
13. Encounter specific weak points, like Sekiro's firecracker against beasts: each chief takes extra from one class or mark, shown on the path map or fight card, so road choices prepare the fight. LIKELY.
14. The tribes learning your parry already behaves like a delayed attack that baits an early Hold. Keep the rule that another answer always stays available (Fall Back dodges, Focus Fire breaks the cast), the same "always a way through" that FFXIV and FromSoftware keep. LIKELY.

Does not transfer, or only partly:
- Frame tight reaction timing. Sekiro's default deflect is 0.2 s; Kindred's 0.5 s Perfect Hold is already 2.5 times wider and goes through an order, so the skill should be choosing to spend points at the right moment, not reflex.
- Per unit positioning checks (stack, spread, towers that need N specific players). Orders are squad wide, so these only work if the AI assigns positions, which moves the skill to the AI. Partial transfer: "everyone out" (Fall Back) versus "everyone together" (Hold) as two answers.
- Many player coordination such as 12 player interrupt rotations. The single player version is resource timing (points, Marksman wind up), not coordination.
- Learning by dying dozens of times. A run ending chief cannot rely on memorisation; it must be readable on first sight.
- Long hard enrages (5 to 10 minutes) do not fit fast roguelike fights.
- Turf wars, ecology, tracking a fleeing monster across a map, and item crafted captures. A rival band crashing a fight is a possible partial echo.
- Tank swaps need two tanks. With one Warden, the nearest version is a stacking debuff that forces the Guard to move to another ally.

### Gaps
- No source shows these mechanics working in an indirect control auto battler; every proposal above needs checking in the deterministic sim (bot clear rates by policy) and in Jacob's own play.
- No sourced data on telegraph lengths in action games or raids, so there is no external benchmark for the 3 s chief tell.
- Other researchers are covering roguelike and auto battler bosses; those may change which of these ideas fit best.
