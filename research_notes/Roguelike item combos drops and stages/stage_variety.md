# Stage variety in roguelikes and squad games: non battle stages, beast bosses, chores and diplomacy

Notes for the report writer. Tags: [dev] a developer statement, [fan] a fan guide site, [forum] a Steam or forum post. Untagged facts come from each game's main wiki or a press outlet. Kindred Siege context comes from `C:/Users/jacob/KindredSiege-Clean/DESIGN_LOCKS.md` (locks of 9 to 13 Oct 2026). The Fandom wikis for FTL, Hades and Slay the Spire blocked direct fetching, so some findings rest on search result summaries of those pages; this is flagged where it matters. Everything is paraphrased except one short quote.

## 1. Non battle stages and events: node types, and choices opened by a class, trait or item

### Takeaway
The proven pattern has two layers: a map of typed nodes whose type and reward you see before you commit (Slay the Spire's map, Hades' door symbols, Darkest Dungeon II's scouting), and short events where most options are open to anyone but one extra option appears only if the squad holds the right crew, trait, class or item (FTL blue options, Wildermyth's mystic choice, Slay the Spire's Wing Statue, Darkest Dungeon's item on curio). That extra option is nearly always the best one, so it pays you for the squad you built rather than testing judgement.

### Cited Findings

**Slay the Spire (Mega Crit)**
- Mechanism, unknown rooms: a ? node rolls its type when you enter. It is most likely an event, with much smaller chances of a monster fight, a shop or a treasure room. Most events are act events; shrines and one time events are rarer. From Ascension 15 some events pay worse (more HP lost, less gold, higher prices, more curses). Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Events](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Events)
- Events check run state before they can appear: Golden Idol (Act 1 only), Hypnotizing Colored Mushrooms (Act 1, floor 7 or later), The Cleric (35 gold or more), Knowing Skull (13 HP or more, Act 2), The Moai Head (HP at or below half), N'loth (2 relics or more, Act 2), The Divine Fountain (a curse in the deck). Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Events](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Events)
- Concrete build gated option, Wing Statue (Act 1): Destroy pays 50 to 80 gold but is locked unless the deck holds an Attack dealing 10 or more base damage in one hit. Multi hit cards (Twin Strike, Riddle with Holes) and cards whose damage is worked out in combat do not count. The open options are Pray (remove a card for 7 HP) and Leave. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Wing Statue](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Wing_Statue) (search summary)
- Map basics: rest sites offer Rest (heal) or Smith (upgrade a card); elites pay a relic on top of normal rewards; the wiki counts 7 map location types. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki (Fandom), map locations](https://slay-the-spire.fandom.com/wiki/File:Shop1.jpg) (search summary)
- Optional objectives priced in other nodes: the Ruby Key costs a campfire action (Recall instead of Rest or Smith), the Emerald Key needs a burning "super" elite (one chance per act), the Sapphire Key replaces a non boss chest's relic. All three before the end of Act 3 open Act 4, and Act 4 itself only unlocks after a win with Ironclad, Silent and Defect. Shacknews sums it up as fighting a tougher elite, giving up a rest and giving up a relic. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Keys](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Keys); [Shacknews](https://www.shacknews.com/article/109494/how-to-unlock-act-4-in-slay-the-spire)
- Path choice carries real stakes: the IGF profile notes that a poor choice of path up the spire can send you back to the start. Source: [Game Developer, Road to the IGF](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/disciplines/road-to-the-igf-mega-crit-games-i-slay-the-spire-i-)
- What can fail [forum]: a player argued that always taking the route with the most rest sites makes map choice boring; replies said timing still matters because hallway fights get harder through an act while elites do not. Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/646570/discussions/0/3160848559779551994)

**FTL: Faster Than Light (Subset Games)**
- Mechanism: blue options are extra event choices, shown in blue, that appear only when the ship has a specific crew race, system, weapon, drone or augment. The FTL wiki keeps a category of them. Listed examples: an Engi distress call (Engi crew), Confused Mantis (Mantis crew, at most once per sector), Rock crew options at a Rock armoured transport and in a Slug drink event, Slug crew options at the Black Raven and in a Slug Home Nebula surrender. Source: [FTL Wiki, Blue Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options); [FTL Wiki, Category: Blue Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Category:Blue_Options) (search summary)
- Some events give a named crew member, and the legendary thief event (Kazaaakpleth, a fully trained Mantis) is tied to unlocking the Mantis Cruiser. Source: [FTL Wiki, Special events crewmembers](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Special_events_crewmembers)
- Concrete example, Giant Alien Spiders [forum, fan wiki talk page]: blue options appear with a boarding drone, an anti personnel drone or the anti bio beam weapon. The beam option kills the spiders without harming the station, and the station crew pays a reward. Without one, players describe roughly even odds of a mediocre reward or a dead crew member, and a Clone Bay brings back a crew member the spiders kill. Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/616189742931234765); [FTL Wiki talk page](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Talk:Giant_Alien_Spiders/@comment-75.73.214.57-20170318143215/@comment-4139698-20180812225414)
- What worked: a 2012 review said blue options are always far better than a standard choice, usually bonus materials and sometimes a new ship unlock. Source: [Alternative Magazine Online review](https://alternativemagazineonline.co.uk/2012/11/19/game-review-ftl-faster-than-light-pc/)
- What failed [forum]: coverage is uneven. A 2024 player count found 10 crew events for Slug, 7 for Engi, 5 for Rock and only 2 each for Zoltan and Mantis. Older forum posts say Engi and Rock had the most, and that some upgrades (helm, oxygen) open no blue options at all, handing some loadouts a clear edge. Another player noted a Clone Bay makes many plain options safe, so blue is not always better. Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/4637114675688929745); [Subset forum](https://subsetgames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=76969); [Subset forum](https://subsetgames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=65693)
- Rule found by modders [forum]: an event whose only options are blue is not shown; blue options always sit beside at least one plain option. Source: [Subset forum](https://subsetgames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=44321)

**Darkest Dungeon (Red Hook)**
- Mechanism: curios are objects in rooms and corridors. A hero can touch one by hand, or you can use a provision on it; the right item usually turns a gamble into a sure reward. Curios never block the path. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- Concrete examples with wiki odds. Holy Fountain (Ruins): Holy Water always removes 20 stress, cures and heals 12 HP; by hand it is a coin flip between a smaller heal and some gold. Locked Sarcophagus (Ruins): a Skeleton Key gives 2 gold or heirloom items and 1 gold or trinket, a Shovel gives less; by hand it is 50% Bleed, 50% Blight. Dinner Cart (Warrens): Medicinal Herbs give 3 food and loot; by hand 37.5% small loot, 25% Blight, 12.5% disease, 25% nothing. Left Luggage (Weald): a Key or Antivenom gives 3 loot; by hand 50% more loot plus a journal page, 50% Blight. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- Items can start fights on purpose: a Torch on the Shambler's Altar always summons the Shambler (by hand nothing happens). Throbbing Cocoons in the Courtyard summon enemies 75% of the time when touched by hand, while a Torch on them removes 30 stress. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- Traits that take the choice away: quirks such as Curious and Compulsive (20%), Kleptomaniac (35% on treasure, and the loot vanishes), Ablutomania and Necromania (40% on fountains or bodies) force a hero to touch corridor curios by hand. The wiki calls Curious and Compulsive among the worst traits and advises curing them. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- A class valued outside combat: the Antiquarian makes curios drop extra loot, which raises gold per expedition a lot. The wiki lists no other class specific curio interactions. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- Region knowledge becomes a shopping list before the run: the expeditions guide names Shovels, Antivenom and Bandages as the main Weald provisions; guides say Holy Water and Shovels matter most in the Ruins and Medicinal Herbs in the Warrens. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Expeditions Guide](https://darkestdungeon.gamepedia.com/Expeditions_Guide); [GameSkinny provisioning guide](https://www.gameskinny.com/qpnhg/darkest-dungeon-provisioning-guide) [fan]

**Darkest Dungeon II (Red Hook)**
- Node types: The Hoarder (shop), Field Hospital (healing), The Desperate Few (travellers you can help), Creature Den, Oasis, Watchtower (reveals every later location in the region), Academic's Study and Cache, Chirurgeon's Table, and Oblivion's Rampart at the end of each region, followed by an Inn. The Lair is always scouted. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Node](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Node)
- Roads between nodes have types: Combat, Hazardous (costs 1 Armor), Rough (costs 1 Wheel), Oblivion Tear (adds 1 Loathing) and Safe. Crossing a Hazardous or Rough road without the spare part triggers a Gaunt ambush, called a Repair Battle. So the item you carry decides whether a fight happens. Debris on the road can drop loot. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Node](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Node)
- Regions roll modifiers and optional goals. Modifiers include Devious Plans (33% lower battle advantage chance) and Strategic Plan (33% higher). Goals include clearing the Lair, visiting a Creature Den, visiting 3 Academic Studies, visiting an Oasis, or avoiding the hospital or the shop; completing one pays Candles of Hope, mastery points, trinkets or stagecoach items. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Node](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Node)

**Wildermyth (Worldwalker Games)**
- Mechanism: events cast heroes into roles by personality score, then offer choices, some locked to a class. "Heart of Stone" (arriving at a hostile site): the hero role needs `max(bookish,greedy,loner,poet,snark) >= 60`; a second required role needs a high healer, leader, hothead or romantic score and must not be the hero's lover or rival; optional roles go to lovers, rivals, heroes with certain themes, or a mystic. Source: [Wildermyth Wiki, Heart of Stone](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Heart_of_Stone)
- Its three choices, all ending in the battle: leave the stone (the hero gains a random +0.5 armor, warding or bonus damage for the rest of the campaign); pry it out (the hero turns Crystalline, a transformation); or, only for a mystic, speak to the stone (the hero gains the Stoneblade Talisman, +1 spell damage, and the battle starts with Fortitude, +2 temporary health for the whole party). Source: [Wildermyth Wiki, Heart of Stone](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Heart_of_Stone)
- More casting rules: "The Eater" needs one hero at 60 hothead and one at 60 in bookish, snark, goofball or healer; "The Strider" needs `max(HOTHEAD, SNARK) >= 80` with an optional bookish volunteer at 60. Source: [Wildermyth Wiki, The Eater](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/The_Eater); [Wildermyth Wiki, The Strider](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/index.php?title=The_Strider)
- Some choices roll instead of gate: all three options in "All the Sweltering Stars" are Charisma checks, and the event editor supports choices that branch on a dice roll. Source: [All the Sweltering Stars](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/All_the_Sweltering_Stars); [Comic Editor Reference](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Comic_Editor_Reference)
- Events change heroes for good: themes (transformations) alter looks, abilities or stats; body part changes are offered at chapter intervals or forced when a hero is maimed; clashing themes are blocked. Source: [Wildermyth Wiki, Theme](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Theme)
- A 2021 review notes some events only fire when the party has heroes with certain traits, and trait strength sets the odds. Source: [Arkansas Democrat Gazette review via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/usa/arkansas-democrat-gazette/20210823/282857964001559)
- [dev, Steam] Developers said transformation events are left open by personality and class, and that because the game is procedural a party built for one event may not get it. Their Wolftouched example needs three heroes, one of them mysterious or wildheart, and most often targets a loner (a hunter first, then a warrior). Source: [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/763890/discussions/0/3720565944176045397); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/763890/discussions/0/1870622978441163088); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/763890/discussions/0/3110270484064410499)
- [dev] Origin: cofounder Nate said the team loved the stories XCOM generated but wanted far more detail about the soldiers. Source: [GameBanshee interview](https://gamebanshee.com/xykr3)
- What failed: RPGamer found the generated scenarios had a sameness that made heroes feel generic. Source: [RPGamer review](https://rpgamer.com/review/wildermyth-review/)

**Hades (Supergiant)**
- Mechanism: most chambers are fights, and each exit door shows its reward before you pick it; some chambers are non combat meetings with other characters. A run can include up to 72 chambers. Source: [Hades Wiki, Chambers and Encounters](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters) (search summary)
- Side rooms spawn on top: any normal encounter can open an extra exit, after its reward, to an Infernal Trove, a Well of Charon, a Pool of Purging, a Chaos Gate or an Infernal (Erebus) Gate. Source: [Hades Wiki, Chambers and Encounters](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters)
- Risk marking: a skull on a door's reward symbol means a harder chamber that pays more, such as double Darkness rooms, Trial of the Gods, minibosses and bosses. Source: [Hades Wiki, Chambers and Encounters](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters)
- Erebus gates are bought from the Contractor and need a minimum Heat (Tartarus 5, Asphodel 10, Elysium 15). Inside, you must clear the waves without being hit; a single hit loses the real reward. [forum] Gates whose Heat requirement the run does not meet cannot be entered. Source: [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/hades-erebus-chambers); [Prima Games encounters guide](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-campaign-encounters-guide); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1145360/discussions/2/3969311271658870734)
- Trial of the Gods: two gods offer a boon and you may take one; the spurned god sends armoured waves using their domain (Zeus lightning, Poseidon water), then forgives you and offers a boon too, so there is no lasting penalty. Source: [Hades Wiki, Boons (old revision)](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Boons?oldid=6594); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/hades/boons/z9d9c9)
- Chaos: you choose to take a curse and a blessing together. Coverage says entry costs some life and the curse lasts a set number of encounters before the blessing applies. Caution: the cost and duration details came from Hades II coverage. Source: [Prima Games encounters guide](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-campaign-encounters-guide); [Rogue Ranker (Hades II)](https://rogueranker.com/?p=11364)

**Into the Breach (Subset Games)**
- Mechanism: each mission shows its reward up front. Most pay reputation; some pay grid power for protecting a named building; some carry a Reactor Core. Clearing every mission on an island adds a bonus: one item, one pilot, or 2 grid power. Three reputation buys a core, and reputation buys grid power one for one after an island. Source: [Into the Breach Wiki, Missions](https://intothebreach.fandom.com/wiki/Missions?oldid=4691); [Cliqist tips](https://cliqist.com/2018/03/22/into-the-breach-10-tips-for-beginners/)
- Islands as regions: four corporate islands (the wiki labels them Museum, Desert, Ice and Factory) and a final Volcanic Hive, open after any two islands are saved. Each island has its own hazards: the R.S.T. desert island has a cataclysm that splits the map into a chasm and seismic activity that sinks tiles with units on them. Its Corporate HQ mission asks you to destroy the Beetle Leader or protect the Corporate Tower. Source: [Into the Breach Wiki, Islands](https://intothebreach.fandom.com/wiki/Islands); [Into the Breach Wiki, R.S.T. Corporation](https://intothebreach.fandom.com/wiki/R.S.T._Corporation?oldid=2867); [Nintendo World Report](https://www.nintendoworldreport.com/review/48016/into-the-breach-switch-review)

**Inscryption (Daniel Mullins Games)**
- Each of four regions ends in a boss with its own rule: the Prospector (Woodlands), the Angler (Wetlands), the Trapper and Trader (Snow Line), and Leshy (the Cabin). Source: [Inscryption Wiki, Act I](https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Act_I)
- The Trapper and Trader fight turns the trading node into a boss: the Trader phase works like the shop, and the creatures you do not pick go to the enemy side. Winning gives a permanent discount on pelts; bosses also offer a pick of rare cards. Source: [PC Invasion boss guide](https://pcinvasion.com/inscryption-boss-guide-leshy-angler-prospector-cabin/4)
- [fan] Node types: plain battles, totem battles, card picks (a random three, by blood cost, or by tribe), campfires that upgrade a card at a risk, Mycologists who merge two identical cards, a Wood Carver who gives totem pieces, and traders for pelts. Source: [Ludo guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/inscryption/overview/map-progression-events); [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/inscryption-map-symbols-act-1-guide)

**The Banner Saga (Stoic)**
- Travel is a resource game: the caravan carries heroes, clansmen, fighters and varl; one unit of supplies feeds about 100 people for a day; renown buys supplies and items and promotes heroes; morale sets heroes' Willpower in battle, and resting raises it. Source: [Banner Saga Wiki, Caravan](https://bannersaga.fandom.com/wiki/Caravan)
- Travel events trade those resources: in one, leaving a small camp alone gives 10 morale, 10 supplies and 5 renown, while robbing it costs morale later. Source: [TrueAchievements, Banner Saga walkthrough](https://i1.trueachievements.com/game/The-Banner-Saga/walkthrough/8); [TrueAchievements, Banner Saga 2 walkthrough](https://i2.trueachievements.com/game/The-Banner-Saga-2/walkthrough/7) (the snippet did not make clear which game)
- [forum] Keeping more fighters and varl alive makes the big army "war" battles easier and triggers some events. Source: [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/237990/discussions/0/616187839151118348)

### Inferences
- The two layer pattern works because the map makes risk and reward visible (so the player plans) and the gated option makes the squad matter outside fights (so the player values a pick). The gated option must sit beside plain options, never replace them (FTL's rule), so a squad without the key still has a real choice.
- Gated options that are strictly better (FTL blue, Wildermyth's mystic choice, an item on a curio) reward building, not judgement. Judgement needs trade offs among open options (Wing Statue: pay HP to remove a card, or leave; Heart of Stone: a small permanent stat or a transformation). The best events carry both: one or two judgement choices plus one build reward.
- Traits that force bad event outcomes (Darkest Dungeon's Curious, Kleptomaniac) are disliked. Traits should open options, not take the player's choice away.
- Event outcomes that feed the very next fight (Wildermyth's Fortitude, DD2 road ambushes, Banner Saga morale into Willpower) tie events to the core loop, so they never feel like a side game.
- Kindred Siege fit. Context from DESIGN_LOCKS.md (sprint 3 calls, 13 Oct 2026): each tribe's road is a visible map of 3 lanes and 3 rows, then the chief; every node shows what it is and what it drops; event nodes offer 2 or 3 choices that use a fighter, a gear piece or materials; taking an event means one fight less; the first row is always fights.
  - The road already copies the Slay the Spire and Hades strengths (full map, visible drops). Adding the FTL layer means each event keeps 1 or 2 plain choices and adds one that appears only if the squad has a named class, a named gear piece, or Pal favour. The check is a pure read of squad state, so it is deterministic and fits the sim rules.
  - Kindred's slice picks already include fight objectives that raise the drop tier and omens that make the run harder for more materials (DESIGN_LOCKS.md). These are the Hades Erebus gate and Heat pattern. A skull style marker on harder nodes would make "harder but pays more" readable on the map.
  - DD2's road items map to hunter ambushes: a lane could be marked as a likely hunter ambush that a Recon or a carried item avoids, exactly as a spare Armor or Wheel avoids a Gaunt ambush.
  - Wildermyth casting maps to champion duels and tribe trials: the event picks which of your fighters stars by quirk or class, and the result sets up the next fight (nerve bonus, revealed formation, an arena hazard). The locks forbid mutations and extra limbs, so Wildermyth style transformations would have to become gear or look changes.
  - Inscryption's Trapper and Trader shows a boss can reuse a node mechanic; a Kindred chief or beast could reuse an event mechanic seen earlier on the same road.

## 2. Diplomacy instead of combat: making talk a real alternative, not a skip

### Takeaway
Talk becomes a real alternative when it has its own gameplay, its own build cost and a different kind of reward or consequence: Griftlands gives it a second deck and remembers who you hurt, Shin Megami Tensei lets talk recruit, Undertale makes mercy cost EXP. It fails when it is a flat skip, a coin flip, or when building for it leaves you helpless in fights you cannot talk out of.

### Cited Findings

**Griftlands (Klei)**
- Mechanism: each character builds two decks, one for combat and one for negotiation. The negotiation deck mirrors combat with the same turn phases, basic attacks and defence; you attack the opponent's core argument, health is called resolve, and block is called composure. Source: [PC Gamer, early access preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/griftlands-complex-double-deck-building-shows-promise-in-early-access)
- Origin: Klei first showed an open world RPG at E3 2017, then rebuilt it in 2019 because the parts felt disconnected from each other and actions felt inconsequential. Source: [Wikipedia, Griftlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griftlands)
- Consequences carry the choice: sentient enemies surrender when their health falls below a panic threshold, and you can spare or kill them. Killing pays cards but makes your violence known and can turn the victim's friends against you. Characters track four relationship levels; one who loves you grants a personal boon, one who hates you a personal bane (for example, new civilians start out disliking you). A merchant you beat up sells to you at hugely inflated prices. Source: [Game Informer review](https://gameinformer.com/review/griftlands/griftlands-review-great-friends-in-low-places); [TechRaptor preview](https://techraptor.net/content/griftlands-preview)
- What failed: PC Gamer's review said negotiations reduce each interaction to plain success or failure, and that investing in the negotiation deck punishes you in the few combat encounters you cannot avoid. Source: [PC Gamer review](https://pcgamer.com/griftlands-review)

**Shin Megami Tensei series (Atlus)**
- Mechanism (Nocturne): you can talk to any enemy demon during battle. It asks for items, money (more for higher level demons) or some of your HP. Success means it joins you or hands over an item. You need a free party slot and cannot hold two of the same demon; a full moon disrupts talks. Source: [Samurai Gamers guide](https://samurai-gamers.com/shin-megami-tensei-iii-nocturne-hd-remaster/demon-negotiation-guide/) [fan]
- SMT V: demons ask ambiguous questions or propose trades; personality and moon phase matter; you can retry after a failure. Strange Journey: you pick Talk instead of fighting at the start; demons demand items, money, HP and MP, and refusing too often offends them. Source: [Prima Games](https://primagames.com/gaming/how-to-talk-to-and-negotiate-with-demons-in-smtv-vengeance); [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/11952-shin-megami-tensei-v-tips-for-demon-negotiating); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/?p=350265)

**Undertale (Toby Fox)**
- Mechanism: ACT options change an enemy's mood or reveal facts about it; once its name turns yellow, MERCY spares it and ends the fight. Enemy lines hint at how to spare each one. Killing gives EXP and LV; sparing gives neither, so the peaceful route costs power. Source: [Undertale Wiki](https://undertale.wiki/w/UNDERTALE); [Wikipedia, Undertale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undertale)

**Fallout: New Vegas (Obsidian)**
- [dev] Josh Sawyer disliked Fallout 3's percentage skill checks because of the randomness and the reloading they caused, so New Vegas used fixed skill thresholds. Source: [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/k9kac)
- [dev] At GDC Europe he said randomness used to settle a gameplay conflict frustrates players and mostly makes them reload. Source: [Game Developer, GDC Europe](https://gamedeveloper.com/design/gdc-europe-obsidian-s-five-hard-lessons-of-rpg-design)
- [dev] Obsidian had people checking that every skill got enough good content, so no build was left out. Source: [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/fallout/fallout-new-vegas-director-josh-sawyer-says-obsidian-has-karma-police-who-go-through-the-game-making-sure-every-skill-gets-enough-cool-stuff-to-do-were-not-gonna-leave-you-out-to-dry/)

**FTL: surrender and bribes**
- [forum] A badly damaged enemy ship may offer to surrender resources, an item or a crew member. Players say destroying the ship usually pays more scrap and boarding to kill the crew pays the most, so they accept surrender mainly when it offers a specific weapon, augment, fuel or crew member they need. Source: [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/412448158154713007); [Subset forum](https://subsetgames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=69723)
- A Slug crew blue option exists in a Slug Home Nebula surrender event, so crew can also change how a surrender plays out. Source: [FTL Wiki, Blue Options](https://ftl.fandom.com/wiki/Blue_Options) (search summary)

**Battle Brothers contracts (Overhype Studios)**
- [dev] Before January 2017 haggling was trivial; the developers wrote "you just ask for more money until the employer denies, and that's it". They judged a negotiation minigame not feasible and changed what you negotiate: pay is set by contract type, difficulty, your renown and a small random factor, and you bargain over terms such as the share paid in advance, pay per head on return, and flat pay. They said negotiating would be rarer but more deliberate. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #95](https://www.gamebanshee.com/kwpkr)

### Inferences
- The working levers across these games: (1) talk is its own minigame (Griftlands deck, SMT dialogue, Undertale ACT puzzles); (2) it has its own build cost (a deck, skill points, a party slot); (3) it pays a different kind of reward from fighting (allies, recruits, relationships, better terms), not the same loot; (4) the peaceful path costs something (no EXP in Undertale, items or HP paid in SMT) and so does violence (Griftlands banes); (5) the talk build is guaranteed content (Obsidian's skill checking); (6) failure leads to a worse fight, not to nothing.
- Fixed, visible thresholds beat hidden percentage rolls when players are meant to build a squad around a check (Sawyer). This also suits Kindred's lock that no hit chance is shown anywhere, only damage numbers (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 13 Oct).
- Kindred Siege fit:
  - Kindred's 17 classes include a Herald (DESIGN_LOCKS.md); by name it is the natural diplomat, but its kit was not checked. A parley node could be a nerve contest without blows, since nerve is already the win condition (a tribe gives up when its nerve bar hits zero).
  - Constraint: the locks say nobody joins during a tribe's path fights; a tribe joins only when you capture its chief (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 13 Oct). So talk on the road should not recruit. Rewards that respect this: the war party stands aside (one fight less, no gear strip), pays tribute in materials, gives intel on the chief, or softens the chief fight (fewer guards, lower starting nerve).
  - Kindred's unique mechanic, chiefs learning from your real fights, gives talk a natural trade off: a fight you talk past teaches the chief nothing about you, but you also strip no gear and bank fewer materials. That is Undertale's "mercy costs EXP" lever in Kindred's own terms.
  - FTL's surrender maps onto nerve: when a war party's nerve is almost gone, it could offer tribute to end the fight early, trading the gear strip for something targeted (materials, a hint, safe passage past a hunter lane).
  - SMT style demands fit Kindred's existing currencies: materials, a gear piece, or a fighter's health or nerve paid to pass.
  - Griftlands' warning applies directly: chief fights are always forced, so a diplomat who is useless in combat will not get picked. The Herald needs a combat role too.

### Gaps
- No Klei statement on why negotiation became a second deck was found; only reviews and the Wikipedia summary of their 2019 forum post.
- Total War and Mount and Blade talk or tribute options were not researched.
- Fallout 1's famous talk down of the final boss was not sourced.
- No data was found on how often players choose talk over combat in any of these games.

## 3. Chores and tasks that skip, soften or pay off a fight

### Takeaway
The chores that work change the world or the next fight, not just the loot: Battle Brothers contracts change settlements, XCOM 2 covert actions take soldiers away for days with an ambush risk, Darkest Dungeon II road items decide whether an ambush happens, Slay the Spire's keys cost a rest or a relic to open the last act, and Monster Train's trials and Hades' Erebus gates make an optional harder fight pay more.

### Cited Findings

**Battle Brothers (Overhype Studios)**
- [dev] Contracts change the world through settlement "situations": roaming direwolves make villagers vanish, cutting recruits and changing prices, and finishing the contract restores the village; an escorted caravan can leave a castle freshly supplied with better goods for everyone; a failed contract can see granaries burned so food gets scarce and expensive. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #95](https://www.gamebanshee.com/kwpkr)
- Update 1.1 added three late game crises (a noble war, a greenskin invasion, an undead invasion), each with its own contracts, events and changes to the world. Source: [GameBanshee](https://gamebanshee.com/kdarp)
- [dev] The event system launched with 47 events: some found while travelling, some tied to places, and some requiring brothers with certain backgrounds or traits in the company. Source: [ModDB, Dev Blog #43](https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-43-introducing-the-event-system)
- [dev] Traits come and go through play: Dev Blog #111 added traits a brother gains or loses through his experiences, background and other traits (for example a fear of greenskins after a bad defeat, which can later be overcome); 16 new traits, most with event interactions. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #111](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4hc8)
- [forum] Example of a background key: players point to a Witchhunter as useful in the Undead Hoggart event. Source: [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/365360/discussions/0/5501743491370463667)

**XCOM 2: War of the Chosen (Firaxis)**
- Covert actions send soldiers away on a timed task, and they are unavailable until it ends. Rewards include extra soldiers, scientists, engineers, intel, faction influence and bond progress; three covert actions against one Chosen reveal its stronghold and unlock the final mission against it. Source: [XCOM.com](https://xcom.com/news/xcom-2-war-of-the-chosen-covert-actions/amp); [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/xcom-2-war-chosen-how-covert-actions-what/); [Feral manual](https://support.feralinteractive.com/docs/en/xcom2warofthechosen/1.3/steam/manual)
- Risk [fan]: soldiers can come back injured, or be ambushed, which becomes a small mission where two or three soldiers must reach extraction against enemies and reinforcements. A Resistance Order can prevent ambushes, and a Covert Operators bond shortens the task by a day. Source: [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/xcom-2-war-chosen-how-covert-actions-what/)

**Darkest Dungeon II (Red Hook)**
- Road chores: spare Armor and Wheels decide whether a Hazardous or Rough road becomes a Gaunt ambush; region goals pay for visiting or avoiding certain nodes. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Node](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Node)

**Cult of the Lamb (Massive Monster)**
- [fan] Crusade maps mix fights with chore nodes: rescuing a trapped follower, prayer altars that grant gifts, shops, tarot readings, healing, and resource nodes (gold, food, lumber, stone, bones). Event (?) nodes range from loyalty boosts to stronger enemies for the rest of the run. Source: [Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/cult-of-the-lamb/all-map-symbols-in-cult-of-the-lamb-explained); [Attack of the Fanboy](https://attackofthefanboy.com/guides/all-cult-of-the-lamb-map-icons-explained/)
- [fan] Miniboss nodes: one guide says beating one lets you pick one of three rewards and recruit a new follower; guides disagree on the icon. Source: [Pro Game Guides](https://progameguides.com/cult-of-the-lamb/all-map-symbols-in-cult-of-the-lamb-explained)
- Follower quests: followers randomly ask for things (build a decoration, fetch an item from a region, marry someone, imprison someone, play at the drum circle). Finishing one gives a big loyalty boost; one guide says refusing or failing costs faith. Levelled followers give Commandment Stones that unlock doctrines. Source: [TechRaptor quest guide](https://techraptor.net/gaming/guides/cult-of-lamb-quest-guide); [PC Gamer followers guide](https://pcgamer.com/cult-of-the-lamb-followers); [Cult of the Lamb Wiki, Follower Quest](https://cult-of-the-lamb.fandom.com/wiki/Follower_Quest)

**Monster Train (Shiny Shoe)**
- Trials: before a battle you can accept a harder version of it for a better reward. [forum] Players say trials pay off in extra resources, and that a build that cannot handle a trial probably cannot handle the later fights either. Source: [SuperCheats](https://www.supercheats.com/monster-train-cheats-tips); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1102190/discussions/0/2953754543766072756/?ctp=4)

**Cross references**
- Slay the Spire's keys are chores priced in other nodes (a campfire action, a relic, a harder elite) that pay off by opening Act 4. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Keys](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Keys)
- Hades' Erebus gates are a no hit challenge for a bonus, gated by Heat. Source: [GamesRadar](https://www.gamesradar.com/hades-erebus-chambers)
- Into the Breach's building protection objectives pay grid power, and a perfect island pays a bonus. Source: [Into the Breach Wiki, Missions](https://intothebreach.fandom.com/wiki/Missions?oldid=4691)

### Inferences
- Three chore shapes recur: (a) pay now to skip or change a fight later (DD2 spare parts, Slay the Spire keys); (b) send someone away for a while for a reward, with a small risk fight (XCOM covert actions); (c) take an optional constraint inside a fight for a better drop (Monster Train trials, Erebus gates, Into the Breach objectives). Only shape (a) truly skips; (b) and (c) soften or upgrade.
- Chores feel worth doing when their result shows up on the map or in a later fight (Battle Brothers situations, Chosen strongholds), not only as currency.
- Kindred Siege fit:
  - Kindred already has shape (c): fight objectives (nobody of yours falls, win inside 40 seconds, break them with 4 still standing) raise the drop a tier (DESIGN_LOCKS.md). It already has a version of shape (a) in events that replace a fight.
  - Shape (b) fits "your roster is your lives" (DESIGN_LOCKS.md): send one fighter off on a chore for the next one or two nodes; they miss those fights, then return with materials, a scouting reveal, or a weakened chief guard. A hunter ambush on the lone fighter is the risk, like XCOM's covert action ambush.
  - Battle Brothers style world change fits the road: a chore done on row 1 or 2 edits a later node (spoil the hunters' supplies, so the hunter ambush brings fewer fighters; help the tribe's own hunt, so the chief starts with less nerve).
  - Cult of the Lamb style requests fit Pal favour nodes: a Pal asks for a specific trophy or gear piece from this road, and handing it over grants favour or a recipe.

### Gaps
- Darkest Dungeon (first game) quest types and FTL's quest beacons were not sourced.
- No sources were found on how often players skip optional chores, or on a chore system that clearly hurt a game.

## 4. Big beast and regional boss fights, and how squads fight one huge enemy

### Takeaway
A giant creature feels different from a band of people when its body is the battlefield: separate parts with their own health, armour or actions (Monster Hunter part breaks, Battle Brothers' Lindwurm and Kraken, Fire Emblem Three Houses' barrier on every tile), a rule that bends the action economy (XCOM 2's Alien Rulers), and loot tied to the parts you broke, which then crafts the counter to that beast (Battle Brothers' taxidermist, Monster Hunter carving).

### Cited Findings

**Monster Hunter (Capcom)** [forum unless stated]
- Breaking a part rolls its own loot table, and a break does not always drop that part's material (one player cites a 33% horn chance on a first Nergigante horn break). Severing a tail adds an extra carve. Blunt weapons can damage a tail but cannot cut it off. Capturing and killing draw separate reward rolls. Breaking parts can change the monster's attacks (a lost tail shortens tail sweeps), though some monsters barely change. Source: [Steam (MH World)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/1735462352481591861); [Steam (MH World)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/599644118125177559); [Steam (MH World)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/582010/discussions/0/1745594817426574132)
- In Monster Hunter Wilds the field guide groups each monster's rewards into Target, Carve, Broken Part and Wound; guide sites list part and wound drops per monster. Source: [Steam (Wilds)](https://steamcommunity.com/app/2246340/discussions/0/596270305199017279); [Push Square, Xu Wu guide](https://www.pushsquare.com/guides/monster-hunter-wilds-how-to-beat-xu-wu-weaknesses-and-rewards)

**Battle Brothers: Lindwurm, Kraken and the taxidermist (Overhype Studios)**
- [dev] The Lindwurm is the only enemy that fills two tiles, one for the upper body and one for the tail. Both share one hit point pool but move and attack separately; the tail can strike several soldiers, stagger them or knock them away. Melee hits risk an acid spray that eats armour over several turns; polearms and ranged weapons avoid it. Its blood can be bottled into acid flasks, and named Lindwurm armour resists the acid. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #98](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4prf)
- [dev] The Kraken is one of three boss type enemies, each guarding a different legendary location; its tentacles lure, grab, crush and drag soldiers into its maw. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #109](https://gamebanshee.com/k4hhp)
- [dev] Every beast has a couple of possible trophies and which one drops is random; the taxidermist turns trophies into charms, cloaks, armour plating, wardog armour, shields, antidotes and drugs. Example: a Lindwurm scale cloak makes the wearer immune to Lindwurm acid and adds armour. Source: [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #103](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k444u)

**XCOM 2: Alien Rulers (Firaxis)**
- [dev] Each Ruler takes one action after every action by any soldier. Firaxis framed this as shaping choices: if the Archon King queues its missile barrage, it lands after one soldier action, so you can move one soldier clear before bracing the rest. Source: [XCOM.com, Archon King](https://xcom.com/news/en-into-the-nest-xcom-2-s-archon-king-exposed/amp); [XCOM.com, Berserker Queen](https://xcom.com/news/en-into-the-nest-xcom-2-s-berserker-queen-exposed/amp)
- What failed and was fixed: even free actions such as reloading triggered a reaction [fan], players complained it felt like the AI cheating [forum], and War of the Chosen limited reactions to attacks or moves made inside the Ruler's line of sight. Source: [Ludo guide](https://www.ludo.guide/guide/xcom-2/dlc-content-overview/alien-rulers-alien-hunters-dlc); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/268500/discussions/0/2592234299564602359); [XCOM Wiki](https://xcom.fandom.com/wiki/File:XCOM2_BerserkerQueen2.jpg)

**Fire Emblem: Three Houses (Intelligent Systems)**
- Monsters fill several tiles, and each tile holds its own barrier with its own durability. Monsters have several health bars, can hit several units at once, and must lose every bar to die. Breaking all barriers puts the monster into a broken state where it cannot move for a while and drops items (per a Famitsu report); a barrier not broken before the monster's charged attack regenerates. Source: [Fire Emblem Wiki, Barrier](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Enclosing_Wall); [Gameranx](https://gameranx.com/features/id/178155/article/fire-emblem-three-houses-how-to-defeat-giant-monsters-battle-guide/); [Perfectly Nintendo (Famitsu)](https://www.perfectly-nintendo.com/?p=434331); [Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/fire-emblem-three-houses/demonic-beast-strategy-guide/)

**Darkest Dungeon I and II (Red Hook)**
- The Shambler can turn up in a corridor when torchlight hits zero, or be summoned by putting a Torch on its altar; the fight opens with a surprise that shuffles your heroes. [forum] The Collector is the base game's other wandering boss. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio); [TheGamer](https://www.thegamer.com/darkest-dungeon-the-shambler-boss-battle-guide/); [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Boss](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Boss)
- [forum] Swine Prince: Wilbur, a small pig, marks heroes for the Prince's bigger hits; hitting Wilbur angers the Prince, so players kill the Prince first. Source: [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/262060/discussions/0/1727575977591015582); [Gamepressure](https://www.gamepressure.com/darkestdungeon/bosses/z175ad)
- [fan] DD2 Lairs: several fights against two waves of region enemies and a very hard miniboss; each region's lair boss pays a trophy. Lair bosses include the Dreaming General (the Tangle) and the Leviathan (the Shroud). Source: [GameSkinny](https://www.gameskinny.com/tips/darkest-dungeon-2-encounter-types-guide/); [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Bosses (DD2)](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Bosses_(Darkest_Dungeon_II))

**Hades (Supergiant)**
- [fan] The Extreme Measures pact remixes bosses rank by rank: at rank 1 Megaera can appear as a support sister with changed attacks; the Hydra changes at rank 2; at rank 3 the Theseus and Asterius fight is reworked (Asterius charges much faster). Source: [Prima Games, Furies](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-boss-guide-furies-megaera-alecto-and-tisiphone); [Prima Games, Theseus and Asterius](https://primagames.com/gaming/hades-boss-guide-theseus-and-minotaur-asterius); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Hades/walkthrough/7)

**Shadow of the Colossus (Team Ico)**
- [dev] Ueda wanted the hero to face foes stronger than himself, felt weaker enemies would raise gameplay problems, and built the whole game around the image of the hero fighting a colossus. Source: [CheatCC retrospective](https://www.cheatcc.com/?p=211613); [Glitterberri, Famitsu translation](https://glitterberri.com/extended-developer-interview/making-of-the-colossi/)
- [dev] The early NICO pilot showed several riders chasing a colossus and then climbing to its weak point, and Ueda has said Battlefield 1942 first pushed him toward an online cooperative design. A squad against one colossus was the original idea. Source: [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/shadow-of-the-colossus-creator-fumito-ueda-says-battlefield-1942-inspired-him-to-pursue-an-online-co-op-design-initially/)

**Monster Train and Inscryption (preview and rule bosses)**
- [fan] Each Monster Train battle opens by previewing the enemies, the boss and a random buff the enemies gain. Source: [Gamereactor beta impressions](https://ipv6.gamereactor.eu/monster-train-beta-impressions/)
- Inscryption's regional bosses each bring a rule (see section 1). Source: [Inscryption Wiki, Act I](https://inscryption.fandom.com/wiki/Act_I)

### Inferences
- Five tools make a beast fight unlike fighting people: body parts as separate targets; parts with their own actions; a stagger or break window when a part or barrier breaks; a mechanic that punishes the usual approach (acid on melee) so other classes shine; and drops tied to the part you broke.
- Squad versus one: the squad's edge is many actions, so the beast needs many bodies (tentacles, a tail, extra tiles) or extra actions. XCOM's extra action after every soldier action was too much and was cut back to visible triggers (moves and attacks in sight). Telegraphed big hits (the Archon King's barrage after one action, FE3H's charged attack) let a squad react.
- Kindred Siege fit:
  - Each tribe already has the monster it hunts, and the Clarth's approved signature gear comes from the sand shark: jaw shield (Warden), spine rifle (Marksman), fin blades (Skirmisher), tooth net (Trapper), tooth totem staff (Medic) (DESIGN_LOCKS.md). A sand shark fight with breakable jaw, spine, fins and teeth that drop the matching material is Monster Hunter's part break plus Battle Brothers' taxidermist, and it fits the lock that Pals forge gear from what you won.
  - 5 versus 5 fit: the beast's parts can fill the enemy's five slots (for example jaw in front, fins in the middle, tail and spine behind), each part a unit that shares one pool like the Lindwurm or has its own pool like the Kraken's tentacles. Kindred already has front and back rows.
  - Class value: give each part a weakness or rule that favours certain classes, like Monster Hunter's cutting versus blunt rule or the Lindwurm's acid that favours ranged and polearm users. Example only, since Kindred class kits were not checked: fins cut by blades, the spine broken by heavy hits, the shark pinned by a Trapper's net.
  - Nerve: tribes lose by nerve. A beast could break by part instead (FE3H's barrier break stagger), with a choice like Monster Hunter's capture versus kill: drive it off for fewer parts and less risk, or kill it for every part. This echoes Kindred's own chief capture versus kill rule.
  - Placement: a Lair style node on the road (always visible, like DD2's always scouted Lair), or an optional summon with bait, like Darkest Dungeon's Torch on the Shambler's Altar.
  - Lock to respect: no riding or surfing monsters (DESIGN_LOCKS.md, 9 Oct), so Shadow of the Colossus climbing is out; weak points must be hit from the formation.

### Gaps
- No primary source on Monster Hunter part break odds; the details are player reports.
- Darkest Dungeon's Hag, Siren and Prophet mechanics, and Dead Cells boss design, were not verified.
- No developer statement on why Battle Brothers made the Lindwurm two tiles was found beyond the blog's description.

## 5. How stage variety feeds builds and makes players value different characters and items

### Takeaway
Players value a character or item for the road ahead when the road is shown in advance and the content checks for specific crew, classes, traits or items: FTL's crew races and systems open blue options, Darkest Dungeon's regions decide which provisions you buy, Battle Brothers' events check backgrounds and its beasts favour certain weapons, Wildermyth's events cast by personality. The failure is uneven coverage, where some picks never get a hook.

### Cited Findings
- FTL: blue options reward crew races and systems outside combat; a review called them always better than standard choices, sometimes unlocking a ship. [forum] The spread is uneven (Slug 10, Engi 7, Rock 5, Zoltan 2, Mantis 2 crew events by one count; helm and oxygen upgrades open none). Source: [Alternative Magazine Online review](https://alternativemagazineonline.co.uk/2012/11/19/game-review-ftl-faster-than-light-pc/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/212680/discussions/0/4637114675688929745); [Subset forum](https://subsetgames.com/forum/viewtopic.php?p=65693)
- Darkest Dungeon: the expeditions guide lists different main provisions per region (Weald: Shovels, Antivenom, Bandages), and the Antiquarian makes curios drop extra loot. Source: [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Expeditions Guide](https://darkestdungeon.gamepedia.com/Expeditions_Guide); [Darkest Dungeon Wiki, Curio](https://darkestdungeon.wiki.gg/wiki/Curio)
- Battle Brothers: events require brothers with certain backgrounds or traits; the Lindwurm's acid makes polearms and ranged weapons the safer choice; beast trophies craft gear that counters that beast. Source: [ModDB, Dev Blog #43](https://www.moddb.com/news/dev-blog-43-introducing-the-event-system); [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #98](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k4prf); [GameBanshee, Dev Blog #103](https://www.gamebanshee.com/k444u)
- Wildermyth: personality scores pick who stars in an event, and a class locked option (mystic) pays more. Source: [Wildermyth Wiki, Heart of Stone](https://wildermyth.com/wiki/Heart_of_Stone)
- Slay the Spire: the Wing Statue's paying option needs a big single hit attack in the deck; key collection is planned around rest sites, chests and burning elites. Source: [Slay the Spire Wiki, Wing Statue](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Wing_Statue); [Slay the Spire Wiki, Keys](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Keys)
- Hades: door symbols show the reward before you choose, and skull doors mark extra risk for extra pay. Source: [Hades Wiki, Chambers and Encounters](https://hades.fandom.com/wiki/Chambers_and_Encounters)
- Into the Breach: rewards are visible per mission, and a perfect island pays an item, a pilot or grid power. Source: [Into the Breach Wiki, Missions](https://intothebreach.fandom.com/wiki/Missions?oldid=4691)
- Monster Train: the battle preview shows the boss and the enemy buff, and trials test whether your build can take a harder fight. Source: [Gamereactor](https://ipv6.gamereactor.eu/monster-train-beta-impressions/); [Steam](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1102190/discussions/0/2953754543766072756/?ctp=4)
- Fallout: New Vegas: Obsidian checked that every skill got enough content, so no build was left out. Source: [PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/games/fallout/fallout-new-vegas-director-josh-sawyer-says-obsidian-has-karma-police-who-go-through-the-game-making-sure-every-skill-gets-enough-cool-stuff-to-do-were-not-gonna-leave-you-out-to-dry/)

### Inferences
- Variety only feeds builds if it is visible early enough to plan. Hades' door symbols let players steer toward the boons a build wants; Darkest Dungeon's regions turn into a provisions list. Kindred's road map already shows every node and drop; the next step is to show the region's beast and the event keys (which classes or items open which options) before the squad is picked.
- A coverage budget beats writing events one by one: list Kindred's 17 classes and give each at least one hook per region, then count. FTL's uneven spread is the warning, and Obsidian's skill checking is the fix.
- Gear from one region acting as a key in another (a shark tooth net opening a capture option on a later beast) makes items matter beyond their stat, like Battle Brothers' counter gear.
- Pattern catalogue for the report writer, with the Kindred slot each fills: blue options on events (event nodes); item on curio (region sites tied to each tribe's monster and wreck); road items that avoid ambushes (hunter ambush lanes); skull marked harder nodes and no loss challenges (fight objectives and omens, already in the locks); covert action chores (a fighter sent away for a node or two); world changing contracts (a chore that edits a later node); part break beast fights with trophy crafting (the regional beast plus Pal forging); talk as its own minigame with consequences (a Herald parley that never recruits on the road).

### Gaps
- No telemetry or developer data was found showing how much stage variety changes character or item pick rates.
- Kindred's class kits were not read, so the class to hook pairings above go by class name only.
