# How trailers sell auto battlers, tactics roguelikes, and enemies that learn (researcher 5, 16 Oct 2026)

The researcher could not watch any video; YouTube would not load. Everything about trailer content comes from written coverage, store pages and developer or editor posts. (snippet) = search summary only. Inferences are opinion.

## Showing the player's choices when fighting is automatic
Takeaway: show the decision, then its result, as one readable chain; add complexity a bit at a time; keep the HUD sparse but present; get to the hook within seconds. The auto battlers that broke out (Backpack Battles, The Bazaar, Super Auto Pets, Peglin, Slay the Spire) were mostly carried by demos and by streamers explaining choices out loud, not by trailers. Almost no written shot breakdowns of auto battler trailers exist.
- Lieu on strategy trailers: crowded screens, invisible decisions and slow change make them hard. Fixes: start with one element and add complexity; group units; hold wide shots until viewers know the pieces; punch in to close ups; show the HUD in only a few shots; colour code the sides; use a card where meaning is unclear; make hidden systems visible; end on the climax before the fight resolves. [Derek Lieu, RTS trailers](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/11/2/how-to-make-a-trailer-for-a-realtime-strategy-game)
- Tactical Breach Wizards (Lieu): the opening shows the basic loop within seconds; rewind gets its own beat (die, rewind, retry, succeed, the success shot added at the developer's suggestion); no card for rewind, instead a giant zoomed in Rewind button plus sound; HUD on for the first command then hidden; dialogue picked for being funny and fast to read. [Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/10/24/tactical-breach-wizards-gameplay-trailer)
- Show the consequence of actions, not just the action: show the switch and the door opening. [Lieu on Premortem Games](https://premortem.games/2023/01/05/want-to-make-a-great-game-trailer-show-the-consequence-of-actions/)
- Never ask people to read text and watch play in the same shot; test by deleting every card. [Lieu, 10 mistakes](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2020/9/14/10-common-indie-game-trailer-mistakes-and-how-to-fix-them)
- Backpack Battles: influencer first, gameplay focused marketing; the publisher asked influencers to explain mechanics and said visuals were not its strength; demo from June 2023 with about 38 weekly patches, peak about 18,960 concurrent; 640,000 units by April 2024. [GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-backpack-battles-sold-650k-copies)
- The Bazaar: popularity came through strategy streamers (Northernlion, Kripparian) in late 2024. [Know Your Meme](https://knowyourmeme.com/memes/subcultures/the-bazaar)
- Mechabellum: a short showcase trailer set off the hype (snippet). [GameStar](https://www.gamestar.de/videos/mechabellum,15883_138/)
- Loop Hero: Devolver's launch press used a joke (Fork Parker claimed it was called Lube Hero); 500,000 sold in week one. [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/news/10867-loop-hero-sells-500-000-copies-in-first-week-of-sale)
- Kingdom Rush Vengeance teaser: no gameplay, cartoony settings, ends on an ominous figure inviting you to join the villain's army. [Pocket Gamer](https://www.pocketgamer.com/kingdom-rush-vengeance/the-kingdom-rush-series-is-making-a-much-awaited-comeback/)
- Into the Breach: telegraphed, nonrandom attacks; Justin Ma wanted every death to feel like the player's own fault, with the reason clear. [Game Developer, 2018](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-)
- Peglin: about 66,000 wishlists a week before launch, 80,204 units in week one; demo median playtime 67 minutes. [HTMAG](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/05/04/how-peglin-made-1049413-in-1-week/)
- Small auto battlers on itch.io: complaints that battles are slow and dull to watch, asking for more player actions in combat (snippets).
- OPINION: Kindred Siege's mid fight orders can do what pure auto battlers can't: show the player's input inside the fight. Treat each order like the Tactical Breach Wizards Rewind button: zoom on it, give it a sound, cut straight to its effect. Show one squad against one tribe first, colour code the sides, punch in on the unit taking the order, then go wide. The "boring to watch" worry is about unseen decisions and slow pacing, not automation itself.

## Enemies that remember or adapt
Takeaway: the hook landed when the memory had a face, a name, a visible mark, and a line or consequence tied to one specific past event. Metal Gear Solid V sold "the enemy learned your trick" in a single beat.
- Shadow of Mordor E3 2014: orcs adapt; an enemy might taunt you for retreating; enemies who beat you gloat. [Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2014/06/e3_2014_shadow_of_mordor_details_its_incredibly_interesting_and_dynamic_nemesis_system). The live demo let the audience pick a captain; Talion died and that captain grew stronger. [GameRevolution, Jun 2014](https://www.gamerevolution.com/previews/64479-middleearth-shadow-of-mordor-preview)
- Design intent (Michael de Plater, DICE 2015): the tabletop villain who survives and returns, sports career stories and Madden's commentary, Batman villains' scars (visible scar tracking); being killed by an enemy was the obvious anchor; enemies feel like they are after you personally. [GamesBeat, Feb 2015](https://gamesbeat.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-draws-from-burnout-football-and-an-architecture-book/)
- Shadow of War: Orc Tales trailer built on spare, scar, recruit, mark [Gematsu](https://gematsu.com/2017/08/middle-earth-shadow-war-orc-tales-trailer); TV spots sold memory as comedy (an Uruk wrecks a real man's car as revenge) [GameRevolution](https://www.gamerevolution.com/news/348133-middle-earth-shadow-wars-tv-trailers-hilarious).
- Metal Gear Solid V, Gamescom 2014 demo: a tranquilliser dart bounces off a new helmet; guards wear helmets if you overuse headshots, heavier armour if you overuse body shots; the enemy learns your patterns, so you vary tactics. [Outcyders](https://www.outcyders.net/news/metal-gear-solid-v-the-phantom-pain-conference-gamescom-2014)
- Alien: Isolation: behaviour tree branches unlock to give the impression of learning; a director pulls the alien away when tension peaks [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/the-perfect-organism-the-ai-of-alien-isolation); players spread the "it learns" claim themselves, again in a 2024 revival [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/articles/newsjp/alienisolation-20240823-307508/).
- Killer Instinct Shadows: your saved Shadow fights the way you do. [Xbox Wire, 2015](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2015/05/29/games-killer-instinct-season-2-shadows/)
- Star Renegades has a Nemesis style Adversary system, but its showcase trailer led with story, not the system (snippets).
- Not checked: a WB patent on Nemesis style systems around 2021; its scope may matter for Kindred Siege and was not researched.
- OPINION: Nemesis read in seconds because the same individual came back with a name and visible scars, referred to one specific past event, changed rank as a consequence, and demos let the crowd cause the change live. The MGS V helmet is the closest template: the habit, the habit visibly failing, a one line rule. Kindred Siege's on screen why line makes the claim checkable, which is stronger and more honest than Alien's illusion. Memory can be sold as comedy, which fits the Kingdom Rush tone.

## Tools for explaining a system fast
- Text minimal; voiceover beats cards; text is fine when it is part of the game. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/business/things-every-trailer-should-do)
- Hades II cards go full screen then shrink to the top of frame for each segment so it works muted. [Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2024/5/19/hades-ii-early-access-trailer-review)
- Before and after: TBW fail, rewind, retry, succeed; MGS V headshot, then helmet; Per Aspera one building into a planet.
- First shot: the first 6 seconds are critical, 1 to 3 on TikTok. [Lieu](https://www.derek-lieu.com/blog/2022/8/1/the-first-shot-of-the-game-trailer)
- OPINION: present the why line as zoomed in game UI with a sound sting, not a trailer card. A freeze or slow beat on the parry, then on the moment the tribe waits out the Hold, would be the natural replay device (untested).

## Showing "the enemy learned your trick" in under 10 seconds (OPINION, synthesis)
1. 0 to 3 s: Hold parries the tribe's cast, with big readable feedback.
2. 3 to 5 s: the same tribe returns, same face, colours and mark; the why line pops in as zoomed UI with a sting and stays long enough to read muted.
3. 5 to 8 s: Hold again; the tribe waits, the Hold ends, the cast lands; the failure is plain.
4. 8 to 10 s (optional button): a different order answers it, or the tribe breaks on nerve and joins.
Show the trick succeeding first so the change reads as an answer to the player's skill, not a random difficulty spike. Keep all beats in the same framing. One lesson per short clip; two or three different lessons across the full trailer. The scary villain's lesson gets no joke.
