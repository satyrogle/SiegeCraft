# AI pipelines for pixel art cutscenes, key art and trailers (researcher 3, 16 Oct 2026)

Labels: [Vendor] tool maker; [Rival] competing vendor; [3rd party]; [Dev] developer or player report; [Press]; [Research]. (snippet) = search summary only. No generation tools were called; PixelLab's free docs agent (search_help) was used and is cited as "PixelLab KB". Reddit and X could not be searched.

## PixelLab, Retro Diffusion, Aseprite plugins
Takeaway: no public developer report was found of anyone making story cutscenes, key art or trailer shots with PixelLab or Retro Diffusion. Developer reports cover sprites and sprite animation only. One Pro scene image can be up to 688x384; every animation tool is capped at 256x256, so a full 480x300 scene can't be animated in one pass.
- Pro scene image [Vendor]: up to 4 labelled references plus a style image; up to 512x512 square or 688x384 at 16:9; non square canvases get the closest supported ratio. Cost on square canvases: 171 to 256 px is 1 image for 10 generations, 342 to 512 px is 1 image for 25 generations. [PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/create-sl-image-pro). The KB documents no guidance on keeping existing characters on model in a new scene and no limit on characters per scene.
- Unzoom artwork before using it as a reference; upscaled pixel art gives poor output (snippet). [PixelLab API docs](https://api.pixellab.ai/v2/docs)
- animate_image: input kept as frame 0, input max 256x256, 4 to 16 frames, cost = width x height x frames / 65,536 rounded up. animate_image_pixminimax (MiniMax H3): 4 to 40 frames, up to 256x256; neither documents a clean pixel grid or fixed palette. Pro text animation: 171 to 256 px refs give 4 frames for 25 generations. [PixelLab docs](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/tools/animate-with-text-pro)
- PixelLab admits errors accumulate over chained generations and animation needs some manual cleanup. [Rotating a character](https://www.pixellab.ai/docs/guides/rotating-a-character)
- Jonathan Yu review [Dev, Dec 2025, updated Feb 2026]: PixelLab "the one standout", style reference makes matching art easy; weak at 16x16 and below; docs out of date; frustrating to "waste" credits experimenting; sprites and animation only. [Review](https://www.jonathanyu.xyz/2025/12/31/pixellab-review-the-best-ai-tool-for-2d-pixel-art-games/)
- Retro Diffusion: animation outputs low frame sprite sheets; Pro takes up to 9 references and an input palette; most styles top out at 256 or 384 px. [Scenario KB](https://help.scenario.com/en/articles/retro-diffusion-models-the-essentials/)
- The one positive shipped precedent: The Great Rebellion used AI images only as a base for some backgrounds and props, "heavily edited" by hand, and disclosed it. [Totally Human](https://www.totallyhuman.io/blog/the-surprising-number-of-steam-games-that-use-genai)
- OPINION and arithmetic: about 2,300 generations buys roughly 92 Pro scene images at 25 each; if each approved still takes 2 to 4 tries, that is roughly 20 to 45 approved stills. Stills are the expensive part; small animated layers are cheap.

## Image to video on pixel art
Takeaway: not on their own. Every source that tried it says raw video output breaks the grid. Working pipelines use video only for motion, then redraw or snap every frame onto a grid with one shared palette and keep only a handful of frames. Motion must stay small and slow. No shipped game trailer made this way was found; showcases are single sprites.
- cueqzapper pixel-video-sprites [Dev]: sprite upscaled to 384x384, Wan 2.2 I2V A14B makes 33 frames, pick 6 or 12, redraw each with Krea 2 Turbo plus pixel LoRA at denoise 0.4, then a MiniMax H3 refiner forces an exact grid and one palette. "Shrinking the video gives mush." Speed LoRA damps motion; not every seed works; small wobbles remain; the smaller Wan 5B redesigned the character. Needs a 24 GB GPU. [GitHub](https://github.com/cueqzapper/pixel-video-sprites)
- Kling's own guide [Vendor]: use the pixel image as the start frame, prompt slow small looping motion; big camera moves blur pixel edges (snippet). [Kling](https://kling.ai/topics/animate-pixel-art-ai)
- Cleanup tools: proper-pixel-art (fixed palette, global palette GIFs) [GitHub](https://github.com/KennethJAllen/proper-pixel-art); Sprite Fusion Pixel Snapper [link](https://www.spritefusion.com/pixel-snapper); build one palette from all frames and quantise each frame to it with dithering off.
- OPINION: on a 12 GB RTX 3060, Wan 2.2 14B would need quantised models and run much slower (not tested). Safer uses: small looping ambient motion (fire, fog, cloth) and animating character layers over a still.

## Character consistency
Takeaway: reference images are the main lever everywhere (PixelLab Pro, FLUX.2, Qwen Image Edit 2511, Nano Banana Pro). Identity holds "mostly" within one call; proportions and small details drift between calls; general models break the pixel grid; chained edits pile up errors; fewer characters per generation is more reliable.
- Nano Banana Pro sprite sets [Dev, Mar 2026]: identity holds "(mostly)" across poses but proportions differ between calls; one character per sheet, two at most; 1 to 2 regenerations per sheet. [roboticape](https://roboticape.com/2026/03/07/generating-game-sprites-with-gemini-image-generation-nano-banana-pro-lessons-learned/)
- What fans catch [Press]: in the James Pond Legacy trailer (Aug 2026) fans flagged mismatched finger counts and a render that swapped a signature prop and dropped details. [Nintendo Life](https://www.nintendolife.com/news/2026/08/james-pond-publisher-denies-ai-generated-trailer-but-no-ones-buying-it)
- Failure modes in order of frequency: broken grid; drifting or vanishing signature details; proportion drift; errors over chained edits; light and shadow disagreeing across shots; hands; 3D style poses instead of sprite poses.
- OPINION: this fits the developer's own result: the general edit model lost the characters at pixel scale; PixelLab at native pixel size with 1x references kept them. Keep one or two characters per generation, then composite. Check every shot against the character sheet: silhouette, colour count, signature props.

## Steam disclosure and player reaction
Takeaway: Steam rewrote its form on 16 to 17 Jan 2026. Developers must disclose AI generated content that players consume, in the game, on the store page and, per press reports of the form, in marketing. The disclosure shows on the store page. Most 2025 and 2026 blowups came from trailers and key art.
- [GamingOnLinux, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.gamingonlinux.com/2026/01/valve-tweak-steam-ai-disclosure-form-for-developers-to-clarify-its-for-content-consumed-by-players/); [VGC, 17 Jan 2026](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/valve-has-significantly-rewritten-steams-rules-for-how-developers-much-disclose-ai-use) (first category covers content in the game, on its store page, or in marketing materials; shown in an "AI Generated Content Disclosure" section). Conflict: the public Steamworks content survey page, read 16 Oct 2026, names neither store pages nor marketing. [Steamworks](https://partner.steamgames.com/doc/gettingstarted/contentsurvey)
- SteamDB lets users filter out disclosed games (Feb 2025). [VGC](https://videogameschronicle.com/news/steamdb-now-lets-players-filter-out-games-that-use-generative-ai)
- Game Oracle study of 9,879 Steam games (Jan to Oct 2025): disclosed games got about 52.6% fewer reviews; correlation only, and a separate paper critiques the control group (snippets). [Notebookcheck](https://www.notebookcheck.net/Study-suggests-AI-is-hurting-video-game-sales.1328725.0.html)
- Bazzaz and Cooper [Research, Aug 2026]: 508,192 Steam reviews; games disclosing generative AI get lower recommendation rates; players read AI use as low developer investment. [arXiv 2608.11539](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.11539)
- Cases: Postal: Bullet Paradise cancelled a day or two after a reveal trailer drew AI allegations (Dec 2025) [VGC](https://www.videogameschronicle.com/news/postal-game-developer-shuts-down-after-its-project-was-cancelled-due-to-allegations-of-generative-ai-use); LEVEL5 apologised for careless AI use in showcase videos and promised to keep AI out of promotional videos (Sep 2026) [Automaton](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/level-5-says-some-suspected-ai-oddities-from-trailers-were-actually-human-errors-apologizes-for-fueling-doubt-with-careless-use-of-ai-in-some-of-its-videos/); James Pond Legacy trailer backlash and a denial that cracked (Aug to Sep 2026); Clair Obscur's Indie Game Awards rescinded over AI placeholder textures (Dec 2025); Larian dropped genAI for Divinity concept art after backlash; Arc Raiders sold over 4 million despite AI voice backlash (counter example).
- Pixel art cases: The Rangers In The South (players flagged intro backgrounds that made no physical sense); Project Shadowglass, a hand made game copying the viral AI dark fantasy pixel look, was suspected of AI. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Shadowglass)
- What avoids the AI look: one true pixel size and one shared palette after generation; exact design details; scenes that make physical sense with one light; less detail per pixel and sprite style poses; small slow motion; marketing in the game's real style; AI only as a base heavily edited by hand, disclosed plainly; keep WIP captures and layered files as evidence.
- OPINION: any AI pixels in Kindred Siege cutscenes, capsule, screenshots or trailer fall under disclosure. The worst risk is in marketing. A clear specific disclosure beats a denial that cracks. Kindred Siege's 80s dark fantasy pixel look overlaps the viral AI pixel trend, so even hand made shots may be accused; keep WIP evidence.
