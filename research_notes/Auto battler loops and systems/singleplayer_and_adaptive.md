# Single player roguelike auto battlers, acting during automatic combat, item combos, and enemies that adapt or hunt the player

Research notes for Kindred Siege, gathered 12 Oct 2026. Every finding carries a link. Sources marked (excerpt) were read through search engine excerpts, not opened in full, so treat their exact wording as unverified. Opinions are labelled as opinion. The shared web search budget for this session ran out partway through, so some planned lookups are listed under Gaps instead.

## 1. Which single player or roguelike auto battlers are notable, how are their runs built, where do their enemies come from, and how do they scale?

### Takeaway
The best reviewed single player relatives as of Oct 2026 are Thronefall, Monster Train 2, Loop Hero, 9 Kings, Hadean Tactics, Kingdom Rush 5, He is Coming, Despot's Game and a 2026 wave (Dwarves: Glory, Death and Loot, Legionbound, Hold The Mine, Primarch), mostly scoring 82 to 96 percent positive on Steam. They replace live opponents in four ways: async "ghosts" of real players' builds, authored boss pools served on a schedule, procedural enemy populations, and AI rival "players" that own their own decks (9 Kings), and they mostly scale difficulty with a clock or with risks the player opts into.

#### Run structures and where enemies come from
- 9 Kings: each king starts with a different deck of buildings, troops and spells, and "you can loot the rival kings who attack your lands to access cards from their unique decks". You "make peace with those whose units don't fit your playstyle, and declare war against kings with the powers and units you covet". Modes: async multiplayer against "eight rival kings", Quest Mode (beat kings to collect their crowns), Endless Mode. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2784470)
- 9 Kings (review, 23 Jun 2025, Early Access build): a run means surviving "33 years"; you "do know which King" attacks next but get no warning of his army; each won round lets you pick a new card from a random pool; some rounds offer artifacts that boost you for the rest of the run; dropping the same card on a plot merges and upgrades it; the challenge "ramps up nicely". [SteamDeckHQ review](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)
- 9 Kings economy: 9 gold at the end of every year whether or not you won the battle; the merchant sells cards for 30 gold, rising by 15 gold per card bought (excerpt). [9 Kings wiki, Gold](https://9kings.wiki.gg/wiki/Gold)
- He is Coming: "Every three days, a terrifying new minion of the Demon King begins his hunt, drawn from a pool of over 30 potential bosses." The player is told to "study each monster's strengths and unique powers, and adapt your build accordingly", and to choose tools for "the powerful enemy that hunts you today, without sacrificing your preparedness for what comes next". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490)
- He is Coming (review, 25 Nov 2025): you can preview the upcoming boss but have "limited steps to get stronger"; enemies chase you across the map at night; a stomping effect warns a strong enemy is near; you cannot flee a fight; losing ends the run; a compendium keeps boss stats for the next attempt. [Turn Based Lovers review](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- Despot's Game: a procedurally generated map of rooms and events, a floor boss, then down a level; fighting makes the army hungry and an empty food store forces you to sacrifice humans; no persistent upgrades between runs (excerpt, reviews). [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/eugene-sax/despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-review/), [Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/despots-game-review/), [But Why Tho](https://butwhytho.net/2022/10/09/review-despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-brings-rogue-lite-to-auto-battlers-pc/)
- Despot's Game PvP: after beating the Despot, "King of the Hill" pits your run winning team against other players' winning teams in fights run by the AI, with month long leaderboard seasons; "Brawl" mode puts an opponent's team in your path every 3 rooms (excerpt, reviews). The store page sells it as meeting "the unstoppable end game builds of other players". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1227280), [Destructoid beta impressions](https://www.destructoid.com/despots-game-auto-rts-battle-beta-impressions/), [IndieRanger](https://indieranger.com/review-despots-game)
- Loop Hero: the player places enemy, building and terrain cards along the hero's loop, so the player authors the enemy population. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1282730)
- Loop Hero boss and exit rules (excerpt, guides): the boss spawns on the camp tile once enough cards have been placed, tracked by a progress bar; retreating from the camp tile keeps 100% of gathered resources, retreating elsewhere keeps 60%, dying keeps 30%. One Steam post claims 50%, which conflicts with the guides. [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/loop-hero-return-to-camp-how/), [PC Gamer tips](https://www.pcgamer.com/loop-hero-tips-guide/), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1282730/discussions/0/3112522283883159725)
- The Bazaar (excerpt, guides): a run is a series of "days"; each day has shop and event turns, one fight against a PvE monster partway through, and one fight against another player's saved build at the end; you start with 20 Prestige, which works as run health, and the run ends at 10 PvP wins or 0 Prestige; matchmaking pairs you with a ghost from the same day. [Mobalytics day guide](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/day-guide), [Mobalytics, what is The Bazaar](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/what-is-the-bazaar), [Massively OP](https://massivelyop.com/?p=519575)
- The Bazaar store text: "You'll face real players during their journey", and the same paragraph promises no real time timers. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1617400)
- Super Auto Pets: Arena mode is "Chill asynchronous multiplayer without timers. Can you get 10 wins before losing all your hearts?"; Versus is a live 8 player game. Packs: standard, custom (build your own pet pool), and weekly random packs. There is no offline single player mode on the store page. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1714040)
- Backpack Battles: you "battle other players" with your backpack; modes are Unranked (no timers), Ranked ("against equal opponents, but still at your own pace"), custom rules, and live lobbies; "See what they came up with, and counter them." [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700)
- Backpack Battles wiki (last edited 20 Sep 2026): players start with 13 gold and 25 health, both rising each round (35 health in round 2); round 8 unlocks the subclass; the shop shows 5 items; rerolls cost 1 gold for the first four, then 2. [Backpack Battles wiki, Game Mechanics](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)
- Dwarves: Glory, Death and Loot: starts with two poorly equipped dwarves and grows to up to 10; "Battles play out automatically, so the real challenge happens before the fight begins"; "As your party grows stronger, enemies become more numerous and more dangerous"; permanent Gem upgrades carry between runs. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2205850)
- Legionbound: up to 50 heroes at once; Battle Mode is an endurance run; in Adventure Mode "all the meanwhile the final foe gains power", and the player chooses to face the Endbringer or "loop back to the beginning to gain more power". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360)
- Primarch: one hero, not a party; the planet's inhabitants are "split into 8 tribes", and you can "hunt down specific tribes and event locations"; after falling in battle the hero enters "Primal Mode" and spends a limited Primal resource instead of hit points. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1668970)
- Hadean Tactics: procedurally generated paths, 9 bosses, 50+ monsters, 20 Corruption (difficulty) levels, events both outside and during combat. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260590)
- Wildfrost: choose a path on a map, recruit frozen companions, a hub town that grows between runs, daily runs and challenges after beating the Frost Guardian. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1811990)
- Mechabellum: alongside its PvP modes, a Survival mode sends waves of AI enemies at solo or cooperative players. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/669330)
- Commander Quest: enemies "grow stronger with every stage", ending in "a final, decisive confrontation". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2697930)
- Super Fantasy Kingdom, Hold The Mine and Thronefall share a day and night loop: build or explore by day, defend at night (in Thronefall the king rides into the fight himself); Thronefall states "The sun only rises when they're dead" and you lose if the Castle Center falls. [Super Fantasy Kingdom](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2289750), [Hold The Mine](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3164960), [Thronefall](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150)

#### Why procedural or scheduled enemies instead of fixed ones (designer source)
- Valve's Left 4 Dead AI talk (Michael Booth, 2009) argues that "Static placement of enemies and loot hinders replayability": players memorise static locations, it "Results in 'optimal strategy' that works every time", and "Even multiple sets of manually placed triggers/scripts fails" because players learn all of them. Its answer was "Structured Unpredictability": population rules that are "Not purely random, nor deterministically uniform". [Booth, The AI Systems of Left 4 Dead (slides)](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Same talk: boss events are dealt from a shuffled set of Tank, Witch and Nothing, and "Successive repeats are not allowed"; the map designer places several possible weapon caches and the Director picks which exist, because designer placement allows "visual storytelling/intention". [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

#### Difficulty scaling patterns seen
- A clock that brings the threat: He is Coming's hunter every three days ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490)); Legionbound's final foe gaining power ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360)); 9 Kings' yearly attacks over 33 years ([SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)); FTL's rebel fleet advancing every jump ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)).
- Threat the player controls: Loop Hero's boss arrives when enough cards are placed (excerpt) ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/loop-hero-return-to-camp-how/)).
- Opponent matched to your stage: The Bazaar pairs ghosts from the same day (excerpt) ([Mobalytics](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/what-is-the-bazaar)); Backpack Battles ranked uses "equal opponents" ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700)).
- Enemies that grow with the party: Dwarves: Glory, Death and Loot ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2205850)).
- Difficulty ladders between runs: Hadean Tactics' 20 Corruption levels ([Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260590)); in Slay the Spire, Ascension 1 makes elites about 60% more common on the map ([Slay the Spire wiki, Elite](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Elite)).
- Target win rate as a design number: FTL's designers wanted a "suicide mission" and tuned around an expected 10% win rate. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)

#### Reported weaknesses
- He is Coming: an automated summary of player reviews lists excessive RNG, negative progression and "item dilution" as common complaints (weak source, machine aggregated). [Vaporlens](https://vaporlens.app/app/2824490/he_is_coming)
- He is Coming's PvP Kingmaker mode is described as hit or miss in balance, with players holding the best items dominating (opinion, review). [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- Despot's Game: one reviewer still "felt in the dark regarding the most powerful strategies" after several runs, another notes shops may never offer the newer, better items (excerpt, reviews, outlet attribution not checked). [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/eugene-sax/despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-review/), [Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/despots-game-review/)
- 9 Kings: performance drops once armies reach hundreds or thousands of soldiers, worst in Endless mode, plus some crashes (Jun 2025 Early Access build). [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)

### Inferences
- The closest structural model for a rival tribe that hunts the player is 9 Kings: rivals are AI "players" with their own decks, they attack on a schedule, you know who comes next but not with what, you can pick which ones to fight, and beating them lets you loot their deck. This maps almost one to one onto Kindred's "break the chief's nerve, recruit his survivors".
- He is Coming is the closest model for a hunter with a cadence: a named threat every N days, previewed in advance, built around one rule that breaks a common build (ignores armour, steals attack). Kindred's camp, two fights, chief rhythm already has the same beat.
- Async ghosts (Super Auto Pets, Backpack Battles, The Bazaar, Despot's King of the Hill) need a server and a player population, and in Despot's Game and He is Coming they sit beside or after the main run rather than replacing it. For a single player game with learning tribes, authored rivals plus AI decks look like the safer path.
- The Left 4 Dead argument (players memorise fixed placements and solve them) supports Kindred's plan of tribes that change with the player, but the Director's own rule set shows the change should be structured (shuffled sets, no repeats), not raw random.
- Review scores cluster at 82 to 96 percent for this niche; the weakest titles drew complaints about RNG, unclear strongest strategies and shop luck, which points at item pool clarity as a risk area for Kindred's new items.

## 2. Which auto battlers or hybrids let the player act during combat, and what did designers or reviewers say?

### Takeaway
A clear group of well reviewed hybrids give the player a few high level actions while units fight on their own: spells and attack orders (Hero's Hour), cards played during semi real time fights (Hadean Tactics, Ratropolis), row repositioning and spells (Legionbound), timed powers (Hold The Mine, 9 Kings), broad squad commands (Bad North), and a fighting king plus heroes and cooldown spells (Thronefall, Kingdom Rush). Others (Mechabellum, Despot's Game, Dwarves) deliberately keep combat hands off, and reviewers flagged "sit back and watch" as a weakness in Despot's Game.

### Cited Findings
- Hero's Hour: overworld and town are turn based, but in battle units fight on their own in real time; the player can "Cast spells and give attack orders to swing the tide of battle", or "just sit back and watch". It ships 83 spells (+18 via skills). [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1656780)
- Hadean Tactics: "Use your cards to influence the actions of your units"; units recruited from souls "automatically battle for you"; the store calls the fight system a mix of deckbuilding control with semi real time combat. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260590)
- Legionbound: "While your heroes are going to be fighting minions automatically, it doesn't mean you can't make a difference! Reposition heroes between rows, cast unlockable spells". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360)
- Hold The Mine: certain buildings give powers to use during the night defence "when the time is right". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3164960)
- 9 Kings: "your spells and odd building defenses break them down" during fights (review, Jun 2025). A third party guide says you also control castle defences such as throwing boulders (excerpt, unverified, the review does not mention boulders). [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/), [Mejoress guide](https://www.mejoress.com/?p=290869)
- Wildfrost: battles are turn based, but "cards don't attack every turn. Instead they have a 'counter' which counts down the amount of turns until they're ready to automatically make their move"; the player reads enemy counters and can buff, stall an enemy attack, or count down their own timers. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1811990)
- Ratropolis: real time deckbuilding fused with tower defence; 500+ cards of economy, building, military and skill types played while waves attack. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1108370)
- Bad North: the store says you command "the broad strokes of your defenses and monitor positioning", "your soldiers do the rest", and you give "high level commands to your soldiers who try their best to carry them out in the heat of the moment". Vikings "each have their own counters to the threats you pose"; lose a commander and "they're gone forever". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/688420)
- Bad North developers (PocketGamer.biz, 24 Jul 2018): Richard Meredith said "When you're controlling squads of units each soldier is making a decision for themselves individually" and "It's a limited amount of rules and actions", with many systems kept out of sight so new players can start fast; the team wanted units to move like people rather than turn on a dime. [PocketGamer.biz interview](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/interview/68638/indie-spotlight-plausible-concept-on-bad-north/)
- Thronefall: the player is a king who rides out and fights: "charging into battle yourself", "lead from the vanguard"; troops and towers fight around him; up to 5 perks and optional difficulty Mutators per run. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150)
- Thronefall design intent (excerpt): Grizzly Games' Paul Schnepf said the team often found strategy games too detailed, complex and time consuming, and Thronefall compresses that play into a compact form; Kingdom: New Lands was a key inspiration. [Games Capital Berlin interview](https://gamescapital.berlin/detail/interview-grizzly-games-jonas-tyroller-paul-schnepf), [berlin.de interview](https://www.berlin.de/gamescapital/interviews/paul-schnepf-easy-accessibility-is-particularly-important-to-us-1576827.en.php)
- Kingdom Rush (excerpt, fan wiki and guides, not official): heroes work as mobile towers that gain experience, and a Steam thread notes levels can be beaten without one; on the Southport level Reinforcements unlock at wave 3, and the wiki advises recalling them as soon as they recharge; Rain of Fire is saved for big waves, and on Steam and mobile it can only target the path; guides say calling a wave early gives bonus money and shortens spell cooldowns, while one Steam player argued the bonus is too small and proposed more gold for more enemies. [Kingdom Rush wiki, Southport](https://kingdomrushtd.fandom.com/wiki/Southport), [AOL tips](https://www.aol.com/news/2014-07-30-kingdom-rush-tips-and-tricks.html), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/246420/discussions/0/522730701032958961)
- SNKRX: "you control a snake of heroes that automatically attack nearby enemies", so the only live input is steering. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/915310)
- The opposite stance, no input during combat: Mechabellum sells "All strategy, no clickfest" and "Victory is determined by the speed of your wit, not your hand" (the store also mentions unlocking upgrades during a battle to counter the enemy; whether that means between rounds of a match was not checked); Despot's Game sells "Indirect combat allows you to focus heavily on tactical considerations rather than reflexes"; Dwarves says the real challenge "happens before the fight begins". [Mechabellum](https://store.steampowered.com/app/669330), [Despot's Game](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1227280), [Dwarves: Glory, Death and Loot](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2205850)
- Reviewer reaction to no input (excerpt, Despot's Game reviews, outlet attribution not checked): with no control during fights the only lever is curating gear; one reviewer complained that once the fight starts "you just sit back and watch". [GameCritics](https://gamecritics.com/eugene-sax/despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-review/), [Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/despots-game-review/), [But Why Tho](https://butwhytho.net/2022/10/09/review-despots-game-dystopian-army-builder-brings-rogue-lite-to-auto-battlers-pc/)
- Pacing evidence from Valve: "Constant, unchanging combat is fatiguing" and "Long periods of inactivity are boring"; "Unpredictable peaks and valleys of intensity" made Counter Strike compelling. [Booth slides, 2009](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

### Inferences
- Kindred's five orders (Focus Fire, Unleash, Hold, Fall Back, borrowed chief's order) sit squarely in the successful pattern: few, high level, and the units still decide the details (Bad North, Hero's Hour, Legionbound). No well reviewed hybrid found here gives direct unit control in a fight.
- Wildfrost shows how to make timing readable: every actor shows a countdown, and player actions push those countdowns. Kindred could show enemy intent or charge timers so that Hold or Unleash are answers to something visible.
- Kingdom Rush's early wave call (if the guides are right) is a template for orders that trade safety for reward. An order like Unleash could pay more when used at higher risk.
- Hands off auto battlers (Mechabellum, Despot's, Dwarves) still sell, but the one reviewer complaint found about passivity suggests in fight orders are a real point of difference for Kindred.
- Because Kindred tribes will learn to counter order habits, the order set must stay small enough that a player can read which habit got punished.

### Gaps
- No designer interview found for Hero's Hour, Hadean Tactics or Legionbound on why they added in fight actions, or on how many actions per fight players can manage.
- Mobile auto battlers with manual ultimate timing (AFK Arena style) were not researched (search budget ran out).
- Kingdom Rush details come from fan wiki and guide excerpts; official Ironhide design notes were not found, and the fandom wiki blocked direct fetching.
- 9 Kings boulder throwing is unconfirmed.

## 3. How do these games build items, relics and combos that create distinct builds?

### Takeaway
The games that produce distinct builds layer a few repeatable rule types: shared clock keywords (speed up, slow, freeze, charge, repeat), event triggers (on faint, on hurt, start of battle, when a friend is summoned), spatial adjacency, tribe or class set bonuses, merging duplicates, looting enemy decks or skills, and counter builds against a previewed threat. The best also steer the item pool so builds can come together (He is Coming raises drop rates for items related to what you equip and gives three banishes).

### Cited Findings

#### Clock keywords (The Bazaar)
- Haste makes an item's cooldown run twice as fast; Slow halves it; Freeze stops it; Flying halves incoming Slow and Freeze; Charge advances a cooldown at once; Multicast repeats an item's effect; Destroy disables an item and its passives; Crit doubles damage, heal, shield, burn and poison effects; Ammo stops an item when empty and refills after each fight (excerpt, Mobalytics glossary, Aug 2026 and Mar 2026 versions disagree on some values). [Mobalytics keywords](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/keywords-and-terms)
- Burn ticks twice per second and loses 1 each tick; Poison ticks every second; an older guide says Shield ignores Poison and halves Burn (excerpt). [Mobalytics keywords](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/keywords-and-terms)
- Enchantments change an item's role: Icy (Freeze an item for 0.5 s when used), Turbo (doubles Haste amount), Heavy (doubles Slow amount); the IllusoRay item adds the number of adjacent items of listed types to its Multicast (excerpt, BazaarDB). [BazaarDB IllusoRay](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/dp45eb9cb7i751ibc1apx66t5/IllusoRay), [BazaarDB Butter](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/5vfx8cve075h2mh0hgme8m3m0/Butter), [BazaarDB Multitool](https://www.bazaardb.gg/card/1gg8udwt0q7u1vrfo28pfha7b/Multitool)
- Combat runs on these cooldowns until one side dies; the store pitches both "one powerful item" builds and "elaborate combos". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1617400), [Mobalytics, what is The Bazaar](https://mobalytics.gg/the-bazaar/guides/what-is-the-bazaar)

#### Event triggers (Super Auto Pets)
- Triggers include Faint, Friend faints, Friend summoned, Knockout (this pet caused a faint), Level up, Sell, Buy, Start of battle (resolved in attack order, highest attack first), Start of turn, and Hurt (fires on damage, but not when a pet is fainted directly by an ability) (excerpt). [Super Auto Pets wiki, Glossary](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Glossary), [Hurt (Trigger)](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Hurt_(Trigger))
- Order matters: the Tiger repeats the ability of the nearest pet ahead of it at the Tiger's level (not for End turn or Start of turn abilities) (excerpt). [Super Auto Pets wiki, Tiger](https://superautopets.wiki.gg/wiki/Tiger)

#### Space and adjacency
- Backpack Battles: items have shapes, sizes, prices and rarities, can be combined into stronger equipment, and placement decides strength ("The smarter you place your items, the stronger you will be!"); 500+ items; 7 classes each with 5 subclasses. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2427700)
- Backpack Battles mechanics: many items have Star effects that depend on neighbouring items, and Diamond works like Star for items with two different effects; "Usually an item can only fill one star of another item, and one diamond"; Heat and Cold stacks make all items trigger 2% faster or slower per stack; items can need Stamina or Mana; items in storage do nothing until moved into the bag. [Backpack Battles wiki, Game Mechanics](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)
- 9 Kings: buildings and units sit on a grid of plots; the same card dropped on a plot merges into an upgrade; some buildings convert enemy units into spores or goblins that fight for you; the store promises "countless combinations of stacking buffs and modifiers". [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/), [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2784470)

#### Tribes, classes and set bonuses
- Primarch: 8 tribes, each with its own elements; "You gain a bonus effect when equipping multiple skills from the same tribe, and when dealing damage with the same element." [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1668970)
- Despot's Game: loot turns humans into wizards, cultists, ninjas and dozens of other classes; "With perks and class combos, there are thousands of ways to build your army." [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1227280)
- Hadean Tactics: 260+ cards, 34 units, 95+ items, 140+ skills and "45+ different alliances to mix and match"; custom heroes can combine any skill and alliance. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1260590)
- Legionbound: 42 classes, each with a hidden synergy subclass, and heroes combine into "over 800 possible Ascension heroes". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360)
- He is Coming: item sets (Woodland and Swampland) unlock by owning similarly themed items together. [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- SNKRX: combining heroes unlocks class bonuses. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/915310)

#### Combining duplicates
- He is Coming: two copies of the same item combine; Golem sites combine items into stronger ones. [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- 9 Kings: the same card merges to upgrade a plot. [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)

#### Taking power from enemies
- 9 Kings: loot cards from the rival kings who attack you, and choose war against kings whose units you covet. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2784470)
- Primarch: "Absorb, fuse, and mutate the skills of your foes to create new effects". [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1668970)

#### Counter builds against a known threat (He is Coming)
- The Razorclaw Grizzly ignores armour, so the player weighs armour against health, speed or damage; the Black Knight absorbs your attack power as his own, so armour, reflect damage (Razor Scales) and timed damage (Ruby Earrings, every other turn) beat him; a powerful magic weapon is a dilemma because it strengthens the Black Knight; the Saffron Feather turns high speed into regeneration; 350+ items. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490)
- Stats are health, attack, armour and speed; inventory starts at 8 slots and grows by 2 per boss beaten. [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)

#### Steering the pool
- He is Coming: equipped items raise the drop rate of related items; three banishes per run remove an item from future chests and graves (single player only). [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- Backpack Battles: a 5 item shop with cheap rerolls (1 gold for the first four). [Backpack Battles wiki](https://backpackbattles.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Mechanics)

#### Relic and perk baselines
- Slay the Spire: every normal elite drops one random relic plus 25 to 35 gold and a card reward. [Slay the Spire wiki, Elite](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Elite)
- Hero's Hour ships 166 artifacts, 80 unique unit abilities and 50 common hero skills; Hold The Mine ships 75+ buildings and relics; Thronefall caps perks at 5 per run. [Hero's Hour](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1656780), [Hold The Mine](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3164960), [Thronefall](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2239150)
- Wildfrost upgrades companions with "charms" and mixes elemental combinations. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1811990)

### Inferences
- A compact recipe for "more items, combos and synergies" in Kindred: (1) a few clock keywords on the fight timer (speed up, slow, freeze, charge, repeat), (2) event triggers that include Kindred's own verbs, for example "on Unleash", "while Holding", "after Fall Back", "when the Focus Fire target dies", "when a chief's nerve breaks", (3) formation adjacency (front rank and neighbour bonuses), (4) tribe count set bonuses (Primarch's tribe model fits Kindred's tribe roster), (5) merging duplicates, (6) loot from the enemy (recruited survivors and borrowed chief orders already do this).
- Item triggers keyed to orders would tie the build layer to the in fight layer, so learning tribes that counter an order habit would also pressure the build, giving the player a reason to diversify.
- He is Coming's counter build loop (preview the hunter, see its one rule, hunt items that beat it) fits a mercenary rival that adapts: if the mercenaries announce what they have learned, the player gets a concrete shopping goal.
- Pool steering (related drop weighting, banishes) is the known answer to the "item dilution" complaint as the item count grows.

### Gaps
- Teamfight Tactics' component combining, Loop Hero's tile combos and a full Backpack Battles recipe list were not collected (search budget ran out; the wiki page fetched had no recipe list).
- No data found on which synergy patterns keep players longest, or on how many items a pool can hold before builds stop forming.
- Despot's Game class list and perk rules were not collected.

## 4. Adaptive and rival enemy systems: Nemesis, the AI Director, counter adaptation, hunters, and how players perceive them

### Takeaway
Rivals were memorable when they were personal, remembered specific events, showed visible consequences (scars, promotions, taunts), and could come back after defeat; adaptation felt fair when it was telegraphed, gated behind repeated player habits, counterable, and adjusted pacing rather than secretly raising difficulty. It felt punishing when hunters interrupted constantly, kept coming after being killed, seemed to know where the player was, or when the rival system was turned into a grind (Shadow of War's endgame, removed in 2018).

### Cited Findings

#### Nemesis system: designers' own accounts
- Michael de Plater (Monolith creative director, GamesBeat, 9 Feb 2015, after his DICE Summit talk): the system "tracks every battle to a fine level of detail", and battle scars show on orcs' faces and bodies; scars came from Batman villains, who nearly all have "some sort of physical scar, and some sort of psychological scar" traceable to the hero; being "killed by an enemy" was chosen as an anchor moment players remember; Dungeons and Dragons inspired the recurring villain idea ("one guy that survived"); sports commentary in Madden inspired career tracking ("following the player's journey through Mordor is not unlike following a rookie sports player"). [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-draws-from-burnout-football-and-an-architecture-book/)
- De Plater's development postmortem, summarised by Kotaku (22 Jan 2015): "The Nemesis System started with a fairly simple idea of personal villains", then feature creep added multiple warring Uruk factions with Morale and Discipline bars and a hierarchy UI that "looked somewhat like a Christmas tree"; the team lacked "clear benchmarks or data" that features would work; playtesting pulled it back to the personal focus; Domination, followers and turning Uruks against each other survived; a climbable "Great Beast" was cut; the team struggled to explain the system's promise until players and reviewers did it for them, which led to Photo Mode for sharing stories. [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-couldve-been-way-more-1681120649)
- GDC 2018 talk by Monolith lead systems designer Chris Hoge, "Helping Players Hate (or Love) Their Nemesis": goal of orcs "you loved to hate" and "you hated to love", covering techniques for memorable orcs, exceptional moments and keeping player and orc relationships going (session description only; the talk is behind GDC Vault membership) (excerpt). [GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1025150/Helping-Players-Hate-(or-Love)), [GDC news](https://gdconf.com/news/attend-gdc-2018-behind-scenes-look-shadow-wars-nemesis-system)

#### Nemesis patent (US 10,926,179 B2)
- Title "Nemesis characters, nemesis forts, social vendettas and followers in computer games"; assignee Warner Bros Entertainment; inventors include Michael de Plater and Christopher Hoge; priority 26 Mar 2015; filed 25 Mar 2016; published 29 Sep 2016; granted 23 Feb 2021; Google estimates expiry on 11 Aug 2036 (Google labels this an assumption). [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)
- Patent Arcade (Feb 2021, excerpt): the application was rejected and revised six times before allowance on 23 Oct 2020; it says WB can maintain it "through until 2035". This conflicts with Google's 2036 estimate, likely due to term adjustment (not confirmed). As excerpted, claim 1 covers a processor controlling events between a player avatar and an NPC, detecting a predefined interaction, then changing parameters of a second NPC. [Patent Arcade](https://www.patentarcade.com/2021/02/warner-brothers-granted-patent-for-nemesis-system-from-middle-earth-video-games.html)
- Mechanisms in the patent description: a ranked hierarchy (overlord, warchiefs, captains, soldiers); soldiers promoted after notable events and when a superior is removed; dialogue chosen from past events (an NPC that fled or lost references it); "a nemesis may recover from a defeat or even death to challenge the player later in the game"; parameter changes after encounters include power level, rank, traits added or removed, battle scar appearance, role, and a "player interaction score" that rises with each encounter and raises the NPC's odds in later fights; social vendettas upload the NPC that beat one player so others can face it, with bounties; followers gain traits; forts whose defences (fire spouts, wall spikes, mines) depend on the assigned NPCs. [Google Patents](https://patents.google.com/patent/US10926179B2/en)

#### Left 4 Dead's AI Director (Valve's own slides, 2009)
- Stated goals: robust behaviour, competent human proxies, replayability, and "Generate Dramatic Game Pacing". [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Survivor intensity rises with damage taken (in proportion), being incapacitated, being pulled or pushed off a ledge, and nearby infected dying (inversely to distance); it decays toward zero over time but not while infected are actively engaging. [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Phases: Build Up (full threat until intensity crosses a peak threshold), Sustain Peak (3 to 5 more seconds), Peak Fade (minimal threat until intensity drops, waiting for a natural break), Relax (no wanderers until the team is calm, no mobs, no new specials). [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- "Boss Encounters NOT affected by adaptive pacing", because pacing suffers if they go missing and bosses are meant to change the pacing anyway. [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- "Algorithm adjusts pacing, not difficulty": "Amplitude (difficulty) is not changed, frequency (pacing) is"; and "Survivor Intensity estimation is crude, yet the resulting pacing works". [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Spawn rules: mobs of 20 to 30 every 90 to 180 seconds on Normal, 75% from behind the team; mob size grows over a duration; a Boomer hit forces a mob and resets the timer. [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Conflict: a 2008 Shacknews preview described a struggling team getting fewer spawns and a strong team meeting more hordes and bosses (excerpt), which reads as difficulty adaptation; Booth's slides say amplitude is unchanged and bosses are unaffected. Press perception and the actual rule differ. [Shacknews preview](https://shacknews.com/article/46769/left-4-dead-preview), [Booth slides](https://steamcdn-a.akamaihd.net/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)
- Booth in 2007 (excerpt, Japanese interview): when action quiets, the Director plans new attacks; when intense fighting drags on, it lets things settle. [Game*Spark](https://www.gamespark.jp/article/2007/04/10/11866.html)

#### Enemies that counter the player's habits
- Metal Gear Solid V: the "Revenge System (enemy preparedness)" adjusts difficulty to the player's methods: repeated headshots lead to helmets; being caught infiltrating at night over multiple sorties leads to night vision goggles (excerpt, fan wiki). [Metal Gear Wiki](https://metalgear.fandom.com/wiki/Revenge_System_(enemy_preparedness))
- MGSV counterplay (excerpt): map icons show the countermeasures the enemy has taken; players report sending combat units to raid supplies so enemies are not supplied with helmets, and report armour after lots of open fighting and cameras after lots of sneaking (community reports, details vary, thread attribution not checked). [Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/metal-gear-solid-v-the-phantom-pain/the-basics-6/gameplay-9/dynamic-difficulty), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/287700/discussions/0/527274698151810828)
- Alien: Isolation (Tommy Thompson, Game Developer, 20 May 2020): locker searching and small vent crawling are "only activated after the player has repeatedly escaped its clutches using these methods several times, thus providing the illusion that the creature is learning"; the alien remembers flamethrower encounters and shifts to ambushes and flanking; a director always knows the player's position but "It never tells the alien exactly where you are"; a menace gauge fills with proximity and line of sight, and at a threshold the alien retreats "backstage" into the vents; "the creature has to earn its kill". [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)
- Alien: Isolation players report it searches lockers more if you hide in them and learns to ignore distraction items (anecdote, excerpt, thread attribution not checked). [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/214490/discussions/0/1484358860944655658)
- Killer Instinct Shadows (Tommy Thompson, Game Developer, 13 Oct 2017): "The shadow system records every single piece of information in a given match, with absolutely zero data being ignored"; a parser turns inputs into 400 to 700 patterns per match; three dojo matches give basic competence; case based reasoning with nearest neighbour retrieval over 40+ metrics; the shadow usually takes its best ranked action but sometimes a lower one to bait; it shifts strategy if repeatedly punished; a behaviour must be seen several times before it enters the model; learning from human play avoids the frustration of expert AI with "perfect blocking and counter reflexes". [Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/programming/the-killer-groove-the-shadow-ai-of-killer-instinct)
- Shadows shipped as a free Season 2 update in 2015 and can be sent to fight friends or other players on Xbox Live. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killer_Instinct_(2013_video_game))

#### Hunters and pursuers inside a run
- FTL: each jump costs fuel and moves the rebel fleet forward; a beacon the fleet has taken triggers a fight with an elite rebel ship whose reward is 1 fuel (more if you have none); eight sectors of about twenty beacons, no revisits, a multi stage Rebel Flagship at the end. [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)
- He is Coming: a new hunter boss every three days from a pool of 30+, previewed, warned by a stomping effect, with night roaming enemies chasing you on the map. [Steam store text](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2824490), [Turn Based Lovers](https://turnbasedlovers.com/review/he-is-coming-review-impressions/)
- Assassin's Creed Odyssey mercenaries (excerpt): crimes raise a bounty likened to Grand Theft Auto's wanted level; higher bounties send more and higher level mercenaries, up to five; you clear a bounty by laying low, paying it, or killing the sponsor; some mercenaries are handcrafted with backstories and unique legendary items, others are generated from a pool. [Assassin's Creed wiki (Access the Animus)](https://assassinscreedwiki.accesstheanimus.com/wiki/Odyssey), [Vulkk guide](https://vulkk.com/2018/10/05/assassins-creed-odyssey-mercenaries-and-bounties-system/)
- Darkest Dungeon, The Crimson Court: the Fanatic is a wandering boss who hunts heroes bearing the crimson curse and appears mid mission as a random encounter (excerpt); a mod author claims more infected heroes raise his chance to appear (weak source). [Destructoid review](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-darkest-dungeon-the-crimson-court/), [Steam Workshop comments](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/comments/1379342046)
- Loop Hero's boss arrives once enough cards are placed (excerpt); Legionbound's final foe gains power while you choose when to face him; 9 Kings tells you which king attacks next. [Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/guides/loop-hero-return-to-camp-how/), [Legionbound](https://store.steampowered.com/app/4283360), [SteamDeckHQ](https://steamdeckhq.com/game-reviews/9-kings/)

#### How players perceive adaptation
- Ernest Adams (Game Developer summary, 14 May 2008): many players feel patronised when a game quietly eases up, "This is the number one reason to think twice about implementing DDA, especially if it can't be switched off"; "Players can learn to exploit DDA by pretending to be worse than they are", which "warps the game, and can make it actively unfair in multiplayer situations"; "DDA ruins pacing and obviates good level design". [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/feature-the-designer-s-notebook-difficulty-modes-and-dynamic-difficulty-adjustment-)
- A 2020 paper on deep player behaviour models says classic heuristic DDA often turns out to be very noticeable, for example as rubber banding (excerpt). [Utrecht University research portal](https://research-portal.uu.nl/en/publications/enemy-within-long-term-motivation-effects-of-deep-player-behavior/)
- An Aalto University master's thesis tested telling players about DDA before play versus not, and whether learning about it afterwards lowers the sense of achievement; only the abstract was reachable, so its results are unknown (excerpt). [Aalto thesis](https://aaltodoc.aalto.fi/items/940fd4e0-96c6-491a-8613-e0983008f1cf)
- Thompson frames Alien: Isolation's learning as a designed illusion rather than real learning, built on gating behaviours behind repeated player tactics. [Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/revisiting-the-ai-of-alien-isolation)

#### What made it punishing
- Assassin's Creed Odyssey player complaints (anecdotal, Steam forums, excerpt, thread attribution not checked): hunters interrupt "almost anything", seem to have "homing radar", other hunters teleport in after you fight one, and more keep coming after kills; one player proposed a delay after each kill. Some players replied the system works as intended. [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/812140/discussions/0/5532118063934204190), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/812140/discussions/0/1640912849406508196)
- Shadow of War (excerpt, multiple outlets): critics said the "Shadow Wars" endgame forced repeated fortress defences and seemed built to push paid loot chests; on 2 Apr 2018 Monolith announced removal of chest purchases, saying the option "risked undermining the heart of our game, the Nemesis System"; a 17 Jul 2018 patch removed the market and streamlined the endgame into "The Epilogue"; Game Informer argued removing purchases alone did not fix the grind (opinion). [Game Informer](https://gameinformer.com/b/news/archive/2018/04/02/middle-earth-shadow-of-war-removes-microtransactions), [PCGamesN](https://pcgamesn.com/middle-earth-shadow-of-war/shadow-of-war-removes-microtransactions-loot-boxes), [PCWorld](https://www.pcworld.com/article/3290351/gaming/middle-earth-shadow-of-war-loot-boxes-end-game.html), [TheSixthAxis](https://www.thesixthaxis.com/2018/04/03/warner-bros-are-removing-microtransactions-from-middle-earth-shadow-of-war/)
- Nemesis complexity creep (factions, morale bars, "Christmas tree" UI) moved the system away from its promise until playtests pulled it back. [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/shadow-of-mordors-nemesis-system-couldve-been-way-more-1681120649)
- FTL's difficulty drew "absurdly, cruelly difficult" (GameCritics) and "punishing at times" (Edge), as quoted by Wikipedia (opinion). [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FTL:_Faster_Than_Light)

### Inferences
- Kindred's "tribes learn to Hold when you spam Unleash" is closest to MGSV's enemy preparedness and Alien: Isolation's gated behaviours. Both suggest: change visible gear or stance the player can see (helmets, map icons), unlock a counter only after the habit repeats several times, and give the player a way to undo it (MGSV supply raids). A Kindred version could be a camp action that sabotages a tribe's learned counter.
- Killer Instinct Shadows show the "learns from you" fantasy works best when the rival does things the player actually did, needs several observations before copying, and keeps some unpredictability (baits). A mercenary tribe could literally copy the player's most used orders and formations.
- Left 4 Dead's rule "adjust pacing, not difficulty" plus "bosses unaffected" is a guard against the rubber banding perception Adams warns about: let the hunter's timing and approach adapt, while its strength follows announced, readable rules.
- Nemesis checklist for the mercenary rival: a named leader, memory of specific events shown in taunts, visible scars or trophies after each clash, promotion when it beats or escapes the player, a chance to return after defeat, and a rising "history with you" stat. Keep it personal and small; Monolith's own cuts came from faction systems and UI sprawl.
- Hunter cadence should be telegraphed (He is Coming preview, stomping warning, 9 Kings "who is next") and bounded (no endless respawns, no instant arrival after a kill), which answers the main Odyssey complaints.
- Legal caution, not legal advice: the Nemesis patent's description covers hierarchies where an interaction with one NPC changes another NPC's parameters, promotions, memory dialogue, and rivals returning after defeat. A mercenary hierarchy that promotes a new captain when the player kills one sits close to that description. A patent attorney should read the actual claims before Kindred ships such a system in the US; the patent runs to about 2035 or 2036.

### Gaps
- The full content of Hoge's 2018 GDC talk and de Plater's original Gamasutra postmortem was not available.
- The Aalto thesis results (hidden versus disclosed DDA) could not be read (403). No large study measuring how often players detect rubber banding was found.
- No auto battler with an enemy that learns the player's in fight habits across runs was found; Kindred may be first in its niche, which could not be confirmed.
- Spelunky's angry shopkeepers, Nuclear Throne's IDPD and Hades style rival systems were not researched (search budget ran out).
- MGSV details come from fan wikis and forums, not Konami documentation.

## 5. What do games give for beating rivals or hunters, and how big must it be?

### Takeaway
Games pay rival and hunter wins in five currencies: build defining access (loot the rival's deck or skills), permanent run capacity (more slots), loot scaled to the rival's grown power, rewards that scale with risk the player opted into, and access to more game (keys, crowns, leaderboards). No study was found on how big a reward must be; the strongest evidence is design practice: Slay the Spire always pays a relic for an elite, Risk of Rain 2 adds one item per player for each doubling of boss budget, and FTL pays its pursuers almost nothing on purpose because the fleet is a clock, not a prize.

### Cited Findings
- Slay the Spire: a normal elite pays a random relic, 25 to 35 gold and a card reward; once Act 4 is unlocked, one elite per act becomes a burning elite with one buff (Strength equal to act number + 1, +25% max HP, Metallicize equal to act × 2 + 2, or Regenerate equal to act × 2 + 1) and drops the Emerald Key, one of three keys needed for Act 4. [Slay the Spire wiki, Elite](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Elite), [Keys](https://slaythespire.wiki.gg/wiki/Keys)
- Risk of Rain 2, Shrine of the Mountain: each activation makes the teleporter boss event harder (more bosses, elite bosses or harder bosses; one guide says it doubles the boss director's credits) and raises the drop to players × (1 + shrines); each extra item rolls separately for a boss item (one guide says 15% per uncommon drop); players report 2 shrines meaning about 3 times the bosses; Rogue Ranker calls it the best farming tool (opinion) (excerpt). [Risk of Rain 2 wiki](https://riskofrain2.wiki.gg/wiki/Shrine_of_the_Mountain), [Rogue Ranker](https://rogueranker.com/yellow-items-ror2/), [Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/632360/discussions/0/2147594989450052091/)
- Shadow of Mordor (excerpt, guides and fan wiki): a captain who gets a Death Threat surrounds himself with bodyguards and gains +5 Power, and then has a 40% chance to drop an Epic Rune; rune level equals the Uruk's Power with bonuses (+2 for a warchief, +1 for intel gathered). So the reward grows with the rival's power, and the player can choose to make a rival stronger for a better prize. The +5 Power figure comes from the Shadow of War fan wiki and may describe either game. [Gamer Guides, Nemesis system](https://www.gamerguides.com/shadow-of-mordor/guide/miscellaneous/the-nemesis-system), [Gamer Guides, Runes](https://www.gamerguides.com/shadow-of-mordor/guide/miscellaneous/leveling-up/runes), [Shadow of War wiki, Death Threat](https://shadowofwar.fandom.com/wiki/Death_Threat)

### Inferences
- For a mercenary rival that "pays big", the precedents say the prize should be something a normal fight cannot give: its deck or units (9 Kings, Primarch), a permanent run upgrade (He is Coming's slots), or a key to more content (Slay the Spire's Emerald Key). Stats alone look weak by comparison.
- Mordor's rule (reward level equals rival power) suits a hunter that grows through the run: the longer the mercenaries hunt and learn, the bigger the payout when the player finally breaks them. This also turns their adaptation into something the player wants, not just endures.
- Risk of Rain 2's linear rule is the clearest sizing reference found: roughly doubling the danger adds one more full reward per player. A rough Kindred starting point (a guess to tune, not a finding): a mercenary win should pay at least a chief's reward, and an opted in harder version should pay about one extra chief reward per step of danger.
- FTL shows the opposite case: a pursuer that pays almost nothing works as a clock that pushes you forward. If Kindred wants the mercenaries to be desirable, being caught must not be a pure loss; the reward has to come from beating them, and it should be visible in advance (He is Coming previews the boss).
- Odyssey's player comment (annoying, but good loot) and the forum complaints together suggest rewards can offset annoyance only if the interruptions stay bounded.

### Gaps
- No controlled study or postmortem was found on how large a risk reward must be before players take it; loss aversion research was not pulled (search budget ran out).
- Darkest Dungeon's Fanatic and Collector rewards, Spelunky's shopkeeper payoff, and Odyssey's named mercenary loot list were not found.
- No data found on how often players choose optional rival fights (for example Slay the Spire burning elite or Risk of Rain 2 shrine usage rates).
