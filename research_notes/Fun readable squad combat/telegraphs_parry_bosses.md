# Telegraphs, parry systems and readable boss fights

Research notes, 25 Oct 2026, for the Hold order (timed parry in the last 0.5 s before a telegraphed hit), chief tells and the chief kneel (posture style) bar.
Source labels: (official) = developer or publisher text. (developer) = developer quoted in press or their own article. (secondhand) = press or fan summary of an interview I could not read in full. (community) = wiki, guide or forum. (search snippet) = taken from a search result summary, page not read in full. Anything I worked out myself is under Inferences.

## 1. Sekiro: deflect window, posture and vitality, deathblow, perilous kanji, teaching, why it feels good

### Takeaway
Sekiro's default deflect window is about 12 frames (0.2 s at 60 fps) per the main community wiki, with an anti mash rule that shrinks the window after every recent guard press; deflects build enemy posture, chains of deflects build more, and low vitality throttles posture regen, which is how "deflect everything" ends in a deathblow. The exceptions (thrust, sweep, grab) share one alarm (red 危 kanji plus a sound) and each needs a different answer, so the universal deflect is never the only input.

### Cited Findings

Deflect timing
- "The time window to deflect enemy attacks is actually 12 frames by default (0.2 seconds before the attack hits)" (community) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- The window "can shrink if the user has recently released the Guard button" ... "with each recent press shrinking the window further, to as little as 4 frames, even 0 frames if you spam it too fast"; "the stacking penalty is removed after a period of time (30 frames)", and the same page says it also clears immediately after a successful deflect (community) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- Pressing guard too early gives a normal block instead of a deflect; the page warns that spamming is a poor strategy but is needed against fast flurries (community) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- CONFLICT: two search engine summaries of that same Fextralife page said "30 frames by default (a full half second)" shrinking to "7 frames". Two direct reads of the live page, one asking for verbatim text, both returned 12 frames default, 4 or 0 frames minimum and a 30 frame reset. No official FromSoftware frame data was found ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- "This posture damage per Deflection is increased if the player manages to Deflect multiple attacks in a short period of time" such as a flurry (community) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- Official manual wording: deflect by "pressing the guard button the moment before a blow lands"; "Deflecting not only keeps you from being harmed by an attack, it also damages your enemy's Posture"; you may need to deflect "multiple attacks in a row before you can land a counterattack"; a good deflect can open a counter slash (official) ([Activision support, Sekiro game mechanics](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics))
- An analysis calls the deflect timing "quite generous", with a missed deflect falling back to a guard (analysis) ([Superjump, The Art and Science of Sekiro's Combat](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/))

Posture and vitality
- "An enemy's Vitality is tied to how fast his Posture recovers" and "the lower the Vitality, the slower the Posture recovery"; some enemies recover posture very fast, so the manual recommends taking their vitality down first (official) ([Activision support](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics))
- Player posture recovery by vitality, as a table: vitality 100% to 75% gives 100% recovery rate; 75% to 50% gives 66%; 50% to 25% gives 33%; 25% to 0% gives 1%. "the posture gauge will flash red if vitality damage is limiting posture regeneration." The page frames this for Sekiro (the player) and does not say enemies use the same table (community) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- "Sekiro's posture generally starts to recover slowly after not taking posture damage for about a second." "Enemy posture doesn't have this delay." Posture "cannot be regained while attacking, sprinting, grappling or being staggered." Holding guard while avoiding attacks "can greatly speed up posture recovery" (community) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- Player posture break: "he is staggered and unable to defend for a brief period"; Step Dodge or Jump shortens it; incoming attacks do "significantly more damage" while broken (community) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- Scale: Prayer Beads give up to 10 boosts of +80 vitality and +30 posture, "for a max of 1120" vitality and "a max of 420" posture (community) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- Flame Vent: a burning enemy takes damage over time and its posture is stopped "from recovering for the duration" (official blog) ([Activision blog, first few hours](https://blog.activision.com/sekiro/2019-03/Sekiro-Shadows-Die-Twice-Strategies-Surviving-Your-First-Few-Hours-as-the-One-Armed-Wolf))
- Pressure matters: "if all you do is wait for them to act you'll give them time to recover their posture" (analysis) ([Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/))

Deathblow
- "If your enemy's Posture is broken, he will momentarily become exposed to a Deathblow." "Deathblows can only be performed for a limited window, so attack quickly." (official) ([Activision support](https://support.activision.com/sekiro/articles/sekiro-shadows-die-twice-game-mechanics))
- "If player doesn't do it within a few seconds, the enemy will recover a small portion of their posture" (community, no exact seconds given) ([Fextralife, Posture](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Posture))
- Most bosses carry several red deathblow markers beside the vitality bar, each needing its own deathblow; boss vitality and posture are fully restored between deathblows; the last one is a unique finisher (community, search snippet) ([Fextralife, Bosses](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Bosses))

Perilous attacks (red 危 kanji)
- "Dealing with perilous attacks correctly is arguably one of the most important things for new players to master." "There are several types of these attacks, and it's up to the player to recognize them and deal with them correctly." Thrusts are answered by Mikiri Counter: "the Step Dodge button (in a neutral direction or towards an enemy) at the moment an enemy's thrust attack lands." Also: "you'd be surprised how many perilous attacks can be easily interrupted with a Shuriken" (official blog) ([Activision blog](https://blog.activision.com/sekiro/2019-03/Sekiro-Shadows-Die-Twice-Strategies-Surviving-Your-First-Few-Hours-as-the-One-Armed-Wolf))
- Guides list three types (thrust, sweep, grab; some add lightning): sweep is answered by jumping (a jump kick follows), grabs cannot be countered and must be avoided (dash back, or a late sideways dodge for long range homing grabs), a thrust can also be side dodged without Mikiri; players note some sweeps look like thrusts, so the animation matters more than the kanji (community, search snippet) ([Fextralife, Combat](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Combat); [GameSpew](https://www.gamespew.com/?p=94165))
- Designer reading: Sekiro's 危 symbol, paired with a sound cue, warns that an attack needs a specific counter. "Not every game needs the 危 symbol"; it works as a "crutch" when patterns are too subtle (developer article) ([Game Developer, Alex Kubodera, Designing for Difficulty: Readability in ARPGs, 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/designing-for-difficulty-readability-in-arpgs))
- Other exceptions to deflect: terror and fire attacks deal part of their damage through guards and deflects; poison, fire and terror buildup cannot be guarded or deflected and need the Umbrella; Divine Confetti lets the sword deflect apparitions' terror attacks (community) ([Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))

How the game teaches deflecting
- Hanbei the Undying "gives you an opportunity to practice the game's key mechanics without the risk of taking damage"; "It is highly recommended to complete all of his training topics"; advanced training unlocks after the first Samurai General and includes "Consecutive Deflections: In this game you're expected to deflect flurries of attacks, not just individual ones." (official blog) ([Activision blog](https://blog.activision.com/sekiro/2019-03/Sekiro-Shadows-Die-Twice-Strategies-Surviving-Your-First-Few-Hours-as-the-One-Armed-Wolf))
- "all enemy moves are telegraphed; to every action there's a specific counter that you need to perform"; bosses are "skill tests as well as tutorials"; most mini bosses kill in two hits (analysis) ([Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/))

Why it feels good (sound, sparks, designer intent)
- Players describe a good deflect as "sparks flying everywhere and a clear, crisp, clinking or clanking sound in high pitch", a mistimed one as no spark and "a dull clunking sound", and half hearted deflects as weak or no sparks with "a less satisfying clang" (community Steam posts, search snippet) ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/814380/discussions/0/4209245188602176493/?ctp=1))
- Fan wiki: "If Sekiro perfectly parries an incoming attack, the enemy loses posture; conversely, if the parry was not perfect, Sekiro will" (community, search snippet; oversimplified, since official text says deflects build enemy posture) ([Sekiro Fandom wiki](https://sekiro-shadows-die-twice.fandom.com/wiki/Unseen_Aid))
- The Genichiro fight "has a certain rhythm noticeable in the ringing song of steel against steel" (analysis) ([Superjump](https://www.superjumpmagazine.com/the-art-and-science-of-sekiros-combat/))
- Miyazaki to 4Gamer: the "kengeki" (sword clash) system is built around clashes and breaking posture and is more strategic than earlier FromSoftware action (secondhand) ([Destructoid](https://vip-develop.destructoid.com/?p=242122)); to Game Informer: offense and defense became "something much more fluid and active, I think, which was a very defining characteristic of Sekiro" (secondhand) ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/adventure/sekiro-was-a-big-turning-point-after-elden-ring-hidetaka-miyazaki-says-theres-one-more-level-we-can-crank-it-up-to/))

### Inferences
- The Hold window (0.5 s) is about 2.5 times Sekiro's 0.2 s default. A wide window can suit a squad game where the player is also reading the field, but Sekiro pairs its window with two guards that the Hold may need: early presses fall back to a weaker block, and repeated presses shrink the next window (to 4 or 0 frames) until a 30 frame quiet period or a success. Without some anti mash rule, a 0.5 s window invites holding constantly.
- A two tier window copies Sekiro's logic: early Hold inside 0.5 s = guard (less damage, some posture cost to the fighter), late Hold inside roughly the last 0.15 to 0.2 s = perfect parry (no damage, big chief posture damage, best sound and spark). The tier boundary is my estimate, built from Sekiro's 0.2 s and the E33 community claim of 0.15 s in section 7.
- The kneel bar lever that makes Sekiro work is not the bar itself but the regen rules: no regen while under pressure, slower regen at low vitality (the 100 / 66 / 33 / 1 percent table), and bonus posture damage for deflect chains. A chief bar without those rules lets the chief "breathe out" every gap.
- Bosses need several kneels with a full reset in Sekiro. For a roguelike chief, the equivalent is phase markers: each kneel ends a phase.
- Feel comes from contrast: big spark and high clang for perfect, dull thud and no spark for mistimed. The difference itself is the feedback.

### Gaps
- No official frame data for the deflect window; 12 frames is wiki data and conflicts with search summaries quoting 30.
- No numbers found for enemy posture regen per second, enemy regen delay, posture damage per deflect, or the deathblow window in seconds.
- Exact rules for "perfect" versus ordinary deflects (thresholds, posture to Wolf) are community lore, not documented.
- Primary 4Gamer and Game Informer interview texts were not read.

## 2. World of Warcraft raid design: mechanic families, cast bars and boss mods, trash, phases, philosophy

### Takeaway
Blizzard's current direction (Midnight, 2025 to 2026) is a built in timeline of upcoming boss abilities, no addon automation, fewer and standardized visual indicators, mechanics that test coordination, movement and a "core puzzle" instead of "more bullets", interrupts kept on tanks and DPS, and healing designed as a roller coaster of threat and recovery. Community critique praises trash that previews the boss (Ulduar, Omnotron) and condemns trash that teaches nothing.

### Cited Findings

Midnight UI and addon rules (official)
- "Addons should not be able to automate combat decisions for the player." (official) ([Blizzard, How Midnight's upcoming game changes will impact combat addons](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24244638/how-midnights-upcoming-game-changes-will-impact-combat-addons))
- The new Boss Warning UI "presents the player with a timeline view of upcoming boss abilities"; addons may restyle it as "a stack of countdown bars" and "add their own custom events to the timeline, such as break timers"; addons may change how an "important cast" looks on nameplates, but may not use combat information to "drive custom logic or make decisions for you" (official) ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24244638/how-midnights-upcoming-game-changes-will-impact-combat-addons))
- Built in text to speech alerts for combat start and end, health and resource callouts and target info, set under Accessibility (official) ([Blizzard](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/24244638/how-midnights-upcoming-game-changes-will-impact-combat-addons))
- Reported: top race guilds kept using their own assignment addons after the restrictions (secondhand, search snippet) ([Inven Global](https://www.invenglobal.com/articles/20392/world-of-warcraft-midnight-addon-restrictions-fail-as-top-rwf-guilds-keep-using-raid-addons))

Encounter designers, 2026 interviews (secondhand summaries)
- Joint Wowhead, Icy Veins and Raider.io interview with Lead Encounter Designers Andrew De Sousa and Taylor Sanders (summary dated 7 Aug 2026): they "wanted to cut back on some of the noise so that the things that are important can stand out"; a UI element glows when something is important and a new VFX pulses on the creature; they admitted "excessive visual effects for this mechanic" on Entombed Sentinels because they had assumed players used an addon; on Kasreth the beams change colour to tell players to act; "No plans for a proper interrupt tracker right now"; they have "taken a big pass on simplicity on trash in Mythic+" and asked whether everything needs a cast bar, so a known signal like a bolt always means the same thing, and trash uses simple shapes like a circle with a rim to spread from; "This is co-op PvE at the end of the day"; signalling failure clearly is the designers' job; they are experimenting with information asymmetry mechanics; raids get set pieces like a "Frogger hallway" and a "BWL trap room" (secondhand) ([Seramate summary, 7 Aug 2026](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/07/blizzard-interview-addon-design-1f1929d1))
- Ellesmere interview with Taylor Sanders and Drew De Sousa (summary dated 22 Aug 2026): whether a mechanic wipes instantly depends on how central it is ("they wouldn't want Halondrus bombs to be recoverable"); they prefer "allowing players to see the encounter fall apart over a few seconds"; lower difficulties use pass or fail "rules" that play out over longer periods, while Mythic is harsher because "they presume Mythic players already have a good understanding of the raid mechanics"; healing challenges are built as "roller coasters" of threat and recovery; healers were deliberately kept off interrupt duty, which goes to tanks and DPS; designers consider how long players need to recognize and respond to each mechanic; on Merektha they want players to track enemy facing and movement without an obvious marker; visual indicators "moved to a more standardized set, which they intend to be the standard for everything going forward"; targeting auras appear a few seconds before damage; "Players like pushing their buttons, so increasing those throughput checks can be fun"; an Anduin intermission sequence was cut because it "added extra phases to an already lengthy encounter"; a Painsmith Raznal idea was dropped because the fight "did not need a rotating interrupt check that got more difficult over time" (secondhand) ([Seramate summary, 22 Aug 2026](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/22/ellesmere-encounter-design-interview-1f19e39f))
- Lead encounter designer Dylan Barker (PC Gamer, via FRVR, 23 Feb 2026): "The way that gets harder from Normal to Heroic to Mythic is not 'we shoot more bullets at you'"; mechanics should "challenge players' coordination and their strategic abilities"; "puzzle and communication and coordination and strategy"; FRVR paraphrase: a move away from "dexterity mechanics", more movement, a deeper core puzzle on higher difficulty (secondhand) ([FRVR](https://frvr.com/blog/news/world-of-warcraft-raids-will-now-be-designed-to-challenge-players-coordination-and-strategic-abilities))
- Post race interview, 2026 (Taylor Sanders, Anthony Trejo): Belo'ren was designed to ramp all mechanics with a fourth wave of echoes, and a third wave of birds was cut after QA and UX testing as "too much of a challenge" (search snippet) ([Wowhead liveblog, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.wowhead.com/it/news/liquid-maximum-post-race-encounter-design-interview-liveblog-381240))
- Morgan Day, 2017: "Mechanically where it makes sense, we do mythic only phases. Not a requirement."; designers "usually start with Heroic difficulty. That's the middle ground." (search snippet) ([Wowhead, Tomb of Sargeras liveblog](https://ko.wowhead.com/news/tomb-of-sargeras-developer-interview-liveblog-265181))
- 2024: Hazzikostas tied the Private Auras tool to limiting addon computation so encounters leave room for coordination and decisions, and Morgan Day framed it as reclaiming design space (search snippet, article body not retrievable) ([Wowhead, 2024 interview](https://wowhead.com/pt/news/wowhead-exclusive-interview-with-ion-hazzikostas-and-morgan-day-private-auras-343147)); he also described an "arms race" between encounter design and addons, where mechanics get added that feel overwhelming without addons (search snippet, page blocked) ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/blizzard-plans-to-reduce-add-on-reliance-in-future-raids/))

Mechanic vocabulary (community definitions)
- Enrage: "Many bosses have a hard enrage timer to limit the possible length of the encounter, while others may have a soft enrage that gradually increases tank and healer stress" (community wiki, search snippet) ([Warcraft Wiki, Enrage](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Enrage_timer)); a hard timer is "the upper limit of time you have to kill the boss or everyone in the raid will die"; a soft enrage is "a building, scaling area effect damage" (press glossary, 2011, search snippet) ([Engadget, raid lexicon](https://www.engadget.com/2011-06-16-wow-rookie-a-raid-rookies-lexicon-of-raiding-language.html))
- Tank swap: a stacking debuff stops the main tank, so the other tank taunts; largely introduced late in Wrath of the Lich King and formalized in Cataclysm (community wiki, search snippet) ([Warcraft Wiki, Tank swap](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Tank_swap)); encounters came to require exactly two tanks with rare exceptions (opinion column) ([BlizzardWatch, 2015](https://blizzardwatch.com/2015/05/05/lightsworn-wow-tanking/))
- Interrupts and cast bars: "if a cast bar doesn't have a shield on it, it can and probably should be interrupted" (community guide, search snippet) ([Wowhead Classic tanking guide](https://cn.classic.wowhead.com/tanking-guide))
- Ground markings players learn: a circle with a central plume means soak, an unfilled circle means spread, a filled circle means get out (player forum convention, search snippet) ([Blizzard forums, Patchwerk fight](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/patchwerk-fight/2045612?page=2))
- Tank busters: no definition found; closest is a guide labelling a boss hit a "Tank Ability" that needs defensives managed properly (community guide, search snippet) ([Method, Raszageth](https://www.method.gg/guides/vault-of-the-incarnates/raszageth-the-storm-eater-heroic))

Trash that teaches the boss
- Omnotron Defense System trash is "superior in that it is educational in nature"; Ulduar trash "often taught you what to expect in upcoming fights" and was never harder than the bosses; Halfus trash is "excruciatingly tedious" and "They teach you nothing at all about the Halfus fight"; respawns that are too fast eat the time needed "to learn a boss's full suite of mechanics" (opinion column, 2011) ([Engadget, Taking out the trash](https://www.engadget.com/2011-01-26-taking-out-the-trash.html))
- Guild Wars 2 raids skip trash and loosely teach the final boss's mechanics through three earlier fights (press impressions, search snippet) ([Ten Ton Hammer](https://www.tentonhammer.com/articles/guild-wars-2-raid-hands-on-impressions))
- A raid practice addon pitches that "most of learning a mechanic is learning what the half-second before it looks like" (third party marketing text, low authority) ([CurseForge, Gearpath Raid Studio](https://www.curseforge.com/wow/addons/gearpath-raid-studio))

Answer variety through class kits
- "Bring the player, not the class" was the Wrath raid motto: encounters built so no single class was mandatory, buffs spread across classes; Ghostcrawler said it did not make class irrelevant (press, 2009) ([Engadget, 2009](https://www.engadget.com/2009-02-27-ghostcrawler-on-the-success-of-bring-the-player-not-the-class.html)); Hazzikostas later admitted it went too far and made classes too similar, and since Warlords each class gets tools no other class has (secondhand) ([mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/wow-legion-bring-the-player-not-the-class-ging-zu-weit,121105/))

### Inferences
- The Midnight boss timeline is the real time cousin of Slay the Spire intents: a visible queue of upcoming chief abilities lets the player plan Holds and other orders instead of reacting blind.
- A cast bar with or without a shield is a two state exception flag, the same job Sekiro's kanji does. Kindred Siege chiefs can use one bar style for "interruptible" and one for "must be answered another way".
- Mapping the WoW families onto chief moves: tank buster = one heavy hit on one fighter, answered by that fighter's Hold; interrupt = a cast bar cut by the right order; soak = send fighters into a marked zone; spread = move apart; adds = target switch; phases = each kneel ends a phase; enrage = a throughput clock where the chief hits harder over time.
- Blizzard's 2026 cuts are useful rules of thumb: do not add intermissions that lengthen an already long fight; avoid a single check that rotates and escalates forever; let a failure play out over a few seconds so the player sees why.
- Roguelike trash before a chief can be the teaching layer: a minion that uses a weaker copy of the chief's signature tell (my reading of the Ulduar and Omnotron praise).

### Gaps
- No official Blizzard glossary for tank buster, soak or spread was found; definitions are community.
- I found no Blizzard statement that trash is deliberately built to foreshadow boss abilities; the support is community critique.
- The 2024 Private Auras interview, the Icy Veins addon reliance piece and the 2026 Wowhead liveblogs could not be read in full; the 2026 points rely on Seramate and FRVR summaries.
- A quote attributed to Hazzikostas that the goal is for "all players can enter combat with the same level of tools" appeared only in a search summary and was not verified.
- No numbers found for how long WoW telegraphs last by mechanic type, beyond "a few seconds" for targeting auras.

## 3. Telegraph length, one threat versus many, readability rules (Hollow Knight, Hades, Elden Ring, Cuphead, Furi, Hi-Fi Rush, Monster Hunter and others)

### Takeaway
The hard floor is human reaction: about 250 to 265 ms (about 16 frames at 60 fps) for one obvious stimulus, slower (about 380 ms in one informal test) when choosing between responses, and only if the tell is visible early rather than hidden in "startup". Across games the rules converge: one consistent visual language, damage that matches how dangerous a move looks, teach patterns one at a time before mixing them, cap how many enemies can attack at once (Doom's tokens), and make harder content change patterns rather than add bullets (Furi, Hades, WoW).

### Cited Findings

Reaction time and how long a tell must be
- Average simple button reaction "around 265 milliseconds", which "converts to approximately 16 frames"; "it is possible to react to moves that have 16 frames of startup or more quite consistently" under single stimulus conditions; a 15 frame move is "virtually unreactable"; a 19 frame overhead is reactable but near the limit; a 27 frame move is easy when it is the only threat; jumps (about 45 frames) are reactable even for beginners; if "the first 20 frames has very little movement, then it will be impossible to react" even to a 30 frame move; "moves that have an obvious visual or aural windup tell are easier to react to"; "You are almost never reacting only to one stimulus in a fighting game" (community expert guide) ([Infil, On Reaction Times](https://ki.infil.net/reaction.html))
- A 254 ms median from the same online benchmark (community, search snippet) ([Dustloop forum](https://www.forums.dustloop.com/forums/topic/10626-ability-to-react-to-overheads/)); about 380 ms when recognising one of two possible stimuli, with display latency (TV versus monitor) skewing results (informal test, search snippet) ([Virtua Fighter forum](https://host.virtuafighter.com/threads/reaction-time-tests-on-different-games-and-setups.22576))

Readability rules from designers
- Telegraph tools are "large gestures, color-coded attacks, and voice and sound cues"; voice lines can mark phase changes (Morgott's line, after which his pattern is fixed); visual language must stay consistent, so a glowing red attack always has the same outcome whatever the weapon; "the relative damage numbers match the perceived danger"; players need "enough time and information to chunk patterns"; windows of opportunity should be proportional to the wait before them; add a second enemy only after each pattern is learned, because "It's much harder to chunk two different patterns at the same time"; a cloned boss shares one moveset so the player learns one set from two sources (developer article; Kubodera worked on Death's Gambit and says it expands a GDC talk) ([Game Developer, Kubodera 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/designing-for-difficulty-readability-in-arpgs))
- Doom (2016) limits simultaneous attacks with tokens: "each type of attack ... has a limited number of tokens available at a given point in time"; a demon must "request a token to make their attack, then release the token back to the system after use"; token counts vary by difficulty; demons "can actually steal tokens from one another"; no new attack starts during a glory kill; "the focus is for demons to be immediately distinct and consistent in their behaviour"; "There's simply no time for fancy and complex behaviours" (AI analysis article) ([Game Developer, Cyber Demons: The AI of DOOM 2016](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/cyber-demons-the-ai-of-doom-2016-))
- Rocksteady on Arkham City: added "simultaneous counters so that Batman can fend off two, three enemies at once" and enemy combo attacks; "its strength is that it focuses on selecting the right move, not long combos or complicated sequences" (developer Q&A, search snippet) ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/the-improvements-to-batman-arkham-citys-combat-system/))
- Furi (creative director Emeric Thoa): "only four buttons used and no real combo"; "It's all about timing, reflex and positioning"; "Controls, signs, and feedback" are "the key to all these games"; enemies "must have very legible attacks and feedbacks when getting hit" (developer) ([Game Wisdom, 2016](https://game-wisdom.com/guest/furi)); the hard mode Furier: "we didn't just increase the damage in this mode, we changed the combat patterns as well" (developer) ([Push Square, 2016](https://www.pushsquare.com/news/2016/03/interview_a_chat_about_furi_one_of_ps4s_most_promising_action_games)); reviews say a parryable melee swing is signposted by a white flash on the weapon and call the parry the coolest and most finicky mechanic (reviews, search snippet) ([Hardcore Gamer](https://hardcoregamer.com/reviews/review-furi/215606/); [God is a Geek](https://godisageek.com/reviews/furi-review/))
- Monster Hunter (Kaname Fujioka): early monsters have "relatively simple attacks, easy tells"; "We need a framework for what kind of monster is going to be needed at each stage of the game"; "What wouldn't be appropriate at the start of the game is a monster that needs you to pull out every tool in your deck"; "Gradually, the player will build up the amount of things that they've learned"; later monsters may need traps prepared; the hammer cannot guard, and the fix is other options rather than adding a guard (developer) ([Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/the-developers-of-monster-hunter-explain-what-its-like-to-build-monsters))
- Hollow Knight versus Silksong (William Pellen): original bosses were "much more reactionary", choosing attacks from the player's position; Silksong bosses were made more proactive (secondhand, syndicated from an ACMI guidebook interview, search snippet) ([Military.com syndication](https://mst.military.com/off-duty/games/silksong-devs-explain-how-bosses-were-designed-differently-those-hollow-knight.html))
- Hades: a review found enemy attacks well telegraphed except some smaller foes (review, search snippet) ([Gaming Trend](https://gamingtrend.com/one-does-not-simply-walk-out-of-hades-hades-review/)); God Mode starts at 20% damage resistance, adds 2% per death, caps at 80% (30 deaths) (guides, search snippet) ([Can I Play That](https://caniplaythat.com/2021/08/11/hades-god-mode-explained-by-supergiant-games); [Dexerto, Hades II](https://www.dexerto.com/gaming/how-to-make-hades-2-easier-god-mode-explained-2695050/)); the Extreme Measures pact gives each region's boss new moves for repeat runs (Furies, Bone Hydra arena changes, faster Asterius charge and Theseus chariot bombing runs, new Hades moves); guides disagree on 3 or 4 ranks (guides, search snippet) ([RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/feature/10287-hades-pact-of-punishment-heat-modifiers-and-how-to-maximize-your-rewards); [TrueAchievements](https://www.trueachievements.com/game/Hades/walkthrough/7))
- Elden Ring: delayed attacks became a meme; earlier FromSoftware games had them "but the number of them in Elden Ring is considerably high" (press) ([Automaton, 2022](https://automaton-media.com/en/news/20220316-10577/)); players complain of "excessive delay without a reasonable telegraph" and contrast Sekiro's "reliable and consistent fallback" of guarding almost any attack (community, search snippet) ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1245620/discussions/0/3719440044270966891))
- Cuphead: Chad Moldenhauer said a boss health bar clashed with the 1930s style and could push players to optimise DPS and miss important visual cues; the death screen shows progress and phase changes instead (secondhand, search snippet) ([Multiplayer.it](https://multiplayer.it/notizie/cuphead-perche-i-boss-non-hanno-la-barra-della-salute-la-risposta-degli-autori.html)); fights are designed from how they play first, using boss size and screen position (developer, search snippet) ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/cuphead-developers-talk-cut-bosses-and-whats-in-the-cup-1820126327)); bosses range from three forms to five phase marathons (review, search snippet) ([Gameblog](https://www.gameblog.fr/tests/2860-test-de-cuphead-un-chef-d-oeuvre-comme-on-n-en-fait-plus)); Glumstone's first phase attacks were proposed and shelved through "tons of back and forth" (feature, search snippet) ([Game Informer, 2022](https://www.gameinformer.com/feature/2022/11/30/building-a-boss))
- Hi-Fi Rush (director John Johanas via MinnMax): your hits always land on the beat; unlike Crypt of the NecroDancer it does not punish off beat play, and timing only changes rewards (secondhand, search snippet) ([Twinfinite](https://twinfinite.net/news/hi-fi-rush-had-an-internal-cult-following-at-bethesda-before-it-was-even-pitched/)); a missed beat's "only punishment is a break in your flow and a ding to your score" (press, search snippet) ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/hi-fi-rush-gameplay-impressions)); enemy attacks are telegraphed and match the soundtrack, and some fights ask you to parry a rhythm sequence on beat (reviews, search snippet) ([VGC review](https://videogameschronicle.com/review/hi-fi-rush))
- God of War Ragnarök ring colours: red = unblockable, dodge; yellow = parryable, perfect parry gives a gong sound and a stagger; blue = interrupt with a shield bash per most guides (one guide calls it a quick parry) (guides, search snippet; sources disagree on blue) ([Push Square](https://www.pushsquare.com/guides/god-of-war-ragnarok-how-to-parry); [PlayerAssist](https://playerassist.com/meaning-colored-ring-attacks-god-war-ragnarok/); [GGRecon](https://www.ggrecon.com/guides/god-of-war-ragnarok-how-to-parry/))

### Inferences
- With a 0.5 s Hold window, a tell that first becomes readable at the start of the window leaves about 235 ms of slack for one obvious threat (0.5 s minus 265 ms), about 120 ms if the player must pick between two responses (0.5 s minus 380 ms), and nothing once several fighters could be targeted. So the tell should start well before the window. My estimate: 0.8 to 1.5 s of readable windup for a normal chief move, with the 0.5 s window at the end. This is built from Infil's 27 to 45 frame "easy" examples, not from any published rule.
- Keep windup length consistent per move family (Kubodera's consistency rule). Two moves that look the same must time the same.
- Cap overlapping threats with a token rule like Doom's: one parryable chief threat in flight at a time, minions take turns. Into the Breach's "one more enemy" warning (section 5) says stacking is very sensitive.
- Harder loops should add patterns and combinations, not damage (Furi, Hades Extreme Measures, Barker's "not more bullets").
- Hi-Fi Rush's lesson for the Hold: a late or early Hold should still do something (block), with the timing deciding the bonus, so the system rewards rather than punishes.
- Cuphead's health bar reasoning suggests the kneel bar should sit on or near the chief, so watching it does not pull the eye off tells.

### Gaps
- No published tell durations in ms or frames were found for Hollow Knight, Hades, Elden Ring, Cuphead, Monster Hunter or Hi-Fi Rush.
- The Game Maker's Toolkit Cuphead transcript was blocked (403), so its pattern and telegraph analysis is missing.
- No Supergiant statement on telegraph design found. Doom token counts per difficulty are not given. Furi's parry window and health refund were not found in a primary source.

## 4. Devil May Cry style meter

### Takeaway
DMC ranks (D, C, B, A, S, SS, SSS, named Dismal, Crazy, Badass, Apocalyptic, Savage, Sick Skills, Smokin' Sexy Style) rise with varied, close range combos and kills, fall one or two tiers on a single hit, decay when you stop attacking, and give diminishing returns for repeated moves; the rank feeds red orb payout, the end of mission grade and, in DMC5, the music.

### Cited Findings
- "The ranking starts at D and progresses through C, B, and A", then S, SS and SSS; every hit and kill gives points scaled by the move's difficulty; "Shooting a demon from a distance is going to give next to no style"; variety is the main strategy; "Getting hit by a single attack from an enemy will drop the style rank down one or two tiers"; it is "exceptionally easy to lose it all"; "The style level that players reach during a mission will impact the rank they receive at the end of a mission" (guide) ([Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/110322/stylish-points-and-style-ranks-in-devil-may-cry-5))
- Rank names "Smokin' Sexy Style", "Sick Skills", "Savage", "Apocalyptic", "Badass", "Crazy", "Dismal"; "Your Stylish rank rises whenever you attack an opponent using various combos without taking damage from enemies"; "Your Stylish Points go down whenever you are hit by enemies or when you're unsuccessful in maintaining your combos"; red orbs earned depend on how well you fare; "Four kinds of music are available, depending on your rank" (guide) ([Samurai Gamers](https://samurai-gamers.com/devil-may-cry-5/stylish-rank-system/))
- The rank slowly deteriorates when you are not attacking, higher ranks decay faster, and a hit usually costs "a whole letter or more" (guide, search snippet) ([Gamer Guides](https://gamerguides.com/devil-may-cry-5/guide/walkthrough/main-missions/main-mission-2))
- Taunting raises the style rank and can stop it dropping between attacks (guides, search snippet) ([Samurai Gamers, improve rank](https://samurai-gamers.com/devil-may-cry-5/how-to-improve-your-stylish-rank/); [GameWith](https://gamewith.net/devil-may-cry-5/article/show/8284))
- "Repeating the same optimal attacks will result in diminishing scores on the style meter"; in DMC3 "It is thus possible for a player to ignore the rank system", because upgrades are rarely essential; currency rewards can add "a negative feedback loop that will make the game more difficult if the player does not aim for high ranks"; defensive play tends to stay reactive while expression lives in offense (developer article by Kevin Wong, 5 Dec 2023) ([Game Developer, Style Meters and Play Aesthetics in Death of a Wish](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/style-meters-and-play-aesthetics-in-death-of-a-wish))
- Death of a Wish's variant: the meter "rewards points for landing attacks while avoiding damage", long combos add multipliers, each encounter has S, A, B and C thresholds, and "Finish a fight with an S-rank, and your corruption will go down by 3%"; "It is not enough to survive in Death of a Wish, players must thrive" (developer article) ([Game Developer, Wong](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/style-meters-and-play-aesthetics-in-death-of-a-wish))

### Inferences
- For a squad game the DMC lessons translate as: score variety (different orders, different fighters, different answers), score risk (perfect Holds, fighting near the chief), drop the rank hard on hits, decay it when nothing happens, and shrink the points for repeating the same order back to back.
- DMC's style is offensive; Sekiro rewards defence. If parries are the centre, a perfect Hold must also feed the style rank or the two systems will pull against each other (Wong notes defence tends to stay outside expression).
- Wong's warning matters for a roguelike: if style rewards gate progression, weak players spiral. DMC3 kept the rank ignorable for progress.
- DMC5 tying music layers to rank is a cheap, strong feedback channel.

### Gaps
- No official Capcom point values, thresholds, decay rates or repetition penalty timers found; the DMC5 Design Philosophy video was not transcribed; no primary Kamiya or Itsuno statement on the meter's intent found; the DMC Fandom wiki was blocked (402).

## 5. Into the Breach and Slay the Spire: showing the enemy's next move

### Takeaway
Both games show the enemy's next action exactly, which turns each fight into a puzzle and makes every loss feel like the player's fault. Slay the Spire started without intents and added them because hidden moves did not suit permadeath, and playtesters preferred exact numbers to vague icons.

### Cited Findings
- Justin Ma (Subset Games): "We wanted to make something where every death felt like your own fault"; "limited reliance on random chance in Into the Breach helps make the player feel in control"; with telegraphed, non random attacks "the game starts to feel like a puzzle"; "even an experienced player can still appreciate solving fresh puzzles every time they play the game"; they wanted players to "react to a situation and adapt their strategy" instead of settling on one tactic; "A single additional enemy turns a battle from 'a fun challenge' to 'completely impossible'"; on failure it should be "very clear how or why you failed so that you can feel like you can improve" (developer, Feb 2018) ([Game Developer, Road to the IGF: Into the Breach](https://gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/road-to-the-igf-subset-games-i-into-the-breach-i-))
- Every Vek attack is shown before you act, with arrows marking exactly which tiles take damage (secondary, search snippet) ([games.gg](https://games.gg/into-the-breach/))
- "Originally, enemies did not show their next intended action"; "this design did not mesh well with the roguelike nature of permadeath"; a first "Next Turn" preview by selecting targets struggled with several enemies; the team moved to "Intents" icons, at first with ranges instead of exact numbers; Giovannetti worried too many numbers would overwhelm, but playtesters found exact numbers more engaging, no longer had to memorise symbols, and planned new strategies (encyclopedia, citing Rock Paper Shotgun and Ars Technica) ([Wikipedia, Slay the Spire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slay_the_Spire))
- PC Gamer's Best Design 2019 credits "how transparent Slay the Spire makes information and enemy intents, which stacks some certainty into each shuffle" (press, search snippet) ([PC Gamer](https://pcgamer.com/best-design-2019-slay-the-spire))
- In Slay the Spire 2 an attacking enemy always shows "the exact damage coming in", and multiple intent icons now sit side by side instead of stacked (guide, search snippet) ([Untapped, StS2 intent guide](https://sts2.untapped.gg/guides/how-to-read-enemy-intent))

### Inferences
- A chief intent can work in a real time squad game as an icon over the chief showing the next move, its exact damage and its target, with the cast bar giving the timing. Exact numbers beat ranges, per the StS playtests.
- Into the Breach's "one more enemy" sensitivity supports limiting how many telegraphed threats run together.
- Showing intent shifts skill from reaction to planning, which suits a game where the player gives orders rather than swinging the sword.

### Gaps
- Giovannetti's GDC talk text was not retrieved; no data on how intent display changed win rates or retention.

## 6. Riot / League of Legends VFX clarity, and similar readability rules

### Takeaway
Riot's rule is that "the amount of attention an ability grabs should match its level of importance", where importance means damage, crowd control, dodgeability and impact; telegraphs use bright visuals plus audio windups, hitboxes must match what is drawn, and noise is kept minimal. Blizzard's 2026 raid team is landing on the same rules: cut noise, glow what matters, use one standard indicator set.

### Cited Findings
- Clarity is "the ability to understand what's happening in League and respond to it"; goals "gameplay should be clearly conveyed", "always preserve hierarchy", "noise should be kept minimal" (official) ([League of Legends, Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/))
- "the amount of attention an ability grabs should match its level of importance"; importance comes from damage, CC strength and duration, dodgeability and impact on play; Zoe's Q and Taric's stun are good examples; do not overstate dodgeability (Arcane Comet's big ground circle suggested more dodge room than really exists) (official) ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/))
- Hitboxes that do not match effects are a main frustration source; telegraphs use bright visuals and audio wind ups (Taric's stun); "Projectiles should convey the direction they're traveling, even without motion" (official) ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/))
- Silhouettes are "the single most important thing for champion recognition in League"; "Every ability should be at least as clear as the base skin because skins should never be pay-to-win"; a distracting recall was fixed and champions must now stay in the recall circle for at least two seconds (official) ([Clarity in League](https://www.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/news/dev/clarity-in-league/))
- 2017 VFX style guide: visual clarity for gameplay is the first goal, and visual impact should represent gameplay impact, so a basic attack should not look as big as an ultimate (official post, search snippet; the post now redirects) ([League Nexus, VFX style guide post](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/en-us/2017/10/dev-leagues-vfx-style-guide/); [style guide PDF](https://nexus.leagueoflegends.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/10/VFX_Styleguide_final_public_hidpjqwx7lqyx0pjj3ss.pdf))
- Summary of that guide: split every effect into primary elements (high value, clear silhouette, sharp focal point, carries the gameplay signal) and secondary elements (lower value, softer, flavour); "avoid using absolute extremes (0% pure black or 100% pure white)" and save the brightest values for the impact flash; saturation ladder of environment lowest, UI controlled, VFX highest; timing in anticipation, impact and dissipation, with linear timing curves called harmful; lingering particles clutter combat and hide targets. The figure "impact peak within the first quarter of the effect's lifespan" is the summary site's, not confirmed as Riot's (secondary summary) ([VFX Apprentice, 10 League VFX tips](https://www.vfxapprentice.com/blog/10-league-of-legends-vfx-design-tips))
- Blizzard 2026: cut noise so the important things stand out, glow plus a pulsing creature VFX for importance, colour change as an action cue, and one standardized indicator set going forward (secondhand) ([Seramate, 7 Aug 2026](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/07/blizzard-interview-addon-design-1f1929d1); [Seramate, 22 Aug 2026](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/22/ellesmere-encounter-design-interview-1f19e39f))

### Inferences
- One fixed vocabulary for the 2.5D battle screen: parryable chief hit (one colour, one sound), perilous hit that needs another answer (a second colour and a distinct sound, like 危), interruptible cast (plain bar), uninterruptible cast (shielded bar). Attention ranks: chief signature move above squad skills above minion hits.
- Keep effect bodies in middle values and reserve the brightest frame for the impact, so the hit moment reads at once.
- Never let an effect overstate what the player can do about it (Riot's Arcane Comet lesson): a big ring must mean real room to act.

### Gaps
- The style guide PDF itself was not read; its details here come from a third party summary and search snippets. The fetched Riot article has no explicit enemy versus ally colour rules.

## 7. Parry in non action genres (Clair Obscur: Expedition 33, Sea of Stars, Mario RPGs, Hi-Fi Rush)

### Takeaway
The best turn based examples put a timed press on top of a system that still works without it: Expedition 33 offers a forgiving dodge (bigger window, no reward) beside a tight parry (+1 AP and a counter), plus jump and gradient counters with their own cues; Sea of Stars makes timed blocks shave damage and adds Locks, a turn based interrupt. Expedition 33's main criticisms are that parry becomes mandatory and some tells are ambiguous.

### Cited Findings

Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 (2025)
- Parry: press just before the hit; enemies make "useless movements" to throw off timing; a correct parry prevents damage and grants 1 AP; parrying at the end of the window triggers a counter that "can also stop enemy combos midway"; parries have a "much tighter window" than dodges (guide) ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/how-to-parry-dodge-jump-in-clair-obscur-expedition-33/amp/))
- Dodge has "bigger windows", is easier against complex combos, and gives no AP and no counter unless a Picto (such as Dodger) changes that; some attacks "can only be dodged by jumping" and are flagged by a large golden mark, with attack after the jump giving a counter; each enemy has an audio cue for when its attack is in range, and most parry cues sound like metal clashing (guide) ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/how-to-parry-dodge-jump-in-clair-obscur-expedition-33/amp/))
- Parries share the timing of perfect dodges but add one AP each; a multi hit move must be parried on every hit to earn the counter; gradient attacks turn the screen grey and slow the swing to a stop before it resumes, and a Gradient Counter pressed as it lands prevents damage and counters automatically; the slow motion can throw timing off (guides, search snippet) ([Automaton, dodges, parries, jumps and Gradient Counters](https://automaton-media.com/en/tips/clair-obscur-expedition-33-how-to-get-the-hang-of-dodges-parries-jumps-and-gradient-counters/))
- Patch 1.3.0 (reported 10 June 2025), Story difficulty only: "Parry and dodge windows increased by 40%" and "Incoming damage reduced by 10% to 50%", which the outlet reads as damage taken going from 60% to 50% of normal; no frame values published (patch notes via press) ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/clair-obscur-expedition-33-patch-adjusts-dodge-and-parry-timing/))
- Difficulties are Story, Expeditioner (default, as intended) and Expert, where one guide says windows are "nearly nonexistent" (guide, search snippet) ([Deltia's Gaming](https://deltiasgaming.com/?p=224511))
- A Steam commenter citing a Nexus mod claims the window is the same on normal and expert, "0.15th of a second or 9 frames" (community, unverified, search snippet) ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1903340/discussions/0/592895445665076373))
- Sekiro players find the timing different, and pressing right as the attack lands does not work (guide, search snippet) ([Push Square](https://pushsquare.com/guides/clair-obscur-expedition-33-how-to-improve-parry-timing-and-make-parrying-easier))
- Player complaints: the window is "TINY" and it is "not clear WHEN"; parry "is 100 percent damage reduction and a counter attack ... so strong it becomes mandatory"; some jumps are attacks and some just reposition; energy weapons hide the animation; late game combo length is "absolutely bonkers"; it "kinda kills any idea of balanced build, tanky chars"; defenders call it the best turn based system in over a decade and say dodge is the forgiving route (community, search snippet) ([Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1903340/discussions/0/592895445665149150))

Sea of Stars (2023)
- Almost all enemy attacks can be blocked with a timed press to reduce damage, usually the moment before the hit; timed hits boost your own attacks (community wiki, search snippet) ([Sea of Stars wiki, Combat](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat))
- Locks: an enemy preparing a strong move shows lock icons for damage types (blunt, slash, sun, moon and so on) and a turn counter; each matching hit breaks a lock and weakens the move; breaking all locks cancels it and delays the enemy's next turn; boosting with Live Mana breaks more locks per hit; timed hits can add extra hits that help (community wiki and guides, search snippet) ([Sea of Stars wiki, Combat](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat); [BlueStacks guide](https://www.bluestacks.com/blog/game-guides/sea-of-stars/soss-beginners-guide-en.html))
- A player reports the developers said in a Q&A they would not add a cue showing exactly when to press (community, unverified) ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1244090/discussions/0/3805028677406464110))

Paper Mario: The Thousand Year Door
- Superguard: a press "within a three frame window of the attack's impact" negates damage, and some enemies use deceptive timing to make players guard early or late (commentary, search snippet) ([Set Side B](https://setsideb.com/?p=7214)); an older StrategyWiki revision gives about 1/4 s for superguard versus about 1/2 s for a normal guard (conflicting, likely loose) ([StrategyWiki diff](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Special:MobileDiff/740599)); the 2024 remake adjusted action command and superguard timing (commentary, search snippet) ([Set Side B](https://setsideb.com/?p=7787))

Hi-Fi Rush: see section 3 (hits always land on the beat; timing only changes the reward).

### Inferences
- Expedition 33 is the closest match to a squad game: party based, defensive presses against telegraphed attacks. What helps: a safe option with a wider window and no reward (dodge), a tight option that pays a resource (AP) and a counter, exception attacks with their own input and their own cue (golden mark for jump, grey slow motion for gradient), per enemy audio cues, and an accessibility knob (+40% windows on Story). What hurts: feints and fake movements when the exact moment is unclear, long late game combos that turn parry into a chore, and a parry so strong it becomes mandatory.
- Sea of Stars Locks are WoW interrupts in turn based form: several damage types can each break part of the lock, partial success partially weakens the move, and full success cancels it. That is the opposite of one key one lock.
- The 0.5 s Hold window is guard sized, not perfect parry sized: it is close to the older Paper Mario normal guard figure (about 1/2 s) and far wider than a superguard (3 frames, about 50 ms) or the unverified E33 figure (150 ms). If E33's base really is 0.15 s, its Story mode is about 0.21 s. A tiered Hold (wide window to block, narrow window to parry) matches how these games pay out.

### Gaps
- No official Expedition 33 frame data and no Sandfall interview on parry design were found.
- No Mario and Luigi or Super Mario RPG data was found.
- No Sea of Stars developer statement on timing windows was found.

## 8. Avoiding one key one lock bosses and keeping answers varied

### Takeaway
The pattern that recurs is one universal answer plus a small, loudly flagged set of exceptions, each needing a different answer read from the animation (Sekiro's kanji, Expedition 33's jump and gradient cues, God of War's ring colours), combined with several tools that can open the same lock (Sekiro's shuriken, Sea of Stars damage types, WoW class kits), and later fights that change patterns rather than adding damage (Furi, Hades, WoW).

### Cited Findings
- Sekiro: deflect answers most attacks; perilous attacks share one alarm and need thrust, sweep or grab specific answers; many perilous attacks can instead be interrupted with a Shuriken; Flame Vent stops posture recovery while the enemy burns; the Umbrella covers terror and fire buildup (official and community, section 1) ([Activision blog](https://blog.activision.com/sekiro/2019-03/Sekiro-Shadows-Die-Twice-Strategies-Surviving-Your-First-Few-Hours-as-the-One-Armed-Wolf); [Fextralife, Deflection](https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Deflection))
- Kubodera: the 危 symbol is a "crutch" when patterns are subtle; teach one pattern at a time, since "It's much harder to chunk two different patterns at the same time" (developer article) ([Game Developer, Kubodera 2022](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/designing-for-difficulty-readability-in-arpgs))
- Expedition 33: dodge, parry, jump and gradient counter each have their own cue, and Pictos can make dodging pay (guides, section 7) ([Beebom](https://beebom.com/how-to-parry-dodge-jump-in-clair-obscur-expedition-33/amp/))
- God of War Ragnarök: red ring means dodge, yellow means parry, blue means shield bash interrupt (guides, section 3) ([PlayerAssist](https://playerassist.com/meaning-colored-ring-attacks-god-war-ragnarok/))
- Sea of Stars: a lock can be broken by several damage types; partial breaks weaken the move (community wiki, section 7) ([Sea of Stars wiki, Combat](https://seaofstars.fandom.com/wiki/Combat))
- Monster Hunter: an early monster should not need "every tool in your deck"; a weapon that cannot guard gets other options instead of a guard (developer) ([Vice](https://vice.com/en/article/the-developers-of-monster-hunter-explain-what-its-like-to-build-monsters))
- Rocksteady: combat "focuses on selecting the right move" (developer, search snippet) ([Siliconera](https://www.siliconera.com/the-improvements-to-batman-arkham-citys-combat-system/))

### Inferences
- In a squad game, answers can vary along five axes the sources cover: who answers (the targeted fighter's Hold, like a tank buster), when (perfect versus early Hold), where (spread, soak, step out, like WoW ground markings), what (interrupt a cast with a specific order, like WoW kicks or Sea of Stars locks) and greed (attack instead of hold while the chief kneels, Sekiro's deathblow window).
- Keep Hold as the universal answer, flag at most two or three exception families per chief with one shared alarm, and teach each exception alone (a trash minion or the chief's first phase) before combining them.
- Repeat fights in a roguelike need pattern remixes on later loops (Hades Extreme Measures, Furier), not bigger numbers.
- Avoid checks that escalate forever and fights that keep adding intermissions (Blizzard's 2026 cuts).
